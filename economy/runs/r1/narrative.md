# Run r1 — summary

## 1. This play-through in three sentences

Machines came to do about half of all desk work, yet 17 million more people had jobs in 2036 than in 2026 and unemployment was under 3%. Pay after prices rose by a third and rents fell by nearly a quarter, so the typical household and the poorest fifth both ended about a third better off. The split of the pie went the other way: workers' share of national income fell from 52.8% to 45.8%.

## 2. The hidden conditions and the events

The players were dealt a kind economy without knowing it. Desk-work AI improved fast and firms adopted it quickly, but robots ran about seven years behind. Cheaper things drew in a lot of extra buying, many new kinds of human work appeared, competition passed most savings on as lower prices, lost wages did not snowball, and homes were physically easy to build. The one harsh condition: left alone, pay would be bid down below its old trend. Washington could act quickly under a growth-first coalition; the central bank was strict; the big states refused to help with housing throughout.

Events drawn by dice: a flat year for AI progress (2027), a factory-housing breakthrough (2028), a data-centre building boom (2029), new jobs that proved automatable (2030), a price war from free open AI models (2031–32), a land squeeze in boom cities (2033–34) and a hit new industry (2035–36).

## 3. The story

- **2027.** Employers automate routine office work but shrink by not replacing leavers, so unemployment does not move. Congress passes housing incentives.
- **2028–29.** A boom. Rents fall 5% in 2028, new firms hire heavily, and the building boom adds about 1.3m jobs.
- **2030.** The only bad year. The building boom stops and the central bank tightens: 5.7m jobs go, only 2.4m of them to automation. Employment falls by 2.2m, though unemployment only reaches 3.7% because most leavers stop looking.
- **2031–32.** Free open models end the AI labs' lock-in contracts. 10.6m jobs are created against 6.9m lost. A small levy on AI profits fails.
- **2033–34.** Congress overrides local zoning in squeezed cities. Voters still hand Congress to the worker-first coalition (a 15% chance).
- **2035–36.** The hit new industry offers so much work that 3.7m openings go unfilled. A 2-point levy on AI profits passes.

## 4. Answer 1 — jobs

Over ten years 37.6m jobs were destroyed and 47.9m created: about 1.3 created per job lost. Roughly 30m openings were new kinds of work and about 18m came from cheaper output selling more. Unemployment fell from 4.1% to 2.9% and never rose above its starting level; participation rose from 61.6% to 64.6%; the working week shortened by 1.5 hours.

A Jevons effect held for the economy as a whole, but not for office workers. Offices lost 19.3m jobs and regained 16.1m, ending 1.3m smaller. Growth was in new industries (4.6m to 13.9m), care and building. It held because four dealt conditions lined up: strong demand, many new tasks, savings passed into prices, and late robots.

## 5. Answer 2 — living standards

Median household income rose to 135 (2026 = 100; the no-AI trend gives about 109). The bottom fifth reached 132.5. The top 1% share rose from 20% to 22.9%. Housing costs fell 23% and professional services 13%; goods fell only 5% and health barely moved. Poverty fell from 12.9% to 7.3%, homelessness from 22 to 12 per 10,000. The deficit fell from 6% to 4% of GDP and debt from 100% to 90%, with no new cash transfers at all.

## 6. What drove it

Mostly the hidden conditions: this result is at or beyond the top of 1,000 dice-only runs. Three decisions mattered. Employers chose attrition and profit-sharing, which, with union gains, cancelled the dealt pay squeeze. Washington passed four housing bills, getting round the states. The labs dropped lock-in pricing in 2031. Dice made 2030 bad and 2035–36 exceptional, changing timing more than direction.

## 7. Where the players were unrealistic

All five sat inside one polite bargain for a decade. Employers volunteered profit-sharing and never ran mass layoffs; labs offered to be taxed; households never panicked or struck in earnest. Real actors would have pressed their advantage harder.
