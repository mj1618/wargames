# World state — January 2027

A fictional economic policy exercise set in the United States. AI labs are the fictional composites from `world/cast.md` (Meridian, Helix, Lumen, Vanta). No real individuals appear. Everything below is public knowledge in the game world. The hidden conditions dealt to each play-through are **not** here.

## The technology

AI systems now do multi-hour professional tasks on their own and write most of the code at the labs that build them. Capability is improving quickly and nobody serious expects that to stop. About one firm in five uses AI in production (more than a third of large firms); AI assists roughly 6% of all work hours and saves perhaps 1.5% of them. Most business use hands whole tasks to the machine instead of assisting a person.

Robots are behind. Humanoids are cheap (from about $16,000) and shipping in the tens of thousands, mostly to labs and pilots; useful commercial humanoid work so far adds up to a few dozen worker-years. The exceptions are structured settings: one large online retailer runs more than a million warehouse robots and plans to automate most of its operations, and driverless taxis give about a million paid rides a week in a dozen cities — still under 1% of trips. Construction, care and skilled trades are barely touched.

## What people argue about

- **Is displacement real yet?** Total employment looks normal. But hiring of 22–25-year-olds in the most AI-exposed occupations is down about 19% relative to others, recent graduates have higher unemployment than the average worker, and "AI" became the most-cited reason for announced layoffs in 2026. Sceptics answer that the hiring slump pre-dates the chatbots and that some firms blame AI for cuts they would have made anyway.
- **Where do the savings go?** Token prices keep falling and open-weight models compete hard; margins are also at a record and a handful of firms dominate.
- **Housing.** Rents, not poverty rates, explain where homelessness is high. Building has been getting less productive for fifty years, and land and zoning dominate costs in the expensive metros. Factory-built and robot-assisted housing exists but is small.
- **Public money.** About 70% of federal revenue rests on wages. Social Security and Medicare are funded by payroll taxes. States must balance budgets.
- **Public mood.** Two-thirds of Americans want AI development slowed. Data-centre power demand is a local fight almost everywhere.

## Politics

The November 2026 midterms have just been held; the new Congress sits this month (its make-up is given in each player's first intel note). Federal elections follow in November 2028 (President and Congress), 2030, 2032, 2034 and 2036. No federal law yet addresses AI and jobs. Existing support is unemployment insurance (about 40% of pay for 26 weeks, patchy coverage), small retraining programmes, and the ordinary safety net.

## What each player sees

Official statistics arrive late and with noise. Company and household players see their own corner more sharply than the national picture. Nobody sees the hidden conditions; they have to infer them from what happens.

## This play-through — public facts at January 2027

- **Who governs.** A growth-first coalition leads the federal government: pro-deployment, tax-averse and sceptical of new entitlements. The opposition is a worker-first coalition that wants protections and transfers and is open to taxing AI profits.
- **States and housing.** The big states are resisting new building; none is moving to override local zoning.
- **The frontier this year.** The labs' new models are cheaper, not much smarter: a flat year at the frontier.

# Record of events

(Appended after each round.)

## 2027

Official figures, end 2027 (provisional): unemployment 3.9%; participation 61.6%; 163.0m jobs, up 0.6m; GDP growth 2.1%; consumer prices slightly lower, housing costs down about 2%; median household income index about 100.5; labour share 52.4%; deficit 6.0% of GDP, debt 102%. Poverty and homelessness for 2027 are not yet published. Announced layoffs totalled roughly 0.46m, with AI the most-cited reason.

The labs raced to sign enterprise customers on bundled multi-year contracts and pledged a joint $2bn-a-year Worker Transition Fund. Large employers said they plan no mass layoffs and shrank through hiring freezes. New firms hired in AI setup, coaching and care. Union drives in logistics, retail and warehouses won little.

Congress passed housing incentives for states that permit building and a small wage-insurance programme that expands automatically if unemployment passes 6%; both begin in 2028. A federal court blocked the executive order requiring quarterly disclosure of AI-related layoffs.

## 2028

Official figures, end 2028 (provisional): unemployment 4.0%; participation 61.7%; 164.3m jobs, up 1.4m; GDP growth 3.1%; housing costs down about 5%, other prices slightly lower; median household income index about 106; labour share 52.1%; deficit 6.0% of GDP, debt 103%. Announced layoffs about 0.71m, AI again the most-cited reason. Now published for 2027: poverty 12.5%, homelessness 21.5 per 10,000.

Factory-built homes won code approval in most states. The labs cut routine-tier prices 40%, kept enterprise customers on multi-year bundles and published workforce data voluntarily. Large employers renewed a no-mass-layoff pledge and shrank by attrition. Organising drives in care, health and trades won contracts.

Congress passed a funded housing programme tied to zoning reform (from 2029) and an AI framework law: layoff disclosure for the largest firms and a temporary standstill on new state AI rules. In November the growth-first coalition kept the presidency and Congress.
