# Orders — employers — round 01 (2027)

## 1. Read of the situation
Profits are a record 14.9% of GDP and labour share a record-low 52.8%, so the board's job is to defend margin without being undercut. Our own books show tools are good at drafting, coding and claims but botch judgement, and a flat frontier means no sudden leap to wait for. The insurer pilot (10% price cut, about 15% more policies) is the one hard datapoint that passing savings on can pay, and rivals are announcing AI programmes weekly.

## 2. Options considered
- Cautious pilots, wait for smarter models: safe, but cedes ground to rivals.
- Normal-to-faster drive in routine functions, quiet headcount attrition: steady margin gain.
- Unconventional: pledge no net layoffs for three years in exchange for no federal deployment rules, funding it by attrition and entry-level freezes.

## 3. Decisions this period
1. **Adoption speed: normal to slightly faster (about +10%)** in routine drafting, coding and claims work only; keep humans on judgement calls. Budget about 1.5% of opex. Why: the gains are real in routine work and rivals are moving, but projects often disappoint, so we skip mass restructuring.
2. **Shed work quietly: cut layoff_share from about 60% to about 40%** by using hiring freezes and not replacing leavers; entry-level hiring stays cut, no top-up of hours. Why: it protects the brand and avoids the AI-layoff headlines and political attention, and it still delivers the headcount saving over the year. Keep hours_share at 10%.
3. **Pricing and pay: copy the insurer pilot selectively.** Roll out price cuts of about 5% in two or three contested product lines where a rival could undercut us, and keep savings elsewhere. Wage sharing: modest profit-sharing for retained staff only, about 1% of payroll. Why: it defends share cheaply and buys retention without hurting margin materially.

## 4. Public statement and messages
**Statement:** "We are deploying AI to make our people more productive and our products cheaper, and we are not planning mass layoffs."

**Messages:**
- To Government: We support deployment-friendly policy and oppose new mandates; we will back a voluntary no-mass-layoff pledge for large employers in return for no deployment rules or AI-specific payroll tax.
- To Frontier labs: We want multi-year enterprise pricing for routine-task models and indemnity for errors.

## 5. What would change my mind
A visible rival share grab from price-cutting, or a credible bill taxing AI profits or restricting layoffs, would push us to a faster drive or a harder lobbying stance respectively.
