# Sitrep — round 01 (January–December 2027)

**Headline: a quiet year on the surface; the churn is underneath.**

**Jobs.** About 1.15m jobs were automated away, 0.6m of them in offices; 1.06m were created, mostly from cheaper output drawing more customers and from new kinds of work. With population growth, employment rose to 163.0m. Unemployment 4.07%; employment rate 59.07% (59.1% a year ago). Machines now do 3.2% of desk work, of 10.4% they could do.

**Pay and prices.** Real pay +1.6%. Median real household income 101.5 (2026 = 100); bottom fifth 101.4. Housing costs fell 2.1%; other prices barely moved. Labour's share slipped to 52.4%; top-1% share 20.2%.

**Business.** GDP +2.1%. Labs raced for enterprise seats and locked customers in. Employers automated routine work and shrank by not replacing leavers, so cuts made few headlines.

**Government.** Congress passed housing incentives and a small wage-insurance programme with an automatic trigger; both start in 2028. A court blocked the AI-layoff disclosure order. Deficit 6.0% of GDP, debt 102%. Poverty 12.5%; homelessness 21.5 per 10,000.

**What mattered most.** Employers choosing attrition over layoffs kept displacement out of the unemployment figure. The court ruling (35% chance) leaves Washington without the layoff data it wanted. The households' organising drive failed (50%).

**What a household would have noticed:** the pay rise went a little further and rent eased where building is allowed, but the graduate in the family still has no first job.
