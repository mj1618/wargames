# Orders — ai-firms — Round 02 (2028)

## 1. Read of the situation
The macro picture is benign for us: unemployment 3.9%, 163.0m jobs (+0.6m), GDP +2.1%, and labour share only slipped to 52.4% — nobody can pin a crisis on AI yet. Our own data says the real story: systems now do roughly twice last year's share of desk work, multi-year bundles are signing well, and the only soft spot is small customers eyeing open weights. It is an election year; the government has offered us no taxes or limits in exchange for clean disclosure and visible price pass-through, and the price of that deal is that no lab-caused layoff headline lands before November.

## 2. Options considered
- Steady pace, hold pricing, bank the bundles: safe, but hands open weights the low end and the frontier to rivals.
- Race hard and pre-empt open weights with a price cut on routine tiers while locking enterprise into 3-year bundles with indemnity.
- Unconventional: offer a profits levy now to buy statutory pre-emption before the election flips. Rejected by majority — the levy is worth more as a chip after November.

## 3. Decisions this period
1. **Pace: race, +20% above baseline.** Desk-work share is doubling; whoever scales enterprise deployment this year owns the installed base. Compute-bound, so prioritise enterprise over consumer.
2. **Pricing: two-tier.** Enterprise: 3-year bundles, premium pricing, with capped error indemnity (up to annual contract value) as the lock-in sweetener employers asked for. Routine-task tier: list prices cut ~40% — denies open weights a foothold and visibly satisfies the government's pass-through ask.
3. **Politics: take the deal.** Voluntarily publish quarterly workforce-impact data on our own definitions despite the court win; lobbying to $200m, split across both coalitions as an election hedge; Worker Transition Fund stays $2bn/yr, with $500m front-loaded into visible retraining in the most AI-exposed metros before November.

## 4. Public statement
"Routine AI work costs 40% less this year, and we will publish quarterly data on how our customers' workforces change — nobody has to force us."

Messages:
- **To federal-gov:** Accepted. 40% routine-tier cut and voluntary quarterly disclosure this year. We'll hold you to no taxes, no limits, and we want state pre-emption drafted for 2029.
- **To employers:** Yes to 3-year routine-task pricing and capped indemnity. In return: shrink by attrition, no mass-layoff announcements before November. Headlines cost us both.

## 5. What would change my mind
A lab breaking ranks on price, or a moratorium/licensing bill polling well enough that the levy must be spent early.
