# Orders — employers — round 02 (2028)

## 1. Read of the situation
Margins rose in 2027 and price cuts won volume, so our quiet-attrition playbook worked: 1.15m positions went (0.6m office) with 0.46m announced layoffs and no scandal. Unemployment is 3.9% and the labour share is 52.4%, so demand is not yet broken and the political heat is low. But wage insurance starts now, federal elections are in November, and factory-built housing will free household budgets, so rules and rivals are the live risks.

## 2. Options considered
- Hold pace: normal adoption, keep freezes and attrition, keep price cuts.
- Accelerate: company-wide drive after the labs' multi-year offer, locking in 2027 pricing.
- Unconventional: sign the lab contracts but with price-down and exit clauses, and pair them with a public "no-layoff, shorter-week" pledge to buy off election-year regulation.

## 3. Decisions this period
1. **Adoption: company-wide drive, about +15% faster than normal, concentrated in routine office and back-office work.** Sign multi-year lab agreements, but only with most-favoured-customer pricing and a 12-month break clause. Rivals are moving, and locking in pricing before the next generation is cheap insurance. Pilots stay cautious in health, education and transport, where liability and union contracts bind.
2. **Shed work quietly: layoff_share 35–40%.** Keep leaving roles unfilled rather than cutting heads; no single announcement above 5,000 people. Raise hours_share to about 15% using the wage-insurance and shorter-week framing where unions are present. Fund it by topping up weekly pay for roughly half of affected staff. Cost is small, and it gives us a story in an election year.
3. **Share the gain: extend 5% price cuts to 4–5 more contested lines (about 1/3 of the savings) and lift profit-sharing to 1.5% of payroll.** It wins share, keeps customers able to buy, and costs a fraction of the record margin.

## 4. Public statement and messages
**Statement:** "Large employers are growing through attrition, sharing gains with staff and customers, and will not run mass layoffs; we ask Congress for stable, predictable rules."

- **To federal government:** We renew the no-mass-layoff pledge, and add shorter-week support, in exchange for no AI-profit tax or deployment mandate before 2029.
- **To AI labs:** We will sign multi-year terms if you add price-down clauses, a break option and indemnity for agent errors.

## 5. What would change my mind
A rival cutting prices harder than we do, a credible AI-profit tax or layoff bill passing, or a visible fall in consumer demand would make me change course.
