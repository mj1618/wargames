# Orders — entrepreneurs — round 02

## Read of the situation

Last year's bet on emerging niches paid off: we placed half a million people into AI-setup, coaching, and care work that barely existed before, and demand still outruns supply. The national economy looks solid (3.9% unemployment, +0.6m jobs, 2.1% GDP growth). But the labs' bundled contracts favour big customers, so our AI costs fell much less than we'd hoped, squeezing our margins.

The housing breakthrough changes the game. Factory-built homes now have code approval in most states and federal incentives are live; building costs per home have fallen sharply. This is exactly where we wanted to be—a sector with massive unmet demand, regulatory barriers suddenly removed, and federal tailwinds.

## Options considered

1. **Stick with people-heavy niches.** Demand still exceeds supply; we have first-mover advantage and rising margins as we scale. Safe.
2. **Pivot to housing.** One-time opportunity with federal tailwinds and cost breakthrough; capital-intensive but potentially huge returns. Risky timing if credit tightens.
3. **Aggressive dual expansion into both niches and housing** (unconventional). Overextend on capital and management attention, but capture two waves at once before incumbents notice.

## Decisions this period

**Decision 1: Moderate dual expansion.** We'll push new firm formation in our existing niches (target: 15% net job creation from new people-heavy services, staying below the 30% ceiling) AND launch a major housing push. Capital: $8–10bn total across both sectors. This captures the housing moment without abandoning our winning niches.

**Decision 2: Housing strategy—factory-built, high-volume.** We'll target affordable/middle housing in fast-growing metros where zoning incentives and cost breakthroughs hit hardest. 60% of new housing effort in three states with strongest federal incentives. This is capital-heavy but turns the code approval and federal support into real market share before large incumbents build their own factories.

**Decision 3: Push on AI costs politically.** The growth-first coalition has been good for us, but bundled contracts locking out small firms are a problem. We'll quietly support worker-first coalition calls for antitrust review of the labs' contract practices, framed as "fair access to AI tools" rather than worker protection. Federal elections in November; this is our moment to split the difference.

## Public statement

"New founders continue to drive job creation in AI-enabled services. We're now backing factory-built housing at scale because code approval and federal incentives make it work—homes built faster, cheaper, and with less waste than ever before."

## Messages

**To:** Federal-Gov  
"We need fair access to frontier AI models for small firms. Bundled contracts lock out startups while favouring incumbents. This isn't about slowing AI—it's about letting the next generation of founders compete."

## What would change my mind

A sharp credit tightening or collapse in housing demand would force us to pull back housing spending and focus on higher-margin, less capital-intensive services.

Expanding cautiously in two directions: our people-heavy niches still have unmet demand and rising margins, which funds a major housing push now that code and incentives align. The labs' contract practices disadvantage us; antitrust pressure on AI access could unlock returns. If credit holds and housing demand is real, we're positioned to capture both waves before incumbents scale. Watching: credit markets, state deployment of housing incentives, the labs' response to antitrust pressure, and whether big firms enter our niches.
