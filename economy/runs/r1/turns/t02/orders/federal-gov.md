# Orders — federal-gov — round 02 (2028, election year)

## 1. Read of the situation
The numbers are kind: unemployment 3.9%, 163.0m jobs (+0.6m), GDP +2.1%, housing costs −2%, receipts on forecast. The soft spots are labour share slipping to 52.4%, 0.46m announced layoffs with AI the most-cited reason, and a deficit stuck at 6.0% with debt at 102%. Rent, not jobs, is the voters' top worry, and factory-built homes have just won code approval in most states — the opening is housing, in an election year.

## 2. Options considered
- Coast: claim credit for 3.9% and pass nothing new.
- Go big on housing and trade pre-emption for statutory disclosure.
- Unconventional: take the labs' offer and negotiate a small profits levy now to fund a pre-election housing cheque.

## 3. Decisions this period
1. **Bill 1 — Housing: funded building with zoning conditions** [`housing_policy` up one step]. About 0.25% of GDP a year from 2029, paid per factory-built home permitted, money only to states and metros that loosen zoning. Deficit-neutral pitch: offsets from existing grant lines, remainder borrowed. Why: rent is the top worry and costs per home just fell. Deregulators win; deficit hawks grumble.
2. **Bill 2 — AI Deployment Framework Act**: federal pre-emption of state deployment rules, paired with statutory quarterly disclosure of AI-related layoffs (replacing the blocked order) [`deploy_regulation` = disclosure]. No levy, no licensing. Why: donors get certainty, we get an answer to "two-thirds want AI slowed" and the data to see trouble early. Security hawks and deregulators win.
3. **No Congress — accept the employers' no-mass-layoff pledge** and stage it publicly; steer a slice of the existing wage-insurance money and the labs' $2bn fund towards first-job placement for graduates. No new spending, `capital_tax_pct` stays 0. Deficit hawks win.

## 4. Public statement
"Jobs are up, rents are coming down, and this year we build: factory-built homes in every state that says yes. AI will be deployed in America under one clear national rule, in the open."

**Messages**
- To AI labs: "Pre-emption is on the floor this year, with disclosure attached. No levy now. Point your $2bn fund at graduate first jobs and make it visible before November."
- To employers: "Deal: no deployment rules, no AI payroll tax. Sign the no-mass-layoff pledge publicly by spring and comply with disclosure."

## 5. What would change my mind
Unemployment above 5%, wage-tax receipts falling short of forecast, or jobs overtaking rent in polling before November would push me to widen unemployment insurance and reopen the levy offer.
