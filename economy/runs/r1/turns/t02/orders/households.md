# Orders — households — round 02 (2028)

**1. Read of the situation.** Pay rose about 1.6% faster than prices and rents are easing where building is allowed, so most of us feel slightly better off, and unemployment at 3.9% is not a crisis. But leavers are not replaced and graduates still cannot land a first job, so the pain is hidden in who never gets hired. Labour share is still 52.4% against record profits, and last year's organising drives won little.

**2. Options considered.**
- Keep broad organising drives in logistics and retail (won little last year).
- Fear-saving and angry protest (no layoffs yet to justify it).
- Unconventional: organise the new employers (care, AI setup and coaching firms, trades) and the "attrition" problem directly, bargaining for backfill guarantees and entry-level hiring quotas rather than layoff bans.

**3. Decisions this period.**
1. **Union pressure, refocused.** Move most of the effort from warehouses to the hiring sectors (care, health, trades, the new small firms), where jobs are plentiful and a roll is likelier to work. Bargaining asks: backfill of leavers, apprenticeship and first-job slots, profit-sharing, a shorter week. Moderate scale, sustained all year so it does not fade.
2. **Spending.** Spend normally and take the easing rents; no fear-saving. Saving rate unchanged, with a small step toward spending (about -0.5 point) if prices stay flat. Most of us have thin savings anyway.
3. **Votes and protest.** Election year. Peaceful, visible demands: fast-track factory-built housing in every metro, wage insurance that works, a real entry-level jobs and apprenticeship programme, and a share of AI profits for workers. Back whichever side commits in writing. The top tenth, who own the shares, will be happy with the status quo and should not be mistaken for us. No boycotts yet.

**4. Public statement.** "Prices are easing and that is welcome, but a job market that stops hiring the young is not a good economy. We want first-job guarantees, a share of the gains, and homes we can afford."

Messages:
- To entrepreneurs: Thanks. Tell us which care, trades and setup firms are hiring and at what pay, and whether you will sign apprenticeship and entry-level commitments.
- To labs: The Worker Transition Fund should pay for first-job apprenticeships, not just retraining people who already lost jobs.

**5. What would change my mind.** A visible wave of layoffs among people we know, or hiring in the new firms turning out to be low-paid and short-lived, would push us toward saving more and disruptive protest.
