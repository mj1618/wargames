# Sitrep — round 02 (January–December 2028)

**Headline: a boom year — more churn, more hiring, cheaper homes; the governing coalition is re-elected.**

**Jobs.** About 1.77m jobs were automated away (0.99m in offices); 2.49m were created, from cheaper output drawing customers, new kinds of work and stronger spending. With population growth, employment rose to 164.3m. Unemployment 3.78%; employment rate 59.33% (59.07% a year ago). Machines now do 6.1% of desk work, of 18.1% they could do.

**Pay and prices.** Real pay +3.4%. Median real household income 105.1 (2026 = 100); bottom fifth 105.0. Housing costs fell 5.1%; other prices edged down. Labour's share slipped to 52.1%; top-1% share 20.3%.

**Business.** GDP +3.1%. Labs raced, cut routine-tier prices 40% and kept enterprise customers locked in. Employers ran company-wide automation drives, shrank by attrition and shorter weeks, and raised profit-sharing. New firms hired heavily and moved into factory-built housing.

**Government.** Congress passed a funded housing programme tied to zoning (starts 2029) and a watered-down AI framework: layoff disclosure for the largest firms, limited pre-emption. Deficit 6.0% of GDP, debt 103%. Poverty 11.7%; homelessness 18.7 per 10,000. The growth-first coalition held the White House and Congress in November.

**What mattered most.** The housing breakthrough plus last year's incentives drove the fall in rents. The households' organising drive in hiring sectors succeeded (50%). The AI bill passed only in part (a 5% band).

**What a household would have noticed:** rent went down, the pay packet stretched further, and the care and trades firms were hiring — though office jobs kept quietly disappearing.
