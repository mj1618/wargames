# Orders — employers — round 03 (2029)

## 1. Read of the situation
Margins are intact: 2028 volumes rose strongly where we cut prices, and about 1.77m positions went (nearly 1.0m office roles) almost entirely by attrition, with GDP +3.1% and unemployment steady at 4.0%. The growth-first coalition is re-elected, so the rule risk we feared (AI-profit tax, deployment rules) has receded, and both labs and government are offering deals that lock in what we want. The risks now are the new layoff-disclosure law, organisers winning backfill clauses in care, health and trades, and a shrinking entry-level pipeline that will draw attention if graduate unemployment keeps rising.

## 2. Options considered
- Keep the 2028 course: steady drive, attrition, selective price cuts.
- Accelerate to mass restructuring now that the election is over (up to 60% faster), taking the layoff headlines.
- Unconventional: fund a joint employer apprenticeship/entry-level guarantee, pre-empting regulation and pairing it with the pledge.

## 3. Decisions this period
1. **Sign both deals.** Take the labs' 3-year routine-task pricing with capped indemnity and shrink by attrition, with no mass-layoff announcements before November. Sign the government's no-mass-layoff pledge publicly by spring and comply with disclosure. This costs little because attrition is already our method, and it buys no deployment rules and no AI payroll tax for the term.
2. **Adoption speed: company-wide drive, about +20% faster than normal, concentrated in office and routine work.** Layoff_share stays at 30-35%, through freezes and non-replacement. Hours_share rises to about 15-20%, shaving hours rather than heads in contested lines. Keep health, transport and education on cautious pilots because of liability and union limits.
3. **Pass-through and pay.** Extend price cuts of about 5% into 5-6 more contested lines, since volume gains justified the earlier cuts. Raise profit-sharing to about 1.5-2% of payroll. Hold a small entry-level cohort of about 30,000 hires across the sector as cheap political insurance.

## 4. Public statement and messages
Statement: "We are investing in AI to lower prices and grow output, and we will keep our no-mass-layoff pledge by adjusting through hiring, not firing."
- To AI labs: Accepted, 3-year routine pricing with capped indemnity. We will shrink by attrition and make no mass-layoff announcements before November.
- To federal government: We will sign the pledge publicly by spring and comply with disclosure. In return, no deployment rules and no AI payroll tax.

## 5. What would change my mind
A rival announcing mass layoffs without penalty, or a credible AI-profit tax or layoff bill with real vote support, would push me toward faster restructuring or toward pre-emptive concessions.
