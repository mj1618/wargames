# Orders — entrepreneurs — turn 03

## Read of the situation

We hired 0.9m people in 2028—nearly double 2027—and margins are recovering as routine-tier AI costs fell 40%. But staff are getting harder to find, squeezing wages. Factory-built homes are selling as fast as permitted; federal money now follows each home where zoning loosens, and housing codes have approved this model in most states. The growth-first coalition was re-elected and Congress passed a housing programme tied to zoning reform—this is our moment.

## Options considered

- **Max-out housing play:** Pour everything into factory-built homes (30% new-job creation). Capital-intensive, federal-backed, and undercuts incumbents on price.
- **Shift to AI-only firms:** More profit per founder, almost no jobs, and we undercut incumbents harder—better margins when staff are scarce.
- **Balanced expansion:** Grow 15% on people-heavy (care, trades) but shift 40% of new firms to AI-only setups; fund housing selectively.

## Decisions this period

1. **Expand cautiously but strategically—15% new-job creation (people-heavy care and trades).** Staff are harder to find, so we don't max out. But demand is real and margins are good. We'll hire where trust and physical presence matter most. ($4bn new capital, 150k new hires expected.)

2. **Scale factory-built housing where zoning permits—20% of new firm investment.** Federal money follows each home; we're positioned to lead this before incumbents scale. This is capital-light relative to returns. ($1.2bn, targeting 80k homes in high-zoning-reform states.)

3. **Shift 30% of remaining new firms to AI-only setups (coaching, setup, light content).** Margins are better; we capture niches before incumbents notice. Almost no jobs created by these firms, but high profit per founder. ($500m, ~20k founders supported.)

## Public statement and messages

**Public:** "We're doubling down on hiring in care, trades and housing where demand is real and rules are loosening. We'll commit to apprenticeship and entry-level pathways in states that match federal housing incentives."

**To households:** "Apprenticeship and entry-level roles open in care (estimated 30k), trades (40k), and housing construction/supervision (20k). Typical entry pay $18–24/hr with advancement. Housing firms will post specific roles by Q2."

**To federal government:** "Housing zoning reform is working. States that loosen further will see capital flow. We're ready to scale if credit holds."

## What would change my mind

A credit crunch or discovery that big firms are entering our niches at lower price would force a retreat.
