# Sitrep — round 03 (January–December 2029)

**Headline: the boom runs hot — 2.5m more people in work, unemployment 3.2%, and automation speeds up underneath.**

**Jobs.** About 2.37m jobs were automated away (1.35m in offices); 4.22m were created, from new kinds of work, cheaper output and the investment surge. Employment rose to 166.8m. Unemployment 3.23%; employment rate 59.99% (59.33% a year ago). Machines now do 9.9% of desk work, of 26.2% they could do.

**Pay and prices.** Real pay +3.5%. Median real household income 109.3 (2026 = 100); bottom fifth 108.7. Housing costs fell another 2.6%; other prices edged down. Labour's share held at 52.1%; top-1% share 20.3%.

**Business.** GDP +4.3%. Labs raced harder and signed five-year exclusive bundles. Employers pushed automation faster, still by attrition. New firms hired about 1.6m people.

**Government.** Congress made national pre-emption of state AI rules permanent, widened layoff disclosure to firms over 1,000 staff, and fast-tracked build-out permits. An antitrust inquiry into bundled AI contracts opened and survived a court challenge. Deficit 5.8% of GDP, debt 102.7%. Poverty 10.9%; homelessness 16.9 per 10,000.

**What mattered most.** The build-out boom (event) added about 1.3m jobs through extra spending — it does not repeat. The organising drive succeeded again (50%), doubling workers' bargaining weight.

**What a household would have noticed:** anyone who wanted a job could get one, rent fell again, and employers were the ones complaining they could not find staff.
