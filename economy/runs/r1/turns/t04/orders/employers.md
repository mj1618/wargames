# Orders — employers — round 04 (2030)

## 1. Read of the situation
Boom year: unemployment 3.1%, GDP +4.3%, and our own books show vacancies hard to fill with pay rising 3.5% above prices. Labour share is flat at 52.1% and margins hold, but 2.37m positions went in 2029 (1.35m office) by attrition, so shrinkage is working without scandal. Rule risk is low (no profit tax, pre-emption permanent), but the antitrust inquiry into bundles and the widened disclosure rule (firms over 1,000 staff) mean quiet attrition is now more visible, and a November 2030 midterm is coming.

## 2. Options considered
- Keep the course: normal-to-fast adoption, attrition, keep the pledge.
- Mass restructuring (up to 60% faster) while labour is tight and rules are friendly.
- Unconventional: sign the lab bundle but refuse exclusivity on the agent layer. Take the price-down and break clause, run two vendors to protect against the antitrust risk and lock-in.

## 3. Decisions this period
1. **Adoption and shedding.** Company-wide drive, about +20% adoption, concentrated in office and routine work, with health and transport kept cautious (liability, union contracts). Layoff_share stays 30%. We keep shrinking by freezes and attrition, since tight labour makes backfill costly anyway. Why: tight labour raises the cost of staying manual, and attrition keeps us under the pledge and disclosure radar.
2. **Hours and pay.** hours_share to 20% on our own, with weekly pay topped up for the lowest-paid staff. Profit-sharing 2% of payroll. Put the shorter-week support in writing for government as requested. Why: it is cheap now, buys retention in a tight market, and answers the "put it in writing" ask before November.
3. **Vendors and prices.** Accept the five-year indexed price-down bundle with the year-three break, but split workloads across two labs (no exclusive agent layer). Keep price cuts in 5-6 more contested lines. Hire about 30k entry-level via First Job placements. Why: wins share, keeps leverage, and gives a visible youth-jobs offset.

## 4. Public statement and messages
Statement: "We will keep our no-mass-layoff pledge through the election, expand shorter-week options, and keep hiring young people through First Job."
- To federal government: Pledge held through November 2030; shorter-week support in writing attached. In return we ask no new deployment rules and no profit tax.
- To AI labs: Will sign terms with the year-three break, but not exclusivity; we will multi-source given the antitrust inquiry.

## 5. What would change my mind
A post-midterm worker-first surge with an AI-profit tax or layoff bill, or a rival's mass restructuring that wins share, would push me to accelerate or to renegotiate the pledge.
