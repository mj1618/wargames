# Orders — entrepreneurs — round 04

## Read of the situation
End-2029 figures show tight labour: unemployment 3.1%, we hired 1.6m people, but staff are very hard to find and pay is rising 3.5% faster than prices. The new-work mirage warning is real—people-heavy services we scaled into face automation risk. Big states still block zoning reform for housing despite federal incentives and permits.

## Options considered
1. Hold the line: maintain 15% people-heavy, 30% AI-only split and absorb wage pressure via pricing power.
2. Pivot hard to AI-only (higher margin, no hiring wall) and wind down people-heavy over 2 years.
3. Bet on zoning breaking: commit capital to housing and wait for states to move.

## Decisions this period

**Decision 1: Pivot AI-only mix to 50%, slow people-heavy to 5%.**  
The wage spiral is unsustainable; we're losing margin on care/trades businesses faster than demand fills them. AI-only firms have no wage exposure. Scale from ~10% of new startups to half our new-business budget (roughly $3-4bn capital deployed). This protects returns when labour supply doesn't loosen.

**Decision 2: Pause factory-built housing expansion.**  
Zoning reform has not materialised despite antitrust inquiry and federal money. We've captured available permitting. Hold existing housing commitments but don't add capital until states move; redeploy that capital to AI-only startups.

**Decision 3: Double down on antitrust pressure.**  
The inquiry is open and labs' bundled contracts still throttle our AI access. Push Congress for faster resolution; support cost-based licensing. This is our only leverage on input prices.

## Public statement
"The market speaks. Founders are pivoting to AI-native businesses because humans cost too much and states won't fix zoning. We'll build what pays; policy can catch up or watch opportunity shift."

## Messages
*To: Unions* — "We're shifting where jobs go. Organise AI-only startup teams if you want a seat at the table."

*To: Large employers* — "You're seeing the same wage math. Don't outbid each other; your business model doesn't depend on it."

## What would change my mind
Sustained zoning reform in California/Texas or a recession that breaks the wage spiral.
