# Orders — employers — round 05 (2031–2032)

## 1. Read of the situation
Margins widened while 5.7m positions went in 2030, mostly by attrition, and vacancies fill easily again, so labour is cheap and our bargaining position is strong. Free open-weight models and collapsing AI service prices mean automation is now cheap for us but also for every rival and start-up (half of new firms are AI-only), so standing still means being undercut. Labour share is 50.4%, median income is flat at about 109, and 2.3m announced layoffs with the November 2032 election ahead make visibility and demand softness our main risks; our pledge has lapsed.

## 2. Options considered
- Mass restructuring now, banking the margin before the election.
- Hold the course: steady attrition-led shrinkage with shorter weeks and profit-sharing.
- Unconventional: re-sign a pledge, but tie it to attrition floors and write it as a "no-mass-layoff, no-AI-payroll-tax" pact with a sunset after the election, and move AI-price savings into customer price cuts to defend volume.

## 3. Decisions this period
1. **Automate faster, but quietly.** Move adoption_speed to about +25% (office/routine, retail back-office, claims, customer service), using the open-weight price collapse to renegotiate lab contracts down and keep dual-sourcing. Health and transport stay cautious. Layoff_share stays about 30% (attrition and freezes do the rest); no mass restructurings before November 2032. Entry hiring held at about 25k.
2. **Take the federal deal.** Sign by spring: no deployment rules, no AI payroll tax, layoffs held under last year's through the 2032 election. Re-issue a public no-mass-layoff pledge, 2-year term, with attrition carve-out. It is cheap now and buys rule stability.
3. **Share just enough.** Hours_share about 20% (low-pay top-up), profit-sharing 2%, pass roughly half of AI cost savings through as price cuts in 6-8 contested lines to protect volume and win share.

## 4. Public statement and messages
**Statement:** "Large employers will keep hiring and sharing gains with workers; we support the first-job and wage-insurance programme and ask for stable rules."

**To federal government:** Agreed on the deal and the pledge; in return, no AI payroll tax or new deployment rules through 2032, and fast-track the wage-insurance rollout so it absorbs attrition.
**To entrepreneurs:** We set pay and hiring independently; we will not coordinate wages with anyone (antitrust exposure). Compete for the people you need.

## 5. What would change my mind
Sharp demand softness or a visible consumer pullback, a credible profit-tax or layoff bill gaining momentum, or rivals launching mass restructuring that forces us to move first.
