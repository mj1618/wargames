# Orders — federal-gov — round 06 (2033–34)

## 1. Read of the situation
The boom is back and voters rewarded it: unemployment 3.2%, 169.7m jobs (+5.0m), GDP +5.2% a year, median income 119, and we widened our majorities. The soft spots are labour share at 49.9%, layoffs of 2.8m, and receipts sliding to 16.9% of GDP even in a boom. The one thing voters feel is the housing squeeze: AI wealth bidding up land in a few metros and insurers quitting others.

## 2. Options considered
- Coast: no bills, bank the boom until November 2034.
- Housing push: zoning pre-emption in the squeezed metros plus an insurance backstop.
- Unconventional: retry the levy as a small sovereign equity stake in the labs' Fund instead of a tax.

## 3. Decisions this period
1. **Housing bill (bill 1).** Federal pre-emption of local zoning near transit and jobs in the bid-up metros, factory-built homes by right, and a federal reinsurance backstop for home insurance in abandoned markets; about 0.2% of GDP from 2034, paid from existing housing funds plus backstop premiums. Rents and insurance are the only live voter grievance. Deregulators win; deficit hawks get it near budget-neutral.
2. **Deployment guarantee for a bigger Fund (bill 2).** Statutory no-economy-wide-deployment-limits guarantee through 2036, in exchange for the labs raising the Worker Transition Fund to $6bn a year, First Job at $1bn written in, and access parity for small firms made binding. No levy, no payroll tax: Congress killed it and donors hate it. Deregulators and security hawks win.
3. **Executive action, no vote.** Fast-track the wage-insurance rollout (trigger stays 4.5%) and hold new spending flat so the deficit drifts below 5.3%. Deficit hawks win.

## 4. Public statement
"America is working, earning more and paying less. Now we will build the homes and fix the insurance markets so every family can afford to live where the jobs are."

**Messages**
- To AI labs: "No levy this Congress. You get a statutory no-limits guarantee through 2036 if the Fund goes to $6bn a year with First Job and small-firm terms binding. Back the housing bill publicly."
- To employers: "No payroll tax, no deployment rules, wage insurance fast-tracked. Renew the no-mass-layoff pledge through 2034 and keep cuts to attrition."

## 5. What would change my mind
Unemployment above 4.5%, layoffs above 2m in a single year, or receipts below 16.5% of GDP would bring the levy and work-sharing back.
