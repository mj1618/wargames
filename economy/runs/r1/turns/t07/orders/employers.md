# Orders — employers — round 07 (2035-2036)

**1. Read of the situation.** Margins are wider, but we cut a third more positions than in 2031-32, vacancies are hard to fill and pay claims keep rising, and a hit new industry is bidding for the same scarce workers (unemployment 2.8%). The worker-first Congress, with a profit-levy and layoff-bill agenda, and the November 2036 election make rule risk our biggest threat to earnings. Our pledge has lapsed, so visible restructuring now would hand the opposition a bill.

**2. Options considered.**
- Repeat the 2033-34 playbook (attrition, +25% adoption, renew pledge).
- Slow automation sharply to rebuild goodwill and hire against the new industry.
- Unconventional: fund a redeployment guarantee (paid retraining into the new industry plus a pay bridge) in exchange for Congress dropping the levy.

**3. Decisions this period.**
1. **Automate steadily, aimed at hard-to-fill roles.** Adoption about +20% (office/routine), health/transport cautious; layoff_share about 25%; hours_share 20%. Automating existing vacancies displaces no one, so it is cheap and quiet.
2. **Pay to retain scarce staff.** Targeted raises and profit-sharing of 2.5%, concentrated on hard-to-fill roles (no coordination, antitrust). Keep about 30k First Job hires; the pipeline is now our problem. Keep price cuts in 6-8 contested lines to protect share and demand.
3. **Politics.** Renew the no-mass-layoff pledge through Nov 2036 and offer the redeployment guarantee, co-funded with the Worker Transition Fund. Lobby against a profit levy and back the work-sharing bill instead, since shorter weeks cost less than a levy.

**4. Public statement.** "Employers renew their pledge against mass layoffs and will fund paid redeployment so no worker is left behind as AI spreads."
- To federal government: Pledge renewed through 2036 on attrition and redeployment; in return, no payroll tax, no deployment rules, no profit levy.
- To Congress (worker-first leaders): We will back work-sharing and the redeployment guarantee if the levy is not tabled.

**5. What would change my mind.** A rival mass restructuring that wins share, or a levy or layoff bill with veto-proof support, would push us to match the cuts or settle on the best deal available.
