# Public record

A fictional economic policy exercise set in the United States. AI labs are fictional composites (Meridian, Helix, Lumen, Vanta). No real individuals appear. Everything below is public knowledge in the game world. Baseline: January 2027.

## The technology

AI systems now do multi-hour professional tasks on their own and write most of the code at the labs that build them. Capability is improving quickly and nobody serious expects that to stop. About one firm in five uses AI in production (more than a third of large firms); AI assists roughly 6% of all work hours and saves perhaps 1.5% of them. Most business use hands whole tasks to the machine instead of assisting a person.

Robots are behind. Humanoids are cheap (from about $16,000) and shipping in the tens of thousands, mostly to labs and pilots; useful commercial humanoid work so far adds up to a few dozen worker-years. The exceptions are structured settings: one large online retailer runs more than a million warehouse robots and plans to automate most of its operations, and driverless taxis give about a million paid rides a week in a dozen cities — still under 1% of trips. Construction, care and skilled trades are barely touched.

## What people argue about

- **Is displacement real yet?** Total employment looks normal. But hiring of 22–25-year-olds in the most AI-exposed occupations is down about 19% relative to others, recent graduates have higher unemployment than the average worker, and "AI" became the most-cited reason for announced layoffs in 2026. Sceptics answer that the hiring slump pre-dates the chatbots and that some firms blame AI for cuts they would have made anyway.
- **Where do the savings go?** Token prices keep falling and open-weight models compete hard; margins are also at a record and a handful of firms dominate.
- **Housing.** Rents, not poverty rates, explain where homelessness is high. Building has been getting less productive for fifty years, and land and zoning dominate costs in the expensive metros. Factory-built and robot-assisted housing exists but is small.
- **Public money.** About 70% of federal revenue rests on wages. Social Security and Medicare are funded by payroll taxes. States must balance budgets.
- **Public mood.** Two-thirds of Americans want AI development slowed. Data-centre power demand is a local fight almost everywhere.

## Politics

The November 2026 midterms have just been held; the new Congress sits this month (its make-up is given in each player's first intel note). Federal elections follow in November 2028 (President and Congress), 2030, 2032, 2034 and 2036. No federal law yet addresses AI and jobs. Existing support is unemployment insurance (about 40% of pay for 26 weeks, patchy coverage), small retraining programmes, and the ordinary safety net.

## What each player sees

Official statistics arrive late and with noise. Company and household players see their own corner more sharply than the national picture. Nobody sees the hidden conditions; they have to infer them from what happens.

## January 2027 — as the exercise opens

- **Washington.** The growth-first coalition leads the federal government after the November 2026 midterms: pro-deployment, wary of new taxes and sceptical of new entitlements. The worker-first coalition is in opposition.
- **States and housing.** Several big states are already overriding local zoning to get homes built; more are debating it.
- **In the news: the new-work mirage.** Last year's fashionable new job category — the "AI supervisor" roles firms hired for through 2026 — is itself being automated. Several large employers have closed the teams they built a year ago.

## 2027 — what happened

**Official statistics (end 2027).** Unemployment 4.5%; participation 61.3%; 161.8m jobs (down 0.6m); GDP up 1.9%; consumer prices flat, housing costs down about 2%; median real income about 101.6; labour share 51.7%, a new record low; deficit 6.2%, debt about 102%. Announced layoffs citing AI: about 0.9m.

**Washington.** Congress passed the AI Leadership and Workers Act (quarterly layoff disclosure by large firms, pre-emption of state AI-employment rules, retraining and wage insurance from 2028) and a housing bill (grants to places that override zoning, from 2028). Antitrust cases on AI pricing were opened.

**Elsewhere.** The labs raced, held prices and announced a $2bn-a-year worker fund. Employers ran company-wide automation drives, mostly through hiring freezes. A union organising drive won contracts. Founders moved into factory-built housing.

## 2028 — opening headline

**Generation leap.** A new model generation does week-long professional projects unaided.

## 2028 — what happened

**Official statistics (end 2028).** Unemployment 4.3%; participation 60.8%; 160.9m jobs (down 0.9m); GDP up 2.6%; housing costs down about 3%, other prices slightly lower; median real income about 100.7; labour share 50.3%, another record low; deficit 6.4%, debt about 104%. Announced layoffs citing AI: about 1.6m. For 2027: poverty 12.7%, homelessness 20.7 per 10,000.

**Washington.** Congress passed a cut-down Worker Transition Act (2.5% levy on AI profits to 2032, wage insurance, 30-day layoff notice, from 2029) and a work-sharing subsidy with small-business credit. In November the growth-first coalition kept its lead; margins narrowed and Congress is divided.

**Elsewhere.** The labs raced, cut old-generation prices and dropped lock-in. Employers automated faster, mostly without layoffs, and began profit-sharing. A union drive in large offices stalled. Founders expanded factory-built housing.

## 2029 — opening headline

**Cheap services catch on.** Households start buying AI-delivered tutoring, legal help and home design in bulk, with people in the loop.

## 2029 — what happened

**Official statistics (end 2029).** Unemployment 3.5%; participation 61.1%; 164.0m jobs (up 3.1m); GDP up 5.9%; housing costs down about 1%, professional services cheaper; median real income about 106.0; labour share 50.1%, another record low; deficit 6.0%, debt about 103%. Announced layoffs citing AI: about 1.5m. For 2028: poverty 12.6%, homelessness 19.0 per 10,000.

**Washington.** Congress passed the Housing Supply Act (funded building with zoning conditions) and a cut-down new-firms and first-job credit, both from 2030. The AI pricing cases settled on open-access terms. The 2.5% levy and wage insurance began.

**Elsewhere.** The labs raced, raised new-generation prices and cut old ones. Employers automated at the same pace, mostly by attrition, and set up advisory worker committees. A targeted union drive failed. Founders launched worker-owned service co-ops and finished their factory-housing expansion.
