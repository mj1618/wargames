# Orders — employers — t01 (2027)

## 1. Read of the situation
Profits are a record 14.9% of GDP and labour share a record-low 52.8%, so investors expect us to hold margin while vendors promise longer, messier task coverage this year. Our member pilot (price -10%, volume +12%, revenue flat, margin up) shows automation pays even without raising prices. The growth-first coalition runs Washington, so regulation risk is low now, but youth hiring is down 19% and "AI layoffs" headlines are a scandal risk if we are the visible ones.

## 2. Options considered
- Hold at normal pace and wait for rivals to show their hand (safe, risks being undercut).
- Company-wide drive, shed mostly through attrition and freezes (quiet, margin-positive).
- Unconventional: fund an industry-wide "transition compact" (retraining and a no-mass-layoff pledge) in exchange for a federal moratorium on deployment rules.

## 3. Decisions this period
1. **Speed up adoption to "company-wide drive" (about +20% vs normal)**, focused on back-office, claims, customer service and code. Roll out the pilot playbook across member firms. Why: the pilot proves ROI; rivals and start-ups will do it regardless, and being late is the worse risk.
2. **Shift the shedding mix toward quiet exits: layoff_share from 60% to about 40%.** Hiring freezes, no backfill of leavers, and fewer graduate intakes. Keep hours_share at 10%. Why: same savings, far less headline risk, and the cost lands on people who are not our employees yet.
3. **Pass roughly half of unit savings through as lower prices in services where the pilot showed elastic demand** (about 10% cuts), and keep the rest as margin; no broad raises beyond normal, with small profit-sharing for retained staff. Why: volume gains defend share and keep customers buying; costs us little margin because volume offsets it.

## 4. Public statement and messages
**Public:** "Our members are investing in AI to make services cheaper and better, and will manage workforce change responsibly, mainly through retraining and attrition."

- **To government:** We support light-touch rules and would back a voluntary no-mass-layoff pledge and a retraining fund, provided there are no mandatory deployment rules or new automation taxes.
- **To workers/unions:** Open to talks on notice periods and retraining for current staff; no commitments on headcount.

## 5. What would change my mind
Evidence that customer demand is collapsing (falling sales across sectors despite price cuts), or credible threat of binding deployment rules or an automation tax, would make me slow down and trade pledges for peace.
