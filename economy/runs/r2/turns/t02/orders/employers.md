# Orders — employers — round 02 (2028)

## 1. Read of the situation
Our 2027 drive removed about 1.9m roles while total jobs fell only 0.6m, unemployment rose to 4.5%, and labour share hit a record-low 51.7%, so margins are intact and the pilot economics held. A generation leap to week-long unaided projects widens what we can automate in office and professional work, and rivals will move. The risks are political: November elections, quarterly layoff disclosure now in force, and a worker-first opposition with a 12.7% poverty and rising unemployment story to run on.

## 2. Options considered
- Continue the 2027 pace (+20%) and keep layoffs at about 40%.
- Mass restructuring (+40-60%) to lock in the generation leap before rivals do.
- Unconventional: sign a joint "transition compact" with households (no-layoff floor for tenured staff, gain-sharing, shorter weeks) in exchange for the pre-emption already won and no deployment rules, funded by the AI-firm locked pricing.

## 3. Decisions this period
1. **Adoption: company-wide drive, about +25% on normal.** Re-platform office and professional workflows on the new generation, concentrated where week-long projects replace teams. We stop short of mass restructuring: projects disappoint, liability and disclosure make a big-bang cut a headline before an election, and the herd is not yet there.
2. **Shedding: keep layoff_share near 35%, down from about 40%, mostly freezes and attrition.** Disclosure counts layoffs, not unfilled seats, so we stay quiet and cheap. Hold back visible cuts from Q2 to the November vote. Raise hours_share to about 15% in back-office roles, keeping weekly pay whole for staff under a set threshold.
3. **Sharing and capacity.** Sign multi-year capacity agreements with AI firms for priority allocation and locked pricing through 2029. Pass roughly half of savings into lower prices in elastic services, and fund profit-sharing equal to about 10% of automation savings, taking this to households as our answer on gain-sharing.

## 4. Public statement and messages
**Statement:** "We are investing in AI to keep prices down and jobs secure, and we back a voluntary transition compact over new mandates."
- **To households:** "Open to gain-sharing and hours reductions with pay protection; no blanket no-layoff clause. Let's draft the framework together before any announcement."
- **To AI firms:** "We will sign multi-year capacity at locked pricing; we want volume discounts and indemnity for liability on agentic work."

## 5. What would change my mind
A sharp rise in unemployment or a poll swing putting automation taxes or deployment rules on a credible path would push us to slow cuts and take the compact seriously.
