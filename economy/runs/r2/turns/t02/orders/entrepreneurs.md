# Orders — Entrepreneurs — turn 02

## Read of the situation

We added 0.5m jobs in 2027 but faced margin collapse: AI pricing lock-in ate profits while niches automated within months. With 4.5% unemployment and a new model generation doing week-long projects, the next wave of displacement is real. Factory-built housing showed genuine traction in zoning-override states, and federal grants are now flowing—this is the sector where we can stay ahead of automation and where labor bottlenecks are real constraints on supply, not choice.

## Options considered

1. **Double down on AI-only (chase the margin recovery).** Fight commoditization with volume and specialization in narrow domains before incumbents notice.
2. **People-heavy pivot (own the labor plays).** Shift capital hard into services and factory-built housing where demand is visible and automation is years away.
3. **Antitrust play (radical).** Publicly join the campaign for AI pricing restrictions, front-run competitors on scale-up once the FTC cases move.

## Decisions this period

1. **Shift capital allocation toward people-heavy services and factory-built housing.** We're moving 60% of new capital into housing and human-contact services (care, trades, skilled support), 40% into AI-only where margins support it. Factory-built housing is the play: zoning is loosening, federal grants reduce our entry cost, and we own distribution through local builder networks. Pace: break ground on 3–4 factory-built housing operations in target states (CA, TX, NC).

2. **Tighten hiring discipline on AI-only ventures.** Only start AI-only firms where we see 18-month runways to breakeven or acquisition at current pricing. Otherwise hold cash and watch. This is a margin war, not a job-creation story, so we size for profit, not jobs.

3. **Push publicly for antitrust enforcement on AI pricing.** Testify to Congress on AI pricing lock-in, join founder coalitions demanding interoperability and price regulation. Weaker voice than large employers, but founders are politically popular. This frames the next 12 months and may shift March 2028 policy.

## Public statement

"Founders are building the economy's real new jobs—not in AI supervision theater, but in housing, services, and skilled trades where humans are irreplaceable right now. We're backing that with capital and hiring. We're also calling on Congress to force fair pricing on AI tools; lock-in is killing margins for the small players who hire."

### Message 1
**To: Congress (growth-first coalition)**
Antitrust on AI pricing is growth-first policy, not regulation-first. Cheap AI means cheap housing, cheap services, and a million new jobs. Locked-in pricing means consolidation and fewer startups. Our votes and donations are with whoever does this.

## What would change my mind

If credit dries up or unemployment hits 5.5%, I'll pull back hiring and return to pure margin plays; if federal housing money vanishes, I'll pivot fast to sectors with visible demand.
