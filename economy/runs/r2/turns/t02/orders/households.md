# Orders — households — round 02 (2028)

## 1. Read of the situation
Unemployment is up to 4.5% and 0.6m jobs are gone, but pay edged ahead of flat prices and rents fell about 2%, so most of us feel squeezed, not broken. Our contracts in care, logistics and big offices are real wins, but they fade if not kept up, and a week-long-project model puts mid-career office staff (48m jobs) next in line. Graduates still cannot land a first desk job, and the new retraining and wage insurance only start this year, with the Act also pre-empting state rules. The top tenth, who own the shares, are doing fine; for the rest of us the risk is the next layoff, not today's bills.

## 2. Options considered
- Hold steady: keep the moderate drive and wait for wage insurance to arrive.
- Escalate: strikes and boycotts of AI-run services before the election.
- Unconventional: take the entrepreneurs up on their offer and build worker co-ops in care and trades, with union pensions and credit unions as seed money.

## 3. Decisions this period
1. **Union pressure up.** Extend the drive from care and logistics into large offices where "week-long project" AI is landing. Ask for 90-day notice, no-layoff or redeployment clauses, and gain-share on productivity. Pace: bargaining and organising campaigns, strikes only as a last resort. This is cheaper while jobs are still plentiful.
2. **Saving up modestly.** Households with a mid-career office worker, or a graduate at home, nudge saving up about 1 point. This is prudence, not panic. We know it costs others' jobs, so we hold it at 1, not the full 2, until we see layoffs near us.
3. **Votes and protest.** Peaceful, organised, aimed at 2028. Demand that wage insurance and retraining actually pay out, a first-job guarantee or apprenticeship funding for under-25s, and a repeal of pre-emption. We back whoever delivers this. No boycott yet. Hold it in reserve, with care homes and robotaxis as first targets if employers stall on notice.

## 4. Public statement and messages
**Statement:** "We are not against the machines. We want a share of what they earn and a ninety-day warning before our jobs go."

**Messages:**
- To Employers: Notice periods and retraining are fine to start with, but put them in writing and add a seat for workers on the automation plan. Otherwise we organise your offices next.
- To Entrepreneurs: We will look at co-ops and hiring in care, trades and repairs. Show us wages, hours and ownership terms.

## 5. What would change my mind
Mid-career layoffs hitting people we know, or wage insurance failing to pay out, would push me to full saving and strikes. Visibly cheaper bills and real hiring of young people would let me relax.
