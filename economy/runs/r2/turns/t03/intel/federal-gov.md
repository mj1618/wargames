# Intel — Federal government — round 03

**January–December 2029.** Growth-first still leads after November, but Congress is now divided.

**Numbers (end 2028).** Unemployment 4.3%; participation 60.8%; 160.9m jobs (down 0.9m); GDP +2.6%; housing costs down 3%, other prices slightly lower; median real income 100.7; labour share 50.3%; deficit 6.4%. AI-cited layoffs: about 1.6m. Poverty 12.6%, homelessness 19.0 per 10,000.

**This year's headline: Cheap services catch on.** Households start buying AI-delivered tutoring, legal help and home design in bulk, with people in the loop.

**Only you see.** Thinner margins: half of small bills pass, few large. The Worker Transition Act passed at half size (2.5% levy, smaller wage insurance, 30-day notice), survived court and starts this year with the work-sharing subsidy. Revenue slipped to 17.0%.

**Messages.**
- From AI firms: "Disclosure will be clean and on time. We are cutting old-generation prices and dropping lock-in before anyone makes us. Hold pre-emption through November and we stay quiet on a levy."
- From entrepreneurs: "Antitrust on AI pricing is growth-first policy, not regulation-first. Cheap AI means cheap housing, cheap services, and a million new jobs. Locked-in pricing means consolidation and fewer startups. Our votes and donations are with whoever does this."
