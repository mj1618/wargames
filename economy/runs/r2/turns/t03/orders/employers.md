# Orders — Employers — 2029 (t03)

## 1. Read of the situation
Our drive removed about 3.0m roles in 2028 with only 35% by layoff, and margins still widened, so the strategy is working. Labour share is 50.3% and median income is 100.7: customers are getting thinner, but price cuts keep lifting volumes. Congress is now divided, the 30-day notice and the hours subsidy start this year, and households are threatening to organise our offices.

## 2. Options considered
- Hold pace at +25%, keep freezes and attrition, take the hours subsidy.
- Mass restructuring (+40-60%) now, while week-long-project systems are cheap and Congress is split.
- Unconventional: give workers an advisory seat on automation plans in exchange for a written statutory ceiling on deployment rules and no further levy increases.

## 3. Decisions this period
1. **Adoption:** stay at company-wide drive (+25%), not mass restructuring. Rivals are moving at the same pace, and a visible layoff spike under the new notice rules, with a divided Congress able to legislate, is the main risk. Redeploy the faster-than-planned team replacements into back-office hours cuts.
2. **Shedding and hours:** layoff_share down to about 30%, hours_share up to about 20% (claiming the federal subsidy, honouring the no-mass-layoff pledge). Keep weekly pay topped up for the lowest-paid, not for everyone. Cheap, and it buys cover.
3. **Pay and the worker seat:** accept the household request in a limited form. Put the notice and retraining commitments in writing and create a non-binding worker advisory committee on automation plans at the 500 largest employers. Raise profit-sharing to about 15% of savings. Keep half of savings going to price in elastic services. Cost is small next to the cost of regulation or an organised workforce.

## 4. Public statement and messages
**Statement:** "Large employers will honour 30-day notice, put retraining commitments in writing, and give workers a voice on automation plans, and we will keep prices falling."
- To households: Written notice and retraining commitments accepted. We will pilot advisory worker seats, not board seats or vetoes. Tell us what the committees need to cover.
- To labs: Keep old-generation prices falling and the lock-in terms off. We will sign multi-year capacity if the pricing holds.

## 5. What would change my mind
A deployment-rule or automation-tax bill with real votes, or sales falling faster than our price cuts can offset, would push me to slow down and bargain harder.
