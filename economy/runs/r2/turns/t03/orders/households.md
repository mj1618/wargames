# Orders — Households — 2029

## 1. Read of the situation
Jobs are down 0.9m and participation has slid to 60.8%, so people are leaving the labour market quietly even though unemployment (4.3%) looks fine. Labour's share fell to 50.3% while rents and prices are falling: our bills are easing but our pay is not. Cheap AI tutoring, legal help and home design are a real consumer win, and small new firms are hiring while whole office teams are not replaced. Wage insurance and 30-day notice arrive this year, smaller than promised. The top tenth, who own the shares, are doing fine and would read all this as success.

## 2. Options considered
- Big push for blanket no-layoff clauses (employers have refused; low odds).
- Quiet consolidation: keep existing contracts alive and take the offered gain-sharing and hours frameworks.
- Unconventional: set up worker-owned co-ops that buy the cheap AI tools and sell tutoring, legal and design services to neighbours, so we capture the gains instead of only paying for them.

## 3. Decisions this period
1. **Maintenance plus targeted union drive (`union_pressure`: moderate).** Put most effort into renewing the contracts we won so they do not fade, and sign the employers' gain-sharing and hours-with-pay-protection framework. Accept no blanket no-layoff clause but demand a 90-day notice and redeployment floor beyond the new 30 days. We join the drafting before any announcement, and push the AI firms to put their clause in writing and route the $1bn through union-run retraining. Why: a signed text is worth more than slogans, and the weak spot is mid-career office staff.
2. **Saving unchanged (`household_saving`: hold).** Bills are easing and wage insurance is starting, so there is no case for extra fear-saving, which would also cost neighbours' jobs. We do not run it down either, because the cushions are thin.
3. **Co-op pilot and consumer use of cheap services.** Back worker and neighbourhood co-ops in the 10-20 biggest metros, funded by member dues and small savings pools. Households use the cheap AI services freely. Why: it captures part of the gain and builds pull toward the small firms that are hiring. No boycott, since the services visibly make life cheaper.

## 4. Public statement and messages
**Statement:** "Cheaper services are welcome, but the gains must show up in pay, hours and security. We will sign real gain-sharing and demand wage insurance that pays out on time."

- **To Employers:** Yes to drafting the gain-sharing and hours-with-pay-protection framework now. We need a 90-day notice and redeployment floor and quarterly pay-out reporting.
- **To Government:** Pay wage insurance quickly and at the promised level, fund it from the AI-profit levy, and keep the layoff disclosure honest. Pre-emption repeal stays on our list for the next election.

## 5. What would change my mind
If mid-career layoffs near us jump or wage insurance is slow or small, we move to strikes, boycotts and fear-saving.
