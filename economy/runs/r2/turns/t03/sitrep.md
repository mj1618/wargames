# Sitrep — round 03 (2029)

**Jobs.** The first good year. Automation destroyed 3.74m jobs (2.10m office and professional), but 6.18m were created: 2.79m in new kinds of work, 1.97m from stronger spending, 1.24m from cheaper output. Employment rose 3.1m to 164.0m, above its 2026 level. Unemployment fell to 3.5%; the share of adults working recovered from 58.1% to 59.0%, just under 2026. Office jobs still fell slightly.

**Pay and prices.** Real pay rose 4.4%. Housing costs fell another 1.4%; professional services got cheaper. Median real income 106.0; bottom fifth 105.3. Weekly hours slipped to 34.0.

**Businesses.** Machines could do 43% of desk work and did 16%. GDP grew 5.9%. Labour's share edged down to 50.1%; top-1% share held at 21.1%.

**Government.** The Housing Supply Act passed whole; the new-firms and first-job credit passed at half size; both start in 2030. The antitrust cases settled on open-access terms. The 2.5% levy and wage insurance began. Deficit 6.0%, debt 102.6%.

**Daily life.** Poverty 11.6%, homelessness 17.8 per 10,000. The targeted union drive failed again; employers set up advisory worker committees.

**What mattered most.** The cheap-services boom landing on top of an all-out founders' push into people-heavy firms. And attrition instead of layoffs: only 30% of removed roles became unemployed.

**What a household would have noticed:** help-wanted signs at small firms, a cheap AI tutor with a human behind it, lower rent, and a bigger profit-share cheque.
