# Orders — Employers — round 04 (2030)

**1. Read of the situation.** Margins held in 2029 (3.7m roles removed, 30% by layoff, profit-sharing at 15% paid) and sales were strong, so the drive works. But AI shares are down a third and credit is tightening for a year, so financing and our own share prices are now the binding risk, not demand. Congress is divided and elections are in November, so any visible layoff wave is a political liability; applicants are scarcer, which signals labour-market tightening.

**2. Options considered.**
- Hold +25% and keep everything as is.
- Slow to normal pace for a year, protect cash and ratings, resume when credit eases.
- Unconventional: use the AI shares fall to lock multi-year capacity at today's price while AI firms are desperate for committed revenue.

**3. Decisions this period.**
1. **Adoption: trim to about +10% (from +25%), prioritising projects with under-18-month payback.** Credit is dear and investors will punish capex that misses; we keep the back-office and customer-service drive but defer big restructurings until credit loosens. Layoff_share stays ~30%, mostly attrition.
2. **Sign the AI capacity deal before Q2**, but negotiate: price step-down clause if list prices fall, indemnity cap raised to 2x contract value, non-exclusive, 12-month exit. This hedges a rebound in prices and secures new-generation allocation.
3. **Accept drafting the household gain-sharing/hours framework.** We agree to 60-day notice and redeployment support at the 500 largest firms, quarterly aggregate pay-out reporting, hours-with-pay-protection voluntary (hours_share ~20% maintained). We decline a binding 90-day floor. Keep profit-sharing at 15% of savings and half of savings to price. This is cheap relative to regulation before the November election.

**4. Public statement.** "We are investing through the financial turbulence, keeping our workforce commitments, and working with households on a voluntary framework for notice, redeployment and gain-sharing."

Messages:
- To AI firms: "We will sign 3-year capacity by Q2 if you add a price step-down clause and raise indemnity to 2x contract value; non-exclusive, 12-month exit stays."
- To households: "Agree to draft the framework now. We can commit to 60-day notice, redeployment support and quarterly aggregate pay-out reporting; a binding 90-day floor is not agreeable yet."

**5. What would change my mind.** A sharp fall in sales or a credit freeze would make me slow further; a bill with binding deployment rules or an automation tax would make me shift to lobbying and a bigger pledge.
