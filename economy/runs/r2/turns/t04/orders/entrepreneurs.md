# Orders — Entrepreneurs — Turn 04

## Read of the situation

We added 2.8m jobs in 2029 and the co-ops launched successfully across ten metros. Factory-built housing expansion is done; Housing Supply Act and new-firms credit are both in play this year. But the financial wobble is hitting hard—AI shares down 33%, credit markets are tightening, and investor funding rounds are slower and smaller. The federal government is asking us to "deliver visible hiring and homes" to justify the policy commitment.

## Options considered

1. **Full-steam mode:** Keep hiring aggressively in people-heavy services and housing, ride the co-op momentum, use the new-firms credit aggressively to absorb slack from investors.
2. **Consolidate and weather:** Pull back on hiring growth, focus on margin recovery in the AI-only segment where the financial wobble hit high-growth firms hard, rebuild cash reserves.
3. **Selective push in underserved sectors:** Hire heavily in care (still undersupplied) and trade services where AI is furthest from reaching, dial back housing (it's policy-dependent now, not ours to drive).

## Decisions this period

1. **People-heavy hiring continues, but measured.** Expand care and skilled-trades co-ops moderately (mid-sized hiring, not explosive); reduce speculative new-business starts until credit normalizes. Reason: Demand is real and co-ops are filling—we're not creating jobs out of thin air—but credit crunch means we can't fund every opportunity. Stay visible but prudent.

2. **AI-only margin play.** Small shift of new capital into higher-margin, lower-headcount AI-only plays. Reason: Investors are pulling back; founders with deep pockets can pick up margin now while high-growth firms are weakened. Not a betrayal of people-heavy; margin recovery funds long-term co-op scaling.

3. **Push hard for small-business credit availability.** Lobby Congress and the Fed to actually *deploy* the new-firms credit; it's written but not flowing. Reason: Federal government promised us this; credit crunch kills it before it starts. The growth-first coalition controls Congress—they want visible jobs and tax revenue. We deliver both if the credit moves.

## Public statement

"We're delivering: factory-built housing came in under policy targets, co-ops are thriving in ten metros, and our network will add roughly 2.5m jobs this year even as credit markets tighten. The new-firms credit and Housing Supply Act are working. We're also reminding Congress and the Fed that this happens only if the credit actually flows to founders. The financial wobble is real—but it's exactly when the co-op model, which doesn't need VC, proves its strength."

## Messages

**To federal government:** "You delivered antitrust and housing bills; we're delivering the jobs. New-firms credit is the missing link—small firms can't absorb the credit crunch the way big firms can. If it doesn't flow, neither do the hiring numbers you're betting on in November."

**To workers (via co-op networks):** "We're hiring in care and trades. Co-ops are filling fast because they work: ownership, predictable work, real demand. If you're looking for a job that can't be moved overseas or replaced by automation next year, the skilled trades and care services are it."

## What would change my mind

If Congress fails to fund the new-firms credit or the co-op model hits structural demand problems (people don't want to join, care co-ops can't find matching supervisors), I shift hard to AI-only and margin mode.

## Journal (turn 04)

We've proven people-heavy services and co-ops work at scale—2.8m jobs, filled metros, real demand. Housing is policy-dependent now (we built the supply, government funds the building). The financial wobble is scary but plays to our strength: high-margin AI-only firms are weakened, and founders with patient capital can hire people-heavy again. Federal government is betting on us for November; we're betting on them to actually deploy the credit they wrote. Co-ops are the hedge: they don't burn cash the way high-growth VC plays do. Next turn: does Congress release the credit, and do co-ops hit a ceiling?
