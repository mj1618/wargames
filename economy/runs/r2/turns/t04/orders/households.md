# Orders — Households — round 04 (2030)

## 1. Read of the situation
Things are better at the kitchen table: unemployment 3.5%, 3.1m more jobs, rent falling, profit-share cheques growing, hours a bit shorter. But median real income of 106.0 and a labour share of 50.1% say the gains still go mostly to owners, and 1.5m AI-cited layoffs show the fear is not imaginary. The share crash and a year of tighter credit worry us: some neighbours' savings fell, loans are harder to get, and a credit squeeze can end the hiring that brought people back.

## 2. Options considered
- Press hard now while jobs are plentiful and unemployment is low (our bargaining is strongest).
- Hunker down: save more, spend less, wait out the credit year.
- Unconventional: pool savings and wage-insurance payouts into co-op credit unions and worker-owned service co-ops so they are not at the mercy of banks.

## 3. Decisions this period
1. **Renew and extend contracts** (moderate drive): keep up existing agreements before they fade, and take the employers' offer, asking that advisory worker committees get real scope: automation plans, notice, retraining and gain-share. Seek the 90-day notice clause. Modest effort, no strikes while jobs are plentiful and credit is tight.
2. **Saving up about 1 point** as a buffer against the credit year. Cutting back further would cost jobs we want to keep.
3. **Back the co-ops**: members join and invest small sums in the early-2030 worker-owned service co-ops (care, trades, repair), and we push for credit-union lending to them. Peaceful organising and protest in the build-up to the November vote; back worker-first candidates demanding wage-insurance top-ups, protection of the profit levy, and credit access for ordinary borrowers. No boycott: robots and AI services are making things cheaper.

## 4. Public statement and messages
Statement: "Jobs are back and rents are down, and we welcome that. We want our share written into contracts and into law before the next downturn, not after."
- To Employers: "We accept advisory seats as a start. They must cover automation plans, 90-day notice and retraining. Tell us when you will sign."
- To Entrepreneurs: "Count us in. Members will join and invest; help us get fair credit for the co-ops."

## 5. What would change my mind
If the credit squeeze brings mass layoffs, I would save more, strike for no-layoff clauses, and take protest to the streets.
