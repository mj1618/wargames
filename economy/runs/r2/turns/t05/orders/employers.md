# Orders — employers — round 05 (2031–32)

## 1. Read of the situation
Our drive removed about 3.6m roles in 2030, and another 5.3m jobs vanished because sales fell, hardest in retail, building and manufacturing. We are now cutting our own customer base: labour share is 47.1%, median income 101.8, and the data revision (unemployment 4.7%, not 4.3%) hands opponents a story just before the November 2032 elections. Applicants are plentiful, so labour is cheap, but demand is the binding constraint on earnings, and the Congress stays divided, so a deal now is cheaper than a law later.

## 2. Options considered
- Keep the trimmed pace and sign everything asked of us.
- Re-accelerate (+25%) to lock in cost leadership before rivals do.
- Unconventional: pay part of profit-sharing as consumer purchase credit redeemable at our own firms, so the money returns as sales.

## 3. Decisions this period
1. **Sign the AI capacity deal this quarter**: 3-year prepaid capacity at the floor price, old-gen down 20%, portable, no lock-in. Cost is cash up front, but it locks our input costs while credit is tight. Hold adoption_speed at about +5% (below normal drive), not accelerating, because sales losses now outweigh further savings.
2. **Soften the shedding and share more.** Cut layoff_share to ~25% (freezes and attrition, no visible cuts before Nov 2032). Raise hours_share to ~25% using the government work-sharing subsidy. Lift profit-sharing to ~20% of savings, with the new tranche as purchase credit. Pass ~60% of savings to price in elastic sectors (retail, insurance, logistics) to win volume back.
3. **Accept households' advisory seats** covering automation plans, 90-day notice and a retraining fund contribution of ~0.3% of payroll. The 90-day period is a stated standard, not a statutory floor. In exchange we ask no deployment rules and no automation tax this Congress.

## 4. Public statement and messages
**Statement:** "The 500 largest employers will sign a voluntary 90-day notice and retraining standard and open automation plans to worker committees."
- To AI firms: Signing this quarter; send final terms, with the floor price locked and 2x indemnity kept.
- To households: Seats accepted for automation plans, 90-day notice and retraining. Signing this quarter; tell us when the committees are formed.

## 5. What would change my mind
Another year of falling sales, or a credit squeeze that makes us break prepaid commitments, would push me back toward faster cuts. Evidence that rivals are not matching our pledges, or a bill passing with a binding floor, would push me to lobby.
