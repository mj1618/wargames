# Orders — Entrepreneurs — Turn 05

## Read of the situation

We added 1.9m jobs in 2030—down from 2.8m and short of our 2.5m pledge. The Main Street credit bill failed, so the new-firms credit stays at half-size with no loan guarantees. Credit markets remain tight, investor funding is scarce, and customer spending weakened through the year. Unemployment revised upward to 4.7%, well above the "normal" baseline. Housing Supply Act money is flowing and factory-built homes continue selling, but demand growth elsewhere has flatlined.

## Options considered

1. **Founder-first profitability:** Pivot hard to high-margin AI-only plays, reduce people-heavy hiring, rebuild cash reserves. Accept lower job creation in exchange for founder exits and dividend returns.
2. **Slow-growth hedge:** Keep co-ops alive and growing at 1m jobs/year (sustainable rate with available credit), focus housing on government-funded builds only, trim speculative AI-only starts.
3. **Unconventional: Demand co-op equity from households in place of credit:** Launch household co-op equity funds (workers and customers own shares, get returns), bypassing the failed small-business credit system.

## Decisions this period

1. **Scale back hiring growth to sustainable levels.** Slow people-heavy expansion to 1.0–1.2m job additions per year instead of 2.5m. Reason: Without new-firms credit flow, we can't fund the hiring promised. Better to deliver steady growth than collapse like we did in 2030. Co-ops and care/trades stay the focus; speculative starts are frozen.

2. **Harvest margins from AI-only and optimize founder returns.** Shift capital allocation toward high-margin, low-headcount AI plays; harvest existing co-op cashflow for founder distributions. Reason: Patient capital and founder resilience matter more than growth velocity when credit is gone. Keep the co-op network stable; let AI-only subsidize long-term people-heavy plays.

3. **Pilot household equity co-ops in three metros to bypass small-business credit.** Test whether workers and customers will fund their own co-ops directly through ownership shares rather than waiting for federal credit. Reason: The federal government has failed twice (Main Street credit, pre-2029 credit wobbles). We need an alternative to public policy for scaling beyond what founder cash can reach.

## Public statement

"We're right-sizing for reality: the Main Street bill failed, credit is constrained, and we're not going to overpromise hiring we can't fund. We're adding about 1m jobs a year—steady, sustainable, real. Factory-built housing works. Care and skilled-trades co-ops work. We're also testing something new: household equity co-ops, where workers and customers own shares and fund their own expansion. Policy was supposed to be the accelerator. It's not delivering. We're adapting."

## Messages

**To federal government:** "The Main Street credit bill's failure means we're slowing growth to sustainable rates. You have the policy tools—small-business credit guarantees, housing funding—but they're not deployed. If federal credit flows again, growth accelerates. Until then, we're at 1m jobs/year."

**To households (co-op networks):** "We're testing household-owned equity co-ops: you own shares, you fund expansion, you share returns. This isn't a substitute for fair public credit—it's a hedge against policy failure. Pilot in care, trades, and co-op services; if it works, we scale it."

## What would change my mind

If Congress passes and deploys new small-business credit guarantees, or if customer spending rebounds sharply, I accelerate hiring back to 2m+/year. If co-ops hit a ceiling or household equity pilots fail, I shift fully to profitability mode.
