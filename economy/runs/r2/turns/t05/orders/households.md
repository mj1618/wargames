# Orders — households — T05 (2031–32)

**1. Read of the situation.** Unemployment was revised from 4.3% to 4.7%, participation is 59.6% and 5.1m jobs are gone, so what we felt in 2030 was real and the official numbers understated it. Labour share is 47.1%, pay and profit-share cheques have shrunk, and the renewal drive gained nothing. Rent is falling sharply, which helps, but many neighbours have stopped looking for work, and the November 2032 election is close.

**2. Options considered.**
- Push another union drive for a binding 90-day notice floor.
- Hold savings steady and put our effort into the 2032 vote.
- Unconventional: pool strike funds and wage-insurance payouts into the co-ops, and organise the care and trades workers the co-ops are hiring.

**3. Decisions this period.**
1. **Union pressure: narrow, not wide.** Take the employers' offered 60-day notice, redeployment support and quarterly pay-out reporting now, and write them into contracts as a floor. Keep pressing for 90 days, and put organising effort into care, trades and co-op shops where jobs are growing and a drive can win. We had two failed drives, so we stop spending effort where it loses. Size: modest, with staff and dues kept going so the contracts do not fade.
2. **Saving: hold, then ease.** Keep the +1 buffer in 2031 because layoffs and the data revision frighten people. If unemployment on the next release is no worse, cut saving by 1 point in 2032 as rent falls and bills ease.
3. **Votes and protest: 2032, peaceful and disciplined.** Back worker-first candidates. Demands are audited layoff disclosure, wage insurance paid within 30 days, credit for co-ops and Main Street, and a 90-day notice law. No boycott. Hold it in reserve if firms start layoffs without notice.

**4. Public statement.** "The revised numbers show what working families already knew. We will take the notice and redeployment commitments in writing today and go on asking for 90 days. Our votes in 2032 will go to whoever puts jobs and pay first."

Messages:
- To Employers: "We accept the 60-day notice, redeployment and quarterly reporting as a signed floor now. The 90-day demand stays on the table, and we will review it after the 2032 reporting."
- To Entrepreneurs: "We will send trained care and trades workers to your co-ops. We need you to say how much credit you need so we can press Government for it."

**5. What would change my mind.** If layoffs resume without notice or unemployment on the next release rises well above 4.7%, I would raise saving again and move to slow-downs or a boycott.
