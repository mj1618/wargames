# Sitrep — round 05 (2031–32)

**Jobs.** A strong rebound. Automation removed 9.7m jobs over the two years, the fastest pace yet, but 13.5m were created: 5.5m as spending recovered, 4.6m in new kinds of work, 3.0m from cheaper output. Employment rose 4.9m to 163.8m. Unemployment fell from 4.5% to 3.5%; the share of adults working rose from 56.9% to 58.2%, still below 2026's 59.1%. Office jobs fell again (43.9m); care, retail, building and new sectors grew.

**Pay and prices.** Real pay rose to 115.9. Housing costs fell another 4%; goods and services got cheaper. Median real income rose from 102.8 to 112.7; bottom fifth 111.4. Weekly hours fell to 33.1.

**Businesses.** Machines do 35% of desk work (could do 72%) and 14% of physical work (could do 47%). GDP grew 6.2% a year. Labour's share fell to 46.0%; top-1% share 22.8%.

**Government.** The Transition Dividend Act failed; the Build and Hire Act passed. Deficit 5.7%, debt 102%. Growth-first kept its lead in November 2032; Congress stays divided. The AI levy has expired.

**Daily life.** Poverty 10.4%, homelessness 15.3 per 10,000. Unions won contracts in care and trades.

**What mattered most.** The central bank's easing as the 2030 credit squeeze ended, which turned the spending collapse into a recovery. And employers shifting to attrition, shorter hours and more profit-sharing while still automating.

**What a household would have noticed:** work easy to find again, mostly outside offices; a shorter week; bigger pay packets and cheaper rent.
