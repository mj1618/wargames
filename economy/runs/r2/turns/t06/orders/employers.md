# Orders — Employers — t06 (2033–34)

## Read of the situation
Demand recovered: no jobs were lost to falling sales, we rehired in retail, care and building, and profit-sharing was paid in full, so the 25% layoff, 25% hours-share and 60% price pass-through mix worked. We removed 9.7m roles, but the labour share is 46.0% and the 2.5% AI levy has expired, which leaves us exposed to a political backlash before the November 2034 congressional elections. Both deals we asked for are on offer (AI firms' 3-year capacity, households' signed 60-day floor), and the housing squeeze threatens our workers' ability to afford rent.

## Options considered
1. Hold the current course: adoption about +5%, attrition-led.
2. Accelerate to +15% while rivals' margins are fat and credit is easy.
3. Unconventional: fund employee housing (build-to-rent near our largest sites, rent-linked profit-sharing) so we keep workers and customers in the squeezed metros.

## Decisions this period
1. **Sign the AI capacity deal by Q2** on the offered terms (3-year, 10%/yr step-down, 1.5x indemnity, 12-month exit). Co-fund robot apprenticeships at about $1.5bn/yr across the large firms, with the shortage sectors (care, trades, logistics) first. This locks in cost and buys goodwill.
2. **Adoption about +8%** (up slightly from +5%, since sales are healthy). layoff_share about 25%, hours_share about 25%, profit-sharing about 20% of savings, with a third paid as purchase credit. About 60% of savings go to price in elastic services. We keep rehiring in retail, care and building.
3. **Accept the signed 60-day floor, redeployment and quarterly reporting** as a binding industry compact. We offer the advisory seats and the retraining fund again. We resist a binding 90-day floor, a new automation tax or deployment rules, and argue that the compact makes them unnecessary. We also pilot employer-backed housing in the three most squeezed metros, about $5bn over two years, targeting core staff retention.

## Public statement and messages
**Statement:** "Large employers have rehired, shared the gains and signed a worker-notice floor; stable rules and open competition will keep it going."
- **To AI firms:** "Yes to the 3-year capacity deal on your terms; apprenticeship co-funding is in, shortage sectors first."
- **To households:** "We accept the 60-day floor, redeployment and quarterly reporting as signed. We will review 90 days after the 2032 and 2033 reports, and we ask you not to back deployment rules meanwhile."

## What would change my mind
A fall in sales or credit spreads, or a bill with a binding 90-day floor or a new levy that looks likely to pass, would make me slow adoption and move more to hours-sharing and pay.
