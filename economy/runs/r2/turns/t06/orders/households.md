# Orders — Households — round 06 (2033–34)

## Read of the situation
Work is easy to find (unemployment 3.6%) and pay is growing, but labour's share is 46.0% while GDP grows 6.2% a year: gains go to owners, and the top tenth who hold shares see it differently. Rent is rising again in the richest metros and insurers are leaving others, so the squeeze is on bills. Our 60-day notice floor fades unless renewed, and the Transition Dividend failed.

## Options considered
- Coast on the good job market; renew only what expires.
- Push hard now for 90-day notice, real committee seats and bigger profit-share.
- Unconventional: pool union and co-op money into a household-owned housing and insurance co-op in squeezed metros.

## Decisions this period
1. **Organising [union_pressure]: renew and extend.** Renew care, trades and co-op contracts; turn employers' offered seats into committees with real scope (90-day notice, paid retraining, written gain-share formula). Moderate drive, care and trades first. Tight labour makes this the best time to roll.
2. **Saving [household_saving]: hold steady.** Jobs are fine, but rent and insurance are climbing and unemployment help is thin.
3. **Votes and protest: 2034 vote on housing and insurance.** Peaceful, worker-first. Demands: zoning override with building quotas, insurer-exit protection, a revived dividend from AI profits. Join the entrepreneurs' equity co-op pilot only with worker board seats and capped fees. No boycott.

## Public statement
"Jobs are plentiful and profits record-high, yet our share is the lowest ever. Rent and insurance are the fight, and we vote in 2034."

## Messages
- **To employers:** Committees form this quarter with elected workers. We sign 90-day notice and paid retraining once the gain-share formula is written down.
- **To entrepreneurs:** We pilot the equity co-ops with worker board seats and capped fees. A hedge, not a substitute for public credit.

## What would change my mind
Layoffs without notice or a sharp rent or insurance spike would push us to higher saving and disruptive action.
