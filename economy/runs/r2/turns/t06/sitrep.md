# Sitrep — round 06 (2033–34)

**Jobs.** Automation outran new work. 13.7m jobs were destroyed over two years, 12.8m of them by machines, a record; 10.1m were created (6.1m in new kinds of work, 3.7m from cheaper output). Employment fell 2.4m to 161.5m. Unemployment rose from 3.5% to 4.0%; the share of adults working fell from 58.2% to 56.9%, as most displaced workers left the labour force quietly. Office jobs fell to 41.1m; factories, shops and logistics shrank; care, building and new sectors grew.

**Pay and prices.** Real pay rose to 121.8, but the week shortened to 32.3 hours. Housing costs fell 5% despite the squeeze. Median real income edged up from 112.7 to 115.5; bottom fifth 113.9.

**Businesses.** Machines now do half of desk work (could do 90%) and a quarter of physical work. GDP grew 5.1% a year. Labour's share dropped four points to 41.8%; the top-1% share rose to 24.7%.

**Government.** The Metro Homes and Insurance Act passed; the levy renewal failed, so AI profits carry no special tax. Revenue 16.0%, deficit 5.8%, debt 100%. Growth-first kept its lead in November 2034, but Congress is now gridlocked.

**Daily life.** Poverty 9.9%, homelessness 14.1 per 10,000. The union drive won nothing new.

**What mattered most.** The labs' race (+20%, then +25%) with robots scaling; and the failed levy vote, which left the gains with owners.

**What a household would have noticed:** cheaper rent and bigger hourly pay, fewer hours, and a neighbour who stopped looking for work.
