# Orders — employers — t07 (2035–36)

## 1. Read of the situation
We cut 12.8m roles over two years, the most yet, and labour share is 41.8% with median real income at 116.5, so margins are safe and the cost curve still favours automation. But 0.8m more jobs vanished with softening sales, hours are down to 32.3, and unemployment is 4.2% with participation at 59.3%: our customers are thinning in places. Congress is gridlocked, which lowers the chance of a levy or deployment rules, but the "state pilot works" headline and November 2036 elections raise the risk of a state-led or post-election push.

## 2. Options considered
- Keep ~+8% adoption and 25% layoff share; sign the usual deals.
- Slow to ~+3% and shift savings to prices and pay to protect demand.
- Unconventional: co-fund the state wage-insurance-plus-cash model through an employer-paid premium in exchange for a federal pre-emption of state deployment rules.

## 3. Decisions this period
1. **Adoption and shedding.** Hold adoption at about +5% (down from +8%), layoff_share 25%, hours_share 25–30%. Prioritise robot and agent roll-outs where sales are firm; pause in sectors where the demand-loss jobs appeared. Rationale: avoid a record cut ahead of the election while keeping the cost lead.
2. **Countersign the AI firms' capacity terms this quarter.** 3-year terms; secure first access, price step-down and indemnity. Cheap, stabilising, and rivals will sign anyway.
3. **Sign the households' deal once the gain-share formula is written down.** Accept elected-worker committees, 90-day notice and paid retraining, with the formula capped (profit-sharing ~20% of savings, ~60% of savings passed to price). Price: no levy, no binding deployment rules. Fund the retraining pool, about $4bn a year, across the largest 500 firms. A deal beats a law, and it is cheap relative to a levy.

## 4. Public statement and messages
**Statement:** "Large employers will sign 90-day notice, paid retraining and elected worker committees, and keep sharing automation gains through pay, prices and profit-sharing."

**To AI firms:** Countersigning this quarter; keep the indemnity and the price step-down as sent.
**To households:** We will sign 90-day notice and retraining once the gain-share formula is written down. Send a draft this month; we will bring the 500 largest firms to the table.

## 5. What would change my mind
Another round of sales-driven job losses above about 1m, or a bipartisan bill with a binding levy or deployment rules gaining ground before November 2036, would make me slow adoption and raise pay and price sharing.
