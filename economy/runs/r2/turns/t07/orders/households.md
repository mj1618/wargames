# Orders — households — T07 (2035–36)

## 1. Read of the situation
Jobs are down 2.4m, unemployment is 4.2% and participation has slid to 59.3%, so the real picture is worse than the headline: more neighbours have quit looking than are counted. Pay and profit-share cheques are up and rent fell, but the week is 32.3 hours and labour share is 41.8%, so the gains flow elsewhere. The levy renewal failed, there is no dividend, and the extension drive won nothing; the state pilot shows what works, and Congress is gridlocked.

## 2. Options considered
- Keep renewing contracts quietly and wait for the 90-day review.
- Make the state pilot the 2036 demand: wage insurance plus cash, copied state by state.
- Unconventional: a coordinated "hours not heads" campaign, bargaining to turn productivity into a shorter week at full pay instead of layoffs.

## 3. Decisions this period
1. **Renew and hold contracts** in care, trades and co-ops (moderate drive, roughly the same effort as last period). Priorities: keep the 60-day floor and redeployment, and add paid retraining and a gain-share formula where we can. We do not stretch into offices again, because that drive failed.
2. **Push the state-pilot model** through votes, petitions and peaceful protest. State campaigns first, since Congress is gridlocked. The demand is wage insurance plus cash, a revived dividend, and housing and insurance relief. Back worker-first candidates in 2036, and push state ballot measures where available.
3. **Saving: hold** (no change). Pay is up and rent is down, but jobs are shaky and participation is falling, so we keep the buffer without squeezing demand further. No boycott.

## 4. Public statement and messages
**Statement:** "The pilot proves it: pay people through the change and they get back to work. We want that everywhere, and our share of the gains written into contracts."

**To Employers:** We accept your 60-day floor, redeployment and reporting as signed. We will hold you to the 90-day review, and we stay open on deployment rules if you put gain-share and paid retraining on the table.

**To Entrepreneurs:** Yes to hiring through co-ops, if the jobs carry worker board seats, fair fees and credit terms we can live with. Send us details on the franchise terms before we send people.

## 5. What would change my mind
A visible jump in layoffs without notice, or in rent and insurance bills, would push us to cut spending, save more and strike or boycott. A federal dividend or pilot-style law would let us ease off.
