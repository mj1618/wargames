# Sitrep — round 07 (2035–36)

**Jobs.** Machines again took more work than appeared. 11.0m jobs were destroyed over two years, almost all by automation; 8.6m were created (5.6m in new kinds of work, 2.7m from cheaper output). Employment fell 1.2m to 160.3m. Unemployment rose from 4.0% to 4.3%; the share of adults working fell from 56.9% to 56.1% (59.1% in 2026). Office jobs fell to 39.1m; care, building and new sectors grew.

**Pay and prices.** Real hourly pay reached 129.6, but the week shrank to 31.5 hours. Housing costs fell 7%. Median real income rose from 115.5 to 120.6; bottom fifth 118.7.

**Businesses.** Machines do 61% of desk work and 38% of physical work. GDP grew 4.8% a year. Labour's share fell three points to 38.8%; the top-1% share rose to 25.8%.

**Government.** The State Transition Match Act passed at half-size: a 2-point AI-profits levy and a smaller match for state wage insurance. The new-firms credit top-up passed. Deficit 5.3%, debt 98%. The November 2036 election closed the period.

**Daily life.** Poverty 9.2%, homelessness 13.1 per 10,000. Unions renewed and strengthened contracts in care and trades.

**What mattered most.** The labs' continued race with robots scaling, against founders' people-heavy hiring; and the dice that halved the levy bill rather than killing it.

**What a household would have noticed:** cheaper rent and a fatter hourly rate, a shorter week, and fewer people at the office.
