# Journal — ai-firms

## Round 01 (2027)
Believe: compute, not demand, is the limit; the market bears margin (Helix's cut was half-matched, no churn); federal risk is low under the growth-first coalition, backlash risk is housing-shaped. Committed: race +20%, premium pricing, $600m politics, $2bn/yr transition fund (to $5bn for pre-emption), $300m housing campaigns. Watch: whether Helix breaks the pricing line, state AI-labour bills, whether rents get pinned on us, any moratorium talk gaining votes.

## Round 02 (2028)
Believe: the wobble is a funding problem, not demand; the election, not the market, is the real risk this year; the government's no-tax line is worth buying cheaply. Committed: steady pace, capex flat; premium holds plus a developer tier (~40% below list for firms under $50m); $900m politics; voluntary layoff reporting; $1bn housing in our metros; fund stays $2bn. Watch: a rival's full cut, November result, whether lenders reopen for 2029 capex, moratorium votes.

## Round 03 (2029)
Believe: downturn was mild (revision to 4.5%), demand intact, lenders still the brake; the worker-first majority will tax us, so shape the levy rather than lose it; dormant fund cost us credibility. Committed: modest race +10%, capex +10%; premium holds; unbundled API tier ~45% off for firms under $100m; $1.2bn politics; offer 4% levy for no caps/licensing and pre-emption; fund actually pays $2bn, creditable. Watch: levy rate and licensing language, moratorium votes, lender reopening, startup uptake.

## Round 04 (2030)
Believe: the boom and the hit industry are our best cover; the real risks are antitrust and the levy returning above 8; the government's $5bn price is cheap against a 12-point levy. Committed: race +20%, capex +25%; premium holds, unbundled floor +10%, compute-access charter as antitrust remedy; $1.5bn politics; $5bn fund ($3bn now, $2bn on levy locked at 8). Watch: levy text, consent-decree talks, rival price breaks, grid or power backlash, moratorium votes.
