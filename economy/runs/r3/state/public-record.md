# Public record

Everything here is known to every player.

## January 2027 — where things stand

**Technology.** AI systems do multi-hour professional tasks on their own. About one firm in five uses AI in production; AI assists roughly 6% of work hours and saves about 1.5% of them. Robots are behind: cheap humanoids are shipping in the tens of thousands, mostly to pilots; warehouses and driverless taxis are the exceptions. Construction, care and skilled trades are barely touched. The fictional labs are Meridian, Helix, Lumen and Vanta.

**What people argue about.** Whether displacement is real yet (hiring of 22–25-year-olds in AI-exposed jobs is down about 19%; "AI" was the most-cited reason for announced layoffs in 2026; total employment still looks normal). Whether savings reach customers or stay as profit. Housing: rents explain where homelessness is high, and zoning dominates costs in expensive metros. Public money: about 70% of federal revenue rests on wages. Two-thirds of Americans want AI development slowed.

**Politics.** After the November 2026 midterms a **growth-first coalition** leads the federal government: pro-deployment, tax-averse, sceptical of new entitlements. The opposition is a worker-first coalition that wants protections and transfers and is open to taxing AI profits. No federal law yet addresses AI and jobs. Existing support: unemployment insurance (about 40% of pay for 26 weeks), small retraining programmes, the ordinary safety net. Next federal elections: November 2028.

**States.** The big, expensive states are resisting new housing; local zoning fights are going against builders.

**Statistics.** Official numbers arrive late and with noise.

## 2027 — opening headline

**Housing squeeze.** AI wealth is concentrating in a few metros and bidding up land; insurers are pulling out of others. Rents and house prices are jumping.

## 2027 — what happened

**Official statistics (end 2027).** Unemployment 4.2%; participation 61.5%; 162.7m jobs (up 0.3m, less than population growth); GDP up 1.7%; consumer prices flat apart from housing, up about 1%; labour share 52.5%, a new record low; deficit 6.0%, debt about 102%. Announced layoffs citing AI: about 0.3m. Poverty and homelessness figures for 2027 are not yet out.

**Washington.** Congress passed a housing bill (grants of about 0.2% of GDP to cities that upzone, from 2028) and a small-business credit and work-sharing subsidy. A federal court blocked the executive rule requiring large employers to report AI-attributed layoffs.

**Elsewhere.** The labs raced and held prices; they announced a $2bn-a-year worker-transition fund. Large employers ran company-wide automation drives, mostly through hiring freezes. A national union organising drive won few contracts. Big states still resist new housing.

## 2028 — opening headline

**Financial wobble.** AI shares fall by a third; credit tightens for a year.
