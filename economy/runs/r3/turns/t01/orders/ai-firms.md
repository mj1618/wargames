# Orders — ai-firms — round 01 (Jan–Dec 2027)

## Read of the situation
Order books are full, compute is the binding constraint, and when Helix cut enterprise prices 20% rivals matched only half and lost no customers, so the market bears margin. Profits at 14.9% of GDP and labour share at 52.8% are headline risks, but the growth-first coalition is pro-deployment and tax-averse, so this year's threat is state-level and reputational. The housing headline blames "AI wealth" for rents; that is the backlash vector two-thirds of the public are primed for.

## Options considered
- Full price war to lock in share before open-weight climbs the stack (Helix's preference; majority says no while compute is short).
- Steady pace, quiet lobbying, no funds.
- Unconventional: lead on housing, not jobs — fund zoning reform in our own metros so the rent story becomes a NIMBY story.

## Decisions this period
1. **`deploy_pace` = Race, +20% above baseline**, limited by chips and power; capex priority on power contracts and data-centre permits. Falling behind is existential.
2. **`ai_pricing` = Premium and bundling.** Hold margins, match at most half of any rival cut, bundle agents into lock-in enterprise contracts. Open-weight takes the low end. (Helix dissents; three of four agree.)
3. **Politics, ~$600m lobbying/campaign spend.** Ask for fast-track power and data-centre permitting and federal pre-emption of state AI-employment rules. Offer a **voluntary worker-transition fund of $2bn/yr**, rising to $5bn/yr if pre-emption passes, plus $300m/yr for pro-housing-supply campaigns in hub metros.

## Public statement
"America's AI lead is this decade's growth engine. We are funding worker transition and new housing where we build — and we ask Washington to let us build."

## Messages
- **To: Federal government** — We back your growth agenda and fund $2bn/yr for transition, rising to $5bn if you pre-empt state AI-labour rules and fast-track power permitting. No profits levy.
- **To: Employers** — Multi-year bundled agent contracts at locked rates, capacity first-come. Adopt before supply tightens.

## What would change my mind
A moratorium bill with coalition votes, or a rival's full 20% cut that holds.
