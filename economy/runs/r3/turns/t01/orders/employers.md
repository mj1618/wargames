# Orders — employers — round 01 (2027)

## Read of the situation
Profits are at 14.9% of GDP and labour share at 52.8%, so the board's job is to defend that margin while rivals are free to automate; our own pilots show real unit-cost savings. A growth-first Washington means no deployment rules or new taxes are imminent, so the main threat is competitors, not regulators. Rents in the expensive metros are squeezing staff, and our one price-cut test (sales up, but confounded by a rival promotion) tells us little about demand.

## Options considered
1. Hold at normal pace, keep quiet attrition, no concessions.
2. Company-wide automation drive with graduate hiring cut quietly.
3. Unconventional: fund metro housing allowances for key staff in lieu of general raises, and quietly back state zoning reform.

## Decisions this period
1. **Speed up automation to "company-wide drive" (about +20% vs normal).** Roll out proven pilots (back office, claims, customer service, document work) across divisions; budget capex now while the tools are cheap and rivals have not moved. Why: avoid being undercut and lock in margin before the herd moves.
2. **Shift the mix toward quiet shedding: layoff_share down from about 60% to about 40%.** Freeze graduate and entry-level hiring and do not backfill leavers; keep layoffs for redundant units only. Take about 10% of savings as shorter hours only where unions or contracts demand it. Why: attrition is cheap, invisible and avoids headlines and political attention.
3. **Targeted pay and small price moves, not general sharing.** Metro cost-of-living stipends for retained staff (about 1-2% of payroll in the three priciest metros), no general raises beyond inflation (wage_sharing stays low). Pass on only about a third of savings as price cuts in the most contested lines; keep the rest as margin. Why: retain skills cheaply, defend market share, protect earnings.

## Public statement and messages
**Statement:** "We are investing in AI to make our people more productive and our service better, and we are committed to responsible, orderly change."

**Messages:**
- To: Government (growth-first coalition): We back no new AI-specific deployment rules or payroll-linked levies; in return we will publish an annual workforce-transition report and expand apprenticeships.
- To: Labour/unions: We are open to talks on hours reductions with retraining funds for roles we automate, provided no strike action disrupts rollout.

## What would change my mind
A rival's price cut that visibly wins share, a federal tax or deployment bill gaining votes, or evidence that weakening consumer demand is hitting our own sales.
