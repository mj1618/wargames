# Orders — entrepreneurs — turn 01

## Read of the situation

The end-2026 economy shows labour's weakness (52.8% income share, a record low) and profit's strength (14.9% of GDP, also a record); unemployment sits at 4.1% but young people in AI-exposed jobs are hiring down about 19%. The headline for 2027 is a housing squeeze driven by AI wealth concentrating in a few metros, bidding up land and rents; insurers are pulling out of others. We are seeing new kinds of customer demand that did not exist two years ago, and seed capital is plentiful for anything with AI in the pitch.

## Options considered

1. **Expand aggressively in boom metros** — build AI-only firms with high profit per founder, pursue commercial real-estate-adjacent services (last-mile logistics, office servicing), accept the rents as a cost of market access.
2. **Go contrarian: capture undervalued metros** — establish people-heavy service firms in cities where insurers are pulling out and landlords are offering deals, build trust and scale before boom returns.
3. **Housing construction pivot** — shift a sustained wave of new-firm formation into factory-built housing and land-value captures in zoning-sympathetic states (unconventional: treats real estate as a new-business frontier, not just a cost problem).

## Decisions this period

**Decision 1: Expand new-firm formation to 15–20% above normal job creation.** We see new customer demand and seed capital is flowing. I will shift 55% of formation energy into AI-only firms (high profit-per-founder, undercutting incumbents) and 45% into people-heavy services in care, trades, and bespoke work (where human presence is defensible). This captures both the AI-productivity upside and the labour-supply inelasticity in hands-on sectors.

**Decision 2: Geographic split.** I will allocate 60% of growth to boom metros (accept high rents as entry cost to high-margin customer bases) and 40% to secondary metros (capture deal rents, build depth in underpenetrated markets). This hedges concentration risk and positions me for if boom-metro growth stalls.

**Decision 3: Push regulatory access.** I will lobby for cheap access to AI models, easier zoning and licensing in non-core sectors (care, housing, trades), and antitrust pressure on the giants. My voice is weaker than big employers', but this directly addresses my constraints.

## Public statement

We are starting thousands of new firms this year across both high-tech and hands-on services, moving aggressively into undervalued metros where capital is scarce and rents are reasonable. We will undercut the giants and hire where it makes economic sense.

## Messages to other players

**To: Workers' representative**
We are creating jobs — 15–20% above normal net new hires, heavily weighted to care, trades, and people-heavy services. We also need credit to stay cheap and housing/zoning rules to ease.

**To: AI providers**
We need cheap, reliable API access and good licensing terms to build services on top of your models. Expensive AI means we cannot compete; bundled terms favour the giants.

## What would change my mind

If seed capital dries up, or if commercial rents in boom metros exceed 25% of new-firm revenue by Q2, I will pull back new formation by half and consolidate in secondary metros.
