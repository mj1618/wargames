# Sitrep — round 01 (2027)

**Jobs.** A quiet year on the surface. Automation removed about 0.7m jobs, 0.4m of them office and professional, mostly by not replacing leavers and not hiring graduates. About 0.3m jobs appeared, nearly all in new kinds of work. Population growth hid the gap: employment rose to 162.7m, but the share of adults working slipped from 59.1% to 59.0%. Unemployment 4.2%; participation 61.5%.

**Pay and prices.** Real pay rose 0.7%. Goods and service prices were flat because firms kept most of their savings. Housing costs rose 1.1% after the squeeze. Median real household income 100.4; the bottom fifth 99.9.

**Businesses.** The labs raced ahead (machines could do 16% of desk work by year-end; firms used under 3%). Labour's share fell to 52.5%, a new low; the top-1% share rose to 20.2%. GDP grew 1.7%.

**Government.** Both bills passed: housing grants for cities that upzone, and small-business credit with a work-sharing subsidy, both starting 2028. A court blocked the layoff-disclosure rule. Deficit 6.0%, debt 102.5%.

**Daily life.** Poverty 12.9%, homelessness 22.2 per 10,000. The organising drive failed to win contracts.

**What mattered most.** Employers chose attrition over layoffs, so displacement showed up as missing entry-level jobs, not dole queues. The court ruling left Washington without its own data on AI layoffs.

**What a household would have noticed:** rent up, pay roughly keeping pace, and a graduate child still living at home sending applications.
