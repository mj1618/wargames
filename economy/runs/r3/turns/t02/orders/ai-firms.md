# Orders — ai-firms — round 02 (2028)

## Read of the situation
Our shares are down a third and credit is tight for the year, but order books are full, premium pricing held and no rival made a full cut, so the problem is funding, not demand. The labour picture is still mild (unemployment 4.2%, jobs +0.3m, labour share 52.5%), and the federal line is "no new taxes or licensing this year" if we report layoffs honestly and put visible money into housing. It is an election year with a 2028 vote in November; what we do before then sets the rules for 2029–32.

## Options considered
- Race on through the crunch with debt-funded capex (lenders say no; a failed raise mid-wobble is how a lab falls off the frontier).
- Cut prices to win share while rivals are cash-constrained (burns the margin that is carrying our capex).
- Unconventional: offer the government a formal pre-emption-for-levy deal now, while we are weak and sympathetic (majority against; keep it in reserve for 2029).

## Decisions this period
1. **Deploy pace: steady (baseline).** Capex held flat in dollars, reprioritised to inference capacity over new training sites; no lab breaks ranks on a slow-down. Why: credit, not demand, binds; a steady line keeps investors calm and the frontier intact.
2. **Pricing: premium holds, plus a developer tier.** Enterprise bundles unchanged. New: a published, non-bundled API tier for firms under $50m revenue at roughly 40% below list, capped so it cannot cannibalise enterprise deals. Why: cheap goodwill with entrepreneurs, grows the ecosystem that locks customers to us, and blunts the "giants" narrative in an election year.
3. **Politics: $900m, and we take the government's deal.** Voluntary quarterly AI-layoff reporting by our enterprise customers through us (the court killed the rule; we pay nothing to comply). Housing: $1bn over the year in visible metro housing finance where our campuses are. Transition fund stays at $2bn/yr; cash is tight. Union request declined: we are not the employers, and no-layoff pledges are not ours to give.

## Public statement
"Demand for AI has not wobbled; the labs are investing steadily, opening cheaper access for small developers, and funding housing in the cities we grow in."

**To federal government:** Deal accepted. Reporting starts Q2; $1bn housing in our metros this year. We expect no pre-emption fight to be reopened by the states.
**To entrepreneurs:** Developer tier launching Q1, published terms, no bundling. Tell us where licensing still blocks you.

## What would change my mind
A rival making a full price cut, or a moratorium bill gaining real votes, would end premium pricing and buy a slow-down pledge respectively.
