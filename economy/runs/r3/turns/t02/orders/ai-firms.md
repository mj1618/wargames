# Orders — ai-firms — round 02 (2028)

## Read of the situation
Shares are down a third and credit is tight, but order books are full and premium pricing held, so the problem is funding, not demand. Labour is mild (unemployment 4.2%, jobs +0.3m, labour share 52.5%), and the government offers "no new taxes or licensing" for honest layoff reporting and visible housing money. Elections are in November; what we do before then sets the rules for 2029–32.

## Options considered
- Race through the crunch on debt (lenders say no; a failed raise is how a lab falls off the frontier).
- Price war while rivals are cash-constrained (burns the margin carrying our capex).
- Unconventional: offer a pre-emption-for-levy deal now, while we look weak (majority against; reserve for 2029).

## Decisions this period
1. **Deploy pace: steady (baseline).** Capex flat in dollars, shifted to inference capacity over new training sites; no lab breaks ranks. Why: credit binds, not demand; steadiness keeps investors calm and the frontier intact.
2. **Pricing: premium holds, plus a developer tier.** Enterprise bundles unchanged. New published, non-bundled API tier for firms under $50m revenue at about 40% below list, capped against cannibalisation. Why: cheap goodwill with entrepreneurs, grows the ecosystem that locks customers in, blunts the "giants" story before the vote.
3. **Politics: $900m, and we take the government's deal.** Voluntary quarterly AI-layoff reporting via our enterprise customers (the court killed the rule; compliance is free). $1bn this year in visible housing finance in our campus metros. Transition fund stays $2bn/yr; cash is tight. Union request declined: we are not the employers.

## Public statement
"Demand for AI has not wobbled; the labs are investing steadily, opening cheaper access for small developers, and funding housing in the cities we grow in."

**To federal government:** Deal accepted. Reporting starts Q2; $1bn housing in our metros this year. We expect no new state licensing push from your side.
**To entrepreneurs:** Developer tier launches Q1, published terms, no bundling. Tell us where licensing still blocks you.

## What would change my mind
A rival's full price cut ends premium pricing; a moratorium bill gaining real votes buys a slow-down pledge.
