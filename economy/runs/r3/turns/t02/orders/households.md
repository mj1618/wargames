# Orders — households — round 02 (2028)

**1. Read of the situation.** Unemployment is 4.2% and few people we know lost jobs, but only 0.3m jobs were added against population growth, and graduates we know are still looking, so the squeeze is on entry and on the next generation. Rents rose about in line with pay, which is a standstill, not relief. Retirement accounts are down with a one-third share fall and credit is tightening, so a lot of us are frightened even though our own jobs are safe so far. The top tenth feels the share fall most; the bottom half barely owns shares and feels it only through pensions and any layoffs that follow.

**2. Options considered.**
- Strike-ready campaign to force no-layoff and notice clauses now.
- Take employers' offer: talks on shorter hours plus retraining funds, with a strike pause only while talks are live.
- Unconventional: pool bargaining with entrepreneurs' care, trades and services firms, with local hiring pledges for young people in exchange for our support on zoning and cheap credit.

**3. Decisions this period.**
1. **Accept employers' talks, with conditions** [`union_pressure`, medium]. No strikes while talks run to mid-year, but we keep the drive going and push for hours cuts without pay cuts, a hiring guarantee for entry-level roles, and retraining paid on the clock. If nothing is signed by June, we resume slow-downs and strike votes. We got few contracts last year because nobody was laid off, so we bargain over hiring, not firing.
2. **Save a bit more** [`household_saving`, +1 point]. Falling accounts and tight credit make us cautious. We stay short of the full +2 because most households have little to cut.
3. **Election-year mobilisation** [votes and protest, peaceful]. Register and turn out working and young voters behind worker protections, entry-level jobs, rent and insurance costs, and taxing AI profits. Back pro-permit candidates locally. No disruptive protest unless talks collapse. No boycotts yet.

**4. Public statement.** "We will talk, but we want jobs for our kids, not only retraining for the ones being replaced. The share fall should not be paid for by workers." Messages:
- To employers: Yes to talks. We want hours cuts without lost pay, entry-level hiring, and retraining on paid time. No strikes while talks are live, but they end if talks stall by June.
- To entrepreneurs: Hire the young people we know. We will back zoning reform and cheap credit if hires are local and permanent.

**5. What would change my mind.** Layoffs hitting people we know, or employers stalling talks, would turn us to strikes and boycotts and to a harder saving cut.
