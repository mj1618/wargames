# Sitrep — round 02 (2028)

**Jobs.** A recession year. The share-market fall and credit squeeze cut spending, and about 4.1m jobs went with it, mostly in retail, office work, construction and manufacturing. Automation removed another 0.7m. Only 0.4m jobs appeared. Employment fell to 158.9m; the share of adults working dropped from 59.0% to 57.4%. Unemployment rose only to 4.9%, because most people who lost work stopped looking: participation fell to 60.3%.

**Pay and prices.** Real pay slipped 0.7%. Prices were flat. Housing costs fell 4% as last year's squeeze unwound and building picked up. Median real income 98.1; bottom fifth 98.3.

**Businesses.** GDP fell 0.8%. Labour's share dropped to 50.7%; the top-1% share rose to 20.9%. Labs slowed to a steady pace; employers returned to normal automation speed and shorter weeks.

**Government.** The Notice Act passed, but a court blocked its notice-and-reporting rule; wage insurance starts in 2029. The Build Act passed in part. Deficit 6.7%, debt 108%. In November the growth-first coalition lost to the worker-first coalition.

**Daily life.** Poverty 13.4%; homelessness down to 20.0 per 10,000.

**What mattered most.** The financial wobble, not the machines, did most of the damage. Employers and unions signed a layoff cap and profit-sharing deal; the labs' transition fund lapsed.

**What a household would have noticed:** rent finally easing, but a neighbour's shop job gone and nobody hiring.
