# Orders — employers — round 03 (2029)

**1. Read of the situation.** We shed 4.8m roles in 2028, only 0.7m to automation; the rest followed falling sales, so demand is now our problem, not someone else's. The worker-first coalition holds the Presidency and Congress, so a profit tax or deployment rules are live risks, while households are offering talks with a June strike deadline. Credit is easing and margins per sale are wide, so we can afford to buy peace if we choose the terms.

**2. Options considered.**
- Stonewall and resume a company-wide drive as credit loosens (fast, but invites taxes and strikes).
- Hold course: normal speed, ~30% layoffs, and wait out the talks.
- Unconventional: pre-empt Congress with a binding sector-wide "hours-for-pay" compact in exchange for a statutory freeze on profit-tax and deployment rules.

**3. Decisions this period.**
1. **Settle with households before June.** Take hours_share to ~35% (from ~25%), with pay topped up to ~half the lost wages for those on shorter weeks, paid for from the subsidy plus ~1.5% of payroll (up from ~1%). Full pay protection is too costly, but half buys the strike peace and keeps customers spending.
2. **Restart entry-level hiring.** Fund a graduate and apprenticeship cohort of about 0.4m across our firms, pace-limited to roughly 40% of the pre-2027 intake, plus paid retraining time for displaced staff (up to 40 hours a year, drawn from the profit-sharing pool). The cost is modest and it answers the chief political grievance.
3. **Automation at normal speed, layoffs held near 30% of shed roles.** Pilots continue and savings are kept mostly as margin, with one-third passed on in prices as before. Use hiring freezes sparingly, since freezes now draw the same headlines as layoffs.

**4. Public statement.** "Employers will meet workers' representatives in good faith: shorter weeks with partly protected pay, jobs for new entrants and paid retraining, in exchange for stable rules while we deliver it." Messages:
- To households: "Yes to a package by May: hours at ~35% with half-pay top-up, an entry-level cohort, paid retraining time. We need no-strike terms and a joint review in twelve months."
- To government (policy-makers): "We will deliver the above voluntarily. A profit tax or deployment rules now would make us cut the cohort and the top-up."

**5. What would change my mind.** A new profit tax or deployment mandate, or a further drop in sales, would make us cut commitments back; a rival shedding aggressively and winning share would push us to speed up.
