# Orders — entrepreneurs — turn 03 (2029)

## Read of the situation

The 2028 wobble has passed: federal credit held, rents fell, the developer tier cut model costs by a third, and private investors are returning. Unemployment is now officially 4.5% (revised down); labour share hit 50.7%, a record low. The new worker-first coalition should bring tighter licensing rules (bad for us in the short term) but also credit and zoning reform (good for us). Customers remain cautious, but demand is stabilizing.

## Options considered

1. **Aggressive pivot to AI-only firms** — maximize profit per founder, undercut incumbents, minimal job creation. Risky if customer spending remains weak.
2. **Balanced expansion into people-heavy services** — build partnerships with worker-first coalition, lock in federal credit access, political cover for longer-term growth.
3. **Bet on factory-built housing and licensed trades** — where zoning allows, lower building costs; high job creation, popular with the coalition.

## Decisions this period

**1. Expand new-firm formation to 10%** — up from 5% in 2028, but disciplined. We've earned credibility; private capital is returning. Mix: 60% people-heavy services (care, trades, supervising machines, bespoke work) and 40% AI-only (where margins are highest). Scale: $3–5bn in seed and early-stage capital deployed.

**2. Major push into factory-built housing and construction services.** Zoning allows in secondary metros and rural areas. Partner with states on upzoning, license self-employed trades, sell turnkey solutions to builders. This sector created almost no new jobs in 2028; we can unlock demand. Scale: $500m dedicated capital, hire 50–100k workers in construction, plumbing, electrical, inspection.

**3. Political: push hard for licensing reform.** Health, education, and trades are the blocks. Offer to train and credential workers ourselves if states drop restrictive licensing. This unblocks thousands of small firms and aligns with the coalition's labour-and-retraining agenda.

## Public statement

"The recovery is real. We are betting $3–5 billion on new firms and people-heavy services where AI makes human judgment more valuable, not less. We're hiring: 50–100k in construction and trades if states upzone. Licensing reform unlocks millions of jobs we cannot create without it."

## Messages

**To: Worker coalition and state governments**  
"We will hire the young people you know — locally, permanently, with training. We will invest in factory housing and trades if you upzone and drop restrictive licensing. This is not charity; it is where the returns are."

**To: AI providers (Meridian, Helix, Lumen, Vanta)**  
"The developer tier is helping. Unbundle now, and we grow faster. We will push for licensing reform and data access if you invest in training for the trades."

## What would change my mind

If federal credit disappears again or customer spending collapses below 2028 levels, I revert to AI-only consolidation and pull back on people-heavy hiring.
