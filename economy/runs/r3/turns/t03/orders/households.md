# Orders — households — round 03 (2029)

## 1. Read of the situation
We won the election and the May talks produced a layoff cap and a profit-sharing pool of about 1% of payroll, so we have something to defend. But 3.8m fewer jobs, participation down to 60.3% and poverty up to 13.4% mean people we know lost work and many have stopped looking, whatever the revised 4.5% says. Rents are falling, which helps, but profits stay near record highs and labour's share is 50.7%.

## 2. Options considered
- Press hard now with a wave of strikes while we hold power in Washington.
- Bank the deal, enforce it, and spend our political capital in Congress.
- Unconventional: a national "first job" pledge campaign, where unions and community groups pool hiring-hall and apprenticeship slots and shame employers who sign nothing.

## 3. Decisions this period
1. **Organising: enforce and extend, not strike.** Medium drive (about the same effort as 2027), aimed at making the layoff cap and profit-sharing pool real in every large workplace: shop-steward monitoring, grievance filings, and sign-ups at firms that signed nothing. Slow-downs only where an employer breaks the deal. Strikes are costly when jobs are scarce and our members have no savings.
2. **Saving: hold at the current elevated level, no further rise.** Rents are easing, but many of us know someone laid off and nobody trusts the recovery. We will not cut back harder, because that costs our own neighbours' jobs, and we will not spend freely either.
3. **Votes and protest: peaceful lobbying of the new government.** Our price for the vote is wage insurance starting on time in 2029, funding for the entry-level and first-job pipeline, and a tax on AI profits to pay for them. Peaceful rallies and town halls, no disruption, since the people we elected are now the ones who can deliver. No boycotts this year.

## 4. Public statement and messages
**Statement:** "We kept our side of the bargain. Employers signed a layoff cap and a share of the profits; now we want it enforced and we want our kids to have a first job."

**Messages:**
- To employers: We accept no wage indexation or automation ban for now; we will watch the cap closely, and any firm that breaks it gets a campaign.
- To the federal government (incoming): We voted for protections. We want wage insurance on schedule, a working notice rule, and a first-job programme for 18–25s. Bargaining rights reform is next on our list.

## 5. What would change my mind
Layoffs among people we know continuing after the cap, or employers dodging the profit-sharing pool, would send us back to strikes; a visible fall in youth unemployment would let us spend more.
