# Sitrep — round 03 (2029)

**Jobs.** A sharp rebound. As credit returned and the central bank eased, spending recovered and brought back about 4.2m jobs, mostly in retail, offices and building. New kinds of work added 0.7m. Automation removed 0.9m, over half in offices. Employment reached 163.8m, above its 2027 level; the share of adults working rose from 57.4% to 58.9%. Unemployment fell to 3.6% and participation recovered to 61.1%.

**Pay and prices.** Real pay rose to 105.8 as the labour market tightened. Prices edged down; housing costs fell another 2%. Median real income 104.6; bottom fifth 103.8.

**Businesses.** GDP grew 4.8%. Labour's share recovered to 52.1%; the top-1% share eased to 20.3%. Labs sped up slightly and opened a cheap unbundled tier for small firms; startups expanded. Employers moved more of the labour saving into shorter weeks.

**Government.** The Worker Security Act (targeted cheques paid for by an 8-point AI-profit levy) failed in Congress. The reporting-and-jobs bill passed and survived the courts: layoff notice is law, and 1m public jobs start in 2030. Antitrust cases opened against the labs. Deficit 6.2%, debt 107%.

**Daily life.** Poverty 12.0%; homelessness 18.6 per 10,000.

**What mattered most.** The end of the credit squeeze did nearly all the work. The dice killed the government's flagship bill (a 70% chance, lost), and the unions' drive stalled.

**What a household would have noticed:** shops hiring again, a slightly shorter week, and rent still falling.
