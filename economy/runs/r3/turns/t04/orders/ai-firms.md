# Orders — ai-firms — round 04 (2030)

## Read of the situation
The boom is ours: GDP +4.8%, unemployment 3.4%, 4.9m jobs added, a hit industry built on our models, lenders reopened, premium intact. The threats are now legal, not electoral: antitrust on pricing and compute access, reporting as law, an 8-point levy whose sponsors are returning. The government's price — $5bn keeps the levy at 8 — beats a 12-point levy or a lost antitrust case.

## Options considered
- Race +30% and refuse the $5bn: maximum share, but hands levy sponsors a villain once every layoff is reported.
- Hold steady, settle antitrust fully, pay $5bn: safe, but cedes the new industry's compute to rivals abroad.
- Unconventional: write the compute-access remedy ourselves, so the antitrust rule binds foreign-linked rivals too.

## Decisions this period
1. **`deploy_pace`: race +20%.** Capex up ~25% on reopened credit; the new industry is compute-bound and whoever serves it wins. Vanta wanted +30%; majority takes +20% to avoid a grid story.
2. **`ai_pricing`: premium holds; unbundled tier stays, floor raised ~10%.** Antitrust defence: a voluntary compute-access charter — published, non-discriminatory API and compute terms for firms under $100m — offered as consent-decree basis. Lumen outvoted.
3. **Politics $1.5bn; fund $5bn this year.** $3bn paid now, half to trades training (buys the entrepreneurs' licensing push); $2bn released when the levy is enacted capped at 8 for five years, fund payments creditable. No licensing; pre-emption kept.

## Public statement
"The fastest-hiring industry in America runs on our models. We will put $5bn into workers and trades training this year and open our compute terms to every small firm."

**Messages.**
- To federal government: "Deal: $5bn this year, $3bn immediately. Lock the levy at 8 for five years, fund payments creditable, antitrust settled on our compute charter, no licensing. Otherwise $2bn and a fight."
- To entrepreneurs: "Compute charter and $1.5bn trades training are yours. Back the charter in the antitrust docket; push licensing reform with us."

## What would change my mind
A moratorium bill gaining real votes, or a rival breaking the premium line, flips us to steady pace and a price match.
