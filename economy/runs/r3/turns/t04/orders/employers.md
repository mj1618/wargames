# Orders — employers — round 04 (2030)

## 1. Read of the situation
Demand is back: sales recovered, we rehired about 4m, unemployment is 3.4% and applicants are scarce with pay rising. No profit tax passed, the doubled work-sharing subsidy failed, and reporting is now statutory, so the peace we bought in 2029 held at a cost of about 1.5% of payroll. The risk now is a tight labour market squeezing margins just as automation (0.9m roles removed) is finally paying, and households are watching the layoff cap.

## 2. Options considered
- Hold course: normal automation, keep shorter weeks and the pool.
- Speed up automation to offset rising pay while labour is scarce.
- Unconventional: convert the profit-sharing pool into an employee equity/ownership scheme, which costs less cash, ties staff to us, and pre-empts a profit levy.

## 3. Decisions this period
1. **Automation: modestly faster (+10%), aimed at back-office and offices, via attrition not layoffs.** Tight labour makes attrition cheap and keeps us inside the layoff cap (layoff_share falls to ~25%). Redeploy displaced staff into the new-industry service lines where we can; pay is rising anyway.
2. **Keep hours_share at ~35% and the top-up at half pay, and hold the pool at ~1.5% of payroll, but pilot an employee equity tranche for part of it.** Cheap insurance against the levy returning after 2030; the equity piece stretches cash and aligns staff. Do not raise it further.
3. **Pay: targeted raises only in scarce roles** (skilled, care-adjacent, tech-operations), roughly in line with market; no general indexation. Prices: continue one-third pass-through; do not cut harder as demand is firm.

## 4. Public statement and messages
Statement: "We have rehired, kept our layoff commitment and extended shorter weeks; we will keep hiring as long as the rules stay predictable."

Messages:
- To households: "We hold the layoff cap and the pool; we will share reporting data early. In return, no campaign against attrition-based restructuring."
- To federal government: "We support statutory reporting and the public-jobs programme. We oppose a profit levy and deployment mandates; offer to fund apprenticeship slots instead."

## 5. What would change my mind
A visible margin squeeze from wage growth, or a revived profit-levy bill with real votes, would push me to faster automation or a bigger political concession respectively.
