# Sitrep — round 04 (2030)

**Jobs.** A second good year. Automation removed 1.3m jobs, the most yet and mostly in offices, but 3.3m were created: 1.4m in new kinds of work led by the hit new industry, 1.0m public jobs, and 0.7m from stronger spending. Employment reached 166.4m. The share of adults working rose to 59.6%, above its 2026 level for the first time. Unemployment fell to 3.3%; participation is back at 61.6%.

**Pay and prices.** Real pay rose to 110.0 in a tight labour market. Prices edged down; housing costs fell about 2%. Median real income 108.8; bottom fifth 107.3. The working week shortened slightly, to 34.0 hours.

**Businesses.** GDP grew 3.6%. Labour's share rose to 52.6%; the top-1% share eased to 20.1%. Labs raced; employers automated faster, through attrition.

**Government.** Congress passed the Wage Insurance and First Job Act: a 5-point AI-profit levy funding wage insurance and a first-job programme from 2031. It survived its court challenge. Deficit 6.2%, debt 108%.

**Election.** The worker-first coalition kept Congress, but with thinner margins: Washington is now divided.

**Daily life.** Poverty 11.2%; homelessness 17.6 per 10,000.

**What mattered most.** The new industry arriving just as the public jobs began; and the levy passing (70%) where last year's bigger bill failed. The unions' effort failed again.

**What a household would have noticed:** employers competing for staff, bigger pay packets, and rent still falling.
