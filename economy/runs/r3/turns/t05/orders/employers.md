# Orders — employers — round 05 (2031–32)

## Read of the situation
Unemployment is 3.1%, 166.4m are in work, pay is up about 4% real and applicants are scarce, but profits are not under pressure and the 5-point AI-profit levy starts in 2031. Automation removed 1.3m roles almost entirely through attrition, so the savings are real and nobody has been hurt loudly. Congress is closely divided, so the levy and any future deployment rules are now negotiable rather than settled, and the energy crunch will slow the labs' capacity.

## Options considered
1. Hold course: normal-plus automation via attrition, targeted raises, keep the pool and shorter weeks.
2. Accelerate (+20%) to offset wage pressure and the levy.
3. Unconventional: offer households a package with published layoff, cohort and pool data in exchange for them backing a levy sunset or ceiling, with a standing joint review.

## Decisions this period
1. **Automation +10% via attrition, not layoffs.** Layoff_share stays about 25%; no mass restructuring. Labour is scarce and wages are rising, so replacing leavers with tools is the cheapest margin defence, and it avoids layoff-cap and reporting trouble. Prioritise offices and back-office; go slower in health and transport where liability bites. Energy delays cap how fast we can buy AI capacity anyway.
2. **Sign the package with households by May.** Publish layoff, cohort and pool data annually. Hold hours_share about 35% with half top-up, pool about 1.5% of payroll, and keep the entry-level cohort at about 0.4m a year plus paid retraining. No blanket no-strike clause, as households asked; we accept a narrower no-strike clause for the term of any signed agreement. Cost is modest against record margins and it buys the quiet that matters most to the board.
3. **Politics: pay the levy, but lobby for a sunset review and a cap, and against deployment caps.** Back a levy review clause tied to unemployment staying below 4%. Raise pay by targeted raises, with pass-through of one-third of savings to prices to keep customers and defend share.

## Public statement and messages
Statement: "Employers will publish our layoff, hiring and profit-sharing data every year and keep hiring new graduates. Stable rules let us keep investing and keep hiring."

- To households: Agreed on a package by May with published data; we will accept a bounded no-strike clause for the agreement's term only. Propose a joint annual review.
- To federal-gov: We pay the levy without a fight. In return, we ask for a review clause and no new deployment rules or layoff caps during the energy crunch.

## What would change my mind
A sharp rise in unemployment, a rival gaining share through big price cuts, or a bill with a deployment cap or higher levy would push me to accelerate automation or fight harder politically.
