# Orders — households — round 05 (2031–32)

**1. Read of the situation.** Work is plentiful: unemployment 3.1%, 166.4m jobs, employers competing for staff, rents still falling and poverty down to 11.2%. But labour's share is only 52.6%, barely above where we started, and the energy crunch plus attrition-based restructuring means the young and the unlucky still face a thin door. This is the strongest bargaining position we have had, and it may not last if data-centre delays or a downturn hit.

**2. Options considered.**
- Spend more and ease off saving, since pay and rents are improving.
- Hold steady and spend our leverage on bargaining reform plus a medium organising drive.
- (Unconventional) Trade quiet on attrition for written hiring floors and a seat on the reporting data.

**3. Decisions this period.**
1. **Organising drive, medium-large**, aimed at attrition-based restructuring and bargaining reform: no-layoff and attrition-replacement clauses, shorter weeks, profit-sharing. Pushing now while hiring is tight gives us the best odds of a roll. No strikes unless the layoff cap or pool is broken. Why: pay gains are real, but share of income has barely moved.
2. **Saving down 1 point.** Wages, rents and wage insurance make us safer, so we spend a bit more. This helps jobs and the shops on our streets.
3. **Peaceful political push** to help the government hold Congress for the 2031 bargaining-reform bill, and to defend the 5-point AI-profit levy and first-job programme from watering down. We turn out for the 2032 election on bargaining rights. No boycotts: jobs are good and nothing is being forced on us.

**4. Public statement.** "Good jobs are back because we organised and held the line. Now write it down: bargaining rights, a floor on replacing people who leave, and a fair share of what AI earns."

**Messages.**
- To employers: We keep the cap, top-up and pool. We will not campaign against attrition itself if you publish the data early and commit to replace a set share of leavers with entry-level hires. Without that floor we organise on attrition.
- To federal government: We will turn out for the bargaining-reform bill. Fund the first-job programme in full and deliver wage insurance on time.

**5. What would change my mind.** If unemployment rises above about 4.5% or layoffs after the cap start to hit people we know, I cut spending, save more, and shift from organising to protecting jobs.
