# Sitrep — round 05 (2031–32)

**Jobs.** Two more good years, despite the power shortage. Automation removed 2.6m jobs, over half in offices, almost all through attrition. But 3.9m were created: 2.4m in new kinds of work and 1.4m from stronger spending. Employment reached 169.1m. The share of adults working rose to 60.1%, a point above 2026. Unemployment 3.2%; participation 62.0%.

**Pay and prices.** Real pay rose to 116.6, about 3% a year. Prices edged down; housing costs fell another 3%. Median real income 114.7; bottom fifth 112.3. The working week shortened to 33.7 hours.

**Businesses.** GDP grew 2.8% a year. Labour's share 52.4%; top-1% share 20.0%. Machines now do 14% of desk work, of 64% they could. Labs spent $60bn on their own power and settled the antitrust cases with a binding compute-access decree.

**Government.** The 5-point AI-profit levy, wage insurance and first-job programme began. The Grid and Household Power Act passed at half size. Deficit 5.7%, debt 109%.

**Election.** The worker-first coalition kept the Presidency and Congress with wider margins.

**Daily life.** Poverty 10.2%; homelessness 16.3 per 10,000.

**What mattered most.** The energy crunch slowed adoption just as entrepreneurs made their biggest hiring push; and the grid bill's roll (55%) came up partial. The union drive failed for the fourth time in five.

**What a household would have noticed:** jobs easy to find, pay beating prices, cheaper rent, and a small rebate on a higher power bill.
