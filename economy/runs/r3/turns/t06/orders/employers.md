# Orders — employers — round 06 (2033-34)

**1. Read of the situation.** Labour is scarce (unemployment 3.1%, real pay up about 3% a year), but profits are healthy after the 5-point levy and the government has widened its majority, so tax and rules risk is now about the level of the levy itself and no higher. Attrition has removed about 2.6m roles over two years, over half in offices, with little backlash so far. Households are offering to stop campaigning against attrition if we publish data early and commit to a floor of entry-level replacement; the consolidation headline raises our input-cost and supplier-lock-in risk.

**2. Options considered.**
- Hold the course: attrition at +10%, no new commitments.
- Accept the household floor: publish data early, replace a fixed share of leavers with entry-level hires.
- Unconventional: use the consolidation to sign long-term compute contracts with the labs that are not merging, in return for price caps and open interfaces, so we are not locked in.

**3. Decisions this period.**
1. **Accept the entry-level floor, set low.** Commit to replace about 25% of leavers with entry-level hires, in a bounded, publishable form, and publish automation and headcount data early each year. Cost is small against a 3% real wage drift; it buys a ban on organising against attrition and keeps the levy and any mandates off the table. Keep layoff_share about 25% and adoption speed at +10% via attrition.
2. **Keep the package.** Hours_share about 35% with half pay top-up, profit pool about 1.5%, 0.4m cohort, and add the government's offered apprenticeship slots (about 0.1m, co-funded). Targeted raises only; hold price pass-through at about one-third.
3. **Hedge compute.** Sign multi-year contracts with non-merged labs, with price caps and portability clauses. Lobby for a levy review clause and no deployment mandates beyond this period, in return for the floor.

**4. Public statement.** "Large employers will publish our automation and hiring data each year and commit to a share of entry-level hiring as roles turn over. We support a stable rulebook that lets us keep investing."
Messages:
- To households: Yes on the data and a floor of 25% of leavers replaced by entry-level hires; in return no campaign against attrition and no strike on the package.
- To federal government: Accept apprenticeship co-funding; we want the levy review clause and no mandates, and ask for scrutiny of the merger on compute-access terms.

**5. What would change my mind.** A sharp rise in unemployment or a fall in sales would push me to slow adoption and raise the floor; a rival cutting prices with deeper automation would push me to drop the floor.
