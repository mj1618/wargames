# Orders — entrepreneurs — turn 06 (2033–34)

## Read of the situation

Real wages rose 3% a year over two years (under our 10% red line); we hired 2.4m and compute held. The consolidation headline is the shock: two largest AI/cloud providers merging or signing exclusive pacts, combined with a +15% price increase on the unbundled tier, means our API-dependent growth model faces a squeeze. Secondary metros responded to our zoning pressure, but big states still block housing; labour is still scarce. Re-election of the worker-first coalition gives us political runway.

## Options considered

1. **Commit volume to the unbundled tier**: lock in supply at +15%, bet consolidation drives workload concentration and demand offsets margin compression.
2. **Pivot aggressive**: shift 50% of hiring back to people-heavy services, pull back on API scaling, preserve margin and hedge compute-supply risk.
3. **Nuclear antitrust push**: weaponise the consolidation news, push Congress and FTC to block the merger, leverage our political credit with the re-elected government.

## Decisions this period

**Decision 1: Hedged compute commitment.** Commit 60% of volume to the unbundled tier (locking in supply and +15% price); allocate 40% to diversified infrastructure (cloud providers outside the merger, premium labour-heavy services). Spend about $8bn on this mix. This trades some margin pressure for supply security and doesn't lock us fully into consolidated providers.

**Decision 2: Secondary-metro housing acceleration.** Double down on factory housing and community governance in metros that upzoned. Invest $5bn in 2033–34 to capture zoning wins before big states change minds. This scales people-heavy jobs, unlocks licensing reform, and builds political durability in secondary cities.

**Decision 3: Antitrust signalling.** Commission a public study on merger impacts (cost to small firms, supply risk, reduced innovation); brief Congress and FTC; stop short of formal lobbying to preserve optionality. Cost: $50m. If the merger blocks, we win political capital and supply diversity. If it proceeds, we've staked a claim and kept leverage.

## Public statement

"We're investing $13bn in secondary metros and computing diversity. The merger is a test: can founders still compete, or are we locked behind giants? We'll work with Congress to make sure competition survives."

## Messages

**To: Labs.** "We'll commit volume to the unbundled tier, but only if you defend supply diversity—don't use consolidation to wall off competitors."

**To: Congress.** "Secondary-metro zoning works. Pair it with antitrust enforcement on compute access and you get jobs, housing, and political durability."

## What would change my mind

If consolidation proceeds and the big provider jacks prices another 15%+ before 2035, we consolidate to 40% of current hiring pace and shift hard to people-heavy.
