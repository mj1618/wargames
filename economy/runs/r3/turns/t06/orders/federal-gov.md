# Orders — federal-gov — round 06 (2033–34)

## 1. Read of the situation
The boom holds: unemployment 3.1%, 169.1m jobs, GDP +2.8% a year, poverty 10.2%, deficit down to 5.7%. Nothing in the numbers demands a rescue, but 1.0m AI layoffs over two years and a labour share stuck at 52.4% keep the base restless. The consolidation is the one visible threat: it could undo falling prices and the compute charter that entrepreneurs' hiring rests on, and my wider margins make this the window to act.

## 2. Options considered
- Coast: enforce existing law, bank the boom until the 2034 midterms.
- Spend the margin on the base: bargaining reform plus an open-access statute for the merged provider.
- Unconventional: trade federal pre-emption of data-centre moratoria for a lab-funded universal dividend.

## 3. Decisions this period
1. **Compute Open Access Act (bill 1).** `competition_policy` → open-access mandates: the merger is allowed only with statutory non-discriminatory compute access, published prices and unbundling; no break-up. Includes pre-emption of local data-centre moratoria where the operator pays full grid costs. No budget cost. Fiscal moderates and growth-minded members win; progressives lose the break-up.
2. **Workplace Bargaining and First Job Act (bill 2).** Bargaining reform (faster recognition, sector-level bargaining over AI deployment terms, no layoff caps); first-job programme funded in full, about 0.1% of GDP, paid from the existing levy, starting 2034. Labour wins.
3. **Hold the line by executive action.** Levy stays at 5 points with a 2035 review clause; no new deployment rules; wage insurance paid on time; antitrust enforcement of the decree with quarterly price reporting. Deficit hawks win: no new tax, no new entitlement.

## 4. Public statement
"The boom belongs to the people who work in it: open access to computing, a real voice at work, and a first job for every young American. No company gets to own the future alone."

**Messages**
- To AI firms: "Merger clears only with statutory open access and unbundling. You get moratoria pre-emption and no break-up. Fight the bargaining bill and that trade is off."
- To households: "Bargaining reform gets its vote this year and first-job is funded in full. Turn out, loudly, before the vote."

## 5. What would change my mind
Unemployment past 4.5%, AI layoffs past 1m a year, or post-merger prices rising would move me to break-ups, a higher levy and targeted transfers.
