# Orders — households — round 06 (2033–34)

**1. Read of the situation.** Unemployment is 3.1%, pay is beating prices by about 3% a year, rents keep falling and poverty is down to 10.2%, so most of us feel better off. But labour share is stuck at 52.4%, our organising drive won few signatures and the package is still unsigned, and no bargaining-reform bill has been brought. Two big AI and cloud providers consolidating means more power for the few over the tools our employers depend on, and a market this tight is the best leverage we will get.

**2. Options considered.**
- Hold steady: organise modestly, lobby, keep spending.
- Big push to sign the package and win bargaining reform before the 2034 midterms.
- Unconventional: demand a seat for worker and consumer reps in the consolidation review, and a worker-owned share of any merged-company gains.

**3. Decisions this period.**
1. **Organising drive, medium-large, aimed at signatures.** Put organisers into attrition-heavy sectors (office, logistics, retail) and sign up the package's terms workplace by workplace. Employers have offered a bounded no-strike clause for the agreement's term, so we accept it and take the joint annual review. No strikes unless the layoff cap or profit pool is broken. Why: they have already moved, and a signed deal makes their terms binding.
2. **Spending vs saving: saving down 1 point.** Real pay is rising and rents falling, so we spend a bit more. We stay cautious because one consolidation shock could change that.
3. **Politics: peaceful midterm 2034 push for bargaining reform, a stronger levy and first-job funding, plus a demand to scrutinise the consolidation.** Tenant and neighbourhood groups also back the entrepreneurs' secondary-metro zoning deal, but only with written local hiring, pay floors and land-trust stakes. No boycotts.

**4. Public statement.** "Wages are finally up, but families should hold the gains in writing: sign the package, pass bargaining reform, and put workers in the room on the AI mergers."

Messages:
- To employers: "We accept a bounded no-strike clause for the term and a joint annual review. Sign now, with published data and attrition floors."
- To entrepreneurs: "We back upzoning if hiring targets, pay floors and community land-trust stakes are in writing first."

**5. What would change my mind.** Unemployment above 4.5%, a layoff-cap breach, or the consolidation producing price hikes or mass hiring freezes would make me save more, raise the pressure and consider disruptive action.
