# Sitrep — round 06 (2033–34)

**Jobs.** Automation doubled its pace: 5.1m jobs went, half of them in offices, still mostly through attrition. 4.7m were created: 3.3m in new kinds of work and 1.3m from stronger spending. For the first time since 2028, fewer jobs were created than destroyed. Employment still rose to 170.0m because the population grew; the share of adults working slipped to 59.9%. Unemployment 3.3%. Office employment fell by 1.4m.

**Pay and prices.** Real pay 123.9, up about 3% a year. Housing costs fell another 4%. Median real income 119.6; bottom fifth 116.1. The working week shortened to 33.2 hours.

**Businesses.** GDP grew 3.1% a year. Labour's share fell to 51.1%; the top-1% share rose to 20.6%. Machines now do 24% of desk work, of 84% they could. The big compute pact went ahead under a new open-access law.

**Government.** Congress passed the Compute Open Access Act in narrower form and the Workplace Bargaining and First Job Act in full; courts upheld both. Deficit 5.5%. The coalition kept Congress.

**Daily life.** Poverty 9.6%; homelessness 15.5 per 10,000.

**What mattered most.** The open-access bill came up partial (80% odds), but even at half size it stopped the consolidation from raising prices. The union drive failed again, though employers and unions signed the package.

**What a household would have noticed:** pay and cheaper rent still winning, but more office colleagues not replaced when they left.
