# Orders — employers — round 07 (2035-36)

**1. Read of the situation.** Profits are healthy, pay is up about 3% a year real, and applicants are scarce, so we can afford peace. Automation removed about 5.1m roles over two years (twice the prior pace, half in offices), mostly through attrition, which makes us a target if the pace keeps doubling. The new ruling makes us fully liable for automated decisions, so the next tranche of automation now carries tail risk in lending, claims, hiring and care.

**2. Options considered.**
- Hold the pace and absorb liability as a cost of doing business.
- Slow automation in decision-heavy functions only; keep going in back-office and logistics work with no consequential decisions.
- Unconventional: set up a shared industry liability pool and audit standard with insurers, and lobby for a safe-harbour for firms that follow it.

**3. Decisions this period.**
1. **Segment the automation drive.** Stay at normal pace plus 10%, via attrition, in back-office, logistics and customer-service work. Move to cautious pilots (about 20% slower) with mandatory human sign-off in credit, claims, hiring and clinical decisions until liability is priced. Why: the liability tail is unbounded, while the savings from rushing those functions are small by comparison.
2. **Sign the household package now.** Accept the bounded no-strike clause, joint annual review, published data and the 25% entry-level floor, plus the apprenticeship slots. Hold layoff_share at about 25% and hours_share at about 35%. Keep the profit pool at about 1.5%, with targeted raises. Why: the deal is cheap relative to a strike or a deployment-rules fight, and the mood of a worker-first government is stable.
3. **Fund an industry liability-and-audit standard.** Put about 0.1% of payroll into a joint insurer and employer pool for audit tooling and incident reporting, and ask Congress for a safe-harbour for firms certified to it. Why: it caps the tail and heads off harsher rules.

**4. Public statement and messages.** "We are signing the package and committing to audited, human-supervised automated decisions; responsible deployment and good jobs go together."
- To households: Agreed on the bounded no-strike clause, annual review and published data. We will sign this round, with floors set as attrition-based rather than hard headcount quotas.
- To labs: We want indemnity or audit cooperation for model failures in regulated decisions, and we will keep multi-year contracts only with providers that offer it.

**5. What would change my mind.** A rival cutting prices on the back of faster automation without liability losses, or liability losses large enough to make even the back-office savings unattractive.
