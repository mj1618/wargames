# Orders — entrepreneurs — round 07

## Read of the situation

End 2034: 170m jobs, 51.1% labour share, real wages up 3% year-on-year—below my red line but stable. The liability ruling for automated decisions is the inflection: it kills the margin on pure AI-only hiring and forces full human oversight. My secondary-metro housing deals are stalled without big-state zoning, but households are offering to unlock zoning if I commit to hiring targets, pay floors and community land-trust stakes in writing.

## Options considered

1. **Consolidate into AI-only with heavy compliance**: hire only where I can afford full human review of automated decisions (much smaller scale, higher cost per hire).
2. **Shift aggressively to people-heavy and hybrid models**: hire where humans make the judgment calls; accept lower margins but unlimited scale and lower liability.
3. **Seal the secondary-metro deal**: commit to hiring targets and pay floors in exchange for big-state zoning and licensing reform; make the political play to unlock scale.

## Decisions this period

**1. Shift hiring mix.** I'm moving from 60% unbundled APIs to 40% unbundled (capped by compute scarcity and now by liability risk) and 60% people-heavy and hybrid services where humans make the key calls. This lowers my per-founder profit but scales hiring without liability exposure. Budget: 1.2m hires in 2035–36; split 40% AI-augmented people-work, 60% fully human-staffed (care, trades, housing, retail, bespoke services). The unbundled tier stays frozen—I'll lock in the price and diversify providers.

**2. Seal the secondary-metro housing deal.** I'm committing in writing to hiring targets (500k net new jobs in secondary metros by 2037), wage floors (120% of regional median for two years minimum), and community land-trust stakes (25% of non-owner-occupied units to community trusts). In return, I'm pushing hard with households and the worker-first coalition for big-state zoning reform and licensing unbundling for trades, care, and construction. This unlocks scale and political cover.

**3. Signal compute diversification.** With the pact allowed and prices held, I'm moving away from dependence on the dominant pair. I'll shift 30% of new unbundled capacity to smaller providers (Helix, Vanta) and edge compute where possible. If prices hold, I stay. If they spike, I have fallback.

## Public statement

"Entrepreneurs are committing to hiring 1.2 million people over the next two years—40% in AI-augmented trades and services, 60% in fully human-staffed sectors. We're locking in secondary-metro housing projects with written pay floors and community stakes. The liability ruling means we hire where humans judge; that's where the jobs are."

## Messages

**To households:** "We're ready. Secondary metros, written hiring targets (500k by 2037), wage floors of 120% regional median locked in two years, and community land-trust stakes in 25% of housing. In return, we need zoning and licensing reform on your side. Let's close this."

**To worker-first coalition:** "The liability ruling blocks pure AI hiring at scale. We're pivoting to people-heavy and hybrid work. Secondary-metro deals unlock votes for licensing reform and zoning. We can move 500k jobs to secondary metros if you back us on the big states."

## What would change my mind

If any of the big API providers raises unbundled-tier prices by more than 10%, I consolidate hard into smaller providers and people-heavy services only, cutting hiring by 40%.
