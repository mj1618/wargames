# Orders — households — round 07 (2035–36)

**1. Read of the situation.** Pay is beating prices by about 3% a year and rents keep falling (housing costs down ~4%), poverty is 9.6% and unemployment 3.5%, so most of us are better off than in 2027. But labour's share is only 51.1%, 2.0m AI layoffs are on the books, and jobs grew just 1.0m in two years; more office jobs now vanish when someone leaves, which is how the young get shut out. The package and bargaining reform are won on paper, but our workplace drive added few members, so paper is all it is until we use it.

**2. Options considered.**
- Take employers' offer as is: data plus a 25% entry-hire floor, in exchange for quiet on attrition.
- Push for a higher floor (40%) and keep organising.
- Unconventional: turn the new liability ruling into a bargaining tool: demand clauses that name a human decision-maker accountable for every automated pay, scheduling or firing decision.

**3. Decisions this period.**
1. **Accept the employers' offer with amendments.** Take the data-sharing and the 25% entry-hire floor, but ask for a 12-month review that lifts it to 35% if unemployment stays under 4%. We do not campaign against attrition itself, but we keep the right to organise and bargain over how it is done. Why: a floor in writing beats a fight we are losing on membership.
2. **Medium organising drive aimed at membership, not strikes.** Target the firms using the liability ruling: automated-decision accountability, appeal rights and notice. No strikes, no boycotts while the cap, pool and package hold. Spend the effort on signing up members now, while jobs are plentiful.
3. **Peaceful, loud turnout for the bargaining reform vote and first-job funding.** Rallies and calls to Congress before the vote, then a 2036 midterm push. Saving rate down 1 (we are comfortable enough to spend, and spending keeps hiring up).

**4. Public statement and messages.**
Statement: "Pay is up and rents are down because we organised. Now put the entry-level floor and the data in writing, and give us a human to answer to when a machine decides."
- To employers: "Yes to data and a 25% floor; we want review language to raise it to 35% in a tight market. No campaign against attrition, but we organise at your sites."
- To the federal government: "We will turn out for the bargaining vote. Fund first-job in full and keep the levy at 5 points."

**5. What would change my mind.** Unemployment rising above 4.5%, a breach of the layoff cap or pool, or the entry-level floor being ignored would put strikes and boycotts back on the table.
