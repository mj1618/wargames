# Sitrep — round 07 (2035–36)

**Jobs.** Automation slowed: 4.3m jobs went (5.1m last time), 2.1m of them in offices. 5.1m were created: 3.8m in new kinds of work, 1.3m from stronger spending. Created beat destroyed again. Employment 172.2m; 60.2% of adults work, more than in 2026 (59.1%). Unemployment 3.3%. Office employment fell another 0.9m.

**Pay and prices.** Real pay 132.8, up about 3.5% a year. Housing costs fell another 5%. Median real income 126.7; bottom fifth 122.1. The working week is 32.7 hours.

**Businesses.** GDP grew 3.2% a year. Labour's share slipped to 50.4%; top-1% share 20.9%. Machines do 31% of desk work, of 90% they could, and 11% of physical work.

**Government.** Congress passed a narrower liability safe harbour, then a court struck it down; the ruling stands alone. The apprenticeship and levy bill passed at half size: levy 5 through 2036, 6 from 2037. Deficit 5.2%.

**Daily life.** Poverty 8.7%; homelessness 14.1 per 10,000.

**What mattered most.** The liability ruling, plus labs and employers choosing caution, cut automation's pace by about a sixth while new work kept arriving. The unions' drive finally succeeded after six failures.

**What a household would have noticed:** bigger pay packets, cheaper rent, a shorter week, and a human to appeal to when a machine decides.
