# Play-through r4 — summary

## 1. In three sentences
Machines took over almost half of all desk work and a fifth of physical work in ten years, yet as many jobs were created as destroyed (about 29.5 million each) and unemployment never passed 4.4%. Ordinary households ended about 30% better off and the poorest fifth about 36% better off, while poverty and homelessness nearly halved. The catch: office work shrank by 8.7 million jobs, graduates struggled to get started, and wages' share of national income fell to a record low.

## 2. The hidden conditions and events
AI improved quickly, firms adopted it fast, and robots were only about four years behind. In its favour: many new kinds of human work appear, pay shares well in productivity gains, housing can be built cheaply, and lost wages do not snowball. Against it: Washington started gridlocked.

Events: a court ruling making firms liable for automated decisions (2027), free AI models (2028), a capability leap (2029), a robot safety recall (2030), a bond-market scare (2031), a consumer boom in cheap AI-assisted services (2035).

## 3. The story
- **2027.** The liability ruling slows automation. Employers freeze hiring instead of laying people off.
- **2028.** Employers start a company-wide automation drive. Voters replace the growth-first coalition with a worker-first one.
- **2029.** The Worker Transition Act (wage insurance and apprenticeships, paid by a 3-point levy on profits) passes on a one-in-three chance.
- **2030.** The robot recall freezes physical automation. For the first time more jobs are created than destroyed.
- **2031–32.** Congress raises the levy to 7 points, pays a dividend to lower-income households and funds house-building.
- **2033–34.** Robots arrive at scale; 7.4m jobs go against 6.9m created.
- **2035–36.** Cheap AI services with people in the loop catch on: 10.3m jobs created against 8.6m destroyed.

## 4. Answer 1 — jobs
Destroyed: 29.5m. Created: 29.6m. About 25m of the new jobs were new kinds of work, 3m came from extra household spending and 1m from displaced people taking lower-paid work. Offices fell from 48.0m to 39.3m; new-economy firms grew from 4.6m to 12.7m, care by 4.3m, construction by 2.4m.

Unemployment went from 4.1% to a 4.4% peak (2028), ending at 3.9%. The share of adults in work dipped from 59.1% to 58.4% and recovered to 59.0%. The working week fell from 34.3 to 32.1 hours.

The Jevons effect held only partly. The textbook version (cheaper goods, so people buy more) barely operated: prices hardly fell, because AI firms kept their margins and pay absorbed most of the savings. New kinds of work replaced the lost jobs instead. It held for care, construction and new-firm workers; it failed for office workers and graduates.

## 5. Answer 2 — living standards
Median real household income rose 30% (the no-AI trend gives about 9%); the bottom fifth rose 36%. The top 1% share of income rose from 20.0% to 22.2%; wages' share fell from 52.8% to 46.5%.

Housing costs fell 19%. Other prices barely moved. Poverty fell from 12.9% to 6.9%; homelessness from 22 to 12 per 10,000. The federal deficit narrowed from 6.0% to 4.5% of GDP; debt ended at 99%.

## 6. What drove it
**Hidden conditions (most of it).** Plentiful new work, generous pay sharing and cheap housing would have delivered a good result under most choices.

**Players' decisions (who gained).**
1. Employers cut through hiring freezes and shorter weeks, not mass layoffs. Unemployment stayed low but graduates were shut out.
2. Government's profits levy, lower-income dividend and housing money lifted the bottom fifth above the middle.
3. AI firms held prices, so shop prices stayed flat.

**Dice (the politics).** The 2028 election flipped on a one-in-three chance and the Transition Act passed on similar odds. Six of seven union organising drives succeeded at even odds. The final-round consumer boom added about 3m jobs.

## 7. Where the players were unrealistic
- Households never struck, even with wages' share at a record low.
- AI firms stopped fighting a profits levy that tripled.
- Industry and government missed the same liability-for-worker-fund bargain six times.
- By 2033 all five players backed the same wage-insurance model.

## Notes for synthesis
- The job-creation split comes from the scorecard columns summed over all rounds: new kinds of work 25.3m, extra spending 3.1m, slow re-absorption 1.2m, own-price demand 0.03m.
- The strong hidden demand response was dealt but almost never used, because prices stayed flat.
