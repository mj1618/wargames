# Orders — employers — 2027

## 1. Read of the situation
Margins are at a record (profits 14.9% of GDP, labour share 52.8%), so the board's job is to protect that while not being undercut. Counsel wants a human sign-off on automated decisions in lending, claims, hiring and clinical work after the full-liability ruling, which slows roll-outs this year, and rivals are doing the same, so nobody gets a head start there. Hiring of young graduates in exposed jobs is already down about 19%, and our one price-cut pilot (-10%, sales up) is confounded by a rival's outage, so it proves nothing yet.

## 2. Options considered
- Full-speed company-wide drive now, ignoring the sign-off caveat.
- Normal pace in back-office and customer service, human-in-the-loop in regulated decisions, quiet attrition.
- Unconventional: offer Washington a voluntary no-mass-layoff pledge in return for a statutory safe harbour from the liability ruling for audited human-in-the-loop systems.

## 3. Decisions this period
1. **Automate at normal pace, concentrated in unregulated back-office and service work** (`adoption_speed` normal; no 15-30% acceleration this year). Put a named person on every sign-off in lending, claims, hiring and clinical work; budget roughly 1-2% of the affected cost base for review staff until insurers price the risk. Why: speed in regulated lines buys liability we cannot yet insure, and rivals are slowed equally.
2. **Shift shedding toward quiet attrition:** move `layoff_share` from about 60% down to about 45%, using hiring freezes and not replacing leavers, especially entry-level roles. Why: layoffs are visible and invite regulation and press, while freezes deliver the same saving at roughly the same speed and cost us nothing politically.
3. **Run controlled price tests:** repeat the 10% price cut in three to four business units with matched control units, pass on about half the saving where volume responds, and hold `wage_sharing` at current practice with only modest profit-sharing for retained staff. Why: we need clean evidence on whether price cuts win share before anyone else does.

## 4. Public statement and messages
**Statement:** "We are adopting AI carefully, with a named person accountable for every automated decision that affects a customer, and we will keep investing in our people through the transition."

**Messages:**
- To Government (White House / Congress): We would support a safe harbour for audited human-in-the-loop systems under the liability ruling, and we would offer a voluntary pledge against mass layoffs in exchange for stable deployment rules and no new AI-profits tax.
- To Insurers (if a seat exists; otherwise via public channels): We want to work with you on a standard for pricing the risk of automated decisions so sign-offs can be streamlined.

## 5. What would change my mind
A rival visibly gaining share or margin from a faster, riskier roll-out, or a credible threat of a profits tax or deployment rules, would push me to accelerate and lobby harder.
