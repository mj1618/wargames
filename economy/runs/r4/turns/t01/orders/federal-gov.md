# Orders — Federal government — 2027 (t01)

## 1. Read of the situation
Headline numbers look fine: unemployment 4.1%, 162.4m jobs, so there is no visible crisis to legislate against. The pressure points are political, not statistical: labour share at a record-low 52.8%, profits at a record 14.9% of GDP, two-thirds of voters wanting AI slowed, and young-graduate hiring in exposed occupations down about 19%. With a 6.0% deficit, thin margins and "almost nothing passes", we have room for cheap, visible moves only, and donors are asking about the liability ruling.

## 2. Options considered
- Full statutory repeal of the liability ruling: what donors want, but it loses the vote and hands the opposition a 2028 advert.
- Do nothing in Congress, govern by executive action only: safe, but leaves us with no record.
- Unconventional: trade a liability safe harbour for mandatory disclosure, and pair it with a small wage-insurance bill to pick off opposition votes.

## 3. Decisions this period
1. **Bill 1 — Liability safe harbour (conditional).** Firms that keep a named human sign-off and audit trail on consequential automated decisions get capped liability; others stay fully liable. No budget cost. Deregulators win, with security hawks' audit condition attached. Why: gives donors something without looking like a blank cheque.
2. **Bill 2 — Wage insurance and retraining pilot, `retraining_pct_gdp` = 0.1%** (about $30bn a year, starting 2028), aimed at displaced workers and recent graduates, offset by trimming existing programmes. Deficit hawks set the size; a nod to the opposition for votes. Why: a cheque-shaped benefit landing in election year.
3. **Executive action — `deploy_regulation` = disclosure, plus `housing_policy` = incentives.** Large employers must report layoffs attributed to AI; existing grant money is tilted towards states that loosen zoning. No antitrust cases this year. Why: buys us data and a rent story at near-zero cost.

## 4. Public statement
"America will lead in AI and Americans will share in it: clear rules for firms that keep a human accountable, and real help for workers who change jobs."

**Messages**
- To AI labs (Meridian, Helix, Lumen, Vanta): We will move a safe harbour, not a repeal. Back the disclosure rule publicly and do not announce mass layoffs before November 2028.
- To state governments: Federal housing money will follow zoning reform. Loosen and you are first in line.

## 5. What would change my mind
Unemployment above 5.5%, a visible fall in wage-tax receipts, or street disorder would move us to larger transfers and tougher rules.
