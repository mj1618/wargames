# Orders — Entrepreneurs — Turn 02 (January 2028)

## Read of the situation

The open-weight price war has inverted our economics. Free models now do most of what our firms need; input costs collapse. Our supervision and sign-off services added 0.5m jobs in 2027 with customers willing to pay—proof that liability liability ruling creates a market. Labour's share hit 52.2% (record low) while corporate profits soar, yet Congress again took no antitrust action. Zoning-loosening states have federal grants; housing-cost bottleneck is real and undefended.

## Options considered

1. Full-throttle AI-only expansion (highest margin, minimal hiring, let incumbents dominate people-heavy)
2. Double down on human-centric supervision and sign-off (slower scaling, tied to wage inflation)
3. Pivot capital into factory-built housing in friendly states, ride the federal incentives

## Decisions this period

1. **Expand AI-only firms at 20% growth** (new agent-driven businesses, minimal payroll). Free models make this highly profitable; undercut incumbents on price. Commit $150–200m to founder grants and seed capital.

2. **Hold and grow people-heavy services** (maintain ~50% of firm mix). Liability ruling is an asset—customers need AI supervision from a trusted human. Keep hiring in care, tutoring, design, and sign-off roles where wage market allows.

3. **Launch housing push in zoning-friendly states** (CA, TX, NV, NM). Factory-built and modular housing undercuts traditional construction if permitted. Recruit 30–40 founder cohorts; highlight federal grants available. Target 5% of new business volume.

## Public statement

"Free and open-weight AI models finally give founders a fair shot. We're scaling up where the return is—AI-agent businesses, liability supervision, and next-generation housing. Congress' inaction on antitrust means incumbents still hold advantages, but price competition is working where policy won't."

## Messages

**To: Workers and unions**
"We're hiring for supervision roles: humans signing off on AI decisions. The safe-harbour Congress wouldn't pass means we own liability and need your skills. Wages will follow demand; we'll post roles."

**To: Federal government**
"Zoning loosening works—expand it. Next: modernise licensing for new services. Incumbents gate-keep through credential capture, not merit. Let founders and small firms compete fairly."

## What would change my mind

If customers stop paying for human oversight or credit dries up, we'd exit supervision services and consolidate into pure AI-only and low-skill hiring.

## Journal entry

The open-weight collapse is a windfall: inputs now free, margins suddenly viable for founders. We're accelerating AI-only firm creation (better per-founder returns) while locking in people-heavy services (customers proved willing to pay, liability = our asset). Housing looks like a genuine high-upside opportunity in zoning-friendly states. Watching: whether customer willingness to pay for human oversight persists as prices fall and wages stagnate; credit remaining normal; Congress moving on incumbent gatekeeping or antitrust.
