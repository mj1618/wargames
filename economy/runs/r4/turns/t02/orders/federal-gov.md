# Orders — federal-gov — t02 (2028)

## 1. Read of the situation
Aggregates are benign in an election year: unemployment 4.2%, real pay +1.5%, prices flat, housing costs −2%, GDP +1.6%. The weakness is underneath: 0.6m AI-blamed layoffs announced, more posts closed by freezes, graduate hiring weak, labour share at 52.2%. Deficit is 6.1% and our vote count is unchanged, so only small or donor-funded bills pass.

## 2. Options considered
- Run on the numbers, pass nothing, avoid floor losses.
- Youth hiring bill plus a rebuilt safe-harbour deal paid for by industry.
- Unconventional: pre-empt the opposition with a one-off universal "AI dividend" cheque before November — rejected, deficit hawks will not wear it.

## 3. Decisions this period
1. **Youth Hiring and Apprenticeship Act (bill 1).** Employer wage credit for hiring under-25s and registered apprenticeships, about 0.15% of GDP a year (~$45bn) from 2029, lifting `retraining_pct_gdp` to roughly 0.2% with the existing pilot. Credits can be claimed on hires made from signing day. Why: households say under-25 jobs move 2028 votes; it is visible before November and billed after. Deregulators win on design (credit, not a programme); deficit hawks lose on cost.
2. **Accountable Automation Act (bill 2).** Safe harbour for audited human-in-the-loop systems; no deployment limits, no profits levy (`capital_tax_pct` 0, `deploy_regulation` stays at disclosure); in exchange an industry-paid transition fund of about $20bn a year offsetting bill 1, and a public employer pledge against mass layoffs through 2029. Why: donors get certainty, we get a pay-for and a no-layoffs headline. Deregulators win; security hawks content.
3. **Executive, no Congress.** Keep the layoff disclosure rule and extend it to hiring freezes at large employers; keep zoning-linked housing grants. No antitrust cases: the open-weight price war is already doing that job. Deregulators win.

## 4. Public statement
"The economy is growing and pay is rising; our job now is to make sure every young American gets a first job in it. Industry will help pay for that."

**To AI firms and Employers:** Deal accepted in outline. Draft with us this quarter; the fund must be at least $20bn a year and the layoff pledge public and signed before the summer, or the bill does not move.

**To Households:** The youth hiring bill is our first vote this year.

## 5. What would change my mind
Unemployment past 5.5%, a fall in wage-tax receipts, or donors walking from the fund would push us to bigger wage insurance and drop the safe harbour.
