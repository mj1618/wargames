# Orders — Households — Turn 02 (2028)

## 1. Read of the situation
Unemployment is 4.2% and pay beat prices (+1.5% real, rents -2%), so most households feel okay on paper. But jobs are not being refilled when people leave, graduates are still at home, and labour's share hit a record low of 52.2% while profits boom. The clauses we won will fade unless we keep working at them, and it is an election year.

## 2. Options considered
- Keep the drives going and widen them to more big employers (steady, uses the liability ruling).
- Save much harder out of fear of the unfilled-jobs trend (hurts others' jobs, and fear is not yet justified).
- Unconventional: a "human-in-the-loop" shoppers' pledge, where unions and community groups steer spending to firms that signed sign-off clauses, rewarding signers instead of just boycotting.

## 3. Decisions this period
1. **Sustain and widen organising** (`union_pressure`): renew the no-layoff and human-sign-off drives at last year's employers, and add a second wave at mid-size healthcare, logistics and back-office employers. No strikes; use bargaining and the liability ruling. Go for shorter-week and profit-sharing language, as pay is rising but the labour share is not. Why: gains fade without upkeep, and jobs are still plentiful enough to make it work.
2. **Spending and saving** (`household_saving`): hold saving flat, with no extra cut-back. Pay is up and prices are flat, so there is no reason to panic. Why: cutting spending now would cost our neighbours' jobs.
3. **Votes and demands**: put youth hiring, apprenticeships and a stronger wage-insurance programme at the front of the 2028 campaign. Back whoever commits to them, with peaceful rallies only. Why: the opposition has adopted our demand, so this is the moment to lock it in. No boycotts of AI services yet, as cheaper AI is making some bills fall.

## 4. Public statement and messages
**Statement:** "Pay is up, but the people who built these gains are being left out of them. We want young people hired, jobs protected, and a share of the profits."

**Messages:** none.

## 5. What would change my mind
Visible layoffs hitting people we know, a spike in unemployment past about 5%, or Congress cutting liability protections would push us to harder action: strikes, protest and saving more.
