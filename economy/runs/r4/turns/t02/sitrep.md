# Sitrep — 2028

**Jobs.** About 2.0m jobs disappeared, all to automation, and 1.5m new ones appeared, three times last year's number. With population growth, employment edged up from 162.05m to 162.22m. Unemployment 4.4% (4.3% a year ago); the share of adults in work slipped from 58.7% to 58.6%. Office work lost 1.1m posts and gained 0.2m. New-economy firms, care and construction grew.

**Pay and prices.** Real hourly pay rose 2.3%. Shop prices were flat; housing costs fell another 1.9%. Median real household income 102.7; bottom fifth 102.2.

**Business.** GDP grew 2.6%. Labour's share fell to 51.6%; the top 1% took 20.5%. Machines now do 6.5% of desk work, double last year, of 19.4% they could.

**Government.** Deficit 6.1% of GDP, debt 104.3%. Poverty 12.3%, homelessness 20.4 per 10,000. Unrest 36.0.

**What mattered most.**
1. Employers launched a company-wide automation drive on cheap open-weight models while keeping cuts quiet: freezes, not layoffs.
2. The dice in Washington. The youth hiring bill failed, the safe-harbour deal never reached the floor (industry offered $3bn against the $20bn asked), and in November voters removed the growth-first coalition. Worker-first now leads a Congress that is still gridlocked.

Also: the organising drive succeeded again; a court blocked the hiring-freeze disclosure rule.

**What a household would have noticed.** Better pay, cheaper rent, free AI tools everywhere, and a new government elected on the promise that the graduate at home gets hired.
