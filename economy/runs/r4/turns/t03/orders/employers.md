# Orders — Employers — 2029

## 1. Read of the situation
The drive worked: about 2.0m posts removed, only 0.8m by announced layoff, margins wider, sales firm. But labour share is 51.6% and unemployment 4.4%, and the worker-first coalition now leads, so a profits tax or deployment rules are live risks, and our pledge deal was struck with the outgoing side. Settlements are rising with the second organising wave, and the new week-long-project models make a further leap tempting and cheap.

## 2. Options considered
- Keep the drive going at the same pace and sign the deal as drafted.
- Slow to normal pace and avoid the political heat until the new Congress shows its hand.
- Unconventional: fund the $20bn ourselves through a private-sector displacement fund, with a public signed pledge, to pre-empt a statutory tax.

## 3. Decisions this period
1. **Adoption: stay company-wide drive (about +20%), on the week-long-project models, concentrated in back-office and claims work.** Rivals will move, and falling behind is the one outcome the board will not forgive. Keep layoff_share near 45%, relying on attrition and freezes, since freezes are no longer disclosed.
2. **Close the safe-harbour deal: back a fund of at least $20bn a year and sign a public no-mass-layoff pledge before summer.** Cheap insurance against a profits tax from the new coalition. Cap the pledge at defined mass-layoff thresholds, with a clause that lets it lapse if profits fall sharply.
3. **Raise hours_share to about 20% and share about a third of the gains as profit-sharing or raises at unionised sites.** It buys labour peace and sales, and costs margin only modestly. Decline the three-year Assured Autonomy bundles for now; sign one-year terms in regulated lines only, since open-weight stacks plus our own sign-off are cheaper.

## 4. Public statement and messages
**Statement:** "Large employers will fund worker transition at scale and pledge no mass layoffs, in return for stable, clear rules on deploying AI."

**To Government:** We accept the outline. We will back a fund of at least $20bn a year and a signed public pledge before summer, provided the bill carries safe-harbour and no new deployment mandates or profits tax this Congress.

**To AI firms:** One-year terms only, with price protection. We will not lock three years in before the liability picture clears.

## 5. What would change my mind
A profits tax or hard deployment rules gaining a floor majority, or a rival visibly taking share with a bigger cut, would change how fast and how openly we move.
