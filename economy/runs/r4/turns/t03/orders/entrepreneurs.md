# Orders — Entrepreneurs — January to December 2029

## Read of the situation

Free open-weight models have collapsed input costs to near zero: new-economy firms grew 400,000 workers (4.8m to 5.2m) and we're hiring for human-oversight roles at twice the 2027 pace, proving customers will pay for AI-supervised services. But labs are signalling the price war is over—they're offering three-year premium bundles and keeping indemnified tiers, which threatens our margin advantage before we've locked in volume.

## Options considered

1. **Lock in volume contracts.** Negotiate three-year fixed-price AI bundles with multiple labs before premium pricing tightens; tie up capital but secure margins.
2. **Consolidate into megafirms.** Build fewer, larger AI-only firms with deeper automation to defend against price rises; risk political backlash on jobs.
3. **Pivot to hard assets.** Shift capital into factory-built housing in zoning-friendly states (California, Texas, Arizona)—lower ongoing input risk, real collateral, larger per-founder returns.

## Decisions this period

**1. Lock in AI inputs (capital commitment: $50B+, pace: 3 months).** 
Sign three-year fixed-price supply agreements with Meridian, Helix, and Lumen before labs pull back discounts. Secure premium indemnified tiers for AI-supervision products. Why: margins vanish if we wait; customers will accept price stability if we offer it first.

**2. Scale factory-housing buildout (capital: $80B+, pace: sustained through 2030).** 
Direct 50% of new founder capital into modular housing production in California, Texas, and Arizona—states with loosened zoning. Partner with municipal development agencies where possible. Why: permits are loose now; rents still compress supply; this is higher-return per founder than AI-only firms and defensible against AI price wars.

**3. Hold people-heavy services at 25–30% of new hiring.** 
Keep scaling AI-supervision, care-tech, design, and tutoring services. Don't abandon this mix even as AI-only firms show better per-founder returns. Why: customers have voted; liability = our asset; less vulnerable to input price shocks.

## Public statement

The free-model era is ending. We're locking in AI inputs at today's prices and building the factories that will house the next generation—and the people-heavy services that customers trust.

### Messages

**To Congress (worker-first coalition):** Zoning deregulation, not training programmes, is the real jobs lever. Housing supply normalizes rents, which raises what households can spend on services and small business. Loosen it and unemployment will fall naturally.

## What would change my mind

If AI-only firm margins compress significantly despite input locking, or if housing demand collapses due to slower household formation and credit tightening.
