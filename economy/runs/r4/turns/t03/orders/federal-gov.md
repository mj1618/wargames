# Orders — Federal government — 2029 (t03)

## 1. Read of the situation
Aggregates still look fine: unemployment 4.4%, real pay +2.3%, GDP +2.6%, median income 102.7, wage-tax receipts held. But labour's share fell to 51.6%, announced layoffs rose to 0.8m, office employment is shrinking, and models now do week-long projects unaided. We are worker-first with a split Congress and a 6.1% deficit, so only small, paid-for bills can pass.

## 2. Options considered
- Go big now: universal transfers plus a 10-point capital tax (fails; donors revolt; no visible crisis).
- Small paid-for package: scale wage insurance, fund it with a modest AI-profits levy.
- Unconventional: trade industry its safe-harbour vote for a statutory levy and disclosure, making the labs co-authors of the worker bill.

## 3. Decisions this period
1. **Bill 1 — Worker Transition Act.** Raise `retraining_pct_gdp` from about 0.05% to 0.3% of GDP (wage insurance, youth apprenticeships, work-sharing subsidy for employers who cut hours not jobs), paid by `capital_tax_pct` = 3 points on AI/large-firm profits, starting 2030. Deficit-neutral. Labour wins the design; fiscal moderates win the size; progressives lose on scale.
2. **Bill 2 — Accountable Automation Act, rewritten.** Safe harbour only for audited systems with human sign-off on high-stakes decisions, in exchange for statutory disclosure of AI layoffs *and* hiring freezes (curing the court block) and the industry fund at $5bn a year, not $1bn. Floor vote before summer recess if industry publicly backs Bill 1's levy. Moderates win; progressives grumble.
3. **Executive action.** Expand zoning-conditioned housing grants (`housing_policy`: incentives, top tier) and order a licensing-reform review for new services. No antitrust break-ups, no deployment limits yet. Moderates win.

## 4. Public statement
"The economy is growing; our job is to make sure the people doing the work share in it. Firms that profit from automation will help pay for the workers it moves."

**To AI firms:** You get your floor vote before recess if the fund is $5bn a year, freeze disclosure is in statute, and you do not fight a 3-point levy. $1bn does not buy a vote.

**To Entrepreneurs:** Zoning grants expand this year; licensing review ordered. Back the Transition Act publicly and small firms get the work-sharing subsidy first.

## 5. What would change my mind
Unemployment above 5.5% or falling wage-tax receipts would move us to large transfers and public jobs; a bond-market wobble would shelve everything unfunded.
