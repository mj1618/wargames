# Sitrep — 2029

**Jobs.** A new model generation that runs week-long projects alone widened what machines can do from 19% to 30% of desk work; firms now use them for 11%. About 2.6m jobs were destroyed, 1.5m of them in offices, and 2.4m created, mostly new kinds of work. With population growth, employment rose from 162.2m to 162.6m. Unemployment 4.3% (4.4%); the share of adults in work slipped again, 58.6% to 58.5%. Office employment fell by 1.0m; new-economy firms added 0.75m.

**Pay and prices.** Real hourly pay rose 3.1%; the working week shortened a little. Shop prices flat; housing costs down 2.3%. Median real household income 105.2; bottom fifth 104.2.

**Business.** GDP grew 3.3%. Labour's share fell to 50.9%; the top 1% took 20.8%.

**Government.** Deficit 6.0% of GDP, debt 105.1%. Poverty 11.9%, homelessness 19.7 per 10,000. Unrest 35.8.

**What mattered most.**
1. The dice in Washington finally landed: the Worker Transition Act passed in full (wage insurance, apprenticeships and work-sharing at 0.3% of GDP, paid by a 3-point profits levy from 2030) and survived a court challenge.
2. The safe-harbour bargain collapsed. Industry offered $12–20bn a year only if there was no levy; government wanted both. No fund, no pledge, no safe harbour.

Also: a third organising wave succeeded.

**What a household would have noticed.** A bigger pay packet, cheaper rent, a slightly shorter week, and fewer office openings for the graduate at home.
