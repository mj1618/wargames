# Orders — Employers — 2030 (turn 04)

## 1. Read of the situation
Margins widened again with labour share at 50.9% and GDP +3.3%, so the automation lever is still working. But the Worker Transition Act is law, the 3-point profits levy lands this year, and the safe harbour and our worker fund both died, so our political insurance bought nothing. Warehouse and home robot injuries bring a recall and new certification, which stalls the robot line while software automation (audited stacks, now at open-weight prices) keeps running. A third organising wave now reaches attrition cuts, so our quiet exit route is narrowing.

## 2. Options considered
- Keep the company-wide drive and absorb the levy through savings.
- Slow down and shift to hours and profit-sharing to defuse unions and the next election.
- Unconventional: turn the levy into a credit. Offer to fund apprenticeships and work-sharing directly, in return for crediting that spend against the 3-point levy and for a safe harbour.

## 3. Decisions this period
1. **Keep software automation at company-wide pace (about +20% on normal) on audited stacks.** Co-sign the AI firms' pledge and resume indemnified deployments in back-office, claims and customer service, because the audit standard cuts liability risk. Hold physical robots to cautious pilots until certification clears; no new warehouse commitments.
2. **Hold layoff_share near 45%, with hours_share up to about 25%.** Announced layoffs stay near 1m. Where unions already hold attrition clauses we trade for hours cuts and profit-sharing, topped up to about 50% of the hours saving. Lowering the visible-layoff share helps us before the 2030 and 2032 elections. We also keep the entry-level floor at today's intake, with no new cuts, to answer the Households message cheaply.
3. **Lobby on levy mechanics.** Push the apprenticeship-credit deal through the trade bodies, pledge a new fund of roughly $10bn a year (half the earlier $20bn, tied to the credit), and oppose any rise in the levy rate. Prices stay put except where rivals cut.

## 4. Public statement and messages
**Statement:** "Employers will keep investing in AI and in people. We will keep entry-level hiring, give shorter weeks where workers want them, and fund apprenticeships alongside the new act."
**To AI firms:** We co-sign the pledge and resume indemnified deployments, conditional on audited stacks and commodity pricing holding.
**To Households:** We hold entry-level intake and human sign-off. In return, no boycotts and no new organising-driven strikes.

## 5. What would change my mind
Unemployment above 4.8%, a visible consumer sales slump, or a rival winning share by pricing aggressively would push us towards faster cuts or deeper price cuts. A levy increase would also do that.
