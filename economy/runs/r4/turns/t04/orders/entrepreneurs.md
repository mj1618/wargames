# Orders — Entrepreneurs — Turn 04 (January–December 2030)

## Read of the situation

We grew by 0.75m workers (fastest expansion yet), signed three-year AI contracts at favorable rates, and are scaling factory housing in zoning-friendly states. The Worker Transition Act's 3-point profits levy hits us, but the licensing review could unlock health and housing entry. Real pay is rising (3.1%), employment holding steady, and customers are still spending; supervision roles are being locked into union contracts, which validates our people-heavy bet.

## Options considered

1. **Accelerate pure AI-only firms** — maximize per-founder returns despite the levy hit, but risks political backlash and regulatory scrutiny.
2. **Pivot toward supervision and trust services** — lock in customers who pay for human oversight, align with union demands and new licensing possibilities, accept lower per-founder margins.
3. **Lean hard into factory housing as the primary wealth driver** — it's the highest-upside play with state support, house it in a separate track from wage employment.

## Decisions this period

**Decision 1: Rebalance the firm mix** — Shift from 60/40 (AI-only to people-heavy) toward 45/55. New supervision, auditing, care, and skilled-trade services where liability and trust are assets. Hire aggressively into these (another 0.3m workers), slow pure AI-only expansion. Rationale: the levy eats AI-only margins; supervision is now contractually protected; licensing opens professional services.

**Decision 2: Accelerate factory housing** — Commit additional $60B+ to housing plants in newly loosening states (Florida, Texas, Arizona, Georgia). Hire 0.2m construction and design workers. Establish separate operating company to isolate from wage-employment levy exposure. Rationale: highest per-founder return; state support expanding; less vulnerable to AI commodity pricing.

**Decision 3: Lock in credit and supplier relationships** — Extend the three-year AI contracts where possible to four years; secure warehousing and land for housing pipeline through 2032. Pace: aggressive, commit capital now while credit is good. Rationale: credit will tighten if employment slips; AI input prices may not stay this favorable post-levy era.

## Public statement

"We back the Worker Transition Act and wage insurance. Supervision and trust services are where entrepreneurs create the most jobs now—and they're profitable. We're hiring aggressively into care, skilled trades, and housing. The levy is manageable; what we need is zoning reform and fast-track licensing in health and construction."

## Messages

**To: Government**
"Backing the Transition Act publicly. We need state zoning grants to flow faster and the licensing review to prioritize health and skilled-trade credentials. Housing permitting is our bottleneck, not capital."

**To: Unions**
"We support supervision and no-layoff clauses in people-heavy roles. These are permanent jobs with skill growth. Let's align on training and apprenticeship models so new workers can command higher pay."

## What would change my mind

If the levy jumps above 5% or the Fed signals a downturn, we'd pull back aggressively and purge AI-only investments to preserve capital.
