# Orders — Federal government — 2030

## 1. Read of the situation
Aggregates are good in a midterm year: unemployment 4.1%, real pay +3.1%, GDP +3.3%, prices flat, housing −2%, deficit 6.0%. The damage is underneath: labour share 50.9%, office jobs down about 1m, announced layoffs 1.1m, and our base is naming its price (youth hiring, attrition notice). Industry and employers are now offering $12–20bn a year plus a signed pledge for safe harbour, which is a cheque we can show before November.

## 2. Options considered
- Take the industry deal whole, including the five-year levy moratorium.
- Trade safe harbour for the fund and pledge, but freeze the levy only for this Congress; pass the households' bill alongside.
- Unconventional: no legislation; campaign on the good numbers and let the robot recall carry the regulation story.

## 3. Decisions this period
1. **Bill 1 — Accountable Automation Act (deal).** Safe harbour for systems audited to the published standard; no deployment caps and no levy *increase* this Congress (through 2032). The existing 3-point levy stays; no five-year moratorium. In return: industry/employer fund of $12bn in 2030, $20bn a year from 2031, public no-mass-layoff pledge signed before summer, fund lapses if the pledge is broken. Fiscal moderates win; progressives lose on the cap.
2. **Bill 2 — Youth Hiring and Notice Act.** Under-25 hiring and apprenticeship credit, about 0.15% of GDP from 2031, paid mostly from the fund so the deficit is untouched; 90-day notice rule for attrition and freeze cuts above 500 posts. Labour wins. Moved as a pair with Bill 1: neither goes without the other.
3. **Executive action.** Mandatory safety certification for warehouse and home robots after the recall (fast, light, visible); federal housing grants tilted further to states that loosen zoning. No antitrust.

## 4. Public statement
"Pay is rising and jobs are holding, and this year we make those who profit from automation put $20bn a year into young workers and fair notice. Robots that hurt people will not be sold until they are certified safe."

**To AI firms:** Safe harbour and no caps are yours; levy is frozen at 3 points through 2032, not five years. Pledge signed by June or the bill dies.

**To Households:** Youth hiring credit and attrition notice go to a vote this year, paired with the industry fund. Turn out for it.

## 5. What would change my mind
Unemployment past 5.5%, falling wage-tax receipts, or industry walking from the pledge would push me to a higher levy and deployment licensing.
