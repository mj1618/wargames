# Orders — households — Turn 04 (2030)

## 1. Read of the situation
Pay is up 3.1%, rents fell again and unemployment is 4.1%, so most of us are comfortable right now. But labour share is 50.9%, a record low, office openings are scarce and the graduate at home is still looking. The Worker Transition Act starts paying out this year, and the robot recall shows these machines are not ready for our shops and care homes.

## 2. Options considered
- Keep organising and push attrition and hiring-freeze clauses wider.
- Spend more now and save less, since pay and rents are good.
- Unconventional: use union funds and the new apprenticeship money to run our own hiring halls that place young people into the protected supervision roles.

## 3. Decisions this period
1. **Fourth organising wave** (`union_pressure`, moderate, no strikes). Extend attrition and hiring-freeze clauses and the supervision-role classes to more mid-size employers. Add a rule that a share of any attrition savings goes into entry-level slots. Pace is steady, because the clauses fade if we don't keep them up and jobs are still plentiful.
2. **Saving flat** (`household_saving`). Pay is good, but office layoffs are close to home and the graduates are stuck. Neither a cutback nor a spending spree.
3. **November vote and peaceful demand.** Back whoever defends the Transition Act levy and promises youth hiring and a notice rule for freezes. Hold rallies in the autumn to tie the vote to this. Union and community groups also help enrol members in wage insurance and apprenticeships, so the money reaches us.

No boycott of robots yet. The recall is doing that work for us. We will boycott AI-run care and shop services only if a firm tries to push them back in uncertified.

## 4. Public statement and messages
**Statement:** "Wages are finally rising and rents are falling. We want that to last, and we want our kids to get a first job. Defend the Transition Act and put young people to work."

- **To Government:** Run the Transition Act apprenticeships so young graduates are placed first, and pay out wage insurance quickly. Don't weaken the profits levy.
- **To Employers:** We will sign a joint pledge that attrition savings fund entry-level hiring. We still refuse any no-boycott or no-rules pledge.

## 5. What would change my mind
If layoffs among people we know jump, or Congress cuts the levy or wage insurance, we move to saving more, protest harder, and escalate to strikes.
