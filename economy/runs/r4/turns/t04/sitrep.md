# Sitrep — 2030

**Jobs.** For the first time more jobs were created (2.6m) than destroyed (2.1m). Machines can now do 37% of desk work and firms use them for 15%; the robot recall froze physical automation for the year. With population growth, employment rose from 162.6m to 163.8m. Unemployment 4.1% (4.3%); the share of adults in work rose to 58.7%, still below 2026's 59.1%. Offices lost another 0.6m jobs; new-economy firms added 0.75m, care 0.45m.

**Pay and prices.** Real hourly pay rose 3.2%; the week shortened again. Shop prices flat; housing costs down 1.7%. Median real household income 108.2; bottom fifth 106.6.

**Business.** GDP grew 3.4%. Labour's share slipped to 50.7%; the top 1% held at 20.8%.

**Government.** The profits levy and Transition Act started: deficit 5.7% of GDP, debt 105.4%. Poverty 11.4%, homelessness 18.7 per 10,000. Unrest 34.6.

**What mattered most.**
1. The recall, plus the executive's robot-certification rule surviving court, slowed automation just as the fourth organising wave succeeded.
2. The safe-harbour bargain missed a third time: labs offered $5bn, employers $10bn with strings, Government wanted $12–20bn and a signed pledge. The paired youth-hiring bill died with it.

Worker-first kept its lead in November, and Congress came back less deadlocked.

**What a household would have noticed.** Pay up again, rent down again, robots pulled from the shop floor, and new firms hiring — though not many office jobs for the graduate.
