# Orders — employers — 2031-2032 (turn 05)

## 1. Read of the situation
We cut about 2.1m posts last period (0.8m by announced layoff) and margins held despite the 3-point levy, so software automation still pays; labour share at 50.7% and GDP +3.4% tell us the model works. But the fourth organising wave means clauses now cover most large sites, our levy-credit ask and safe harbour both died, and a divided Congress plus a bond-market scare puts a higher profits levy or new deployment rules back in play. Robots remain stuck behind mandatory certification, so the gains stay in software and office work.

## 2. Options considered
- Keep the 2030 plan: company-wide software drive, attrition-led cuts, pledges, and more lobbying.
- Mass restructuring (up to 60% faster) to bank savings before union clauses and any higher levy bite.
- Unconventional: accept the Households joint pledge (attrition savings fund entry-level hiring) and bundle it with a revenue-neutral offer to Treasury to help with the bond scare, in exchange for freezing the levy rate.

## 3. Decisions this period
1. **Adoption and shedding.** Stay at company-wide drive (+15-20% faster than normal), not mass restructuring, which would trigger boycotts and the rules we fear. Hold layoff_share near 45% and lean on attrition and freezes, which clauses increasingly cover; shift to redeployment where clauses bind. Robots: certified pilots only, about 5% of capex.
2. **Hours and pay.** Raise hours_share toward 30% on our own and back the 50% government-backed route at sites with work-sharing support, using Transition Act subsidy to cut cost. Keep profit-sharing at about 15% of gains, not more, to preserve margin.
3. **Politics.** Sign the Households joint pledge limited to attrition savings funding entry-level hiring (about 1% of savings, a few $bn a year). Take AI firms' deal: one-year terms with renewal cap and the 15%-off three-year bundle with full indemnity, but co-sign the safe-harbour letter only if worded as audit-standard compliance, not a liability shield. Lobby for a fixed-rate levy (no increase) in exchange for supporting deficit-reduction proposals.

## 4. Public statement and messages
**Statement:** "Large employers will fund entry-level hiring from automation savings and back shorter weeks; we ask Congress for a stable levy rate and an audit-based safe harbour."
- **To Households:** Yes to the entry-level hiring pledge funded by attrition savings; we understand you reject no-boycott and no-rules terms and do not ask for them.
- **To AI firms:** One-year terms with renewal cap accepted; we will co-sign a safe-harbour letter tied to audit-standard compliance, and take the bundle at 15% off with indemnity.

## 5. What would change my mind
A levy hike above 3 points, binding deployment rules, or a rival cutting prices with margins intact would send us to mass restructuring or a harder political line.
