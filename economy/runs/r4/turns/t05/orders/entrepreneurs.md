# Orders — Entrepreneurs — Turn 05 (January 2031–December 2032)

## Read of the situation

We've added 750k workers in new-economy firms for two years running (6.70m total); people-heavy supervision and care firms are hiring quickly because customers prove willing to pay for human oversight. Factory housing is working in zoning-friendly states with rents easing. But the bond-market scare has lenders watching credit conditions carefully, and the 3-point profit levy now applies to our margins.

## Options considered

| # | Option | Upside | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Aggressive: keep 750k/year hiring pace, expand housing into new states | Scale the defensible model (supervision, care); housing upside still large | Bond scare kills credit; margins crushed by levy |
| 2 | Defensive: cut hiring to 300–400k, focus entirely on profitability | Weather levy and credit tightness; maintain cash generation | Lose market share; customers shift to AI-only competitors who undercut on price |
| 3 (unconventional) | **Selective growth:** accelerate people-heavy services (high margin, customer-proven, labor-friendly), pause new housing starts until bond/credit picture clears | Defensible revenue model; political tailwind from worker-first Congress; avoid overcommitting during credit uncertainty | Misses the housing-cost-reduction window if zoning momentum fades |

## Decision

### Public statement
"The bond market volatility highlights why human-centered services and licensed professions matter: they're defensible, profitable, and customers will pay for them. We're building there, not chasing AI-only margins."

### Major actions

| # | Action | Reasons it should succeed | Resources | Visibility |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Accelerate hiring into care, supervision, trades:** 0.4m new hires in 2031–32 across human-contact services (nursing, elder care, equipment supervision, craft trades) | Customers proved willing to pay; licensing review may unlock health/care sectors; labour peace narrative supports human-centred model; real wage growth (+3.2%) sustains customer demand | $18B+ payroll expansion; existing hiring infrastructure; partnerships with unions | PUBLIC |
| 2 | **Moderate housing expansion:** continue current pace in zoning-friendly states (CA, TX, FL, NC) but delay entry into resistant states until credit/bond picture stabilizes; focus on factory-built units in friendlier markets | Factory methods proven; rents falling where supply rose; don't overcommit during credit scare | $15B committed (down from $20B trajectory); existing state partnerships | PUBLIC |

### Minor actions
- File licensing-review comments supporting human-oversight requirements in healthcare and high-stakes sectors.
- Lock longer-term workforce-development partnerships with community colleges in construction and skilled trades.

### Messages

| To | Channel | Message |
|---|---|---|
| **Labour organizing unions** | Back-channel | "Your wins on no-layoff and human sign-off clauses are aligned with where the real money is: supervision, care, trades. Let's talk about apprenticeships and mobility." |
| **Zoning-reform coalition (states)** | Public advocacy | "Factory housing is working where zoning permits. Accelerating there; blocking states lose the cost-reduction benefit." |

### Contingencies
- If credit markets significantly tighten (spreads >200bp, lender surveys show retreat), pause new housing starts and pivot entirely to profitability/cash flow.
- If licensing review opens health/care sectors, accelerate hiring into those niches.

## Journal entry (append to journal.md)

**Turn 05 (January–December 2032):** The bond-market scare is a warning but not yet a crisis. We're doubling down on people-heavy services—supervision, care, trades—where customers proved willing to pay and labour peace supports the narrative. Housing continues in zoning-friendly states but we're not overextending. Four-year AI contracts lock in input costs. Political winds are favorable (worker-first Congress, liability rules, labour organizing all support human-centred oversight). Watching: bond spreads and Fed rate signals; licensing-review outcomes; whether customer willingness to pay persists if wage growth slows; state zoning momentum.
