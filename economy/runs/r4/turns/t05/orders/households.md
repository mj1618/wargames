# Households — Turn 05 (2031–2032)

**1. Read of the situation.** Pay is up 3.2%, rents are down 2% a year, unemployment is 4.2%, and wage insurance and apprenticeships are finally paying out, so the broad middle is better off than at any point in this decade. But labour share is 50.7%, a record low, and office openings are still scarce. The bond scare is the new worry: if it forces cuts, our new protections are the first thing a nervous Congress could trim. The top tenth, who own the shares, are doing far better than we are and would back "calm the bond market" more readily than we would.

**2. Options considered.**
- Keep organising and push the clauses further down to mid-size employers.
- Take Employers' deal: no boycotts and no new strikes in return for entry-level intake and sign-off.
- Unconventional: set up household-owned co-operatives and a community-run apprenticeship network alongside the Entrepreneurs, so we hold a stake in the new jobs and not just clauses.

**3. Decisions this period.**
1. **Organising: fifth wave, aimed at mid-size employers and care, retail and logistics.** Maintain the existing clauses (they fade if neglected), with a modest budget and no strikes. Entry-level intake and human sign-off are already on the table, and jobs are plentiful enough for a roll to work.
2. **Accept Employers' offer in part.** No boycotts, and no new strikes while the entry-level and sign-off commitments hold. We keep the right to resume if intake is cut or they break the deal. We say so publicly, so that a deal over entry-level jobs is a record we can point to.
3. **Vote and spending: back the youth hiring credit and attrition notice, and defend the Transition levy.** Turn out for the vote. We accept the industry fund only if it is additional to wage insurance. Saving stays flat. We will not cut spending over the bond scare, because that would cost our neighbours' jobs. If layoffs near us clearly rise, I would raise saving by 1 point.

**4. Public statement.** "The clauses are working, so keep them and finish the job: youth hiring, attrition notice, no cuts to wage insurance because bond traders got nervous. We keep our side of the deal as long as the entry-level jobs are real."

Messages:
- **To Employers:** "No boycotts and no new strikes while entry-level intake and human sign-off hold. If either is cut, we resume."
- **To Government:** "You have our votes for youth hiring and attrition notice if the industry fund adds to wage insurance. Do not pay for the bond scare out of the Transition Act."

**5. What would change my mind.** Rising layoffs among people we know, or a cut to the Transition levy or wage insurance, would send me back to higher saving and a harder line.
