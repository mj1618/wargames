# Sitrep — 2031–32

**Jobs.** Over two years 5.2m jobs were destroyed and 5.2m created — a draw. Population growth lifted employment from 163.8m to 164.9m. Unemployment 4.2%; the share of adults in work was 58.6% (2026: 59.1%). Offices lost 1.8m jobs; new-economy firms added 1.5m, care 0.8m, construction 0.5m. Machines now do 25% of desk work (51% possible); certified robots returned, doing 6% of physical work.

**Pay and prices.** Real hourly pay rose 6.5% over the two years; the week shortened to 33.5 hours. Shop prices flat; housing costs down 4%. Median real household income 113.8; bottom fifth 115.3.

**Business.** GDP grew 3.3% a year. Labour's share fell to 49.4%; the top 1% took 21.2%.

**Government.** The profits levy rose to 7 points, paying a dividend to lower-income households and trimming the deficit to 5.4% of GDP (debt 105.6%). Poverty 9.7%; homelessness 15.7 per 10,000. Unrest 32.7.

**What mattered most.**
1. Both of Government's bills passed a divided Congress and the levy survived court: the dividend and the Housing Build Act lifted the bottom fifth above the median's gain.
2. The safe-harbour bargain missed a fourth time — the labs said yes just as Government withdrew the offer. The fifth organising wave failed.

Worker-first held the presidency and Congress in November, with a firmer majority.

**What a household would have noticed.** Pay up, rent down, a dividend cheque for those on lower incomes, new homes going up — and still few office openings.
