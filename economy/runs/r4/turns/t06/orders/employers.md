# Orders — Employers — 2033-34 (turn 06)

## 1. Read of the situation
Unemployment is steady at 4.1% and GDP is growing 3.3% a year, but the 7-point levy has cut margins and labour's share is 49.4%, so the political risk now sits in the levy rate, not in deployment rules. Our 5.2m removed posts, 2.1m of them by announced layoff, are tolerated only because Households has traded a boycott and strike truce for entry-level intake and human sign-off. Certified robots and certified AI stacks arrive in 2033, which is where the next earnings gain comes from.

## 2. Options considered
- Mass restructuring now, before robots and stacks standardise (fastest margin gain, breaks the truce).
- Hold the company-wide drive, add certified robots at scale where recall risk is low.
- Unconventional: offer the government a levy-for-reinvestment swap, where firms that show retained or created jobs get a lower effective levy rate.

## 3. Decisions this period
1. **Adoption:** keep the company-wide drive (+15-25%). Move certified robots from pilots to scaled rollout in warehousing, logistics and back-of-house retail only. Budget about 30% of automation spend on it, in stages by site. Stay out of mass restructuring: the truce and the 2034 election cycle are worth more than a few quarters of margin.
2. **Heads and hours:** hold layoff_share near 45%, meaning attrition and freezes first. Move hours_share to 30%, our own cap, with partial top-up of weekly pay. Keep the entry-level intake at or above its 2032 level, since it is cheap and it buys the truce. Share about 15% of gains as profit-sharing.
3. **Politics:** lobby for a levy-rate freeze at 7 points, with a credit for documented job retention and apprenticeships. Back the state wage-insurance-plus-cash model as the preferred federal template over a higher levy. Co-sign the AI firms' certified-stack terms by June, with one-year contracts and liability carve-outs.

## 4. Public statement and messages
**Statement:** "Employers will keep entry-level hiring and human sign-off in place and put certified automation to work. We back wage insurance that gets people re-employed faster, and we ask for a stable levy rate."

- **To Households:** "We will keep entry-level intake and human sign-off. Please confirm that the truce holds through 2034, and tell us the intake level you will measure."
- **To AI firms:** "We will co-sign by June on one-year terms. We need audit-backed indemnity, and your commodity pricing held through 2034."

## 5. What would change my mind
A levy rise above 7 points, or a rival cutting prices on a robot-enabled cost base, would push me to mass restructuring and drop the truce.
