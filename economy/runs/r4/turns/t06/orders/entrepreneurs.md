# Orders — Entrepreneurs — Turn 06 (January 2033–December 2034)

## 1. Reflection

The 2031–32 period confirmed our model: people-heavy services and housing expansion in zoning-friendly states deliver predictable returns and political shelter. We've grown new-economy employment by 1.5m workers (our seat, 6.7m to 8.2m); supervision, care and trades filled quickly despite wage growth. Housing continues steady. Credit is solid, the bond scare passed, and the 7-point levy on our profits is stable. Labour's share is at record lows, but the worker-first Congress backs our people-heavy model because it creates jobs and keeps voters off the street. Key uncertainties: Will the licensing review deliver on health and trades credentials? Will four-year AI contracts—now expiring—hold margins if models cheapen further? Can housing permitting keep pace as states tighten budgets?

## 2. Options Considered

## 3. Decisions This Period

**Decision 1:** Expand people-heavy services, targeting 0.3m new hires across supervision, care, and trades over the two years. Maintain four-year AI contract rolling strategy to lock input costs ahead of any model-price shifts. Scale: $40B capital + operating. Why: Demand proved durable through wage growth; labour peace and organizing support narrative; households showing willingness to pay.

**Decision 2:** Housing—commit $50B to factory-built homes in zoning-friendly states, especially those with federal Housing Build Act matching. Scale and pace aggressive where permits flow, conservative where zoning stays tight. Why: Margins solid, state policy now favorable, federal building money unlocks more permits, per-founder upside highest in this category.

**Decision 3:** Political—publicly back Housing Build Act in divided states and Congress; amplify licensing review momentum by supporting health/trades credential prioritization. Why: Aligns with government message; unlocks regulatory wins; constituencies (workers, states, feds) all move in same direction.

## 4. Public Statement

"New-economy firms are the jobs engine in this cycle: 1.5m workers, now 8.2m, growing care, supervision and skilled trades where customers choose human oversight. We're committing to housing and people-heavy growth because both deliver jobs and margins. Support for the Housing Build Act and fast-track licensing on health and trades keeps the model working."

## 6. Contingencies

If licensing reviews stall: shift capital temporarily toward housing and supervision (lower regulatory risk) until health/trades rules clarify. If state zoning tightens: pull back housing spend and reallocate to care/supervision expansion. If models cheapen sharply: accelerate four-year contract lock renewals immediately.

## 7. Journal Entry

**Beliefs:** People-heavy services and housing are defensible against commoditization; worker-first Congress and labour organizing support this model long-term; the 7-point levy is manageable. Customer willingness to pay for human oversight proved durable through 3% real wage growth. **Committed:** 0.3m new hires (people-heavy), $50B housing, public Housing Build Act support, AI contract rolling strategy. **Watching:** licensing review outcomes, state zoning velocity, model pricing, federal housing money velocity, whether wage-growth slowdown eventually cracks customer willingness to pay.
