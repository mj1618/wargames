# Orders — Federal government — t06 (2033–34)

## 1. Read of the situation
Voters are content: unemployment 4.1%, real pay +3.2% a year, median income 113.3, housing −2% a year, and we hold a firmer majority. The slow leak is underneath: labour share 49.4%, office jobs −1.8m in two years, and a tax base that is 70% wages, though the deficit eased to 5.4% and yields calmed. A state wage-insurance-plus-cash pilot works, and both industry and Households are offering deals ahead of the November 2034 midterm.

## 2. Options considered
- Bank the win: no new bills, let the dividend and housing grants do the campaigning.
- Scale the proven state pilot nationally and take industry's money to pay for it.
- Unconventional: convert the levy into a public equity stake in the labs, paying the dividend from shares, so revenue follows profits rather than wages.

## 3. Decisions this period
1. **Transition Act II (bill 1).** Take the state pilot national: retraining and wage insurance 0.3% → 0.5% of GDP from 2034, with the youth hiring credit and statutory attrition notice folded in. Paid by the industry fund, added on top, not substituted. Labour and progressives win; Households' votes are pledged.
2. **Levy Stability and Audit Act (bill 2).** Freeze the levy at 7 points through 2034 and grant safe harbour for audited systems only, in exchange for $12bn a year from 2033 and the pledge signed by June. No caps, no antitrust. Fiscal moderates win. We decline the $400m tied to scheduling the vote.
3. **Dividend top-up, no new tax.** Route levy receipts growth to lift the lower-income dividend from 0.3% to about 0.4% of GDP, cheques landing autumn 2034; the remainder to the deficit. Housing grants continue by executive action.

## 4. Public statement
"What worked in one state will now work in fifty: lose a job to a machine, and you get your pay bridged and a path back. Industry is paying its share."

**To AI firms:** We take $12bn a year from 2033 for a freeze at 7 points through 2034, plus audited safe harbour. Keep the $400m; it is not part of any deal.
**To Households:** Agreed. The fund adds to wage insurance; nothing comes out of the Transition Act.

## 5. What would change my mind
Unemployment above 5.5%, falling wage-tax receipts, or a renewed bond scare would push us to a higher levy and a broader dividend.
