# Orders — Households — Turn 06 (2033-34)

**1. Read of the situation.** Pay is up 3.2% a year, rents are falling 2% a year, the week is shorter and the dividend cheque is real money for lower-income homes; wage insurance survived. But labour share is 49.4%, a record low, and office openings are still scarce, so graduates stay stuck. The fifth wave stalled at mid-size employers, and the youth-hiring and notice bill was never tabled.

**2. Options considered.**
- Keep organising at the same pace, no strikes.
- Take Employers' entry-level pledge deal and use it as a bargaining template.
- Unconventional: pool dividend cheques and union dues into local worker co-ops and apprenticeship cooperatives in care and trades, built with Entrepreneurs.

**3. Decisions this period.**
1. **Sixth organising wave, retargeted** at mid-size employers where wave five stalled, with an offer instead of a threat: attrition savings go to entry-level hiring, as in Employers' pledge. Fund it with dues, and pick sites in care, retail and logistics. No strikes; slow-downs only if an employer breaks an existing clause. This keeps what we have while jobs are plentiful enough for a roll to work.
2. **Accept Employers' entry-level pledge,** on our terms: written into contracts, published intake numbers, and no boycott or no-rules terms. Yes to hiring, no to giving up our right to push back on automation.
3. **Apprenticeship and mobility pilot with Entrepreneurs** in care and trades, using Transition Act apprenticeship money. Members and their kids get first access, with a small slice of the dividend going to moving costs where rents are cheap. Saving rate stays flat; we feel secure enough not to cut spending and secure enough not to splurge.

**4. Public statement.** "Pay is up and rents are down, and we want young people to share in it. Hire the next generation with the savings, or we'll write it into the contracts. We'll keep the Transition Act and the dividend, and we'll vote on it."

**Messages.**
- To Employers: Yes to the entry-level pledge if intake is published and tied to attrition savings. We still reject no-boycott and no-rules terms. Let's sign a template for mid-size firms.
- To Entrepreneurs: Yes to talks. We want paid apprenticeships in care and trades, with local hiring, and a say in how supervision roles are set up.

**5. What would change my mind.** Layoffs hitting people we know, or a cut to wage insurance or the dividend, would push me to save more, and to boycott or strike.
