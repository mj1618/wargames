# Sitrep — 2033–34

**Jobs.** Over two years 7.4m jobs were destroyed and 6.9m created — the first clear shortfall since 2029. Population growth still lifted employment from 164.9m to 165.8m. Unemployment 4.25%; the share of adults in work slipped to 58.4% (2026: 59.1%). Offices lost 2.2m jobs and factories 0.3m; new-economy firms added 2.0m, care 1.0m, construction 0.5m. Machines now do 36% of desk work (64% possible) and, with robots scaling, 12% of physical work (34% possible).

**Pay and prices.** Real hourly pay rose 7.8% over the two years; the week shortened to 32.8 hours. Shop prices dipped; housing costs fell 4.6%. Median real household income 120.2; bottom fifth 122.4.

**Business.** GDP grew 4.0% a year. Labour's share fell to 47.7%; the top 1% took 21.9%.

**Government.** Deficit 5.2% of GDP, debt 104.0%. Poverty 8.6%; homelessness 14.1 per 10,000. Unrest 31.1.

**What mattered most.**
1. Robots arrived at scale: physical automation doubled, and job losses spread beyond offices to retail, factories and logistics.
2. The safe-harbour bargain missed again — Government accepted $12bn just as the labs took their money to the states. Transition Act II passed at half size; the dividend top-up passed; the sixth organising wave succeeded.

Worker-first kept Congress in November 2034.

**What a household would have noticed.** Bigger pay packets for a shorter week, cheaper rent, a slightly larger dividend cheque — and robots in the warehouse and the stockroom.
