# Orders — AI firms — 2035–36

## Read of the situation
Demand is not the constraint: GDP grows 4.0% a year, labour's share is 47.7%, and households now buy tutoring, legal help and home design in bulk. Federal politics is settled against us — worker-first leads, levy 7 points, safe harbour never tabled, the $12bn offer expired with 2034 — so liability protection lives in the states, where our $2bn match pays out. Certified robots sell at scale; Employers co-sign by June if we hold commodity pricing and audit indemnity.

## Options considered
- Re-open the federal deal cheaper for a freeze through 2038.
- Pull the state match now that due-care rules are in force.
- Unconventional: a household bundle at cost; cheap service in living rooms softens "slow it down".

## Decisions this period
1. **Deploy pace:** race +20% on models and robots (certification done; the moat is ours). Compute sets the ceiling.
2. **Pricing:** commodity at open-weight parity through 2036. Employers: one-year terms, audit-backed indemnity, 15% off for co-signers, renewals capped at inflation. New consumer tier: tutoring, legal and home-design bundles at $40 a month with human review, three-year price lock.
3. **Politics and funds:** no federal money for bills that never reach the floor. $300m lobbying to remaining large states for due-care presumptions; $2bn/yr match to every state adopting by end-2036. Government's $12bn declined; if a freeze at 7 points through 2038 is tabled, $3bn/yr from signature, no escrow.

## Public statement
"Households now get professional services at a fraction of the old price. We hold commodity prices through 2036 and extend our worker fund to every state passing due-care rules."

**To Government:** The $12bn was for 2033–34, now gone. Table a freeze at 7 points through 2038 and we pay $3bn a year from signature. Nothing for a bill in committee.


**To Employers:** Done — one-year terms, audit-backed indemnity, commodity held through 2036, 15% off if you co-sign by June.

## What would change my mind
A levy-hike, cap or moratorium bill polling a majority before November 2036 — then $3bn becomes $8bn for a floor vote.
