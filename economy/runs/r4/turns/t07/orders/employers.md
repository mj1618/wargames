# Orders — Employers — Turn 07 (2035-36)

**1. Read of the situation.** Margins are still the prize, but labour share is 47.7% and the 7-point levy is now baked in, with worker-first firmer after 2034 and no freeze or credit in sight. We cut about 7.4m posts over two years (3.0m by announced layoff) and unemployment is 4.4%, so the quiet-attrition route is wearing thin politically, and a sixth organising wave reached mid-size peers. Household buying power is rising (median income 120.7, prices slightly lower), so demand is not yet our constraint, while cheap AI services are starting to take customers from incumbents in tutoring, legal and design.

**2. Options considered.**
- Mass restructuring now to bank savings before rules harden.
- Hold the course: company-wide drive, attrition-led, sign the AI-firm and Households templates.
- Unconventional: convert part of the saving into a published "intake-for-attrition" scheme and a customer-facing AI service line of our own, to beat start-ups at their game.

**3. Decisions this period.**
1. **Keep the company-wide drive, no mass restructuring.** Software stacks everywhere certified; certified robots scaled in logistics, warehousing, stockrooms plus pilots in retail back-of-house. Layoff_share held about 45%; hours_share stays 30%. A scale-up spike would hand worker-first a rules and levy-hike case.
2. **Sign both templates.** Join the AI firms' co-signer terms (15% off, indemnity, 3-year) and the Households entry-level pledge with published intake tied to attrition savings (about 0.5% of headcount in graduate intake); we accept the audit cost. We will not concede no-boycott or no-rules terms.
3. **Defend margin with our own cheap-service lines.** Launch human-in-the-loop AI advice and tutoring products under our brands, pass about a third of savings in price where start-ups undercut us, and keep profit-sharing near 15% of gains.

**4. Public statement.** "We are scaling certified automation and publishing our graduate intake, because a stable workforce and stable rules serve customers and shareholders alike." 

Messages:
- To AI firms: Co-signing on your terms. Add a most-favoured price clause for mid-size peers, and we back state due-care presumptions only if they preempt conflicting local rules.
- To Households: Yes to the template, with published intake. Dividend and levy talk stays out of it; please keep boycotts off signatories.

**5. What would change my mind.** Unemployment above 5%, a levy hike beyond 7 points, or a rival gaining share through deep price cuts would push me to either mass restructuring or deeper profit-sharing.
