# Orders — Entrepreneurs — Turn 07 (January 2035–December 2036)

## Read of the situation

We've added 2.0m workers to new-economy firms in two years (8.2m to 10.2m), vastly exceeding our 0.3m target. Cheap services with human-in-the-loop (tutoring, legal, design) are catching on; supervision, care and trades posts filled fast. Factory housing is maxed out in friendly states—permits have run out and money alone won't unlock more. Credit remains easy. Households want talks on paid apprenticeships and local hiring (favorable signal for labour peace and structural advantage).

## Options considered

1. **Push harder:** continue the hiring wave, risk saturating labour markets and pricing ourselves out of low-margin services. 
2. **Consolidate carefully:** slow expansion to ~0.2m hires; lock in apprenticeship model; shift housing strategy to less-permit-dependent niches (retrofits, renovation, modular small-scale). 
3. **Pivot to AI-only again:** test whether margins have recovered enough with input costs down; requires accepting lower employment upside but higher per-founder returns.

## Decisions this period

1. **Moderate hiring and shift mix** — add 0.2m workers, rebalancing from supervision-heavy to apprenticeship-rooted roles in care, trades and tutoring. This locks in customer willingness to pay for structured human oversight, harder for incumbents to replicate, and eases pressure on wage bids. Target: 60% people-heavy (apprentices + supervision), 40% AI-only high-margin founders.

2. **Housing pivot** — shift $50B investment from greenfield factory housing (zoning-constrained) to retrofits, modular renovation and small-scale infill in labour-shortage regions and secondary metros where permits flow. Partner with local development authorities. Lower permit risk, addresses actual housing shortage in non-coastal areas, serves labour-attraction story.

3. **Political push for licensing and antitrust** — broker talks with Households on apprenticeship standards (credentialing, pay floors, local hiring targets). Push Congress for streamlined licensing across states; emphasize that independent access to AI models—not lab-bundled tiers—is non-negotiable for startup competitiveness. Call for FTC to pursue dominant labs' pricing and bundling practices.

## Public statement

"We're betting on the pairing of AI and human judgment in services—tutoring, legal, care, trades. Apprenticeships are the bridge: they train people, lock in quality, and build communities. We're talking with Households and Congress on how to move fast on licensing and how to keep AI inputs cheap and open."

**Message to Households:**
To: Households | "We're ready to scale apprenticeships on your terms: paid, locally hired, and with worker input on supervision roles. Let's lock this in."

**Message to Congress:**
To: Policy makers | "Antitrust scrutiny of lab pricing is in your interest and ours. Independent AI access, not bundled indemnities, is the moat for startups. Streamline trades licensing nationwide and we'll hire the people you're worried about."

## What would change my mind

If the apprenticeship model with Households falls apart or if real wage growth turns negative, we revert to AI-only hiring and accept smaller headcount for higher per-founder returns.
