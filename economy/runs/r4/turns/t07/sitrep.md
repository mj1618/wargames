# Sitrep — 2035–36

**Jobs.** Over two years 8.6m jobs were destroyed and 10.3m created — the best balance of the decade. Employment rose from 165.8m to 168.7m. Unemployment 3.9%; the share of adults in work recovered to 59.0% (2026: 59.1%). Offices lost 2.0m jobs; new-economy firms added 2.6m, care 1.4m, construction 0.8m, retail 0.5m. Machines now do 48% of desk work (78% possible) and 20% of physical work (48% possible). Since 2026: 29.5m jobs destroyed, 29.6m created.

**Pay and prices.** Real hourly pay rose 9.9% over two years; the week shortened to 32.1 hours. Housing costs fell 4.1%. Median real household income 130.4; bottom fifth 136.5.

**Business.** GDP grew 5.1% a year. Labour's share fell to 46.5%; the top 1% took 22.2%.

**Government.** Deficit 4.5% of GDP, debt 99.3%. Poverty 6.9%; homelessness 12.0 per 10,000. Unrest 27.1.

**What mattered most.**
1. Cheap AI services with people in the loop caught on: new kinds of work created 8.1m jobs and extra household spending 2.0m more.
2. Congress raised the profits levy to 10 points and the lower-income dividend to 0.7% of GDP; a court upheld it. The housing-and-credentials bill passed; a seventh organising wave succeeded.

Voters went to the polls in November 2036 with these numbers.

**What a household would have noticed.** A bigger pay packet for a 32-hour week, cheaper rent, a larger dividend cheque, a $40 tutor-and-lawyer subscription — and fewer office jobs to apply for.
