# Journal — ai-firms

## Round 01 (2027)
Belief: the leap is ours and the window is this year; Washington is friendly but public mood (two-thirds want a slowdown) is the real threat. Committed: race at +20%, premium bundling, $250m political spend, $2bn/yr transition fund offered only for federal pre-emption and no licensing. Watching: whether government takes the deal, whether any moratorium bill gets coalition votes, and how fast pilots convert to contracts.

## Round 02 (2028)
Belief: adoption, not capability, is the bottleneck; household services are the growth path and the organising drive is the main threat to it. Politics stays cheap until a bill has votes. Committed: race +20%, enterprise premium / consumer price cuts, $300m political spend, $500m/yr reskilling fund paid now, voluntary disclosure, 90-day notice template for top customers; $2bn Compact still reserved for pre-emption. Watching: November result, licensing bills, consumer uptake, rival pricing.

## Round 03 (2029)
Belief: 3.4% unemployment kills the displacement story for now; adoption, not capability or politics, limits revenue. Government wants us to move first on the $2bn — we won't be burned twice. Committed: race +20% aimed at deployment, two-year bundles with capped repricing and a usage tier, consumer prices −20%, $2bn into an escrow trust released on enactment, $350m political spend. Watching: whether government accepts escrow, Q2 bundle signings, any licensing bill with coalition votes.

## Round 04 (2030)
Belief: the share-price fall makes capital the binding constraint; one-sixth utilisation means adoption, not capability, pays. The escrow-for-pre-emption trade is proven and worth repeating. Committed: steady pace, capex growth frozen, enterprise bundles held, −25% usage tier for SMB/consumers, $1bn trust payment, $2bn escrow offered for pre-emption through 2034 plus consent decree, $300m political spend. Watching: whether any rival races through the squeeze, antitrust remedy terms, SMB uptake.

## Round 05 (2031–32)
Belief: the recession, not AI, now carries the political risk; capital is binding and one-sixth utilisation means lock-in and volume beat margin. Pre-emption to 2032 for $1bn/yr is cheap cover. Committed: steady pace, capex flat, robotics pivot into factory-built housing, two-year enterprise terms at −15% for 40% committed volume, $1bn released, fund doubled, $2bn escrow held for 2034 pre-emption plus consent decree, $300m political spend. Watching: rival pacing, antitrust remedies, November 2032 result, modular-home orders.

## Round 06 (2033–34)
Belief: the recovery buries displacement but a worker-first Congress plus the bond scare means a levy is coming regardless; the only thing worth buying is one national rule in place of fifty state licences. Committed: 4-point levy on all AI sold in the US, antitrust decree with open access and SMB tier, $2bn on enactment, fund $1bn/yr, for pre-emption to 2036; three-year enterprise terms; capex +10%; $400m political spend. Watching: whether Congress attaches caps, state bills, Vanta breaking ranks.

## Round 07 (2035–36)
Belief: the levy is sunk and displacement is dead politically; the only live threats are a trial and fifty state licences, so the decree is now cheaper than fighting it. Lock-in is finished anyway; volume at one-quarter utilisation pays. Committed: decree accepted for a federal licensing standard to 2038, $2bn on enactment, employer terms signed at −15% with 10% floor, steady models, +15% robots, $450m political spend. Watching: whether the standard is tabled, state moratorium bills, Vanta.
