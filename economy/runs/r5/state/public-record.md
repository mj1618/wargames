# World state — January 2027

A fictional economic policy exercise set in the United States. AI labs are the fictional composites from `world/cast.md` (Meridian, Helix, Lumen, Vanta). No real individuals appear. Everything below is public knowledge in the game world. The hidden conditions dealt to each play-through are **not** here.

## The technology

AI systems now do multi-hour professional tasks on their own and write most of the code at the labs that build them. Capability is improving quickly and nobody serious expects that to stop. About one firm in five uses AI in production (more than a third of large firms); AI assists roughly 6% of all work hours and saves perhaps 1.5% of them. Most business use hands whole tasks to the machine instead of assisting a person.

Robots are behind. Humanoids are cheap (from about $16,000) and shipping in the tens of thousands, mostly to labs and pilots; useful commercial humanoid work so far adds up to a few dozen worker-years. The exceptions are structured settings: one large online retailer runs more than a million warehouse robots and plans to automate most of its operations, and driverless taxis give about a million paid rides a week in a dozen cities — still under 1% of trips. Construction, care and skilled trades are barely touched.

## What people argue about

- **Is displacement real yet?** Total employment looks normal. But hiring of 22–25-year-olds in the most AI-exposed occupations is down about 19% relative to others, recent graduates have higher unemployment than the average worker, and "AI" became the most-cited reason for announced layoffs in 2026. Sceptics answer that the hiring slump pre-dates the chatbots and that some firms blame AI for cuts they would have made anyway.
- **Where do the savings go?** Token prices keep falling and open-weight models compete hard; margins are also at a record and a handful of firms dominate.
- **Housing.** Rents, not poverty rates, explain where homelessness is high. Building has been getting less productive for fifty years, and land and zoning dominate costs in the expensive metros. Factory-built and robot-assisted housing exists but is small.
- **Public money.** About 70% of federal revenue rests on wages. Social Security and Medicare are funded by payroll taxes. States must balance budgets.
- **Public mood.** Two-thirds of Americans want AI development slowed. Data-centre power demand is a local fight almost everywhere.

## Politics

The November 2026 midterms have just been held; the new Congress sits this month (its make-up is given in each player's first intel note). Federal elections follow in November 2028 (President and Congress), 2030, 2032, 2034 and 2036. No federal law yet addresses AI and jobs. Existing support is unemployment insurance (about 40% of pay for 26 weeks, patchy coverage), small retraining programmes, and the ordinary safety net.

## What each player sees

Official statistics arrive late and with noise. Company and household players see their own corner more sharply than the national picture. Nobody sees the hidden conditions; they have to infer them from what happens.

## This play-through — January 2027 (public)

- **Who leads in Washington.** After the November 2026 midterms a growth-first coalition leads the federal government: pro-deployment, wary of new taxes and sceptical of new entitlements. A worker-first coalition is the opposition.
- **States and housing.** State housing politics are mixed: a few states are loosening zoning, several big ones are resisting, most are doing little.
- **Headline, January 2027 — Generation leap.** A new model generation does week-long professional projects unaided.

# Record of events

(Each round's public summary is appended below.)

## 2027

Official figures for end 2027 (preliminary): unemployment 4.0%, participation 61.6%, employment 162.8m (59.0% of adults). GDP grew 1.6%. Median real household income index about 100.2. Consumer prices flat in real terms; housing costs up slightly. Labour's share of income 52.6%, a new record low. Federal deficit 6.0% of GDP, debt about 103%. Poverty and homelessness figures for 2027 are not yet published.

Announced layoffs blamed on AI were about 170,000; most large employers said they were cutting through hiring freezes. AI firms released the new generation on a faster schedule and sold multi-year enterprise bundles; list prices held. Congress rejected the Workforce Transition Act and passed a half-size housing-incentives bill, starting 2028. A federal court blocked the executive order requiring firms to report AI-related layoffs. A union organising drive in offices, call centres and warehouses won recognition at a string of sites.

## 2028

Official figures for end 2028 (preliminary): unemployment 3.8%, participation 61.6%, employment 163.7m (59.1% of adults). GDP grew 2.0%. Median real household income index about 101.2. Consumer prices flat; housing costs steady. Labour's share of income 52.6%. Federal deficit 6.0% of GDP, debt about 105%. Now published for 2027: poverty 12.8%, homelessness 22 per 10,000.

Announced layoffs blamed on AI were about 260,000. Households bought AI-delivered tutoring, legal help and design in bulk, and new firms hired to supply them. AI firms cut consumer prices, kept enterprise bundles, began voluntary workforce disclosure and a $500m-a-year training fund. Large employers agreed 60 days' notice and profit-sharing; unions won first contracts. Congress passed the First Job and Wage Insurance Act (from 2029); the AI Deployment Framework Act never reached a vote. In November the growth-first coalition kept its lead; the new Congress looks readier to pass laws.

## 2029

Official figures for end 2029 (preliminary): unemployment 4.1% (2028 was revised to 3.4%), participation 61.5%, employment 164.0m (59.0% of adults). GDP grew 1.7%. Median real household income index about 103.0. Consumer prices flat; housing costs steady. Labour's share of income 52.3%, a new record low. Federal deficit 6.1% of GDP, debt about 107%. Now published for 2028: poverty 12.6%, homelessness 21 per 10,000.

Announced layoffs blamed on AI were about 450,000. AI firms placed $2bn in a Workforce Compact trust; Congress then passed the AI Deployment Framework Act (state licensing pre-empted through 2030, workforce disclosure mandatory) and the Housing Supply Act (funded building with zoning conditions, from 2030). Washington opened two antitrust cases on enterprise AI bundling; a court let them proceed. Large employers put attrition-first in writing and began a shorter-week pilot. A second union organising drive fell short.

## 2030

Official figures for end 2030 (preliminary): unemployment 5.5%, participation 60.7%, employment 160.4m (57.5% of adults). GDP shrank 0.6%. Median real household income index about 101.2. Consumer prices flat; housing costs down about 2%. Labour's share of income 50.5%, a new record low. Federal deficit 6.8% of GDP, debt about 112%. Now published for 2029: poverty 12.5%, homelessness 21 per 10,000.

AI shares fell by a third and credit tightened; announced layoffs reached about 1.9m, most blamed on weak orders, not AI. Many large employers laid staff off despite written attrition-first pledges. AI firms slowed their build-out and opened a cheaper usage tier. Congress passed the Main Street Credit and Work-Sharing Act (from 2031) and extended pre-emption of state AI licensing to 2032, tied to a larger training fund. A union renewal drive fell short. In November the growth-first coalition narrowly kept its lead; the new Congress is more divided.

## 2031–32

Official figures for end 2032 (preliminary): unemployment 4.1%, participation 61.4%, employment 165.7m (58.9% of adults). GDP grew about 3.3% a year. Median real household income index about 108.7. Consumer prices flat; housing costs down about 4%. Labour's share of income 51.7%. Federal deficit 6.2% of GDP, debt about 113%. Now published for 2030: poverty 13.1%, homelessness 19 per 10,000.

Announced layoffs were about 700,000 over two years. Credit eased and hiring recovered. Large employers used the work-sharing subsidy for shorter weeks; AI firms held their pace, doubled the training fund and began supplying robots to home factories. Congress rejected the Paycheck Bridge Act and passed a half-size Factory Homes Act. Pre-emption of state AI licensing expired at the end of 2032; the antitrust cases continue. Local union drives fell short. In November the worker-first coalition took the lead; the new Congress looks readier to pass laws.

## 2033–34

Official figures for end 2034 (preliminary): unemployment 3.8%, participation 61.5%, employment 167.4m (59.0% of adults). GDP grew about 2.4% a year. Median real household income index about 111.9. Consumer prices flat; housing costs down about 2%. Labour's share of income 51.3%. Federal deficit 6.1% of GDP, debt about 116%. Now published for 2032: poverty 11.4%, homelessness 17 per 10,000.

Announced layoffs were about 950,000 over two years. The bond-market scare passed without a credit squeeze. Congress passed the Fair Share and Stability Act at half size (a 3-point levy on profits from 2034, cash for the bottom two-fifths, more retraining) and renewed Main Street and work-sharing money for 2034–35; a court let the levy stand. Pre-emption was not renewed and the antitrust cases continue; several states advanced AI licensing bills. Local union drives fell short. In November worker-first kept its lead.
