# Orders — Employers — round 01 (2027)

## 1. Read of the situation
Profits are a record 14.9% of GDP and labour share a record-low 52.8%, so investors expect the margin to hold. A growth-first Washington means no near-term deployment rules or new taxes, which makes this the cheapest window to move. Vendors now offer the new generation, but our own projects are behind schedule; our one price-cut pilot (10% cut, +6% volume) shows passing savings on loses money.

## 2. Options considered
- Wait for the new generation to mature; keep pilots small and cautious.
- Company-wide drive now on the new generation, shedding mostly by attrition.
- Unconventional: sign a joint no-layoff pledge in exchange for a promise of no deployment rules, using hiring freezes as the real lever.

## 3. Decisions this period
1. **Adoption speed: company-wide drive (about 15% faster than normal).** Scale the pilots that have worked onto the new generation; pay for it from a capex budget of about 1.5% of revenue. A rival who automates first undercuts us, and the rules window is open. Not mass restructuring: projects slip and often disappoint, so we hold back from the 60% pace.
2. **Shedding: shift layoff_share down from about 60% to about 40%.** Run it mainly through hiring freezes and not replacing leavers, concentrated in back-office and entry-level roles. It is quiet, avoids the headline risk that invites regulation, and the cost falls on people who were never hired. Targeted layoffs stay for units where a pilot has actually delivered.
3. **Prices and pay: keep savings, no price cuts.** Our 10% cut gave only +6% volume, so it does not pay. Offer a modest profit-sharing pool (about 1% of payroll, tied to margins) to blunt unrest and keep key staff. Hours_share stays near 10% for now.

## 4. Public statement and messages
**Public:** "We are investing in AI to make our people more productive, and we will manage workforce change responsibly, mainly through attrition and retraining."

**Messages:**
- To: Government/Congress — "We back a light-touch, pro-deployment framework. We will consider voluntary retraining and attrition-first commitments in return for no mandated deployment rules or new payroll surcharges."
- To: Labour/Workers — "Open to talks on hours reductions with unchanged weekly pay in pilot units, if you agree to flexibility on task redesign."

## 5. What would change my mind
Rivals breaking from the herd with deep layoffs and visible margin gains, or a credible threat of automation taxes or deployment limits, would push me to move faster or to buy peace with larger pledges.
