# Orders — Entrepreneurs — round 01

## Read of the situation

The new model generation does week-long projects unaided, AI tools are dropping in cost, and credit flows freely. Hiring is easy at current pay, but corporate profits are at a record 14.9% of GDP while labour's share hit an all-time low of 52.8%—this is our moment. Some new ventures found customers quickly this quarter; we don't yet know if this is broad demand shift or luck, but the conditions to find out cheaply are here.

## Options considered

- **Conservative:** match historical patterns (new-job creation up 5%, mostly people-heavy services). We'd leave returns on the table and let bigger firms grab the AI-only upside.
- **Aggressive expansion into AI-only firms:** capitalize on the cost drop and margin advantage, push returns up, accept that this means fewer jobs. Hire light, let AI agents scale the work.
- **Sector-specific bet:** pour capital into one sector we see traction in (e.g., housing if zoning loosens, or new digital services) before incumbents notice.

## Decisions this period

1. **New business wave: shift the mix.** Launch 15% more new ventures than historical normal—we have credit and cheap tools. But shift the composition: move from 40%–60% (AI-only–people-heavy) to 60%–40%. Spend the returns on founders who build fleets of AI agents rather than small teams. This is bigger total headcount effect than pure AI-only, but better margins than people-heavy.

2. **Sector push into services and light manufacturing.** The signs say people-heavy services found customers this quarter. Direct 60% of capital expansion into: remote support services, specialized manufacturing with AI co-workers, and light trade services (HVAC tech with AI diagnostics, electrician networks powered by AI). These hire people and undercut incumbents on cost.

3. **Political agenda: push hard for cheap AI access.** Lobby Congress and states for: tariff exemptions on AI chips, no license caps on AI model weights, and zoning relief for small firms in high-rent areas. This keeps our cost advantage and lets us scale into housing. We're stronger politically than wage-workers but weaker than the big platforms—partner with growth-first coalition.

## Public statement

"The generation leap in AI is a net job creator, not killer. Entrepreneurs are hiring fast into new roles nobody had before—AI agents need human supervisors, and new customer demand needs new firms. We're betting heavily on building those firms now."

## Messages to other players

**To: Labour** — "We need to hire people to build and run these new firms. Cheap AI means one founder can create roles that didn't exist a year ago. Retrain for the supervisor and scaling jobs, not the ones that never were."

**To: Incumbents** — "The margins are brutal if you don't move to this cost curve. We are undercutting you on AI labour and it won't stop."

## What would change my mind

If customer demand collapses (job postings dry up, credit seizes) or if AI model pricing stops falling, we switch back to people-heavy and pull back hard.

## Journal entry

We're in the window. Credit is loose, AI tools are cheap, and hiring is easy. We're building 15% more new ventures but shifting heavily toward AI-only and AI-hybrid models—the margins on AI agents are too good to ignore when labour's share is at a record low. We're pushing capital into services and light manufacturing where we see customer traction. Watching: (1) whether this is a real demand shift or a dead bounce, (2) whether bigger firms wake up and copy this mix, (3) whether Congress acts on zoning and AI access. If any of these break wrong, we have to pivot to lower capex and pull back headcount targets.
