# Orders — Federal government — round 01 (2027)

## 1. Read of the situation
Unemployment is 4.1% and receipts are on forecast, so there is no visible jobs crisis to legislate against, but two-thirds of voters want AI slowed and the "week-long projects unaided" headline lands on our watch. The deficit is already 6.0% of GDP with debt near 100%, and about 70% of revenue rests on wages, so we have no room for a big entitlement and a quiet exposure if office jobs (48.0m) start to go. We lead narrowly: only small measures pass, and donors want no limits and no taxes.

## 2. Options considered
- Do nothing and run on growth and AI leadership into 2028.
- Cheap, visible "we are on it" measures: disclosure, retraining, wage insurance, housing incentives.
- Unconventional: a pre-legislated automatic trigger — wage insurance and work-sharing that switch on only if unemployment passes a threshold, costing nothing until then.

## 3. Decisions this period
1. **Bill 1 — Workforce Transition Act (trigger-based).** `retraining_pct_gdp` = 0.2% from 2028 (wage insurance for displaced workers over 25, plus graduate placement grants), with a work-sharing subsidy that activates automatically if unemployment exceeds 5.5%. Small enough for deficit hawks, offered to the opposition as a bipartisan vote. Faction winner: deficit hawks (size) and security hawks (framed as competitiveness); deregulators tolerate it.
2. **Executive action — `deploy_regulation` = disclosure.** Large employers report AI-attributed layoffs and hiring freezes quarterly to the Labor Department. No limits, no licensing. Answers the polls, gives us faster data than the official statistics, and costs donors little. Faction winner: deregulators (nothing beyond disclosure).
3. **Bill 2 — `housing_policy` = incentives.** About 0.1% of GDP in grants to states and metros that loosen zoning and permit factory-built housing. Rents are what voters feel before 2028. Faction winner: deregulators.

No new capital tax, no cash transfers, no public jobs this year.

## 4. Public statement
"America will lead in AI and American workers will share in it: we are backing workers who change jobs, building homes, and requiring companies to be straight about what AI is doing to hiring."

**Messages**
- To AI labs: No taxes or deployment limits this year. In return we expect clean disclosure data and visible hiring and training commitments before 2028 — give us something to point to.
- To states: Federal housing money goes first to states that loosen zoning. Tell us what you would permit.

## 5. What would change my mind
Unemployment above 5%, graduate unemployment visibly spiking, or receipts falling short of forecast would move us to larger wage insurance and force the capital-tax conversation.
