# Sitrep — round 01 (January–December 2027)

**A loud year in the headlines, a quiet one in the numbers.**

- **Jobs.** Machines took over about 0.42m jobs' worth of work, 0.25m of it in offices; about 0.21m new jobs appeared, mostly in new kinds of work. Population growth added 0.65m, so employment rose to 162.8m. Unemployment 4.14% (was 4.1%); the share of adults working slipped from 59.1% to 59.0%.
- **Pay.** Real hourly pay up 0.9%. Median real household income 100.7 (2026 = 100); bottom fifth 100.3.
- **Prices.** Flat. Firms kept their savings; housing costs up 0.3%.
- **Businesses.** What AI could do in desk work jumped from 6% to about 16% of the work; what firms actually handed over rose only from 1.5% to 2.2%. Labour's share fell to 52.6%; top-1% share 20.1%. GDP grew 1.6%.
- **Government.** Deficit 6.0% of GDP, debt 102.6%. Poverty 12.8%, homelessness unchanged at 22 per 10,000.

**What mattered most**
1. The dice went against Washington: the Workforce Transition Act failed, a court blocked the layoff-disclosure rule, and the housing bill passed at half size (from 2028).
2. Employers chose hiring freezes over layoffs, so losses showed up as jobs never offered, not as people fired. The household organising drive succeeded.

**What a household would have noticed:** almost nothing in the pay packet or the shops — but the graduate in the family is still looking, and the union is suddenly signing people up at the office.
