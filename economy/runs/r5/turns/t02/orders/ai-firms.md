# Orders — AI firms — round 02 (2028)

## Read of the situation
Unemployment 4.0%, GDP +1.6%, AI-blamed layoffs only 170,000 — the macro picture is quiet, but labour share fell to 52.6% and two-thirds of voters still want a slowdown. Our generation shipped fast and bundles sold, yet customers use a fraction of capability; adoption is the bottleneck, so the household "cheap services" boom is our growth path. Washington declined pre-emption but wants disclosure and hiring commitments before November — a cheap ask; the organising drive at our biggest customers is the real threat.

## Options considered
- Race, price premium, ignore politics until a bill has votes.
- Consumer price war to own household services before open-weight rivals.
- Unconventional: back a modest profits levy ourselves to split the worker-first coalition and bury licensing.

## Decisions this period
1. **Race, +20%** [`deploy_pace`]. Compute, not demand, is the constraint; slowing cedes ground abroad. Pricing splits [`ai_pricing`]: premium bundles and lock-in for enterprise; aggressive cuts and open APIs for consumers and small business (labs split 3–1; majority carries).
2. **Buy the election-year talking point cheaply.** Political spend $300m (up from $250m) for the growth coalition. Voluntary quarterly disclosure of AI-attributed workforce changes. A $500m/yr reskilling fund, paid now, no strings. The $2bn Compact stays reserved for pre-emption.
3. **Blunt the organising drive.** Offer our ten largest customers a template clause — 90 days' notice plus retraining credits. Strikes stalling adoption cost more than notice.

## Public statement
"We are shipping faster, pricing lower for families and small firms, publishing our workforce data and funding $500m a year in training — growth and fairness are not opposites."

**Messages**
- To Federal government: "Disclosure and a $500m/yr training fund go live this quarter. Pre-empting state licensing still unlocks the $2bn Compact. Hold the line on taxes and limits through November."
- To Households: "We back 90-day notice and retraining credits at our largest customers now. In return, no support for moratorium or licensing bills."

## What would change my mind
A moratorium or licensing bill gaining coalition votes, or a rival's slowdown pledge winning public traction.
