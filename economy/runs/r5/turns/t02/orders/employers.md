# Orders — Employers — Round 02 (2028)

## 1. Read of the situation
Margins held through our 2027 drive (0.4m roles removed, 0.25m in offices, mostly via attrition) while unemployment stayed at 4.0% and labour share slipped to 52.6%; the half-size housing bill is the only legislation. The Transition Act died and the layoff-disclosure order was struck down, so the rules window is still open before the November 2028 elections. Unions are winning recognition at our sites and Entrepreneurs are undercutting us, so standing still is the losing move.

## 2. Options considered
- Hold at the current pace and wait out the election.
- Accelerate hard with a mass restructuring before any post-election rules arrive.
- Unconventional: trade a bargained notice-and-gain-sharing framework with the unions for a wider cross-industry no-strike, no-rules settlement, to pre-empt a worse election-year bill.

## 3. Decisions this period
1. **Adoption speed: company-wide drive, about 20% faster than normal (up from 15%).** Sign selective AI bundles of 2 years or less, not 3 years. A 3-year lock-in is risky if the next generation reprices the market, and early-signer priority capacity is only worth it where we are capacity-bound (offices, call centres). Why: Entrepreneurs' cost curve is a real threat, and our margin is the thing investors watch.
2. **Shedding: keep layoff share near 40%, with attrition and freezes doing the work.** No big visible layoff rounds in an election year. Hours near 10-12%, with no new top-ups. Why: quiet cuts avoid scandal and new rules, and the cost falls on people who are not yet hired.
3. **Labour and pay: accept a sector-level notice agreement (60 days' notice before AI-driven role eliminations, plus profit-sharing of about 1.5% of payroll).** Recognise unions where already won, and do not fight new drives openly. Why: this is cheap against a strike or a bill. The offer to Congress still stands: voluntary attrition-first and notice commitments in exchange for no deployment rules and no new payroll or automation tax.

## 4. Public statement and messages
**Statement:** "Large employers are investing in AI to stay competitive, are keeping layoffs a last resort, and will give workers notice and a share of the gains."

**Messages**
- To Households: "We will talk on 60 days' notice and about 1.5% of payroll in profit-sharing. A hard line on hours or job guarantees means we walk away."
- To AI firms: "We will sign 1-2 year bundles at current rates with a repricing clause. We will not sign 3-year lock-ins."

## 5. What would change my mind
Rival layoffs or a margin squeeze showing that our 20% pace is too slow, or a credible post-election bill with an automation tax, would push me to either accelerate or cut a bigger deal.
