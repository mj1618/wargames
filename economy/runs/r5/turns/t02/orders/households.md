# Orders — households — round 02 (2028)

## 1. Read of the situation
Official numbers look calm (unemployment 4.0%, prices flat), but labour share hit a new record low of 52.6% and profits keep winning the split, while the Transition Act died and a court killed layoff disclosure. Locally, pay is just ahead of prices and rents slightly ahead of pay; few of us were laid off, yet first jobs are scarcer and the shelter is as full as last year. Our drive won recognition at a string of sites, which is real leverage, but it fades if we do not keep at it, and November 2028 is our best chance to be heard.

## 2. Options considered
- Press the organising drive harder and bank the Employers' hours offer.
- Save hard (+2 points) out of fear; costs other people's jobs and we are not yet frightened enough.
- Unconventional: use the recognised sites to bargain a shared "AI dividend" clause (profit-share tied to productivity gains) and have union members run a voter-registration push tied to it.

## 3. Decisions this period
1. **Consolidate and extend the organising drive** (`union_pressure` up one notch): move from winning recognition to first contracts at the recognised offices, call centres and warehouses. Priorities: no-layoff-without-notice, retraining on paid time, profit/productivity sharing. Needs a roll; most of the effort is keeping recognised sites from lapsing.
2. **Take the Employers' hours offer, with conditions.** Agree to pilot-unit shorter weeks at unchanged pay and some task-redesign flexibility, but only with written no-layoff terms, worker say over how tasks are redesigned, and a sunset review at 12 months. Pilot units only, no blanket concession.
3. **Hold the saving rate steady** (no change). Jobs are mostly intact, so we keep a small cushion and keep spending. Politically organised: demand notice rules, longer jobless pay and zoning reform; peaceful, concentrated on the November vote. We back whoever commits in writing to those, and no one gets a free pass.

## 4. Public statement and messages
**Statement:** "Pay is barely ahead of rents while profits and AI gains pile up at the top. We will take shorter weeks and retraining, not just thanks, and we will vote on it in November."

**Messages**
- To Employers: "Yes to pilot hours reductions at full pay, if it comes with no-layoff and notice language and a seat for workers on task redesign. Put it in writing and we talk."
- To Entrepreneurs: "Hire locally and train people on paid time and we will listen. Telling us to retrain for jobs that do not yet exist, on our own rent, is not an offer."

## 5. What would change my mind
Visible layoffs among people we know, or a jump in rents or shelter numbers, would push us to save more and escalate to stronger action; real employer follow-through would let us ease off.
