# Sitrep — round 02 (January–December 2028)

**More jobs made than lost, and an election that changed little.**

- **Jobs.** Machines took over about 0.64m jobs' worth of work (0.39m in offices). About 0.87m jobs were created: 0.53m in new kinds of work, 0.32m from the burst of household spending on cheap AI-delivered services. With population growth, employment reached 163.7m. Unemployment 4.04% (was 4.14%); share of adults working back to 59.1%.
- **Pay.** Real hourly pay up 1.7%. Median real household income 102.2 (2026 = 100); bottom fifth 101.3.
- **Prices.** Flat; housing costs edged down to 99.9.
- **Businesses.** AI could do 22.8% of desk work; firms handed over 3.3%. Labour's share held at 52.6%. GDP grew 2.0%.
- **Government.** Deficit 6.0%, debt 104.6%. Poverty 12.6%; homelessness 21.1 per 10,000 (was 22.0).

**What mattered most**
1. The services boom (event) and entrepreneurs' swing back to firms that hire: creation outran destruction for the first time.
2. Washington and the labs deadlocked over who moves first on the $2bn, so the pre-emption-and-disclosure bill never got a vote. The smaller First Job and Wage Insurance Act passed (from 2029), and the organising drive won first contracts. In November the growth-first coalition kept its lead; the new Congress is more willing to legislate.

**What a household would have noticed:** a cheap tutor, a slightly fatter pay packet, a union contract at work — and the graduate finally got an offer, from a firm that did not exist two years ago.
