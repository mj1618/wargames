# Orders — Employers — round 03 (2029)

## 1. Read of the situation
Margins held and the 1.5% profit-share is being paid, so the 2027–28 playbook (faster drive, attrition, bundled contracts) is working: about 0.64m roles removed, 0.39m in offices, with no visible layoff scandal. Unemployment is 3.4% after revision, so Washington has no urgent reason to act and neither does our labour supply, but unions hold first contracts and new service firms are absorbing people we did not hire. A growth-first Congress that "looks readier to legislate" is the main risk to predictability; the Government has just offered no mandates in exchange for attrition-first in writing and no mass layoff announcements before November.

## 2. Options considered
- Stay the course: drive ~20% faster, attrition only, accept the Government's offer.
- Mass restructuring (up to 60% faster) now, before rules arrive; high earnings, high political risk.
- Unconventional: sign Households' pilot-hours deal on a narrow basis (a few sites, fixed term, our terms) to buy labour peace and seat at the table before the Framework Act returns.

## 3. Decisions this period
1. **Accept the Government deal.** Put attrition-first in writing and hold mass layoff announcements until after November 2029 (the election-year window, 2028 is passed but the pause is cheap since attrition does the work anyway). Layoff share stays ~35–40%; adoption speed ~20% faster, bundles of 2 years or less. Cost: near zero; benefit: no mandates or surcharges, predictability.
2. **Bounded hours pilot.** Run a 12-month pilot at about 15–20 sites (roughly 3% of headcount), hours cut 10–12% at full pay for the affected staff, tied to a no-layoff commitment and 60-day notice at those sites only. Offer a worker seat on task-redesign review panels, advisory, not a veto. Overall hours_share rises only to ~12%. Why: cheap relative to the savings, wins Households and unions, and makes the case that we police ourselves.
3. **Keep wage-sharing at ~1.5% of payroll**, no increase; keep prices unchanged (cuts do not pay).

## 4. Public statement and messages
**Statement:** "Large employers are committing to attrition-first workforce change in writing, and to a pilot of shorter weeks at full pay with worker input on how tasks are redesigned."
- **To Federal government:** Agreed in writing: attrition-first, no mass layoff announcements before November. We expect no mandates, surcharges or deployment rules in exchange, and consultation before any draft bill.
- **To Households:** Pilot shorter weeks at full pay at selected sites, with notice and no-layoff terms limited to those sites, and worker seats on redesign panels as advisory. Send the term sheet and we will sign inside 60 days.

## 5. What would change my mind
Margins slipping materially, a rival breaking attrition-first with big layoffs and winning share, or a bill with an automation surcharge getting traction.
