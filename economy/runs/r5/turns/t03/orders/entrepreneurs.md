# Orders — Entrepreneurs — turn 03 (2029)

## Read of the situation
End 2028 saw 0.5m new jobs from new firms and startups, double 2027, with hybrid people-in-the-loop services filling their order books. Unemployment fell to 3.4% (revised), but enterprise AI bundles still price for incumbents and Congress took no antitrust action despite growth-first control. Households are signalling that local hiring and paid training on our dime, not retraining on theirs, is the deal we need.

## Options considered
1. **Doubledown-with-training**: Scale people-in-the-loop to 70% of expansion, hire locally, fund paid on-the-job training—higher labour costs but satisfy households and lock in customer loyalty.
2. **Pull-back-pivot**: Slow hiring, shift hard toward AI-only firms with skeleton crews to maximize margins while incumbents still lack speed.
3. **Housing play**: Sustain services growth at 60% but bundle factory-built housing into regional expansions where zoning allows—new market, new customers, higher stakes.

## Decisions this period

**1. Scale people-in-the-loop to 70% of expansion capital.** Hire 0.3–0.4m net new workers in tutoring, care, trades, design, local services. Fund 4–8 week paid on-the-job training programs (costing ~15–20% of new-hire wage for first year). Rationale: Demand is real and growing; households are signalling the hiring model they'll back; lock in customers before incumbents copy; paid training beats retraining promises on wages. Cost impact: margins down ~5–10% vs. AI-only, offset by volume and customer retention.

**2. Double down in jurisdictions loosening zoning.** Sustain the factory-housing push in states moving; target mixed-use, mixed-income builds. New job creation secondary; revenue primary. Rationale: Capital unlocked, land costs falling, lock out incumbents who move slow.

**3. Push Congress harder on open-access AI models.** Coordinate with small-business groups; remind growth-first coalition that SMBs are the growth story. Antitrust is still off the table, but lower-cost consumer-grade models level the field. Rationale: Enterprise bundles won't budge without legislative pressure; my sector's growth depends on cheap tools.

## Public statement
"We're hiring locally, training people on paid time, and betting on the work that machines still can't do alone. Congress should remember that small and new firms are the job engine—give us access to the same AI tools the big firms hoard."

## Messages to other actors

**To Households:** "We hear you. This round we're committing to local hire-and-train programs. The tutors, care workers, designers, trades folk we're bringing on get paid while they learn. Expect ~0.35m net new jobs in our sector."

**To AI Providers:** "Open your consumer and SMB pricing. We're your growth story now, not the enterprise holdouts. You cut prices to consumers; we'll scale."

## What would change my mind
If enterprise bundles drop 40%+ below consumer prices or Congress passes open-access legislation, I pull back training and shift to AI-only growth; if customer demand crashes, I pull back everywhere.
