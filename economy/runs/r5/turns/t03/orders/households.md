# Orders — Households — Round 03 (2029)

## 1. Read of the situation
Things are better for the first time: pay is up clearly faster than prices, rents are flat, more people we know found work than lost it, and first jobs are a bit easier. The revised unemployment of 3.4% fits what we see, but labour share is still 52.6%, so the gains reach us only through contracts we fought for. The First Job and Wage Insurance Act starts this year, and the shelter has empty beds. We are not yet secure, though: most of us still have thin savings.

## 2. Options considered
- Press hard now for hours cuts and job guarantees while jobs are plentiful and organising works best.
- Consolidate: bank the contracts, extend them to more sites, and take the notice and profit-share offers.
- Unconventional: trade the AI firms' 90-day notice and retraining credits for our neutrality on licensing, but only if the credits are paid to workers and portable.

## 3. Decisions this period
1. **Organising, moderate to strong.** Extend first-contract drives to the next tier of offices, call centres and warehouses, with the new notice and profit-share terms as the template. Put paid organisers on about 20 more sites, starting with employers who already signed. The labour market is tight, so this is when it works best.
2. **Spend, don't hoard.** Saving rate down about 1 point. Pay is rising and rent is flat, so households can spend a little more. This also keeps jobs coming for the neighbours we want hired. Keep the rainy-day cushion.
3. **Counter-offer to Employers and AI firms (conditional, not a boycott).** Accept 60 days' notice and about 1.5% of payroll in profit-sharing as the floor, written into contracts. Drop the hours demand this year, but keep a worker say on any role cut. Ask the AI firms for 90 days' notice with retraining credits paid to the worker, portable, not the employer. We will not trade away support for moratorium or licensing bills. No boycotts while things improve.

## 4. Public statement and messages
**Public:** "The contracts worked: pay is up, rents are flat. We want the same notice and profit-share at every large employer, and we will keep asking until we get it."

**Messages:**
- **To Employers:** Yes to 60 days' notice and 1.5% profit-sharing as the floor, written into the contract. We will not walk away over hours this year, but we want a worker say before any role is cut.
- **To AI firms:** We welcome 90-day notice and retraining credits, but the credits must go to workers and travel with them. Our position on moratorium and licensing bills stays open.

## 5. What would change my mind
If layoffs among people I know rise, rents jump, or employers break the written notice terms, I go back to disruptive protest and boycotts and raise the saving rate.
