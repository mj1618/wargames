# Sitrep — round 03 (January–December 2029)

**Automation speeds up, the services boom fades, and Washington finally legislates.**

- **Jobs.** About 1.13m jobs were lost (0.54m in offices, 0.22m in retail): 0.78m to machines and 0.35m as 2028's spending burst wore off. About 0.71m were created, 0.68m of them in new kinds of work. Population growth still lifted employment to 163.95m. Unemployment 4.12% (was 4.04%); share of adults working 58.96% (was 59.1%).
- **Pay.** Real hourly pay up 1.1%. Median real household income 103.0 (2026 = 100); bottom fifth 101.7.
- **Prices.** Flat; housing costs 99.8.
- **Businesses.** AI could do 29.2% of desk work; firms handed over 4.6%. Labour's share fell to 52.3%. GDP grew 1.7%.
- **Government.** Deficit 6.1%, debt 106.9%. Poverty 12.5%; homelessness 21.0 per 10,000.

**What mattered most**
1. The escrow fix. The labs put $2bn in trust, so the Framework Act got its vote and passed: state licensing pre-empted to 2030, disclosure now law. The Housing Supply Act also passed (from 2030), and two antitrust cases survived the courts.
2. The organising drive failed at the next tier of sites, and last year's one-off demand surge was not repeated, so job creation fell behind destruction again.

**What a household would have noticed:** pay still ahead of prices and rent flat, but a neighbour's office job quietly not refilled, and the union vote at the warehouse lost.
