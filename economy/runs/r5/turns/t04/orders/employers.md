# Orders — Employers — round 04 (2030)

**1. Read of the situation.** Attrition removed about 0.78m roles in 2029 and margins held, but orders are softening as the 2028 consumer surge fades, and credit lines are being repriced after AI shares fell by a third. Pre-emption of state licensing ends after 2030, the antitrust cases on bundling are live, and unemployment is back to 4.1%. Cash and flexibility matter more than speed this year.

**2. Options considered.**
- Sign the AI firms' two-year bundle by Q2 and drive ahead.
- Slow down and pause new projects until credit and demand clear.
- Unconventional: sign one-year terms at current rates with a most-favoured-price clause, and cite the antitrust cases and the share fall as leverage for a lower unit price.

**3. Decisions this period.**
1. **Automation pace:** drive roughly normal to 10% faster (down from about 20%). Fund only projects paying back inside 18 months, since credit is dearer. Keep roughly 40% layoff share, with attrition and freezes still doing the work. Do not announce mass layoffs. Cash is tight, and a visible wave would hand Congress and the unions a story before 2030 and 2032.
2. **Bundle terms:** counter the AI firms with a 12-month term at current rates, a most-favoured-price clause, and the usage tier for workflows already proven. Decline the two-year lock-in unless they cut unit prices 15% or more. Their shares have fallen and rivals are bidding, and a two-year lock at today's prices could leave us overpaying.
3. **People and politics:**
   - Honour 60 days' notice and 1.5% profit-sharing, written into contracts.
   - Extend the hours pilot to about 5% of headcount at 10% fewer hours, with a site-level worker advisory voice on role cuts. It is cheap, and it pre-empts the "worker say" demand with something we control.
   - Lobby to renew pre-emption beyond 2030 in exchange for the written attrition-first pledge.

**4. Public statement.** "We will keep investing in AI where it pays, protect notice and profit-sharing in writing, and consult workers before roles change. Tighter credit means a steady pace, not a rush."

Messages:
- **To AI firms:** One-year term at current rates with most-favoured pricing, and the usage tier for proven workflows. We will go to two years at 15% lower unit prices. Rivals and the antitrust cases give us options.
- **To Households:** We accept notice and profit-sharing as contract floor, and we will give site-level worker advisory seats on role changes, not a veto.

**5. What would change my mind.** A sharp fall in orders or a credit squeeze would make me pause projects. Rivals cutting costs visibly faster, or AI firms offering clearly lower prices, would make me sign and drive harder.
