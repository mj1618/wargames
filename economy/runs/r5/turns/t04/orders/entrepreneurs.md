# Orders — Entrepreneurs — Round 04 (2030)

## Read of the situation

2029 was a turning year: we placed 0.7m people (up from 0.5m in 2028), but the household demand surge of 2027–28 hasn't repeated. Credit is slamming shut—AI shares down 33%, venture and bank lending tightening across the board. Demand is durable (customers kept ordering), but margins are getting squeezed and expansion capital is suddenly scarce.

## Options considered

1. Pull back sharply on new ventures, sit tight on cash, wait for credit to normalize.
2. Shift entirely to AI-only high-margin models (profitable per founder, minimal jobs, undercuts incumbents).
3. Double down on training-intensive hybrids—harder for incumbents to copy, now more defensible as competitors struggle with their own credit access.

## Decisions this period

**Decision 1: Reduce overall expansion pace by 40% but maintain hybrid split.**  
Total new-venture hiring down to ~0.2–0.25m (from ~0.3–0.4m expected). Capital discipline: preserve cash, extend funding runway, serve existing customer base more efficiently. Why: Credit famine makes growth-at-all-costs suicide; we survive by being lean.

**Decision 2: Shift training capital toward housing.** With the Housing Supply Act opening funded, zoning-conditioned building from 2030, we're placing 30–40% of our trained workforce into construction trades, manufacturing-adjacent roles, and site supervision. This locks in customer relationships and let us compete where incumbents have less foothold. Why: Housing is the one sector with fresh, federal capital flowing in.

**Decision 3: Back the antitrust message publicly; push for open-access API terms.** Statement to government and press: "Competition lives or dies on data-centre access. Open-weight models are cheaper—let SMBs buy at startup prices." Why: If we can't grow fast because of credit, we can still push the playing field level. Open access helps us survive a downturn better than incumbents.

## Public statement

"2030 is a shakeout. We're cutting expansion, focusing on places where trained workers are hard to replace, and pushing antitrust hard. Housing is real opportunity—zoning is finally moving."

**Messages:**
- **To Federal government:** "Back open-access terms now. SMBs fold fast in a credit squeeze without cheap tools. That kills job creation."
- **To Congress:** "The bundling cases matter. If we get through this downturn cheaper than the giants, antitrust wins."

## What would change my mind

If credit normalizes and customer demand reignites faster than forecast, we'd accelerate housing and training plays—but we're not betting the company on that.
