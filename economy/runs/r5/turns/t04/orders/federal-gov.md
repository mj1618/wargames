# Orders — Federal government — round 04 (2030)

## 1. Read of the situation
Unemployment is 4.1% (up from a revised 3.4%), employment 164.0m, median income 103.0: no crisis voters can feel, but the direction turned and AI-linked layoffs reached 450,000. The new risk is financial: AI shares down a third and credit tight for a year, with receipts already soft at 17.2% and the deficit at 6.1%. Congress faces voters in November, pre-emption expires at year end, and labour share (52.3%) is the opposition's best line.

## 2. Options considered
- Stand pat: let the Housing Supply Act and wage insurance run, pass nothing.
- A small, temporary credit-crunch package that keeps layoffs off the front page before November.
- Unconventional: let pre-emption lapse and hand the states the licensing fight, taking the heat off Washington.

## 3. Decisions this period
1. **Main Street Credit and Work-Sharing Act (bill 1).** One-year small-business credit guarantees plus a work-sharing subsidy so employers cut hours, not jobs: 0.15% of GDP, 2030–31 only, sunset built in, no new tax. Benefits land before November. Deregulators and security hawks win; deficit hawks get the sunset.
2. **Framework Extension Act (bill 2).** Extend pre-emption to end 2032, keep disclosure, in exchange for the labs' $1bn release on schedule and the training fund doubled to $1bn a year. No licensing, no limits. Deregulators win.
3. **Executive action only.** No rescue for AI shareholders or lenders; antitrust cases continue at current pace as leverage, not escalated; publish disclosure data quarterly. Deficit hawks win.

## 4. Public statement
"Wall Street's wobble will not become Main Street's layoffs: we are backing small firms and keeping people on payrolls, with no bailout for AI investors and no new taxes."

**Messages**
- To AI firms: "Pre-emption extends to 2032 if the $1bn releases on time and the training fund doubles to $1bn a year. No bailout. Hold disclosure and announce no mass cuts before November."
- To Employers: "Work-sharing subsidy and credit guarantees are coming; you were consulted. Use hours cuts, not layoffs, and keep attrition-first through 2030. No mandates or surcharges this year."

## 5. What would change my mind
Unemployment past 5%, receipts below 17.0%, or a bond-market sell-off would move me to triggers on transfers or to cutting the package.
