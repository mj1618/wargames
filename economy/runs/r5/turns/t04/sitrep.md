# Sitrep — round 04 (January–December 2030)

**A financial wobble becomes a real recession; Washington's help arrives a year late.**

- **Jobs.** About 4.87m jobs were lost and 0.67m created. Only 0.72m went to machines; 4.16m went because spending fell (retail 1.43m, offices 1.41m, care 0.58m, building 0.5m). Employment fell to 160.4m. Unemployment 5.33% (was 4.12%); share of adults working 57.45% (was 58.96%).
- **Pay.** Real hourly pay fell 1.3%. Median real household income 100.2 (was 103.0); bottom fifth 99.4, below 2026.
- **Prices.** Flat; housing costs down 2% to 97.8 as the Housing Supply Act began.
- **Businesses.** GDP shrank 0.6%. Firms handed 5.8% of desk work to machines, of 34.5% possible. Labour's share fell to 50.5%.
- **Government.** Deficit 6.8%, debt 112.2%. Poverty 13.1% (was 12.5%); homelessness down to 19.4 per 10,000. Unrest 38.9.

**What mattered most**
1. The event and the reactions to it. AI shares fell by a third and credit tightened; households saved more, new firms cut expansion, and the central bank did not help. Lost spending, not automation, did the damage.
2. The employers' attrition-first pledge broke (roll failed), so more of the cuts were layoffs. Both federal bills passed, but the credit and work-sharing money only starts in 2031. Growth-first narrowly kept Congress, which is now divided.

**What a household would have noticed:** someone in the family laid off from a shop or an office, no pay rise, the card limit cut — and rent, for once, a little lower.
