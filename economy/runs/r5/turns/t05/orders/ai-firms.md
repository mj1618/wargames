# Orders — AI firms — round 05 (2031–2032)

## Read of the situation
Unemployment 5.5%, GDP −0.6% and 1.9m layoffs mean the political risk now comes from the recession, not us; shares down a third and tight credit make capital, not capability, the constraint. Customers use a sixth of model capability and have shortened to one-year terms, so revenue is a lock-in problem. Washington offers pre-emption to 2032 for a $1bn release and a $1bn-a-year fund — cheap next to a state licensing patchwork.

## Options considered
- Race through the downturn to bury weakened rivals (markets would punish it).
- Refuse employers' two-year ask and ride one-year churn (loses share to open weights).
- Unconventional: pivot the robot makers into factory-built housing now codes have opened.

## Decisions this period
1. **Pace steady; capex flat; a quarter of robotics investment redirected to factory-built housing.** The labs split on racing; the majority holds. Robot makers sign supply deals with modular-home factories — a new and popular market.
2. **Pricing: two-year enterprise terms at −15% unit price, conditional on committed volume at least 40% above current usage; most-favoured pricing within cohort only.** At one-sixth utilisation the cut costs little if volume grows; two years of lock-in through the antitrust cases beats margin. Usage tier stays open.
3. **Politics: take the government's deal.** Release the $1bn on time, double the fund to $1bn a year, hold disclosure, no mass cuts before November. $300m political spend. The further $2bn stays in escrow, re-offered for pre-emption through 2034 plus a consent decree settling the antitrust cases.

## Public statement
"We are holding investment steady through the downturn, doubling our training fund to $1bn a year, and putting robots to work building affordable homes."

## Messages
- **To Federal government:** "The $1bn releases on schedule and the fund doubles. The escrowed $2bn is yours when pre-emption runs to 2034 and the bundling cases settle by consent decree."
- **To Employers:** "Two years at −15% for 40% committed volume growth, MFN within your cohort. Rates hold whatever the cases decide."

## What would change my mind
A rival racing at +20% through the squeeze and taking enterprise share.
