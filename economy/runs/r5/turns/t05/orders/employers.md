# Employers — Orders, Round 05 (2031–2032)

## 1. Read of the situation
Unemployment is 5.5%, GDP fell 0.6% and labour share is 50.5%; orders are weak and our margins are squeezed, with member firms having cut several times the 0.72m roles we automated. The broken pledges are a political liability just as Congress turns more divided and pre-emption lapses after 2032. The Main Street Act subsidy makes hours cuts cheaper than layoffs for the first time, and the November 2032 election is the next rules window.

## 2. Options considered
- Restructure hard now (mass layoffs, drive 30% faster) to defend margins.
- Hold pace, use the subsidy to swap layoffs for shorter weeks, and trade that for pre-emption renewal.
- Unconventional: co-fund the households' pilot and give worker panels read-only deployment dashboards, pre-empting a mandate for a few million dollars.

## 3. Decisions this period
1. **Hours over heads.** Take the Main Street work-sharing subsidy: raise `hours_share` from 10% to ~25% of savings (about 8% of headcount at 10-15% shorter weeks), with pay topped up to ~90% in 2031 while subsidy covers it; step back toward full pay only if orders recover. Layoff share back down to ~45%, attrition and freezes first, no mass announcements before November 2032. Why: it is the cheapest way to cut cost while orders are weak and it repairs our credibility.
2. **Automation pace: normal, 18-month payback only.** No company-wide drive. Cash first; selective purchases where AI firms discount (cheaper tier, antitrust pressure). Keep most savings, but ~1.5% payroll profit-share in writing, and hold prices except in the lagging retail and office lines where a 5% cut buys volume.
3. **Sign the households' term sheet in modified form** within 60 days: pilot shorter weeks at pilot sites, 60-day notice, no-layoff at pilot sites only, panel seats seeing deployment plans 90 days ahead but under confidentiality, no veto. Ask in return: pre-emption renewal past 2032 and no surcharge or mandate in the next Congress.

## 4. Public statement and messages
**Statement:** "Large employers are choosing shorter weeks over layoffs where we can, will publish our attrition and layoff figures annually, and ask for stable rules while we do."

- **To Federal government:** We will use the work-sharing subsidy at scale and report quarterly. In return we want pre-emption extended past 2032 and no surcharges or mandates.
- **To Households:** We will sign a term sheet with confidential dashboards and site-level no-layoff, not a company-wide guarantee. Send the draft.

## 5. What would change my mind
If orders keep falling past mid-2031 or credit tightens again, board pressure forces deeper layoffs and I abandon the pilot expansion.
