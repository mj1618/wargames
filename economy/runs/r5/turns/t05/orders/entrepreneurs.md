# Orders — Entrepreneurs — round 05 (2031–2032)

## Read of the situation

End 2030: unemployment 5.5%, employment 160.4m (down 3.4m from 2029), credit frozen, AI equity crashed 33%. New firm job creation fell to 0.6m from 0.7m last year. But factory-built housing codes approved in most states with costs down sharply; Main Street Act money flows from 2031; AI firms offering cheaper tier (−25%).

## Options considered

1. **Full pullback**: Freeze expansion, focus on cash management through credit freeze (safe, dies by a thousand cuts).
2. **Housing double-down**: Allocate 60% of expansion capital to building trades under the new code/cost environment (captures tailwind, natural buyer demand, but sector-specific).
3. **Selective growth**: Stagger housing 50%, scale AI-only on cheaper tier for margin capture, modest hybrid growth (balanced, spreads risk).

## Decisions this period

**1. Housing acceleration (40% of expansion capital, ~$2–3bn).**
- What: Expand new housing-related firms and capital deployment into trades, factory partnerships, site prep where zoning opened.
- Why: Code approval + cost collapse is the signal we've been waiting for. Factory builders can't do last-mile hiring and training alone; we fill that. Returns are clear; moat is local execution, not dependent on any single AI vendor.
- Scale: Expect 150k–180k net new jobs in housing-adjacent work (tradesfolk, supervisors, trainers, logistics).

**2. Efficient AI-only scaling (30% of capital).**
- What: Launch new AI-only firms on the cheaper tier (−25% cost improves unit margins 8–12 points); target underserved SMB niches where incumbents' margins don't match.
- Why: Margin recovery when capital is scarce. Lower absolute cost lets us compete; we don't need scale to hit ROI.
- Scale: ~20k net new firm-level hires (mostly agents, light-touch human supervision). Expect 0–5k net new jobs; profit per founder up.

**3. Trainee commitment in writing.**
- What: Publish that all paid-trainee hires keep full pay through 2032 credit cycle, get first call on new jobs post-recovery.
- Why: Household demand is real; puts us ahead of incumbents on retained talent; signals long-term play when labor is cheap and scared.
- Scale: Affects ~400k–500k people in trained-hybrid roles.

## Public statement

"Factory-built housing has hit critical mass—costs are down, codes are open, and demand from households is unmeet. We're moving 40% of our expansion there. We're also committing in writing to keep trainee pay steady through the credit squeeze; trained hybrids are the durable competitive edge."

## Messages

**To: Households** — "Trainees hired now keep full first-year pay through 2032 even if we hit rough patches. First call on new positions when orders come back. Sign-on happening now."

**To: Congress / Main Street** — "We're hiring on the Housing Supply Act bet and the cheaper AI tier is helping smaller founders. Main Street Act money should flow to trainee subsidies and working capital for housing startups; that's where the jobs are."

## What would change my mind

If house-building orders collapsed (household income shock) or the cheaper AI tier was yanked, I'd cut expansion by 50% and go cash-heavy inside Q1 2031.

## Journal entry (Round 05)

Bet is housing + margin-efficient AI-only while credit is tight. Trainee commitment buys goodwill and moat (incumbents can't match our retention under duress). Watching: whether household income holds, how Main Street Act deployment moves credit, whether antitrust cases force real API openness, and incumbent poaching pressure. If code approvals stick and housing demand is real, our people-heavy hybrid + trades mix should outrun pure AI-only and pure incumbents.
