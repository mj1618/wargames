# Sitrep — round 05 (January 2031–December 2032)

**The recession ends, the jobs come back, and the government that presided over the recovery loses anyway.**

- **Jobs.** About 1.75m jobs went to machines and 5.71m were created: 3.45m as spending recovered, 1.86m in new kinds of work, 0.4m slow re-hiring. Employment 165.7m. Unemployment 4.05% (was 5.33%); share of adults working 58.86% (was 57.45%).
- **Pay.** Real hourly pay up about 8% over two years. Median real household income 108.7 (was 100.2); bottom fifth 106.5. Average week slightly shorter, 34.1 hours.
- **Prices.** Flat; housing costs down another 4% to 93.6.
- **Businesses.** GDP grew 3.3% a year. Machines do 9.2% of desk work, of 45.1% possible. Labour's share recovered to 51.7%.
- **Government.** Deficit 6.2%, debt 113.3%. Poverty 11.4%; homelessness 16.6 per 10,000. Unrest 35.3.

**What mattered most**
1. The credit squeeze ended and nobody tightened further: households held their saving steady, employers chose shorter weeks over layoffs and new firms hired.
2. The dice in Washington. The Paycheck Bridge Act failed, so no cheques, no levy, and pre-emption of state AI licensing lapsed at the end of 2032. Then growth-first lost the November 2032 election on a one-in-four roll; worker-first now leads a Congress readier to pass laws.

**What a household would have noticed:** the laid-off relative back at work, often in a job that did not exist five years ago; a real pay rise; cheaper rent and new factory-built homes going up nearby.
