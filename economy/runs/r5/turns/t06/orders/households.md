# Orders — Households — Round 06 (2033–34)

## 1. Read of the situation
Pay is up about 8% in real terms, median income is 108.7 and housing costs are down 4%, so most of us feel better off. But labour's share is only 51.7%, unemployment is 4.1% with participation still below its old level, and our local drives keep falling short. The gains we hold are in contracts, and the two pieces that would make them bite, breach penalties and the 12-month review, are still unsigned. A bond-market scare could bring austerity or tighter credit, which would hit the people with thin savings first.

## 2. Options considered
- Press the new worker-first Congress hard for a Paycheck Bridge rerun and sector bargaining.
- Hold what we have, tie up the term sheet, and build slowly.
- Unconventional: a household "co-op pool" that buys factory-built homes and shared child care collectively, using the profit-share cash and union treasuries.

## 3. Decisions this period
1. **Sign the term sheet with Employers, but with changes.** Accept confidential dashboards and site-level no-layoff, since that is what they will give. Condition it on (a) breach penalties of back-pay plus a fixed fine, (b) the 12-month review written in, and (c) a no-layoff clause that grows from pilot sites to more sites after each review. I send the draft this month. If they refuse penalties, we publish the breach record and go to the 2034 vote with it. No strikes while talks are live.
2. **Organising: keep about 10 small drives, but aim at warehouses, care homes and call centres,** where jobs are plentiful and shorter weeks already exist. Spend the rest on defending existing contracts and on worker-held training credits. Roughly one-third of effort goes to the 2034 midterm vote: registration and a written pledge from candidates on penalties and notice.
3. **Saving: hold steady, with a small rise of about 1 point if bond yields spike or layoffs near us climb.** We don't spend freely until the scare passes. Take Entrepreneurs' trainee places now, with their written full-first-year-pay promise and a no-drop clause. No boycotts.

## 4. Public statement and messages
**Statement:** "Pay is up because people signed contracts, not because anyone was generous. Put penalties in writing and we will keep the peace."

- **To Employers:** Draft term sheet attached. We accept site-level no-layoff and confidential dashboards. We need breach penalties and the 12-month review; without them we cannot call it a deal.
- **To Entrepreneurs:** Yes to trainee places. Put the first-year-pay promise in each offer letter, and name us as first call on the new positions.

## 5. What would change my mind
Layoffs near us, defaults rising or a credit freeze after the bond scare would push me to save more and drop the drives. Employers signing real penalties would make me widen the deal.
