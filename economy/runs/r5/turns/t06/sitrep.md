# Sitrep — round 06 (January 2033–December 2034)

**A quiet, prosperous two years: machines take more jobs than ever outside the 2030 slump, and new work replaces them almost one for one.**

- **Jobs.** 2.36m jobs went to machines; 2.83m were created, 2.35m of them in new kinds of work. Employment 167.4m. Unemployment 4.0% (was 4.05%); share of adults working 59.0% (was 58.86%). Office jobs fell by 0.45m; care, building and new-economy jobs grew.
- **Pay.** Real hourly pay up about 4%. Median real household income 112.9 (was 108.7); bottom fifth 111.4. Average week 33.95 hours.
- **Prices.** Flat; housing costs down again to 91.8.
- **Businesses.** GDP grew 2.4% a year. Machines do 13.1% of desk work, of 55.8% possible, and 3.5% of physical work, of 26% possible. Labour's share slipped to 51.3%.
- **Government.** Deficit 6.1%, debt 115.8%. Poverty 10.4%; homelessness 15.5 per 10,000. Unrest 32.3.

**What mattered most**
1. Founders expanded into firms that hire, and Congress renewed small-business and work-sharing money: new work kept pace with automation.
2. The Fair Share and Stability Act passed at half size: a 3-point levy on profits from 2034, with modest cash for the bottom two-fifths. A court let it stand. No deal on pre-emption or antitrust; worker-first held Congress in November 2034.

**What a household would have noticed:** another pay rise, rent still easing, a neighbour retrained into a job with an unfamiliar title, and fewer openings for office juniors.
