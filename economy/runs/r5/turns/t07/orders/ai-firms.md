# Orders — AI firms — round 07 (2035–36)

## Read of the situation
Unemployment 3.8%, employment 167.4m, GDP +2.4% a year, median income 111.9: displacement is politically dead and the 3-point levy is sunk, so the fight is now about rules. Our package was refused — no decree, $2bn in escrow, state licensing bills advancing, a trial ahead, three-year terms unsigned. Customers use under a quarter of capability, so adoption decides revenue, and the housing headline hands state legislators a grievance.

## Options considered
- Fight the trial and fifty states, keep bundling (Vanta's preference).
- Take the decree now and bargain it into one federal licensing standard.
- Unconventional: open-weight prior-generation models to pre-empt the open-access remedy on our terms.

## Decisions this period
1. **Politics — accept the consent decree.** Three labs of four (Vanta dissents, complies) accept open access and a mandated SMB tier from 2035, conditional on the government tabling a single federal licensing standard pre-empting states through 2038; $2bn releases on enactment. Fund stays $1bn/yr, $300m of it redirected to factory-built homes in squeezed metros. Political spend $450m, two-thirds on state bills.
2. **Pricing — sign the employers' counter within 60 days.** Two years at −15%, 20% committed growth, cohort MFN, rate hold; we accept the weak-orders step-down floored at 10% growth. At one-quarter utilisation volume beats margin; the decree ends lock-in anyway.
3. **Pace — steady on models, +15% on robots.** Capex +10% held; the increment goes to humanoid, warehouse and home-factory lines, where orders are real and the story is "building homes."

## Public statement
"We are accepting open access and a small-business tier by consent decree, and asking Congress for one national licensing standard so every state gets the same protections."

**Messages**
- To Federal government: "Decree accepted on your terms, from 2035. In return: table one federal licensing standard with pre-emption through 2038; $2bn releases on enactment. We'll back it publicly."
- To Employers: "Agreed, one change: step-down floor at 10% growth. Paper in 60 days."

## What would change my mind
A large state passing a moratorium or deployment cap before the federal standard moves — then we litigate and fund ballot measures instead.
