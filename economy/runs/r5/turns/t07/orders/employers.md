# Orders — Employers — round 07 (2035–2036)

## 1. Read of the situation
We removed about 2.4m roles in two years, half in offices, and margins are healthy even with the 3-point levy; unemployment is 3.8% and the economy is growing 2.4% a year. But worker-first holds Washington, state licensing bills are advancing with no pre-emption, and the Federal government is explicitly tying "no mandates" to layoffs staying below the 2030 pace. The 2036 election is the real risk: a visible layoff spike now hands worker-first a mandate and a surcharge.

## 2. Options considered
- Keep the current playbook: steady drive, attrition-led, hours at pilot sites.
- Accelerate hard while labour is tight and margins are strong (rivals may, so we would be herded into it).
- Unconventional: fund a joint Employers-AI-firms-Households standards body that sets one national deployment and notice code, so a federal framework replaces fifty state bills on our terms.

## 3. Decisions this period
1. **Pace and shedding:** adoption pace about +10% with 18-month payback; layoff share held near 45% and below 2030 levels. Net headcount reductions come through attrition and freezes. Aggregate announced layoffs stay well under the 2030 pace through the November 2036 election. This protects the "no mandates" promise at low cost.
2. **Households deal:** sign the term sheet with site-level no-layoff and confidential dashboards. Accept breach penalties capped at about 0.5% of site payroll and a single 12-month review, with no veto. It is cheap insurance against a mandate and answers the Federal condition.
3. **Federal framework:** back one federal AI framework (licensing standard, workforce disclosure, notice) in return for pre-emption of state licensing. Decline the AI firms' three-year terms ahead of the levy and keep one-year, most-favoured pricing. Shorter weeks stay at about 8% of savings at pilot sites; profit-share stays at 1.5%.

## 4. Public statement and messages
Statement: "We are investing in AI to grow and are keeping our commitments on notice, attrition and profit-sharing; we support one national framework."
- To Federal government: we will keep layoffs below the 2030 pace through 2036 and accept reporting; in return we ask for a federal framework with pre-emption of state licensing and no new surcharge.
- To Households: we will sign with breach penalties and a 12-month review; the penalty cap and no-veto language are firm.

## 5. What would change my mind
A rival mass-layoff wave, a credit squeeze or orders collapse, or a surcharge in a draft bill would make me accelerate cuts or pull the Households and no-layoff commitments.
