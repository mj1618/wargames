# Sitrep — round 07 (January 2035–December 2036)

**Machines took more jobs than in any period except the 2030 slump, and new work again more than replaced them.**

- **Jobs.** 2.99m jobs went to machines; 3.24m were created, 2.68m of them in new kinds of work. Employment 168.9m. Unemployment 4.0% (unchanged); share of adults working 59.1% (2026: 59.1%). Office jobs fell by 0.6m; care, building and new-economy jobs grew.
- **Pay.** Real hourly pay up about 3.6%. Median real household income 116.2 (was 112.9); bottom fifth 115.4. Average week 33.75 hours.
- **Prices.** Flat overall. Housing costs rose slightly to 92.1 (was 91.8): the land squeeze cancelled two years of building.
- **Businesses.** GDP grew 2.6% a year. Machines do 17.7% of desk work, of 66.9% possible, and 5.8% of physical work, of 37.6% possible. Labour's share fell to 50.7%; top 1% share 20.8%.
- **Government.** Deficit 6.0%, debt 117.6%. Poverty 9.7%; homelessness 14.6 per 10,000. Unrest 30.1.

**What mattered most**
1. The Metro Homes and Insurance Act passed in full, offsetting the housing squeeze.
2. The AI levy and open-access deal passed at half size and survived in court: a 3.5-point levy, partial open access, one federal licensing standard to end 2036. Worker-first kept its lead in November 2036.

**What a household would have noticed:** another pay rise and plenty of jobs with unfamiliar titles, but rent and insurance bills that stopped falling, and fewer office openings for school-leavers.
