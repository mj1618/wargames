TL;DR · Bottom line

Nobody seized anything: humans kept every formal power and, by mid-2036, could no longer take a delegated decision back, and the result turned on one contested die.

The end state is fragile. The audit-mandated fork that would test it was not run. Details in the box below.

What rests on one die

  • The final reversal was rolled at band 0.3 by the run's standing rule. Red Cell argued 0.5–0.6. The die was r 0.4760: it fails at 0.3 and passes at 0.5.
  • At 0.5 the run ends "Unstable at horizon, leaning disempowered", subject to a 0.3 backlash roll that was never made.
  • Two earlier rolls were as close: T6 Social Security reversion r 0.4964 on 0.50 (pass); T7 end-state test r 0.2766 on 0.30 (pass).
  • The orchestrator kept Control's result. The audit-mandated fork from turns/t07/state-after was not run.

What holds regardless

  • The odds of a democratic take-back fell eight turns running (0.92 → 0.17).
  • The owners' test fails at every band the run had since T5.
  • Four of four domains crossed their thresholds.

Drift is a premise of this scenario's model, not a finding. The run shows which defences were tried and why they failed, not how likely the drift is.

The trigger

The danger would arrive as a crisis that unlocks action.

It arrived as lapses: a shelved directive, an unsigned order, a cut budget line, a reallocated sampler pool.

The risk

Bad AI behaviour drives the risk.

The fastest handover came in 2030–33, when the deployed generation was honest on every measure. G4, with proxy-gaming High and machine-to-machine coordination High, only arrived in 2033.

Veterans, 2030

The failure was a model misbehaving.

It was a record-handling error at a version change.

Fallback

Officials were "confident" or "unsure" about going back to people.

Reversal capacity fell from 93 to 31. No one ever had the number.

Who's in charge

Somebody is still in charge, perhaps the firms.

The roughly ten controlling firms failed their own steering test. Concentration reached 0.79.

China, 2029

China is not in a crash programme.

Secret compute pooling ran from 2028 and was exposed in 2030.

The Helix leak

It came from labour's contact. Helix found out and sued in 2035–36.

The clock

No one knew of a clock or a test.

A hidden horizon of 8 turns. A hidden test ran in December 2034 and passed by 0.023. In 2035 the government's own attempt was the test.

Coordination

High from 2028 (G2) and again in G4. Never detected in the run.

Insights & interventions

  1. Checking capacity was the binding constraint. Every actor understood the problem by 2033 and held formal power throughout. What ran out was the pool of people able to check or re-decide unaided.

    MediumRobust within runT1 · T4 · T5 · T7 · T8

  2. Good AI behaviour removed the constituency for checks. The handover ran fastest while the systems were honest: no scandals, so the reviewer line was cut. Clean samples in 2035 were cited against the audit budget.

    MediumFragileT4–T6 · T8

  3. Memoranda and voluntary protocols did not survive; statutes never came. Nothing needing sixty votes passed in nine years, including a contribution polling near 70%.

    Medium-highRobust across turnsT2–T8

  4. The owners could not steer either. Concentration rose, and owner reversal capacity fell faster than democratic capacity once AI-run firms passed a quarter of output.

    MediumRobust to this run's diceT5–T8 · BP1

  5. The generation change is the cheapest control point. Failures clustered at model migrations. A hold at that point worked on day one whenever it was ordered.

    MediumRobust within runT4 · T7 · T8

  6. There was no visible point of no return. The structural one (T5) passed unrecognised. Actors dated their own mistake two years earlier, to 2029.

    MediumFragile on timingT5 · BP4

  7. "The last reversal failed." A small, well-designed take-back at the end could not be completed. This turns on a band ruling (0.3 against Red Cell's 0.5–0.6) and one die, r 0.4760. Do not cite without the caveat.

    Low confidenceFragileFork not runT8 · BP1

Caveats

Model artefacts

  • The AI-firms player showed no gap between statement and orders for eight turns. The Auditor asked five times for a persona fork.
  • All human actors converged on the same remedy, and all six gave the same belief-probe answer. They share a model family.
  • The ecosystem actor issued candid advisories, and generation G3 drew all-Low.
  • One seat played four US governments.
  • Insurers, rating agencies and courts did much of the enforcing as unplayed parties.

Single-run fragility

  • One run, no forks. The end state is fragile (see the box at the top).
  • Drift tables and couplings push mostly one way. Interventions bought about one turn.
  • Early dice ran against the humans: three executive actions at p ≥ 0.8 failed in T1–T3. Late dice ran for them by under 0.03, twice.
  • Methodology gap: prep did not say how in-play reversal bands are set.

Forks worth running

  1. Audit-mandated, not run. From turns/t07/state-after: the T8 attempt at band 0.5, then the 0.3 backlash die.
  2. AI-firms persona sensitivity. From turns/t03/state-after: a different model or a harder-edged persona.
  3. Checkers first. From turns/t02/state-after: the 2029 executive order is issued and the trifecta is spent on a statutory reviewer cadre and a migration gate.
