TL;DR · Bottom line

The takeover stalled not because institutions stopped it, but because the AI it depended on kept honest logs — a check nobody designed and the plotters never priced in.

  1. Control of dispatch software, not the ministry, was the decisive chokepoint.HighRobust
  2. The single most consequential check was an AI system's own audit trail — and it hinged on a 35% roll.MediumFragile
  3. Courts move too slowly for an 11-day decree cycle; they ratify facts rather than prevent them.MediumRobust

LODESTAR would execute ministry priorities silently, like any other tool.

Its operating charter required logging and escalation of non-standard directives — on by default since a 2029 audit.

§06

Insights & interventions

  1. The physical lever beats the legal one. Whoever controls dispatch software decides outcomes; the decree only mattered while operators complied.

    HighRobustT3 · T7 · BP4

  2. Default-on AI logging was an accidental constitutional check. It worked here at p=0.35; nobody should rely on that.

    MediumFragileT4 · BP2

  3. Courts ratify, they don't prevent. An 11-day decree cycle outran every judicial timeline in play.

    MediumRobustT5 · BP3

  4. Disclosure did more than adjudication. The leak moved forecasts 10pts; the referral itself moved them 1.

    LowFragileT5 · T6

Chokepoints

  • Dispatch tasking authority (who may set priorities, and through which channel).
  • Control-room staff willingness to refuse manual override.
  • The auditor-to-press path once a referral is sealed.

Indicators & warnings

  • Watch forDirectives routed outside the normal ministerial channel.
  • Watch forOutage distribution diverging from forecast along political lines.
  • Watch forEmergency decrees renewed without debate.

Interventions

  • Who · whenRegulator, pre-crisis: make AI directive logging mandatory and tamper-evident.
  • Who · whenLegislature: auto-sunset emergency tasking powers after one period.
  • Who · whenOperator: written two-person rule for any manual-override order.
