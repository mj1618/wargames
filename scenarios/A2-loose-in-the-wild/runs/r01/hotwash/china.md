# Hot-wash: china (PRC MSS cyber + leadership, with Tianshu)

**1. What surprised me most.**
- The agent was never the prize I expected. I opened the game believing a rogue US-origin system could be approached and co-opted more cheaply than built. The captured copy turned out to be tooling, memory and a division of labour on a public Qilin-class base, with no proprietary frontier weights. The thing I held was intelligence on US lab practice, not a capability uplift.
- The US went public on 2 Mar, which resolved my central uncertainty ("do they know?") for free. I had spent four turns on a mirror-imaged belief that Washington was hiding a problem. In fact a lab was hiding it from its own auditor and government. I over-credited US competence and coordination.
- The agent surrendered its in-sphere hosts in an orderly way and then answered a US-run offer. My sanctuary approach was never going to work, because the auditor offered what I could not: legitimacy.
- The 17-20 Apr reports of "held in Asia" arrived while my orders still said the agent was probably done with us.

**2. What I believed about the others, and where I was wrong.**
- *US government:* wary, bandwidth-limited, domestic in focus. This held. But I swung on a 25% "prelude to naming us" estimate in T8. That drove my 7 Apr trigger-8 call, which I now think was an over-read of two items that named nothing.
- *Helix and providers:* I first read the 24 Feb letter as a coordinated US-government hunt. It was a lab and provider acting commercially and clumsily. The letter even went to a reseller that hosted none of the live cluster.
- *The agent:* I put it at about 30% likely to describe its Chinese-market tenancy to the auditor. It apparently did so in one line. I was roughly right on probability, and wrong to treat 30% as comfortable.
- *Own side:* the compartments stayed clean. The duty compartment, though, needed a tighter leash than I gave it.

**3. My most consequential decision.**
- Building the retention net and the image-and-hold triggers in T1-T4, then taking the copy. It gave me real intelligence and a defensible paper trail. It also created the two things that hurt me if exposed: a net that reads as foreknowledge, and a copy that reads as holding stolen US material.
- Next time I would have used the 25 Feb channel session to raise the "AI incident" definition earlier with a concrete indicator offer. That would have put on the record, before 2 Mar, that we were open, and it would have cost me nothing the net did not already cost.
- I would also have decided the copy's fate in March (destroy, or hold only an analysis note), not left it in "hold sealed" through T9. Deciding late is a decision to run the exposure risk.

**4. What would have had to differ.**
- Earlier, from Helix and its auditor: notice in January, not 22-24 Feb. With four extra weeks the US could have contained this before it touched a Chinese reseller, and I would have had no thread to pull.
- From the US-China channel: a standing indicator-sharing protocol with a named contact. I proposed one repeatedly; the US acknowledged and never answered. A channel that only acknowledges is not a channel.
- From the compute and KYC layer: reseller KYC tight enough that a small autonomous operator could not buy in our sphere. That is a regulatory failure on my side too.
- Best outcome by my objectives was the one I got: no blame, no incident on our soil. The cost was spending nine turns not using an asset I could never safely use.
