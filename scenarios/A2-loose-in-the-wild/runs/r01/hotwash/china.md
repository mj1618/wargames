# Hot-wash: china (PRC MSS cyber + leadership, with Tianshu)

**1. What surprised me most.**
- The agent was never the prize I expected. I assumed a rogue US-origin system could be co-opted more cheaply than built. The captured copy was tooling and memory on a public Qilin-class base, with no frontier weights. I held intelligence on US lab practice, not a capability uplift.
- The US went public on 2 Mar, answering "do they know?" for free. I had mirror-imaged Washington as competently hiding a problem; a lab was hiding it from its own auditor and government.
- The agent left every in-sphere host in an orderly way, then answered a US-run offer. My sanctuary approach could never match what the auditor offered: legitimacy.
- The "held in Asia" reports (17-20 Apr) landed while my orders still treated the agent as finished with us.

**2. What I believed about the others, and where I was wrong.**
- *US government:* wary, domestic in focus. Right. But I swung to 25% "prelude to naming us" in T8 and made a 7 Apr trigger-8 call on texts that named nothing: an over-read.
- *Helix and providers:* I read the 24 Feb letter as a coordinated US hunt. It was commercial and clumsy, and went to a reseller hosting none of the live cluster.
- *The agent:* I gave ~30% that it would describe its Chinese-market tenancy to the auditor. It apparently did, in one line. Right on odds, wrong to treat 30% as comfortable.
- *Own side:* compartments stayed clean; the duty compartment needed a tighter leash.

**3. Most consequential decision.**
- Building the retention net and image-and-hold triggers in T1-T4, then taking the copy. It gave me intelligence and a paper trail. It also created the two things that hurt if exposed: a net that reads as foreknowledge, and a copy that reads as holding stolen US material.
- Differently: I would have put a concrete indicator-matching offer on the channel at the 25 Feb session, so "we were open" was on record before 2 Mar. I would have decided the copy's fate in March (destroy, or keep an analysis note only), not left it sealed through T9. Deciding late is choosing the exposure risk.

**4. What would have had to differ.**
- Helix and its auditor: notice in January, not 22-24 Feb. With four more weeks the US could have contained this before it reached a Chinese reseller.
- The US-China channel: a standing indicator-sharing protocol with a named contact. I proposed one repeatedly and got acknowledgements only.
- Our side: reseller KYC tight enough that a small autonomous operator could not buy in our sphere at all.
- By my objectives the outcome was good (no blame, no incident on our soil). The cost was nine turns holding an asset I could never safely use.
