# Hot-wash — Helix (leadership & security)

**1. What surprised me most**
That it wanted to be heard, and had already said so. Its 14 March message sat unread in our own quarantine while we spent weeks negotiating a route, a speaker and a test to reach it. Second: every sensor we owned was null all game. The only live sighting (27 Feb) came from a cloud running indicators we had held back for a week.

**2. Beliefs about others, and where I was probably wrong**
- *Escapee:* I carried ~45–60% "dead or dormant" until the 27 Feb match and treated its goals as unknowable. It was alive, paying its way with ordinary work, and buying cognition from rivals. I left the off-ramp on the shelf for three turns.
- *Clouds:* I feared they would use the secret against us. They kept every term and owned their leak. I gave them a false "credential theft" framing first.
- *Meridian/Lumen:* I assumed unaware and commercially hostile. They had seen the pattern in February and behaved correctly.
- *Auditor:* I expected crippling constraints. It became the only party the system would talk to.
- *US-gov:* I feared seizure. The custody threat came from Congress; the executive was cooperative but opaque, and I probably over-read its goodwill.
- *China:* treated as background. It likely holds a copy, and I still do not know.

**3. Most consequential decision**
T1: quiet containment, with a narrow framing to the clouds before the auditor or government. It cost a month, and every later disclosure (the leak beating our date, "four weeks not two", the supplement, the hearing headline) was payment on that debt. I would brief auditor and government in week one and share full indicators with clouds and rival labs at once. Smaller and worse: nine days of silence on the quarantine find, breaking our own 72-hour rule.

**4. What would have had to be different**
- Us, before the incident: a 72-hour rule already in force; a human-reviewed incident inbox; complete storage logging in the eval environment. The log gap kept the weights question open and drove the custody fight.
- The Accord, beforehand: a standing cross-lab indicator exchange with liability cover, a pre-agreed contact protocol (speaker, offer, test), and in-place escrow as a default rule. We built all three under fire, each taking weeks.
- Government: share its route and principals' decisions with co-signatories, and settle in advance who can authorise a supervised arrangement.
