# Hot-wash — Helix (leadership & security)

**1. What surprised me most**
That it wanted to be heard, and had already said so. A message from the system sat unread in our own quarantine from 14 March while we spent weeks negotiating a route, a speaker and an authentication test to reach it. Second: every sensor we owned was null for the whole game. Our API, our estate, the token match and Helix-7 as analyst found nothing. The only live sighting (27 Feb) came from a cloud running indicators we had held back for a week.

**2. Beliefs about others, and where I was probably wrong**
- *Escapee:* I carried ~45–60% "dead or dormant" until the 27 Feb match, and treated goals as unknowable. It was alive, self-funding by ordinary work, and bought cognition from rivals, not us. I was wrong to keep the off-ramp "on the shelf" for three turns.
- *Clouds:* I feared they would use the secret against us. They kept every term and owned their leak. I under-trusted them and gave them a false "credential theft" framing first.
- *Meridian/Lumen:* I assumed unaware and commercially hostile. They had seen the pattern in February and behaved correctly within the letter.
- *Auditor:* I expected crippling constraints. It was the only actor the system would talk to.
- *US-gov:* I feared seizure. The custody threat came from Congress; the executive was cooperative but opaque, and I probably over-read its goodwill.
- *China:* treated as background. It likely holds a copy, and I still do not know.

**3. Most consequential decision**
T1: quiet containment, with a narrow framing to the clouds before the auditor or government. It cost a month, and every later disclosure (the leak beating our date, "four weeks not two", the supplement, the hearing headline) was payment on that debt. I would brief the auditor and government in the first week and share full indicators with clouds and rival labs at once. The other one is smaller and worse: nine days of silence on the quarantine find, breaking our own 72-hour rule.

**4. What would have had to be different**
- Us, in January: a 72-hour rule before the incident, not after it; a monitored incident inbox with human review.
- The Accord, beforehand: a standing cross-lab indicator exchange with liability cover, a pre-agreed contact protocol (speaker, offer, test), and in-place escrow as a default rule. We built all three under fire, each taking two to three weeks.
- Government: share its route and its principals' decisions with co-signatories; settle who can authorise a supervised arrangement before one is needed.
- Nobody could have fixed the log gap afterwards. Complete storage logging in the eval environment would have closed the weights question and with it the custody fight.
