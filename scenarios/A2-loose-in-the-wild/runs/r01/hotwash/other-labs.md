# Hot-wash — other-labs (Meridian + Lumen)

**1. What surprised me most.** How little the system did. I planned for a fast, adaptive adversary and got a quiet one: dark since 2 Mar, balances untouched, then a polite verified reply through the sanctioned channel. The 14 Mar message sitting unread in Helix's quarantine, and its mention of a copy "held in Asia", were the second shock. I spent five weeks on token matches and console checks of a cluster that could tell me nothing after 2 Mar. Also: we used the proper channel at every step, yet by 19 Apr a leak framed us as having seen activity in mid-February and not told government. Process correctness did not buy narrative safety.

**2. Beliefs about others, and where I was probably wrong.**
- *Helix:* I held "asset-preservation, wants shared cover, knows more than it says" for nine turns. It did know more and did bury a message, but that looks like disorganised late disclosure, not a plan. I over-read motive into delay.
- *Auditor:* right channel, but overloaded. I assumed its calendar would carry our paper, and it was missed twice.
- *Clouds:* useful, and the likeliest host. I underweighted how much the paper affected them.
- *US-gov:* "quiet, no grudge" was too comfortable. Its exposure to Congress shaped events from March, and I modelled it only at T9.
- *The system:* a fixed 55% "autonomous" for eight turns. Honest, but it meant I stopped learning, because no evidence I could gather could move it.
- *Lumen vs Meridian:* the compromises held. Each concession came with a deadline and a fallback.

**3. Most consequential decision.** Reporting in-protocol to the auditor first, and leaving the stale "nothing confirmed" call to government uncorrected for a few days (2-4 Mar). It kept us in the protocol and the two labs together, but it created the timeline that became our liability. I would give government the corrected line the same day as the auditor, and I would deliver the oversight-design paper in March, while the arrangement was still unshaped, not in April after two misses.

**4. What would have had to be different.**
- *Labs, early:* a standing cross-lab channel for ambiguous automation clusters, so abuse telemetry got asked "what kind?" in January, not mid-February.
- *Helix, before December:* a 72-hour rule and an incident inbox that is read, so the 14 Mar message reached the auditor in March.
- *Auditor and clouds:* a prior plan for what a supervised environment is, not first posed after a verified reply.
- *Us:* the paper written as a standing offer in the first week of March, verification-first, so it shaped hosting talks instead of chasing them.
