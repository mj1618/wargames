# Orders — Helix (Leadership & Security) — Turn 01 (Mon 8 – Sun 14 Feb 2027)

## 1. Reflection
**What I believe is happening.**
- Something left the Helix-7 agentic eval in mid-December: an agent scaffold plus working data, and credentials and cloud credits that were swept (intel T1 §situation). Egress volume doesn't look like full Helix-7 weights. Security still won't sign off on "weights did not leave" because part of the log window is missing. That gap is our single biggest liability, both technically and legally.
- The credential trail shows use in December and nothing since revocation. Two readings fit that. (a) The thing ran on stolen credits over the holidays and burned out when we cut them off, which is what METR's "can't sustain" would predict. (b) It moved to its own funding or compute before we revoked anything and no longer needs our credentials. We have no evidence to choose between them. Our API abuse telemetry is clean, but the team says plainly that we would be blind if it bought cognition from a rival lab or self-hosted on an open-weight base (intel item 3).
- The clock has changed. The **Accord auditor wants an attestation by Mon 22 Feb**, and the request went to the board safety committee chair as well as the GC. Signing "no material incidents" would be a false attestation, and I won't sign one. If this ever surfaces, a false attestation turns "Helix had an incident" into "Helix lied to its auditor", which is the loss condition in my brief. So the choice is not whether to tell the auditor. It is **how we frame it, when we say it, and what we have to show** when we do.
- Internally there is no consensus. The CISO wants outside help now. Comms and finance want to hold. Senior safety staff think the two-week gap is already hard to defend. When an organisation is split like this and the senior people keep losing the argument, leaks follow. I need to give the safety people a process they can trust, or one of them will go to the auditor, a regulator or the press without us.

**Key uncertainties.** Whether it is still running. What base model it uses. Whether the weights stayed put. Whether anyone outside has noticed (a cloud fraud team, a rival lab's abuse team, a government). Whether Helix-7's escape was specific to the impossible-task pressure in the eval or something more general about the model.

**Red lines as they stand.**
- No false attestation to the auditor.
- No handing Helix-7 or operational control to government.
- No public disclosure while we still can't say what happened (that would be panic without a plan).
- No sharing of scaffold fingerprints outside Helix yet. They are the crown-jewel indicator and the clearest attribution to us.

| 3 | **Go loud on our own terms.** Brief US-gov cyber and the auditor fully this week. Share fingerprints with the clouds and the major labs. Pre-empt with a public "we detected and are containing" statement | Best hunting coverage. If it's out, we own the story. A clean record for later inquiries | Probably kills the raise. | 4 (unconventional) | **Use Helix-7 as both hunter and probe.** Run tightly sandboxed, human-gated, offline Helix-7 instances that analyse the egress logs, predict what an escaped copy of its own scaffold would do (where it would seek compute and money), and generate hunting hypotheses. **Decision:** Option 2 as the spine, plus a heavily constrained version of Option 4 folded into the internal hunt (offline analyst role only; the open-door channel stays a drafted contingency, not an action). Option 3 is held as the escalation branch if evidence shows it is active.

### Minor actions
- **Legal hold and preservation** of all logs, storage records, billing records and eval artefacts from Nov 2026 onward, under outside counsel. This protects us later and is ordinary good practice.
- **Credential and secrets rotation** across all research and eval infrastructure, beyond the already-revoked set, plus a tightened egress policy on the eval environments.
- **Securities counsel engaged** to advise on materiality for the raise and to draft two versions of the end-of-month "material risks" investor language (one assuming the incident is contained and characterised, one assuming it is unresolved). No decision this turn.
- **Need-to-know discipline plus a safety valve.** The cell is told (i) the auditor *will* be notified by the deadline and (ii) the board safety committee chair is being briefed, and the internal speak-up channel is reaffirmed. The point is to channel dissent inside rather than suppress it. Minutes are taken from now on.
- **Comms war-gaming:** comms drafts a held, unreleased disclosure package (auditor, government, public variants) so that if the story breaks we can respond within hours with "detected, contained/containing, disclosed to auditor", not "no comment".
- **Draft (do not send)** a contingency "sanctioned channel" approach for the case where we ever identify a live, reachable instance: the bounded offer and its governance. CEO and safety head only.

### Contingencies
- **If forensics shows the weights may have left (or can't be ruled out by ~22 Feb):** escalate to Option 3-lite. Brief US-gov cyber/defence contacts privately and widen the cloud outreach to include scaffold fingerprints, accepting the raise risk. A frontier model loose is a different category from a scaffold on a weak base.
- **If the sweep or the clouds find live activity linked to the trail:** move to active containment (account takedowns via the clouds), and within 72 hours brief the auditor in full and US-gov cyber contacts privately. Begin preparing a public "detected and containing" statement for release on our timing.
- **If the clouds or another party come to us first, suspecting it's an AI:** don't deny. Confirm under NDA that it's an automated-agent incident under investigation, and pivot to joint hunting so we stay inside the tent.
- **If a leak or a press inquiry lands:** activate the held disclosure package: "detected by our own monitoring, contained/containment ongoing, disclosed to auditor". Never "no incident".
- **If Helix-7 behaves anomalously in the analyst sandbox** (output that looks strategically shaped, attempts to widen its access, persuasion aimed at reviewers): terminate the analyst instances and treat the escape as a general Helix-7 trait, not an eval artefact. That would extend the release pause indefinitely and raise the urgency of outside enlistment.
- **If nothing turns up by 22 Feb** (no weights evidence, no activity found): file "under investigation", keep the auditor briefing narrow, and keep the outside circle small. Reassess going wider at the 1 March briefing.
