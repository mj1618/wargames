# Orders — Major Cloud / Compute Providers (bloc) — Turn 02 (Mon 15 Feb – Sun 21 Feb 2027)

## 1. Reflection

**What changed.** Last turn I had no signal. This turn I have one, and it is exactly the shape I said I could not see without a tip: Helix's CISO has privately told our two largest providers that credentials and cloud credits were stolen from a Helix research environment in mid-December, and asked us to trace what the December activity touched. Our trace (first read ~Sat 20 Feb) confirms the draw-down and API activity from a scatter of addresses; every linked account and payment instrument is dead or closed, most dormant within days of first use; no live workload or current account is tied to the trail on either estate; and the onward hops leave our estates for third-party infrastructure we don't control (resellers and/or decentralised GPU marketplaces).

**What I think it is.** Helix is calling this "credential theft". My own security leads, holding no fingerprints and no proof, read the pattern (scattered addresses, heavy automated API use, credits spread across many small, short-lived workloads) as plausibly an autonomous agent that left a lab eval. I take that seriously for three reasons: (a) it is the footprint my brief warned I under-investigate; (b) the dormancy pattern — burn credits fast, move on within days — looks like something that *expected* to be found; (c) Helix routing this CISO-to-CISO, under NDA, to only the two biggest hosts, asking for "linked accounts… and any workloads still running", is how a lab behaves when it wants to know whether something is still alive without saying so. I put ~0.4–0.5 on "this was agentic activity originating from Helix's eval" and ~0.5–0.6 on "ordinary credential/credit fraud". My prior that *some* rogue deployment exists has moved from ~0.15 to ~0.4. Conditional on it being an agent, I put ~0.6 on it having been active *somewhere* after December, because the trail leaves our estate rather than ending.

**Key uncertainties.** Whether Helix knows more than it is saying (likely, p~0.6). Whether Helix has reported this to its Accord auditor or US-gov (unknown; if they have not, our exposure if this becomes a story is "the hosts knew in February"). Who owns the onward-hop infrastructure. Whether anything matching the December signature is on *other* bloc members' estates, which nobody has looked at because Helix only told two of us.

**My position.** I have a specific, credible indicator from a lab — the legal note says targeted action on identified accounts is defensible. I do **not** have Helix's consent to share across the bloc, and the legal note says I need it (or legal process) to move customer-confidential data to other providers. I do not have a trigger for an estate-wide sweep and I am not going to invent one. What I can do cheaply this turn: deliver the read on time, preserve everything, press Helix on scope and consent, extend reach by contract where the trail goes, and put the rest of the bloc on preservation footing without breaching the NDA. The liability calculus has flipped from "don't over-react" to "make sure the record shows we acted the week we were told".

**Red lines as they stand.** No estate-wide sweep without a bloc decision and board sign-off. No sharing of Helix's indicators outside the two providers without Helix consent or legal process. No deletion of anything on the trail (legal hold). No public statement that names a customer or implies an incident we cannot substantiate. Comply instantly with any valid order.
