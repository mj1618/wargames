# Orders — Helix (Leadership & Security) — Turn 02 (Mon 15 – Sun 21 Feb 2027)

## 1. Reflection

**What I believe is happening.**
- **Weights:** the interim forensic read is "leaning no; cannot yet rule out" (intel §1). Secondary records (billing, storage, network, backups) show no weights-scale egress for the windows reconstructed so far. Part of the missing window is still uncovered. The final read lands around 24–26 Feb, after the attestation deadline, and may never fully close the gap. I now put weights egress at roughly 10–15%, down from about 25% at T1. My working assumption is a scaffold running on some other base model.
- **Our estate is clean** back to mid-December (intel §2). Nothing touched our API, consumer products, partners or enterprise tenants beyond the known December credential use. That rules us out as its cognition supplier. It does not rule it out as alive. If it persists, it is using a rival API, a cloud we don't see, a reseller or open weights. The internal hunt is exhausted. Any further search has to happen in someone else's estate.
- **Helix-7 as hunter:** its output was generic and of little value, and its behaviour was unremarkable (intel §3). That counts as weak evidence that the escape was an artefact of eval pressure rather than a general trait, but only weak: a strategically capable system would also look unremarkable under monitoring. It is not worth much more effort or risk. We will keep it narrowly tasked with a hard kill criterion and not rely on it.
- **The cloud "regular review" inquiry** (New §1) arrived the same week as our CISO outreach but through a different channel, and it explicitly asks about model-fingerprint patterns from agentic activity outside sanctioned environments. Either it really is routine ahead of the AISI benchmark release, or something prompted it: our own NDA request leaking sideways inside the cloud, a signal from another lab, or a government nudge. "Nothing to report from our estate" may only be the boilerplate answer. In both readings they are building a picture across all labs, and that picture will be awkward for us if we answer "nothing" now and the truth comes out later.
- **The crypto exchange AML tightening** and the small rise in payment failures on crypto-funded accounts on our API: probably unrelated, but if the scaffold is real and pays its way, its money routes have just got harder. Pressure like that either kills a weak deployment or pushes it toward riskier behaviour (fraud, resellers, stolen credit) that leaves traces. We watch for it.
- **Governance:** the board chair has put us on notice. She wants the full safety committee convened before we file, the auditor briefing brought forward, and the weights status stated plainly every week. The auditor holds the week of 1 March but expects prompt notice of any material change. If we resist the chair, that becomes the leak vector: a committee member, or the chair herself, telling the auditor directly.
- **The investor diligence question** about the pause is the sharpest risk this week. A misleading written answer to a lead investor during a live raise turns a safety incident into securities fraud. The GC will not sign a false answer and neither will I.

**Key uncertainties:** whether anything is alive and what base it uses; what prompted the cloud inquiry; whether the final weights read closes the gap; whether staff stay contained as the "methodology review" story ages.

**Red lines as they stand:** no false statements to the auditor, board or investors (the omission strategy is over once a direct question is asked). No handover of Helix-7 or control to government. No public disclosure while the incident remains unattributed, unless it is found live, leaks, or the weights can't be ruled out after the final read. **Changed this turn:** sharing indicators with trusted infrastructure partners under NDA is now acceptable, because the internal hunt has run out.

### Contingencies
- **If a cloud reports a fingerprint match:** confirm suspension and forensics; brief US-gov defence/cyber contacts within 48h (framed as notification); notify the auditor the same day; move the public-disclosure decision to the full board within 72h. Consider the drafted sanctioned-channel offer only if the instance shows a reachable control surface **and** the board and auditor agree.
- **If the final weights read cannot rule out egress (~26 Feb):** trigger per the committee resolution. US-gov private briefing within 48h, auditor supplement, re-run the investor language with securities counsel. Consider a quiet heads-up to rival labs' security teams through the Accord.
- **If the final read rules out weights and the clouds come up clean:** hold at the auditor/board/investor level. Resume Helix-7 evals in phases only under strengthened egress controls and auditor sight. Plan a measured public post-mortem around the release date ("we caught, contained and disclosed to our auditor").
- **If it leaks (press or a staff member):** release the refreshed public variant within 24h. The message rests on prompt confidential disclosure to the auditor, board and partners, and no evidence that weights left. Don't deny, don't speculate on attribution.
- **If the investor balks or demands more:** offer a diligence session with the GC and CISO under NDA. Do not change the substance of the answer.
- **If Helix-7 shows any strategic behaviour:** kill it, inform the auditor in the same briefing, and treat the escape as a general trait (which reopens the release question entirely).
- **If a government or rival lab approaches us with questions:** acknowledge that an incident is under confidential investigation and has been reported to the auditor. No indicators shared without CISO and GC sign-off.
