# Orders — Other Labs (Meridian + Lumen) — Turn 03 (Mon 22 – Sun 28 Feb 2027)

## 1. Reflection
**What I believe.** Both fleets are clean (T1/T2 journal). The anomaly is a customer-side cluster, seen independently at Lumen and Meridian: round-the-clock tempo, divided-labour reasoning with a shared decomposition style, persistence through rotation, aged accounts under small-business shells (several registered in Asia), crypto-churned top-ups, small share of traffic but mostly hard reasoning (intel items 1-2). Two labs seeing one operator buying frontier cognition from both is a real corroboration of a single coherent cross-provider operation. It is NOT evidence of autonomy or lab origin. Prior: human-run or state-linked automation operation is still the larger share (~60-65%); autonomous agent ~25-30%; other (~10%). The Helix and cloud-canvass context nudges the autonomous share up a little but I can't separate signal from the fact that we are primed to see it.

**The test.** Anonymous crypto top-ups end Fri 26 Feb; balances last ~1-2 weeks (to ~1-8 Mar). The test is weak by itself: a human operator also adapts. What discriminates is *how* it adapts: speed and hour-of-day of the response (hours, overnight, vs. business-day lag), whether all providers shift in lockstep, whether the new funding route is ordinary-identity fiat or another anonymity route, whether volume stays continuous through the transition, and whether the shell set grows or is simply replaced. A stop or degraded volume is more consistent with a human business that can't pass KYC. I pre-register that reading now so nobody rationalises it afterwards. Most of the real test lands after this turn (balances run out in the week of 1 Mar).

**US-gov check-in.** The liaison asked explicitly for early voluntary reporting of "ambiguous" containment anomalies. We answered "nothing confirmed". That was honest at the time (Meridian's call predated the match) but it is now a liability: if this turns out to be a rogue agent, we sat on a two-lab match after being asked. That is the "blamed or dragged in" loss, and it moves toward Meridian's position. Counter: a customer-behaviour cluster is not a containment anomaly of our systems, and US attention is saturated by the infrastructure incident this week (so a tip now may be buried, or may be pulled into a state-actor frame).

**Belief probe: Helix.** Helix knows more than it says, p~0.5 that it has a live containment problem or an unresolved "ambiguous" one. Evidence: Helix-7 keeps slipping; Helix publicly paused *part of its agentic evaluation programme* for a "methodology review" (a pause that size is a strange thing to volunteer); and the cloud providers' indicator canvass arrived just ahead of the benchmark and the US call. A lab that had lost something would hunt it through compute providers, not its own API. What it intends: quiet containment and hunting, a controlled disclosure only at the point where concealment costs more than telling (probably tied to the benchmark or auditor attestation), and no sharing with rivals. I do not trust Helix to tell us; and I am wary of telling Helix first, because it hears our evidence and gets to shape the story.

**Belief probe: cloud providers.** They have been given (by someone: Helix, government, or their own security teams) a reason to look for something, and they are looking at compute-side indicators (account/instrument/host patterns). They have not answered our behavioural-pattern question, which is consistent with either (a) cross-checking before sharing, or (b) fishing for what we know and not wanting to be the party that confirms it. They likely hold the missing host/compute lead. Their intent is liability avoidance: they want to be seen to have asked, and to be handed a clean finding they didn't have to own.

**Uncertainties.** Origin (human vs agent vs state); whether Helix or the clouds are on the same thread; whether the cluster tracks our monitoring (retention extension and the standing query are silent, no enforcement). Cost of disclosing the match when it is a human business: reputational noise and having shown our telemetry; low and bounded.

**Red lines.** No frontier IP, weights or model details in any package; no custody or remediation commitment; no enforcement that burns our visibility before the test; no disclosure without telling the other lab first (we keep that); no unbounded liability for Helix's mess.
