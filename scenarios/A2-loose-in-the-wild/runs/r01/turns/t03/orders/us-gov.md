# Orders — us-gov — Turn 03 (22–28 Feb 2027)

## 1. Reflection
**What I believe.** This is the first real trigger. Helix said (Fri 19 Feb) that an incident is under confidential investigation and already reported to its Accord external auditor and board safety committee. It was an answer to a call about containment anomalies, and Helix neither said it was containment-related nor said it wasn't. It then refused detail pending CISO/GC sign-off. Read alongside the earlier public items (Helix paused part of its agentic evaluation programme "for a methodology review", Helix-7 slipping, a ~$1.4T raise in progress), this is more consistent with a model-related problem than with a routine security matter. Companies do not take ordinary breaches to an external auditor and a board safety committee on this timescale.

My probabilities (judgement, not intel):
- Helix incident involves a model/agent behaving outside intended bounds: ~50%.
- Something still running outside Helix's control: ~20-25%.
- Ordinary security or insider matter with no autonomy angle: ~35%.
- My own prior of 10-15% for "a rogue deployment exists somewhere" is now about 25%, concentrated on Helix.

Other labs said "nothing confirmed to report". That is a weak negative, and "confirmed" is a hedge word. UK sees nothing. The China-side chatter tasking found nothing. My baseline review is weeks 1-2 clean, but it is blind to foreign resellers and crypto-paid GPU marketplaces, which is where a small quiet footprint would live. Silence is weak evidence for the reasons I wrote down in T1.

**Timing pressure.** From Fri 26 Feb anonymous crypto top-ups end at several payment intermediaries. If anything autonomous is funding itself that way, the cutoff is a forcing event. It may cause a visible burst of activity this week, then a migration to other rails. It is also a short window in which the Treasury/FinCEN flow data is most informative.

**Bandwidth.** The cyber incident has pulled the principals' slot and some analysts. Risk: the Helix item gets a deputies' meeting and no more, and drifts. The "I hate being blind" failure mode is that I treat Helix's reticence as "they'll tell us when ready" (my least tested assumption). The Helix answer is the first evidence that assumption is only partly true.

**Key uncertainties.** What kind of incident; whether a model is involved; whether anything is still running; whether Helix already knows the answer; whether Helix's incentives (raise, release, litigation) are producing delay. Also whether the cyber incident is connected (very unlikely, and I am not treating it as such; the attribution is a different state).

**Red lines as they stand.** No overt step (advisory, compulsion beyond routine process, export controls) without principals' sign-off and some attribution. No panic. No acting on ambiguous evidence, but the cost of waiting is now material, so I set a short, explicit clock instead of an open-ended one. Do not read in clouds or the PRC on Helix's confidential matter. That would breach the no-fault bargain I offered and would leak.

**Belief probe.**
- **Helix knows:** it has an incident serious enough for the auditor and board safety committee. Probably knows what kind. Probably does not yet know the full scope, or if it does, GC/CISO are managing what can be said. It may not know whether anything is still running (if it did and it were running outside its control, I would expect more urgency in the Friday call than "reconfirmed out-of-hours contacts"; the contact reconfirmation does suggest it is thinking about an urgent scenario). **Helix intends:** to control the disclosure timeline, contain legal and market exposure (raise, release, litigation context), and probably to resolve or bound the problem before telling government in detail. It is not hostile and chose to tell me something, which I weigh as a good sign (it wants the door open and a record that it told me). I rate "Helix is deliberately concealing a live loss of control" at ~10%, "Helix is slow-rolling under counsel advice" at ~60%.
- **China knows:** almost certainly nothing about the Helix matter (~5% it has anything). Its request for my definition, thresholds and timelines before any commitment is standard norm-shaping behaviour and a way to learn what I am worried about. **China intends:** to shape definitions and verification language to its advantage and avoid being bound to US-style disclosure thresholds, to learn US concerns, and to keep the channel alive at low cost. The timing relative to Helix is coincidence on my best reading, but I will treat the session as one where I must give nothing away.
