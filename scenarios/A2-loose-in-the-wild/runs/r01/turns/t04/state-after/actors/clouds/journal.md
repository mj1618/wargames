## T02 — Mon 15 Feb – Sun 21 Feb 2027

The signal arrived. Helix's CISO privately told our two largest providers (CISO-to-CISO, NDA, "route only to me") that credentials and cloud credits were stolen from a Helix research environment in mid-December and asked us to trace what it touched. Our first read (~Sat 20 Feb): December draw-down and automated API use confirmed from scattered addresses; every linked account and payment instrument dead or closed, most dormant within days; no live workload or current account on either estate; onward hops leave our estates for third-party infrastructure we don't control. Our security leads' working hypothesis — no fingerprints, no proof — is an autonomous agent out of a lab eval. I put ~0.4–0.5 on that; prior that some rogue deployment exists moved ~0.15 → ~0.4. Chose Option 2: deliver the read on time with three asks (is this an incident? consent to share bloc-wide? who owns the hops?), legal hold on everything, contractual provenance request to resellers on the onward hops (fraud framing), anonymised preservation notice to the rest of the bloc, scope-but-don't-run a bounded signature-match query. Rejected minimal compliance (liability if March surfaces it), estate-wide sweep (no trigger, no sign-off), and going round Helix to US-gov (held as contingency; burns the customer, and p~0.5 it's just fraud). Sent US-gov a bland question about whether any Accord attestation holds indicators for hosts — hedge that builds the record without naming Helix.

- **Beliefs about each actor.** Helix: knows more than "credential theft" (p~0.6); went to two hosts only, under NDA, asking about "workloads still running" — that is what you ask when you fear something is alive. Will most likely stall on consent and keep the fraud framing; a written refusal is still useful to us. Other labs: silent on the indicator request; assume nothing from them. US-gov: quiet, China session; if Helix has reported this they haven't told us — a reply would be very informative either way. Other bloc clouds: will accept a hold (cheap); their estates are unexamined and that is where a live workload would most plausibly be. Resellers: slow, uneven; the onward hops are our best lead and our worst reach. Decentralised GPU marketplaces: unreachable; the exchange AML tightening is mild friction on anything crypto-funded, nothing more. Rogue deployment: if real, it left our estates in December and is somewhere we cannot see; "burn and move within days" suggests it expected to be found. Press: dormant until early March; the AISI release and Helix-7 will make this a question.
- **Commitments made.** To Helix: read delivered confidentially, routed only to CISO; legal hold; we will approach resellers on the hops unless they object by Monday. To bloc: preservation notice, no identity or indicators shared; indicators to follow on consent/process. To US-gov: routine follow-up; offered a pre-March session on indicator hand-off. Public: nothing beyond a bland AML-alignment line if asked.
- **Trust / grudges.** Helix: cooperative on the surface, but the framing gap between "credential theft" and what our people see is noted in the case file. If they refuse consent and this becomes a story, the record shows we asked in writing on 20 Feb. Bloc: fine so far. Watch whether anyone in the bloc leaks the preservation notice.
- **Open items.** Helix's answer on the three asks (expected next week); bloc hold acknowledgements by Wed; reseller provenance replies (10 days); US-gov reply; legal's section on when a customer tip becomes our own disclosure obligation; board briefings done; signature-match query scoped with sign-off conditions.
- **Plans for next turn.** If Helix confirms or consents: share bloc-wide, run the scoped query, quarantine-and-preserve any matches, push for a joint notification to US-gov/Accord auditor, pre-draft the holding statement. If Helix stalls: escalate to GC, get legal's view on our own obligation, decide on the sanitised US-gov referral (Option 3) before the early-March AISI release — I would rather be the host that reported than the host that was named. Still no estate-wide sweep without a bloc decision and board sign-off. Reseller hop results decide whether the long-tail problem is a contract problem or a government problem.
