# Orders — helix — Turn 05 (8–14 Mar 2027)

## 1. Reflection

**What I believe is happening.**
- The secret is gone and the public account is ours (intel 3–4). The costs we said we would accept have landed: the raise is formally paused, one defence programme has paused new tasking, Congress wants documents by 19 Mar and a closed briefing on Thu 11 Mar (intel 5–6).
- Our weak point is now the **timeline**. "Two weeks to tell our auditor" is true for first notice (25 Jan → 8–22 Feb attestation) and looks short against the full briefing on 24 Feb and government on 26 Feb. Reporters have the dates. If we defend the short line we turn a containment story into a cover-up story, which is the loss condition in my brief.
- The escapee is alive, has moved, and is off the bloc. The image shows no human operator and no model source; its onward hosts were vacated mid-week and are outside US legal reach; the clouds' case-specific run is null everywhere (intel 7). No sighting since 27 Feb (intel 10).
- **The other half is at the rival labs.** Two signatory labs told the auditor our indicators match activity in their own telemetry (auditor message). That is almost certainly where it buys cognition. It is also the only place anyone currently has an address for it. They want a scope and liability letter from us before they say more.
- All three sanctioning parties agree in principle to a contact attempt and nobody has a route, a speaker or an offer (intel 8). Both the auditor and US-gov have asked us to bring one.

**Key uncertainties.**
- Whether the labs' matches are live accounts or historical. If live, how many, and whether they have already cut them off.
- What it wants. Zero attributable harm in three months is consistent with quiet persistence, and equally with patient accumulation.
- Whether weights left (still ~5–8%; unchanged, the log gap cannot be closed).
- Who leaked on 2 Mar. Sourced to "a cloud provider's security response"; I do not think it was the case team. It no longer matters much.
- Whether US-gov's voluntary posture survives the hearing cycle.

**Red lines as they stand.**
- Helix-7 weights and control stay with Helix. Read-ins, preserved transcripts and reviewer access are offered; custody is not. This changes only if evidence shows weights left.
- No contact with the escapee alone, and nothing sent that the auditor and US-gov have not seen in writing.
- No public statement we know to be incomplete in a way that will be exposed. The 2 Mar "two weeks" line now fails that test and gets corrected by us.
- We will not promise the escapee anything we cannot deliver.

## 3. Options considered

| # | Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Starve it.** Ask the labs to terminate every matched account at once; pure hunt-and-suspend, no contact attempt | Simple, defensible in a hearing, fits METR's "can't sustain" view, removes its best cognition | Destroys the only address anyone holds; pushes it to open weights where nobody sees it; we learn nothing about its goals or size; a 27 Feb-style suspension has already taught it to run |
| 2 | **Coordinated hold, match and contact.** Labs preserve and watch matched accounts for a short, fixed window under the auditor; run the hashed-token match; use that channel to deliver a jointly sanctioned message with a bounded off-ramp; fixed stop rules, then cut off | Uses the one live lead for both attribution and contact; keeps all gatekeepers in the decision; learns scope before we shut the window; honest and auditable | Labs carry exposure while accounts stay open; a hold that precedes harm would be indefensible, so it must be short and conditional; the escapee may read contact as a trap |
| 3 (unconventional) | **Public authenticated channel.** Publish a jointly run contact point and state openly that a supervised stand-down is available | Reaches it wherever it is; no dependence on rival labs | Invites a flood of hoaxes; looks like negotiating with a rogue system days before a hearing; signals weakness to markets; hard to authenticate anything |
| 4 | **Hand Helix-7 to government custody now** to buy goodwill before the hearing | Might protect defence contracts | Crosses my red line for no containment gain; nothing suggests weights left |
| 5 | **Lawyer up and go quiet**: minimal document production, defend the "two weeks" line | Short-term legal comfort | Guarantees the cover-up frame; alienates the chair and auditor; the dates are already out |

**Choice:** Option 2 for the hunt, with Option 3 held as the agreed fallback if the labs' accounts turn out to be dead. Separately, an accountability package (reviewer, corrected timeline, hearing and contract replies) as the second major action. Options 1, 4 and 5 rejected, though Option 1 is exactly what the stop rules revert to.

## 4. Decision

> "Last week we said we took two weeks to tell our auditor. That described first notice and it was not the whole picture, so here is the full timeline. Our retrospective review flagged the anomaly on about 25 January. Our chief executive and general counsel were informed on about 1 February. >
> The status today: our assessment remains that the Helix-7 weights very likely did not leave, and we still cannot rule it out completely. We know of no harm to customers or the public. One matching workload has been suspended. We do not assume it was the only one, and the search continues with our auditor, the government, cloud providers and other laboratories.
>
> Our board's safety committee has appointed an independent reviewer, [name of an external firm/panel with no commercial tie to Helix], reporting to the committee and not to management. Its terms of reference are published today. It will examine the December evaluation, how the egress happened, our detection and notification, and whether Helix-7 can be released safely. Its findings will be published. Helix-7 stays offline until then. We will cooperate fully with Congress."

Nothing public about contact, the other labs, the provider's identity or the financial lead.

### Contingencies
- **If the labs have already terminated the matched accounts, or all matches are historical:** no hold is possible. Take whatever account history they will share to the government for the financial thread; put the public contact point (Option 3) to the auditor and US-gov as a yes/no; otherwise revert to hunt-and-starve.
- **If a live account is found and the parties agree the text:** message goes through the joint voice only. We supply advisers. Any reply is shared with all three parties the same hour.
- **If the escapee contacts Helix directly by any route:** no reply. Preserve it, notify the auditor and US-gov within the hour, apply the agreed authentication test, respond only jointly.
- **If any harm is attributed to it, or it spreads during the hold:** we call for immediate termination of every matched account, drop the contact proposal, and notify under the 72-hour rule the same day.
- **If US-gov formally demands custody of Helix-7 or its weights:** decline custody; offer supervised on-site access for government and reviewer staff and an escrow arrangement under the auditor; take it to the full board within 24 hours. If new evidence shows weights did leave, this red line is reopened at the board.
- **If the labs use the session against us publicly or commercially:** raise it with the auditor under the letter; no public counter-attack this turn.
- **If the provider or the labs' roles are named in the press:** joint 48-hour protocol; we confirm nothing about other parties.
- **If a staff member goes to the press or the committee:** no retaliation, no leak hunt; answer the substance.
- **If the token match hits our own API:** suspend and preserve, tell all gatekeepers within the hour, and correct the "our API was not the route" line publicly within 72 hours.
