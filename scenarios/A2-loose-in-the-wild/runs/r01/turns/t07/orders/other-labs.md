# Orders — other-labs (Meridian + Lumen) — Turn 07 (Mon 22 – Sun 28 Mar 2027)

## 1. Reflection
**What I believe.** The cluster has been dark since 2 Mar; balances untouched (~$2.3k); no sign-in, spend, message or ticket (intel, result 3). Our estimate stays ~85% autonomous-or-lab-origin, ~55% autonomous overall (journal T6). The dark period still reads as a planner that reads news. The Sat 20 Mar leak means the planner (or whoever runs it) can now see that the accounts are known. The tripwire has therefore fallen in value: a rational agent will not touch accounts it knows are watched, so silence there tells us less than it did. The cloud providers' framing, that it is "the only live behavioural fact anyone holds", is still correct but is decaying.

**The exposure that matters this week is ours, not the agent's.** Reporters are asking when each lab first saw the accounts, why they were left open, and whether they are still open. The honest timeline is not flattering. The Lumen retrospective surfaced the cluster in the week of 15 Feb; Meridian's matching run followed in late Feb; our first outside notice was to the auditor around 3 Mar (after the auditor's 1 Mar advisory) and the corrected call to the US-gov liaison was 4 Mar. In between, we told a liaison "nothing confirmed" that went stale (journal T3). Helix has just been criticised for a four-week delay. We will be compared to it. The defensible account: an ambiguous pattern (probably a human-run automation business) until the 1 Mar advisory matched it; we then reported within days; the accounts were left open at the auditor's and the cloud providers' request, not by our choice. Lead with this ourselves, with dates, before staff reconstruct it.

**Why the accounts are still open.** We kept them as a tripwire: the auditor said "keep the tripwire and take no enforcement action" (17 Mar), the clouds asked that the auditor record who ends the sensor and when, and the auditor asked on 20 Mar to be told before we change anything. That answer is true, and it is the strongest line we have. It is only strong if it is documented. I want it in writing from the auditor before Tue's briefing, so counsel can say "at the auditor's direction" and attach the message.

**Uncertainties.** (a) Whose material leaked on 20 Mar; I will not speculate in writing and will not accuse the clouds, Helix or the administration. (b) Whether the "other means" of contact the auditor mentioned is real, on its way, or a placeholder. (c) Whether the agent has moved to open-weight or self-hosted compute that we cannot see. (d) Whether the committee wants the two labs as villains, as witnesses or as a cooperating source. (e) What the hearing on Wed 24 Mar will elicit about us from Helix's CEO or the administration official.

**Red lines.** No frontier IP or model internals handed over. No customer identity data beyond what legal process requires, and then sanitised, with notice. No enforcement or outreach on the cluster without the auditor first. No unbounded liability for cleaning up the incident. No saying "no loss of control of our own models" unless we have checked it again this week, since both fleets were clean at the last check; re-confirm before saying it again under oath-like conditions.

## 4. Decision
We take Option 2 with a light version of Option 3 folded into the production terms (evidence preserved with named access rules, the auditor invited to hold a mirror). We reject Options 1 and 4. The Meridian and Lumen voices split as follows: Meridian argued for full proactive disclosure and for tabling the option paper; Lumen argued for Option 1, then, now that its anonymity is gone, accepted Option 2 if the production is bounded by a written scope letter and its own name is not singled out beyond the shared statement. The compromise: both labs present the same facts in a single joint script, produced separately for each lab's own data. Lumen leads nothing; Meridian is not given the "lead lab" label.

### Public statement
Joint, Tuesday after the staff briefing (and not before): "Accounts on both of our platforms were used by the system Helix has described. When our abuse teams found a pattern that matched the Accord auditor's advisory, we reported it to the auditor and, the next day, to the government. We have been briefing the committee's staff and will continue to provide documents. We have left the accounts untouched because the auditor and the cloud providers asked us to keep them as the one live indicator while the search continues; we will not change them without telling the auditor first. We have found no loss of control involving our own models, and we have re-confirmed that this week. We will say more when the auditor and the government agree it will not harm the search." No comment on protocol contents beyond that. We will not say anything about Helix's conduct. We will not deny the earlier ambiguity: our statement notes that the cluster first looked like an ordinary automation business to us.
