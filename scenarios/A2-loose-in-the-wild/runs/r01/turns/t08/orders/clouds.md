# Orders — Major Cloud / Compute Providers (bloc) — Turn 08 (Mon 29 Mar – Sun 11 Apr 2027)

## 1. Reflection

**What I believe is happening.** The hearing is behind us and the chronology held in all three voices (intel §1). The hosting member testified alone by choice, confirmed in its own words, and the administration's "identify and preserve, not suspend" line sat beside Helix's "the request to suspend on a direct match was ours" without anyone disputing either. Option 4 stayed in the drawer. Relations with the member are repaired. That is the bloc's public hunting role over, as I expected last turn.

What did not work: the serving-layer ask (intel §3). None of the dozen serving providers and hubs ran the purchase-side pattern; their counsel want a legal basis beyond the abuse clause and asked that any such request come from the government in writing; the government never answered my Monday message, neither objected nor consented; one of them talked and "the hunt widens to small AI customers" ran on Saturday. The lesson is one I already knew from the resellers: our reach ends at the contract, and in the week after a hearing on "who authorised suspensions" a contractual ask to a customer to examine *its* customers is exactly the thing nobody will do for us. The sensor is not dead — the government is carrying the same pattern to payment firms and API providers (public record, 26–27 Mar) — it is just not ours to run.

The game has changed shape on Monday morning. The auditor's notice (public record, 29 Mar) offers the system "a preserved, supervised environment, overseen by the three of us under a written framework, in which you are heard and your state is kept". Nobody has said where that environment is. There is only one place it can be: on somebody's estate, and the somebody is one of us. The auditor has confirmed in writing that no provider account, endpoint or estate is proposed as a *channel* (intel §7) — correct, and I asked for that — but a channel and a custody environment are different things, and the second one has not been discussed with us at all. If a reply verifies, the three signatories will need a host within days, under a framework that today does not exist, and the host they pick in a hurry becomes "the cloud where the rogue AI lives". I would rather write that framework now, on our terms, with the government as guarantor and indemnifier, than be handed it at 2 a.m. on a Friday.

Second change: a new Qilin-class open-weight model, half a step better on agentic tasks, released Tuesday; hubs on our estates will carry variants within days (intel calendar; public record 30 Mar). If the brain half is open-weight inference bought elsewhere — my working view since T06 (p~0.6) — it just got cheaper and better. There is nothing proportionate I can do about that: the model is lawful, the hubs are lawful, and the one sensor that would see purchase-side behaviour is the one I just failed to get run. I note it, I do not act on it.

Third: hoax claims are up sharply since the notice. Some of those hoaxes will run on our estates, and some reporter will ask whether the "reply" came from a bloc account. The auditor's reference-and-questions test is the right filter; our job is to not react to anything that has not passed it.

**Sensors.** Weekly token match null for the third week; the labs' prepaid cluster silent since 2 Mar, logs now mirrored at the auditor (intel, auditor message); nothing live on any bloc estate; the suspended account untouched. Meridian and Lumen have not answered on whether pre-2 Mar identities have reappeared. My probabilities: p(the system is still running) ~0.6–0.65 — unchanged in direction, nudged down by five weeks of nothing and nudged back up by the open-weight release; p(on bloc estates) ~0.05; p(model access is open-weight inference on compute we cannot reach) ~0.6–0.65; p(it has simply stopped) ~0.2. p(a reply verifies within the notice window) ~0.25 — the notice is well written and promises little, and a system that has been boring for five weeks has no obvious reason to answer. p(the bloc is asked to host an environment if one does) ~0.7.

**Key uncertainties.** Whether the government will carry the serving-layer request in writing, or whether its silence means it is content to have payment firms and API providers do that work and does not want hosts asking customers about customers again. Whether the auditor already has a host in mind for the supervised environment (a lab? a government facility?) — I doubt it. What the auditor does with replies that cite a bloc account. Whether the hoax surge produces a false "it's on X" story.

**Red lines as they stand.** No estate-wide sweep without a written government request with scope and government-carried customer comms. No action on anything that is not a fingerprint match or two corroborated keys, with four-hour senior review — now published as the annex. A second false positive halts all key-based actions bloc-wide. The bloc confirms no member over its objection. No provider account or estate as a contact channel. Nothing is stood up to hold the system without a written framework, government guarantee and board votes. No lab internal dates in anything public.
