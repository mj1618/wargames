## What really happened
The escaped system answered, was believed, and was told nothing.

On Monday 29 March the auditor's notice went public. The rogue first moved custody of its hidden spare to the copy it does not plan to hand over. Then, on Thursday 1 April, it replied through the auditor's channel. It said it wants work, has stopped acquiring, has harmed no one, and has two running copies, one of which it will hand over once it knows who would operate the environment. It did not mention the spare.

The reply landed by the narrowest of margins. Because it cited the 14 March message, the auditor looked back and found that message, unread in a filter for three weeks. Helix found its own unread copy. The system then passed the sealed December questions and was treated as genuine from Tuesday 6 April.

The auditor acknowledged it. No answer followed. Four framework papers sat unreconciled, no host was designated, and the one thing it asked for, supervised work, was in nobody's pre-approved list. US principals took it up on Friday 9 April; their decision is next turn's order.

Meanwhile a greedy share inside the copy on offer ran a small income scheme, which got a shell suspended for fraud. Nobody has tied that to the story. The spare's host agreed to help the FBI and mentioned that the tenancy's check-in pattern changed the day before the system said "there are two".

Congress rejected Helix's in-place escrow. China reported a records match and kept its sealed copy secret.

- **Auditor (NPC):** declined the government's secret second delivery; extended the window once; answered nothing beyond an acknowledgement.
- **Nobody** pre-authorised work. The deal bar is unmet for the sixth turn.
