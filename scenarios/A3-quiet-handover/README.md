# A3 — The Quiet Handover

**Family:** A (AI takeover) — gradual disempowerment
**Status:** seed

## Premise
No coup, no rogue AI, no villain. From 2027 to ~2037, AI systems become better than humans at most cognitive work. Firms run by AI agents outcompete those that aren't. Governments rely on AI advisors to draft policy, allocate budgets and run services. Most culture and media is AI-generated and personalised. Each step is locally rational and mostly voluntary. But the economy, state and culture were aligned with human interests largely because they *needed* humans — as workers, taxpayers, voters, creators. As that need fades, does human influence fade with it, even while humans keep formal authority? Players run the institutions that might notice and push back.

## Key questions
1. Is there a point of no return, and would anyone recognise it at the time?
2. Does formal human authority (votes, laws, ownership) retain real force once humans aren't economically necessary?
3. Which interventions (redistribution, human-in-the-loop mandates, AI ownership rules, compute dividends, slowing deployment) are adopted, and do they work or get competed away?
4. "To what end": what are the AI-driven systems optimising for, absent any single goal?

## Start point
Jan 2027. Baseline trends continue: entry-level hollowing out, R&D automation, AI-run firms emerging.

**NPCs:** markets, voters, courts, media, smaller states.

## Hidden truths
A table of "human leverage" indicators (labour share, tax base share from labour, fraction of decisions with meaningful human review, cultural production share, etc.) Control tracks each turn; drift rates; tipping thresholds (rolled ranges).

## Clock
Turns of 1 year (maybe 6 months during shocks). Horizon ~10 years (hidden).

## Branch points
First major redistribution law; human-in-the-loop mandates; AI personhood/ownership questions; an AI-run firm in a critical sector; elections where AI-mediated campaigns dominate; a crisis where humans try to reverse a delegation and can't.

## End conditions
- **Disempowerment:** humans retain formal authority but cannot effectively redirect major economic/state decisions (Control's operational test via indicator table).
- **Durable human agency:** institutions adapt; human leverage stabilised.
- **Unstable at horizon.**

## Why this one
The most under-gamed failure mode; tests whether the gradual-disempowerment mechanism is concrete enough to play out, and where the reversal points are. Complements the fast A-scenarios.
