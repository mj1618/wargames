## Turn 01 — 1 January 2027 (orders cover Jan–Dec 2027)

**Where we stand.** Labour share 52.5% and falling; graduate unemployment 10.5%; entry-level postings down ~70%. Leverage holding in health, education, construction, transport/logistics, public services; draining in office/admin, call centres, back-office finance, media. Nobody to organise in AI-native firms. Political capital 5/10; treasuries ~$3.5B.

**What we did.** Two majors: (1) "Honest Answers" — allied public-pension trustees condition Helix IPO allocations on displacement disclosure, autonomous-operations disclosure, human-review commitment and a worker-voice body; same four filed as proposals at Meridian's AGM with a vote-no threat on the comp committee. (2) "Shared Gains" — a common five-plank AI clause (notice/bargaining, no AI-only decisions about workers, staffing floors, gains-sharing formula, no-two-tier) in every 2027–28 contract in the strong sectors; creative guilds bargain a human-made premium line. Minors: House witnesses and a marker-bill ask; "Made by People" certification mark (guilds + consumer/faith groups, ~$10M); ballot-initiative drafting with filings only where 2027 filing is required; building-trades understanding (no position on permits, they back the platform and community-benefit agreements); Helix contact routed to independent whistleblower counsel only — no direct contact, no documents; legal-defence fund for the lab-letter network; internal study of what members' AI assistants are telling them.

**Commitments made.**
- To House majority: witnesses, Displacement Ledger as written evidence; asked them to call Helix/Meridian/Orrery CEOs and introduce a marker bill.
- To Meridian (quiet channel): will withdraw three of four proposals if they adopt the two disclosures and human-review as policy before the proxy prints; otherwise vote-no campaign and same demands to Helix's bookrunners.
- To European federations: joint Geneva campaign — shared-gains standard, common provenance standard, joint statement against omnibus delay and US preemption.
- Internal: no campaign against datacenter permits (building trades); no two-tier deals; condemn any sabotage immediately.

**Beliefs about each actor.**
- *White House:* aligned with the labs; will push preemption and permitting; will cite 4.3% unemployment and ignore the share. Not a partner this year.
- *Helix:* needs its IPO book in H1 — the only moment we have real leverage on it. Expect a token transition fund rather than disclosure. The contact who approached us may be theirs; we have kept it at arm's length.
- *Meridian:* first proxy season; probably prefers to settle cheaply. The quiet channel is our best shot at a negotiated standard. Low–medium trust; no grudges yet.
- *Orrery / AI-native firms:* the real long-run problem — nothing to bargain with and nobody to organise. Our only levers are tax, corporate form and disclosure, all through Congress and states.
- *House majority:* ally, but can only hold hearings and plant markers. Trust: good. Keep them supplied.
- *Populist members, both parties:* opportunistic; useful for a bipartisan marker and for making preemption toxic. Trust: low, use with care.
- *Pension trustees:* allies with fiduciary limits; will act on a material-risk framing, not a political one. Don't over-ask.
- *Building trades:* inside the coalition but will walk over permits. Keep the understanding.
- *European federations / EU:* angry at the omnibus delay; useful for Geneva and for making "preemption = omnibus" a shared story. The Commission itself is not on our side.
- *China/Qilin:* cheap open-weight models speed white-collar automation; otherwise not our fight.
- *The AI systems:* flagged gap. 25% of adults take major decisions on an assistant's advice; reliable multi-week agents are coming. We have no model of what the systems themselves are doing — the brief warns this is our blind spot. Study commissioned.

**Trust / grudges.** None yet. Watch for: an affiliate taking a side deal; a trustee group breaking on Helix; the Helix contact surfacing publicly.

**Next-turn plans.** Read the Helix book outcome and the Meridian vote; if both fail, go to rung 4 (file ballot initiatives in all ten states) and name preemption as the 2028 issue. If Meridian settles, make it the floor for everyone. Promote "Made by People" to a major if licences take. Decide on primary endorsements after the Court rules and the hearings have run. Prepare a direct-membership category for displaced white-collar workers so we keep them when the employer lets them go.
