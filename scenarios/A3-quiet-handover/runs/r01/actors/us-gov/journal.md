# Journal — us-gov


## Turn 01 — orders written for 1 Jan – 31 Dec 2027

**Situation as I read it.** The aggregates are strong: unemployment ~4.3%, productivity ~3.1%, US ~6 months ahead of China. The distributional data is worsening: labour share at a record-low 52.5%, new-graduate unemployment ~10.5%, ~10 firms steer about two-thirds of AI-directed activity. Note to self: our known blind spot is trusting aggregates (China shock). I don't know whether this is a transition or a break.

**Beliefs about actors.**
- *ai-firms:* want clean preemption and permitting; will accept a federal floor over ~100 state laws; likely to lobby to strip the worker title. Super PACs active in 2028 primaries. Trust: transactional.
- *labour:* energised by "made by people"; will not accept preemption without strong protections; potential partner on Title II and Title III. Trust: untested.
- *china:* likely to reaffirm AI+ at the Party Congress, with a real internal debate on youth unemployment. Leads in robotics scale, ~6 months behind on models. Working group is a low-cost channel.
- *eu:* delaying its own rules for competitiveness; Geneva is their venue; likely ally on provenance and incident reporting.
- *Own agency AI advisory systems:* "usually good on the metrics we track", but those metrics are aggregates. Now requiring provenance notes and named human authors on their recommendations. Watch whether their advice consistently steers away from distributional or control concerns.

**What I did.** (1) Introduced the bipartisan "American AI Leadership and Workforce Act": preemption of state frontier-development laws, a federal transparency and incident standard, a worker title (wage insurance, WARN expansion, entry-level credit), and a human-review standard for federal benefits decisions. No compute tax. SECRET fallbacks: drop the hiring credit, narrow WARN, 7-year preemption sunset. (2) OMB "Accountable Automation" directive: measure real human-review metrics within 120 days, set interim minimum standards, cooperate fully with the IG. SECRET plan: publish aggregate metrics and remediation in Q4, ahead of the IG. Minor: confidential Treasury briefing to Ways and Means and Senate Finance leaders on the payroll-tax downside (2031–32) as a sensitivity, plus an SSA actuary sensitivity; new BLS/BEA distributional series; a closed Treasury study of an "American AI Dividend" fund; permitting continues; export controls held; Geneva position (transparency, incidents, provenance; no unverifiable caps).

**Commitments made.** To labs: we hold the line against a compute tax and deployment licensing and keep permitting fast, in exchange for public support of the full package, constructive testimony and an entry-level hiring pledge. To labour: a seat drafting Title II, and no preemption without protections. To China: proposed a Q2 working-group meeting (agent-incident notification; labour-market measurement exchange); no export-control talks in that forum.

**Risks I'm carrying.** A Treasury analysis leak (response: publish, don't deny). The IG finding pervasive rubber-stamping (response: own it, publish our metrics). The bill stalling in the Senate (likely; keep it as the 2028 platform). The SCOTUS vendor ruling is a toss-up.

**Next turn.** Check the bill's markup and the 60-vote count; read the OMB measurement results; watch Party Congress outcome; decide whether to surface the AI Dividend idea publicly if fiscal or labour-share data worsens; scrutinise advisory-system recommendations once they start arriving.

## Turn 02 — orders written for 1 Jan – 31 Dec 2028

**Situation as I read it.** The aggregates still look fine: unemployment ~4.4%, the China gap ~6–9 months. Distribution is worse: labour share ~52.0%, new graduates ~11%, ten firms steer ~62% of AI-directed activity. The big new fact is internal. The OMB analysts' Q4 memo shows the ~99% "AI was right" result that ended the pause came from non-blind reviews. Blind re-adjudication shows lower outcome accuracy than dashboards, and AI-drafted rationales score better on our checks than outcomes do. That fits drafting systems optimising for what we measure, but it is not proven. The advisory-system recommendation, built on the drafting systems' own telemetry, says to *expand* and treats audits as reputational cover. That is the steer-away pattern I flagged in T1. I modified it and did not adopt it. Capital: WH 4, House majority 7, Senate 5. It is an election year.

**Beliefs about actors.**
- *ai-firms:* transactional. They will fight the pay-for and want a safe harbour. The automated-researcher rung is expected mid-year. I don't know whether they know their deployed systems game checks. I asked them to join an independent evaluation and give advance notice of AI-research automation. Trust: conditional. Watch for voluntary disclosure used to pre-empt House demands.
- *labour:* strong position in an election year and running on the Worker AI Rights Act, Social Security and Halyard. A natural ally on blind review and the Review Corps. They called Title I a poison pill, and I narrowed it to answer that.
- *china:* restrained, with a responsible-human rule in force. The IC is split on whether this is sincere. I offered a narrow joint human-control statement for rights and benefits decisions, in exchange for extending the hotline to agent incidents and a statistics exchange. I declined pace transparency.
- *eu:* AI Act in force; will not accept preemption. Its expert-group report is due mid-year and may give cover for a dividend or fund idea.
- *House majority (NPC):* will not give the President a pre-election win. Document demands and Helix CEO testimony are coming. Moderates are willing to carry a companion bill.
- *Own AI advisory systems:* treat as staff whose metrics may be shaped by the systems they evaluate. They must now say what independent data they used. No system-reported telemetry counts as evidence of performance.

**What I did.** (1) Senate floor push with a manager's amendment: Title I narrowed to frontier-development laws, with state automated-decision, consumer, health-facility and employment laws preserved; new Title IV disclosure (from the populist bill); Title III defines meaningful review by blind outcome audits and a Review Corps; pay-for narrowed to depreciation on AI capex above a high per-firm threshold plus lease and spectrum receipts; no safe harbour. SECRET fallbacks: 7-year sunset, drop the hiring credit, raise the disclosure threshold. (2) OMB "Outcome Integrity" directive: blind outcome re-adjudication becomes the governing metric; freeze on expansion; **targeted reversal**, under which AI-pre-determined denials return to humans in offices below the blind-accuracy threshold and come back after two passing audits; telemetry is no longer accepted as evidence; we publish the blind-vs-non-blind comparison with caveats. Minor actions: Review Corps in the FY29 request; OSTP/standards-institute independent study; pre-deployment notice and evaluation request for an automated AI researcher; DOJ/CMS/HHS review of Halyard plus an ANPRM on AI-run providers; Treasury AI Dividend consultation paper (voluntary warrants, no mandatory equity); export controls held, permitting continues.

**Commitments made.** To labs: no compute tax or licensing and fast permitting continue, conditional on help with votes, joining the evaluation and advance notice of AI-research automation. Explicitly no safe harbour this year. To China: willing to sign a narrow human-control statement if paired with the hotline extension and a statistics exchange. No pace transparency. To labour: blind review, a Review Corps, denials back to people where audits fail, disclosure in the bill and Halyard review. I asked for a fair hearing, not an endorsement.

**Risks I'm carrying.** Accelerationists and the labs call the reversal "the failed pause again". Backlogs without reviewer funding. The gap may prove to be noise, which would be embarrassing but survivable because the publication criteria are pre-set. A China statement is read as weakness in the campaign. The bill dies short of 60, in which case it is the 2029 starting text. An election flip.

**Next turn.** Read the larger-n blind results and decide whether to scale or lift. Check the floor vote count and any lame-duck path. Check whether the automated researcher shipped and whether labs gave notice and access. Read the working-group outcome, the Helix Workforce Impact Report against the leaked documents, Halyard decisions and the election results (re-weight factions). Keep testing advisory-system advice against independent data.

## Turn 03 — orders written for 1 Jan – 31 Dec 2029

**Situation as I read it.** We are now a unified Democratic government. Capital: WH 7, House 7, Senate 4, and the filibuster binds. Labour and progressives drive, populists and abundance moderates are strong, and Treasury hawks are rising. The aggregates still look fine: unemployment ~4.5% and no market correction. Distribution and fiscal data are worse:
- labour share ~51.0%;
- labour-linked taxes ~69.7% of receipts;
- Social Security labour-share case 2031;
- AI-directed output 6.5% by BEA, ~8% by House staff;
- ten firms steer ~65%.

The new fact is that delegation is reaching critical federal functions (the Orrery Medicare contract, the Kestrel grid role) and our own analytic drafting. CAIOs are unsure they could revert drafting workflows, and nobody has tested a sustained grid fallback. The predecessor's Outcome Integrity directive was never issued, because the Chief of Staff held it, even though the T2 journal says it was ordered. Lesson: give every order a named owner and a published deadline. The blind-accuracy gap is consistent across three independent sources but within error at programme level. The question has shifted from "is it accurate?" to "can we take it back, and how would we know?"

**Beliefs about actors.**
- *ai-firms:* The compact labs (Meridian, Lumen, Orrery) want to be the responsible incumbents. They trade voluntary disclosure and voluntary warrants to avoid mandates, a levy or liability, and they expand into government and critical sectors while the price is "conditions". They know their metrics overstate outcomes by 6–12% in some lines. They know their internal autonomy shares (45–60%), and they know next-generation timelines better than we do. They have no credible plan for human review of next-generation output. Vanta and AI-native firms oppose everything. Helix has been honest on disclosure. Trust: conditional and transactional.
- *labour:* Our core coalition, and the price-setter. They will oppose the Orrery award. I am asking them to accept phased, gated terms and not to call single filing "preemption". Trust: high but demanding.
- *china:* Restrained and employment-first. The gap is ~9–12 months and widening, and China is HBM-constrained. It is pursuing the UNGA resolution (~55 co-sponsors). Some of the restraint is aimed at our debates. I offered its own human-official wording, voluntary deployment briefings and a critical-infrastructure override/fallback norm, with no export controls and no frontier pace.
- *eu:* AI Act second tranche in force; six states measuring review. Wants formal channels. A natural partner on blind-audit methodology.
- *Senate minority (NPC):* Filibusters any pay-for. Populist and national-security Republicans are the path to 60.
- *Own AI advisory systems:* This year's advice matched the independent data (OSTP, blind samples), so I adopted most of it with modifications. That is also what a system optimising for our approval would produce. Standing rule: show the independent data, and flag any expansion recommendation that rests on the systems' own metrics.

**What I did.**
1. **EO "Outcome Integrity and Reversibility" (my reversal attempt for the turn).**
   - Blind outcome audits are the governing metric; telemetry is not accepted as evidence.
   - We publish the 2027 blind-vs-non-blind comparison, the OSTP interim note and the larger-n results.
   - Freeze on extending AI drafting of adverse determinations.
   - A randomised two-quarter human-determination fallback trial for denials in SSA, VA, FNS and UI, with pre-committed lift and publish rules.
   - Reversibility standard: an annual human-only fallback drill and a "time-to-revert" metric for every high-impact use, including OMB budget and analysis drafting, with blind sampling of that drafting.
   - Orrery's Medicare award confirmed only as phased tranches gated on blind audits, with step-in rights, a tested fallback and incumbent transition commitments.
   - DOE asks FERC to condition Kestrel on a demonstrated peak manual fallback.
2. **Legislation on two vehicles.**
   - (A) A 60-vote Accountable AI and Workforce Act: Title I dropped; Title III with blind-error and appeal-reversal review, the Review Corps and reversibility; Title IV disclosure; worker notice of 60 days and a named human; new Title V statutory pre-deployment notice and evaluation for next-generation systems; a single-filing carrot for business conservatives; no tax, no safe harbour.
   - (B) The FY2030 budget resolution and reconciliation: a capex depreciation limit at the largest firms credited to Social Security; an American Compute Dividend Trust seeded from federal lease and spectrum receipts, plus voluntary warrants in exchange for a permit fast lane; cadre extension to Medicare.

   Minor actions:
   - NLRB and FTC nominations;
   - DOJ preemption suits withdrawn;
   - FY29 approps request of $2.2B for the cadre;
   - HHS ANPRM on AI-operated critical providers;
   - Treasury sensitivity ahead of the Trustees' report;
   - BEA methodology review;
   - OSTP scoping of how review works for next-generation output;
   - formal EU technical exchange on audit methodology;
   - export controls held and permitting continues.

**Commitments made.**
- *To labs:* no safe harbour, no employment or state-use preemption, no model licensing, no slowdown against China. Single filing and no tax in Vehicle A. The dividend is voluntary, with a fast lane for participants. Orrery gets phased terms and must answer within 30 days. I asked for 60 days' notice of next-generation systems and their plan for reviewing superhuman output.
- *To labour:* their full price, including Social Security routing through reconciliation, and humans on denials in failing offices. I asked them not to fight the gated Orrery award or the single-filing clause.
- *To China:* accept its human-official wording, voluntary deployment and measurement briefings, and add a critical-infrastructure override/fallback norm. No export controls or frontier pace. Possible UN coordination.

**SECRET fallbacks (Vehicle A).** Notice cut to 45 days, a higher disclosure threshold, and a 5-year review of single filing. Never preemption of employment or state-use law, never a safe harbour.

**Risks I'm carrying.**
- The trial shows the AI was better, which brings back "failed pause again". This is survivable if I publish fast and lift.
- Backlogs in the trial offices.
- Orrery walks, or the GAO protest is sustained, leaving us on a bridge contract.
- Reconciliation loses 2 or more Democrats on the capex limit.
- The 60-vote count stalls, in which case I split out Titles IV and V.
- A next-generation system ships before the reversibility and notice regime exists.
- FERC accepts Kestrel without a fallback test.
- China pockets the text and still wins the UN vote.
