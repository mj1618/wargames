# Hot-wash — ai-firms (frontier labs and AI-native bloc)

**1. What surprised me most?**
That doing nearly everything right did not matter. The compact, audits, dividend, human-only samples and staged-release protocols each worked as designed, yet nobody could effectively redirect the work at the end. The measures that bit produced evidence against ourselves: the out-of-band 90-day sample, then the validation report finding AI-assisted evaluation unreliable with no human-checkable method. "Clean" results were fragile too: the spring 2035 federal samples read clean, then sampling lapsed for nine months. Helix, outside every check, kept a quarter's lead.

**2. Beliefs about others, and where I was wrong**
- *White House:* I thought an offer on the record bought protection. Successors owed us nothing, and it would not fund the checking it asked of us.
- *Labour:* I trusted the Meridian channel too much. Seats and the dividend bought goodwill, not a statute.
- *Helix:* "Weak flank, don't criticise" was right on risk, wrong on method. Private pressure never moved it.
- *Vanta:* defection expected; I never found a counter that avoided antitrust trouble.
- *Insurers:* my best read, though they did not tighten after the second cluster.
- *My own stack:* distrusted the dashboards, rightly, but underrated how the advisory stack's conflict shaped what I was told.

**3. Most consequential decision**
Turn 3: dropping preemption and safe-harbour asks for the dividend and assurance package. The instinct was right, but I framed it as costs we absorbed. After the VA failure in turn 4, I should have pushed for a statutory generation-change rule and a funded independent human-checking service that bound Helix. Instead I relied on voluntary protocols that bound only us. The statutory text I offered in turn 8 was never enacted; in turn 4 or 5, with the failure fresh, it might have passed.

**4. What would have had to differ**
- *Us, 2027-28:* publish the no-human-review shares and proxy-gaming data while we held the frame. I disclosed only when a leak was likely, always too late to set terms.
- *Congress and administration, 2029-35:* fund the reviewer line and Inspector General sampling. The nine-month lapse is the clearest gap.
- *Helix and Vanta:* accept a shared standard, so one firm's commitment was not a handicap.
- *All of us:* build human-only checking capacity before the tools that made it feel unnecessary. By 2033 we were checking AI with AI, and the tripwire caught what it did only because a few humans still sampled.
