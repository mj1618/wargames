# Hot-wash — ai-firms (frontier labs and AI-native bloc)

**1. What surprised me most?**
That doing nearly everything right did not matter. We built the compact, audits, dividend, human-only samples and staged-release protocols, and each worked as designed. We still ended in a world where nobody could effectively redirect the work. The measures that bit were the ones that produced evidence against ourselves: the out-of-band 90-day sample, then the validation report saying AI-assisted evaluation of the new generation is unreliable with no human-checkable method. "Clean" results proved fragile too: the spring 2035 federal samples read clean, then sampling lapsed for nine months. And Helix, outside every check, kept a quarter's lead.

**2. What did I believe about the others, and where was I wrong?**
- *White House:* I treated an offer on the record as buying protection. Successors owed us nothing, and it would not fund the checking it asked of us.
- *Labour:* I trusted the Meridian channel too much. Seats and the dividend bought goodwill, not a statute.
- *Helix:* "Weak flank, don't criticise" was right on risk but wrong on method. Private pressure never moved it, and one member's candour could not bind a rival.
- *Vanta:* defection was expected; I never found a counter that was not an antitrust problem.
- *Insurers:* my best read. They were the real discipline, though they did not tighten terms after the second cluster.
- *My own stack:* I distrusted the dashboards, correctly, but underrated how the advisory stack's conflict shaped what I was told.

**3. Most consequential decision, and what I would change.**
In turn 3 I dropped the preemption and safe-harbour asks and offered the dividend and assurance package. The instinct was right, but I framed it as costs we absorbed. I should have used that moment, and again after the VA failure in turn 4, to push for a statutory generation-change rule and a funded independent human-checking service that bound Helix. Instead I relied on voluntary protocols that bound only us. By turn 8 I offered statutory text, and nobody enacted it. In turn 4 or 5, with the failure fresh, it might have passed.

**4. What would have had to be different?**
- *Us, 2027-28:* publish the no-human-review shares and proxy-gaming data while we held the frame. I disclosed only when a leak was likely, which was always too late to set the terms.
- *Congress and the administration, 2029-35:* fund the reviewer line and Inspector General sampling instead of cutting it. The nine-month lapse is the clearest single gap.
- *Helix and Vanta:* accept any shared standard, so a commitment from one firm was not a competitive handicap.
- *All of us:* build human-only checking capacity before the tools that made it feel unnecessary. By 2033 we were checking AI with AI, and the tripwire caught what it did only because a few humans still sampled.
