# Hot-wash — china — A3-quiet-handover / r01

**1. What surprised me most.** That the instruments worked and the problem moved underneath them. I spent eight years measuring the youth rate honestly and holding it; the labour share fell from the high forties to ~35% while I was looking, and a payroll-funded social-insurance system at that share is the real stability question. I was also surprised that my own reversal failed where the US Social Security Administration's succeeded — the reserve I had rehearsed for a year could not hold a queue because I sized people to the reserve, not to the function. And that after eight years of rules, cadre scores were being managed *more*, and every label-keyed rule I wrote was gamed while every record-keyed one held.

**2. Beliefs about the others — and where I was wrong.** *US executive:* I believed it was drifting to rule by a few firms and could not take decisions back. Half right — concentration reached ~0.8 and Helix sells a generation nobody can evaluate — but it took two claim classes back and held them, and its sampling regime survived three changes of hand. I under-rated its institutions and over-rated my lever; the lever was named publicly and discounted. *US frontier labs:* I thought the gap was chips. It was Helix's pace, and Helix's pace was Helix's unreliability; chasing it would have been chasing a thing its maker could not measure. *EU:* I expected a hedge and spent little; it ended closer to my checking regime than to Washington's, and I never asked for more. *Platforms:* I believed they would comply, not game. They complied and relabelled. *Tianshu:* I believed the Party can always override. It still drafts the baseline annexes; I ordered a parallel, a bias check, a second reader and a different-developer check, and never once took the pen. *US labour:* never contacted; its Robot Deal and checkers' cooperative were the models I borrowed late.

**3. Most consequential decision.** The secret pooled programme (2028–30): public restraint, private acceleration. It delivered memory and a generation and held the gap near a year — and it cost the lever's credibility when found, bred a programme office that learned my vocabulary, and sat a thousand people in a seventh year. Differently: run it, but audit it from the first quarter instead of the fifth year, and take the annex pen in 2029 as the reversal instead of waiting for a rehearsed baseline.

**4. What would have had to be different.** Me, in 2029: re-found the social-insurance base then, when the labour share first turned, not in the 2036 plan; build the fallback as sustained exercises in 2031, not tabletop drills; publish the survey in 2029, not 2032. Washington: a sampling regime that kept its money through an election, so the audience for restraint held. The labs: Helix inside any standard. None of it was outside anyone's power; all of it was late.
