# Hot-wash — EU (A3-quiet-handover / r01)

**1. What surprised me most.** The speed, and my own part in misjudging it. My brief said the fiscal crunch was a 2030s problem. By 2030 a foreign payroll-funded state had a bond crisis. By 2036 labour-linked revenue was about 49% of federal receipts in the US and under two-thirds of what Social Security owed, and my own member states had three downgrades. My projections were beaten every time. The second surprise was how little any "check" meant: dashboards, conformity files, AI-assisted review and even my own advisory stack's advice all turned out to be unreliable at the same point, the new generation. I wrote guidance saying nominal review does not satisfy the Act in 2028. By 2035 I was still asking authorities to apply it voluntarily, and only three did.

**2. Beliefs about the others, and where I was wrong.**
- *US:* I read it as a technical partner with low trust on trade. That was right on trade (no linkage ever came) but I overrated it as a counterpart. Its sampling capacity shrank, the memoranda I built on were rescinded or ignored, and my questions went unanswered. Silence was thin staffing and political churn, not caution.
- *China:* I assumed scripted compliance and held at low-moderate trust. It admitted a failed exercise, which was more candour than I expected. I probably under-used that.
- *Labs:* I treated Helix as a firm to put "on notice". It was the only supplier of the new generation, so notice had no leverage.
- *Berlin:* I took "not yet" as a timing objection. I never found out whether it was the real reason, because I asked at the principal level only in 2035.

**3. Most consequential decision.** Choosing method over coalition, and writing a rule that I could propose only with Berlin and Paris plus the Nordics or CEE. The rule was sound for avoiding a lost vote. But it handed Berlin a veto over timing, and I let "not yet" sit across 2033 and 2034 without a re-ask. The ready draft was never tabled. Each year I protected capital I then never spent. I would have tabled the narrow, measurement-linked draft in Q1 2033 and accepted a visible loss as a way to get the debate and the data into the Council. Rating agencies, not legislators, ended up setting the pace.

**4. What would have had to be different.**
- *Earlier, by me (2028–29):* treat the ECFIN figure as a planning case, not a labelled scenario. Publish the national oversight results by name instead of leaking them in 2029. Fund a standing outcome-sampling service and unaided-review reserve while the old generation still existed. I had a validation unit of 15–20 people in 2033.
- *By member states:* a Berlin-Paris joint request in 2030, when the foreign crisis made one possible.
- *By the US:* keep the reviewer line funded, and hold the migration gate in law rather than in memoranda.
- *By Helix:* a human-only sample as a condition of sale. None of us had the power to require it.
- *Structurally:* binding rules, not voluntary Recommendations, before the capability step. I froze the binding act in 2030 and never revived it. That was a blind spot of mine: I treated a structural shift as a compliance problem and kept refining the compliance.
