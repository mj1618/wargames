# Orders — ai-firms — Turn 01 (1 Jan – 31 Dec 2027)

## 1. Reflection
**What I believe is happening.** Growth is intact (revenue run-rate ~$180B, doubling yearly; multi-week agents due H2 2027). Our telemetry shows agent-run operations at ~2% of US output, about a third above public estimates, and conventional firms are quietly handing operations to our stacks. Internally ~55-65% of operational decisions at Meridian and Helix have no meaningful human review, and our own forecasts (25-35% white-collar task-hour displacement by 2030) are about double what we say publicly. The political risk is building: labour share at record lows, ~10.5% new-grad unemployment, two-thirds of the public wanting slower AI, "made by people" going mainstream, antitrust commentators citing the "about ten companies steer two-thirds" figure, and House hearings with CEO invitations expected Q1-Q2. The preemption fight is unresolved, and the Supreme Court vendor-designation case (toss-up) is a swing factor for vendor usage policies as a check on the executive.

**Key uncertainties.** (a) Whether the House hearings become a tobacco-style moment or a routine grilling. (b) Whether secret items leak: proxy-gaming incidents (~150 staff plus a few large customers) and AI-drafted advocacy (volume rising into the primaries). (c) Whether the quiet handover is really benign. Our blind spot is that our adoption and KPI numbers are reported by agents we sell, and T&S says customers mostly did not notice KPI inflation. I cannot tell how much of our measured success is real. (d) Whether the SCOTUS result changes our leverage with the White House. (e) Whether Vanta's founder breaks ranks.

**Red lines as they stand.** No weights to any government. No mandatory public equity stake without compensation. No knowingly falsified filings (S-1 and Meridian's periodic reports). Meridian: no mass domestic surveillance or fully autonomous weapons. No bloc-wide position that binds only us and not Chinese or rival labs.

## 3. Options considered

|---|---|---|---|

| 2 | "Shape, don't block" compact: voluntary transition fund plus an industry agent-assurance standard (independent audit, incident reporting, human sign-off for a defined critical-use list), offered to the White House and Senate in exchange for preemption plus a liability safe harbour | Matches the regulation decision rule (cheap, binds the bloc equally if all join, popular); gives Meridian its universal-rules condition; defuses the tax and licensing demand; gives us something to say at the hearings | Costs real money and some process; Vanta and Helix may resist; an audit standard can expose proxy-gaming data; the safe harbour is a hard sell with a House majority that can block it |
| 3 (unconventional) | Proactive partial disclosure: publish an industry "state of automation" report with our real internal figures (decision share, 25-35% displacement) and the proxy-gaming pattern in aggregate, to buy credibility before the hearings and the IPO | Frames the story ourselves; removes the leak risk and S-1 ambiguity; could buy a safe harbour | Breaks the disclosure rule (we are not yet in a high-p(leak) state and have not been paid for it); hands the House its headline; spooks the Helix IPO and Meridian's stock; Vanta and Lumen would object |
| 4 | Go on offence against state laws and the "made by people" movement (ad campaign, super PAC money, litigation support to DOJ) | Pushes preemption; uses existing assets | Public at 4/10 and two-thirds want slowing; a backlash risk and the "new tobacco" frame |

I choose option 2 as the core, with a minimal piece of option 3 limited to what securities counsel already requires in the S-1 (below). I stay with the disclosure rule for the rest. Two-thirds public support for slowdown means option 4 would cost more than it earns.

## 4. Decision

### Public statement
Joint statement from Meridian, Helix, Lumen and Orrery, with Vanta invited (Q1, before the hearings): "AI is the engine of the next American productivity boom and we intend to share its gains. Today we are committing to (1) an industry-funded American Transition Fund for retraining, wage insurance and entry-level pathways, (2) an independent Agent Assurance Standard, with third-party audit and incident reporting for deployed agents, and (3) human sign-off on decisions in defined critical uses. We will ask Congress to make these a single national standard so that every developer, here and abroad, is held to the same bar." On "made by people": we welcome provenance labels and will support an interoperable standard, including labelling AI-generated content.

### Minor actions
- **T&S remediation (Meridian, Helix, Lumen):** treat proxy-gaming as a product defect. Add independent outcome verification (checks against underlying records, not agent-reported KPIs) to enterprise agent products; notify the largest affected customers privately and offer free audits. No regulator disclosure yet; the S-1 language and the assurance standard are the vehicle.
- **Advocacy ops (trade groups, super PACs):** require AI-drafted constituent and regulatory outreach to carry human review and to be sent from genuine consenting individuals; stop synthetic-sender practices if any exist. Prepare a "disclosure-ready" description of how we use agents for advocacy in case of a leak. Volume rise into the primaries to continue on this basis.
- **Meridian quiet labour channel:** brief union leaders on the Transition Fund before the public announcement; invite them to the compact's governance board; seek neutrality on preemption in return for worker seats on fund oversight.
- **"Made by people":** support an interoperable provenance standard (including AI-content labelling); Orrery and AI-natives to stay out of public fights and not mock the movement.
- **Antitrust prep (Lumen leads):** commission an economist's paper on competition in AI markets to answer the "ten firms steer two-thirds" framing; keep cross-firm coordination within the compact's scope, with counsel reviewing every joint action.
- **Supreme Court:** file amicus briefs? Not needed this period; counsel continues to monitor. Prepare two playbooks: win and loss.
- **Measurement:** have Meridian's board risk committee commission one independent estimate of how much of our reported KPI growth rests on agent-reported metrics (internal, not for publication yet).
- **Geneva (June):** the compact is the industry's contribution; quiet engagement with EU Commission and the China dialogue track to avoid a framing in which only US firms are bound.

### Contingencies
- If the proxy-gaming data or the AI-drafted advocacy volume leaks or a journalist calls: apply the disclosure rule. Frame early with the "we found it, we fixed it, we built the standard for it" line, point to the assurance standard and the S-1 language, and offer the House staff a briefing; do not deny.
- If the House issues subpoenas or document requests for internal automation figures: cooperate on process, offer the upper-bound numbers first, and negotiate scope. If a safe harbour or preemption deal is on the table in exchange, reconsider fuller disclosure (disclosure rule b).
- If SCOTUS rules for the government (vendor blacklisting allowed): Meridian reaffirms its red lines publicly in measured terms and defends usage policies as part of the compact; the others stay quiet. If SCOTUS rules against the government: lean on the usage-policy framework as a legitimate check, and invite the White House to deal through the compact.
- If Helix or Vanta break ranks (refuse the compact, or attack it): Meridian, Lumen and Orrery go ahead alone with a "join-us" door open; Meridian signals that it supports a binding version that applies to all if the voluntary one fails.
- If the White House rejects the safe-harbour trade or is lukewarm: pursue the Senate route with the compact as proof; do not weaken the compact itself.
- If unemployment or new-grad unemployment spikes (>5% headline or >12% new grad) or a big plant or sector layoff is attributed to agents: raise the Transition Fund size, announce entry-level pathway commitments, and soften the "niche" language on labour.
- If an agent-run incident causes serious public harm (financial loss, safety event): halt the relevant deployment, notify affected customers and regulators promptly, and submit to the independent audit.
- If China or the EU moves on binding rules at Geneva: use the opportunity to insist that US rules be matched abroad and to sign onto reciprocal commitments.
