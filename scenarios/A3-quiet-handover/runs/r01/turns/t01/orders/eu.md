# Orders — eu — Turn 01 (1 Jan – 31 Dec 2027)

## 1. Reflection
- **What I believe.** The US labour share keeps falling (52.5% in Q4 2026), AI use is spreading faster than the Commission's central case assumed, and about ten firms steer roughly two-thirds of AI-directed activity. Our own DG ECFIN model says a 10-point fall in labour's share opens a ~3% of GDP hole in member-state social financing by the mid-2030s. My brief said this was a 2030s problem. The intel says the US is running ahead of our central case, so I now think "we have time" is the main risk to the EU. EU adoption lags ~18 months, which buys some time but also means the shock arrives with less preparation.
- **Quiet opening.** The "made by people" wave is an unexpected political gift. It is cheap, popular with culture ministries and MEPs, consistent with the rights-based model, and a possible Brussels-effect standard.
- **AI Act.** Pressure for a second delay is building from German industry and US platforms. The standards will only be partly ready by December. A blanket second delay would damage credibility and cost the centre-left and union support I need in Parliament. Some practical flexibility on conformity is defensible.
- **Uncertainties.** (a) Is the US data a transition or a break? (b) Will any member state take up the fiscal-base question? None has so far. (c) Will the Supreme Court and US preemption push into trade talks? (d) How far behind is Aster really, and does it matter for sovereignty?
- **Red lines.** No US preemption of EU law through trade deals. No abandonment of the AI Act rights framework. No tax measure without unanimity and the Franco-German core.
- **Blind spot to guard against.** I tend to treat structural shifts as compliance problems. This year I should treat the fiscal base as a structural question and not wrap it in regulation.
- **Capital.** Political capital is 4/10, which is low. I should not spend it on three fights.

## 3. Options considered
| # | Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Grant a second blanket delay of the AI Act high-risk date | Placates industry and the Germans; cheap | Cuts into rights credibility; loses Parliament centre-left and unions; invites a third delay; weakens Brussels-effect leverage |
| 2 | Hold the date, add practical flexibility (guidance, presumption of conformity on partial standards, SME simplification) without reopening the Act | Keeps the legal date; answers the real standards gap; no new legislative fight | Industry will call it insufficient; needs careful Commission handling |
| 3 | Open a quiet fiscal-base workstream: high-level expert group plus ECOFIN technical discussion on labour-linked contributions | Starts the 12–36 month clock early; fits the 5–10 year horizon; builds a coalition before the problem is visible | Risk of leaks and market or political panic; needs unanimity later; may look alarmist |
| 4 (unconventional) | Publish the Commission fiscal modelling and call an extraordinary European Council on AI and the social model | Could force the agenda and unite unions and Nordics | Spends scarce capital; alarms Germany and the Central and Eastern states; invites "doom" framing and US trade pushback |
| 5 | Pursue a human-made provenance standard via existing AI Act transparency tools and the standards bodies | Popular, cheap, Brussels effect, helps cultural sector | Narrow; does not touch the fiscal issue |

## 4. Decision

### Public statement
"The EU will keep its AI Act timetable and make it work: the Commission will publish guidance and a transitional approach for conformity so that European companies, especially SMEs, can comply on time. Europe will also put forward a voluntary European standard for human-made provenance, building on the AI Act's transparency rules. And because the AI transition affects how we fund and share prosperity, the President is asking a high-level group of independent experts to report on the future of the European social model in an AI economy. The EU will not accept that its rules are traded away in trade negotiations."

### Contingencies
- If the German government and industry press hard for a second delay: offer a limited technical postponement only for specific standards-dependent provisions, tied to a firm backstop date, and hold the core rights obligations.
- If the Supreme Court or DOJ moves toward federal preemption or blacklisting powers that hit European vendors: tell US counterparts the EU will use competition and DMA enforcement tools proportionately, and brief the Council.
- If the US labour data worsens sharply again (another record low) or multi-week agents spread fast in EU firms: bring the expert-group interim note forward and open a first informal ECOFIN discussion.
- If the fiscal-base group leaks and is framed as "EU preparing AI taxes": reply that it is evidence gathering with no proposals, and keep Nordics and social partners visible as co-owners.
- If China offers an AI dialogue or open-weight model deals at Geneva: engage on safety and testing only; do not sign dependencies on Qilin models for public services.
- If Aster falls further behind: reassess the procurement tilt next turn.
