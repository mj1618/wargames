# Orders — labour — Turn 01 (1 January 2027 → 31 December 2027)

## 1. Reflection

**What we think is happening.** The numbers in the public record read, to us, like the start of a break rather than a transition: labour share at 52.5% and falling every quarter, graduate unemployment at 10.5%, entry-level white-collar postings down ~70%, productivity at a twenty-year high. Headline unemployment at 4.3% is the number the White House and the labs will cite; the share and the entry-level collapse are the numbers that describe our members' future. Our organisers' Q1 read (intel §4) confirms the brief's suspicion: leverage is holding in health, education, construction, transport/logistics and public services, and draining in office/admin, call centres, back-office finance and parts of media. In the AI-native firms there is nobody to organise at all; Orrery does $34B with fewer than 400 people. About ten companies steer two-thirds of AI-directed economic activity.

**Three things make this year unusual.**
1. **The Helix IPO (H1)** puts ~$6T of public-pension money on the table at the one moment a frontier lab actually needs something from the people whose jobs it is automating. Trustees allied with us are open to attaching conditions. This is the strongest, most time-limited lever we have.
2. **"Made by people" went mainstream** without us. It proves consumers will pay a premium for human work, which is a lever no employer and no Congress controls. The creative guilds have a window; it will close when the labs' "a niche, and a welcome one" becomes true.
3. **Washington is divided and the 2028 cycle has begun.** The House majority wants our witnesses; populists in both parties want a "tech oligarch" enemy. Nothing will pass this year, but the markers we plant now define what a 2029 Congress can pass.

**Key uncertainties.** Whether the Supreme Court ruling (by June) widens federal power enough to make preemption of state AI law more likely — which would knock out our ballot-initiative rung. Whether the Helix contact is genuine, a plant, or about to be caught; a plant timed to blow up during the House hearings would cost us the hearings and our credibility. Whether the AI-native share (~1.5% of output) is doubling annually or merely growing. And something we don't understand yet: 25% of adults now take their major decisions on an AI assistant's advice. That is a question about *voice*, not jobs, and it is not clear which employer, if any, is behind it.

**Red lines as they stand.** No two-tier deals that abandon new entrants. No campaign against datacenter permits (the building trades have told us privately they will walk). No endorsement of violence or sabotage against AI infrastructure — we condemn it on sight. No big strike we could lose. No political strike.

**Leverage-first check.** Where we are strong — nurses, teachers, transport, logistics, public services — contracts are landing. Where we are weak — office and creative — we have a consumer market and a shareholder vote. This year we spend the strong sectors' bargaining power and the pensions' capital to win *terms that are written to cover everyone*, including the weak sectors and the people who have not been hired yet.

## 3. Options considered

| # | Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Fiduciary conditions on the Helix IPO + first Meridian proxy season** (rung 3) via allied public-pension trustees: allocation conditioned on workforce-displacement disclosure, an autonomous-operations disclosure, a human-review commitment on decisions about workers, and a worker-voice mechanism | Uses a unique resource at the single moment Helix needs the capital; time-boxed to H1; fiduciary framing (displacement and autonomy are material risks) gives trustees cover; even partial wins set a disclosure standard every lab gets measured against | Resolutions are advisory; trustees are not ours to command; Helix can fill the book elsewhere; could be read as politicising pensions and hand the White House a line |
| 2 | **"Shared Gains" coordinated bargaining platform** in the holding sectors (rung 2): common AI terms across health, education, transport/logistics and public-sector contracts expiring 2027–28, with the creative guilds bargaining a "made by people" line | Spends leverage where it is strongest; pattern-setting; wins are real and enforceable; unites affiliates; longshore-M&M model (accept the tools, take a share, protect the jobs) is one our members understand | Mandatory-subject fights over whether AI deployment is bargainable; public-sector affiliates can't strike in many states; costs money; terms cover members, not the unorganised, unless written deliberately |
| 3 (unconventional) | **Union-owned "Made by People" certification mark**, licensed to studios, platforms, publishers and retailers, open to non-union creators and small businesses as members-of-the-mark | A consumer-side lever that needs no employer and no Congress; revenue; recruits crafts, small business, faith groups, parents; makes the guilds the standard-setter before the platforms write their own | Small in dollar terms; could become a boutique niche exactly as the labs say; trademark and antitrust complications; risks the coalition looking like it is defending artisanship rather than livelihoods |
| 4 | **File 2028 ballot initiatives now** in 8–10 states (rung 4): a "Worker AI Rights Act" (notice, human review of AI decisions on hiring/firing/discipline, displacement levy) | Locks protections while state law still stands; mobilises for 2028 | Preemption unresolved — a June ruling could gut it; moves up the ladder before lower rungs have stalled; expensive (~$50–150M per cycle) |
| 5 | **Formalise the ~1,100 lab-employee network** as a frontier workers' association with a legal-defence fund, and front it at the hearings | Gives us a voice inside the labs; whistleblower protection is popular across parties | Exposes signatories; the Helix contact risk gets bigger, not smaller; small numbers |
| 6 | **Coordinated strike in health/education** (rung 5) | Maximum pressure | We could lose it; illegal for many public-sector affiliates; no stalled lower rung to justify it; splits the coalition |

**Decision logic.** Options 1 and 2 are the two majors: they are the two places we are strongest, they come due this year, and each one's terms are drafted to cover the weak sectors and new entrants. Option 3 is cheap enough to run as a minor action by the creative affiliates and consumer groups; if it takes, it becomes a major next year. Option 4 is prepared, not fired — we draft, poll and file only in the states where 2028 qualification requires a 2027 filing, and we wait for the Court. Option 5 is handled quietly (legal defence, no formalisation). Option 6 is not on the table.

## 4. Decision

### Public statement

*Issued by the federation president on behalf of the coalition, January 2027; repeated in hearing testimony.*

"Labour's share of what this country produces has fallen to the lowest level ever recorded, while productivity is growing faster than at any time in twenty years. Someone is getting the gains. It is not the nurse whose shifts are now set by software, the graduate who can't get a first job, or the call-centre worker being scored by a machine. This coalition is not against the tools. We are for the people. This year we will bargain for a share of the gains in every contract we touch, we will ask the people who manage America's retirement savings to demand honest answers from the companies they are being asked to buy, and we will make sure that when a machine makes a decision about a human being's livelihood, a human being is accountable for it. And we will say it plainly to the companies now listing on the public markets: tell the public what you really forecast for American jobs."

### Minor actions

- **House hearings (public).** Provide witnesses on request from the House majority: a nurse, a teacher, a call-centre worker scored by software, an unemployed 2026 graduate, a transit worker, and one signatory of the "Pacing the Frontier" letter (chosen by the network, with counsel). Ask the committee to call the CEOs of Helix, Meridian and Orrery. Research department's "Displacement Ledger" is submitted as the coalition's written evidence. Meet publicly with populist members of both parties in the Senate to seek bipartisan co-sponsors for a marker "Worker AI Rights Act" (notice, human review, disclosure) — public meetings, no endorsements.
- **"Made by People" certification mark (public; creative guilds + consumer and faith groups).** Register the mark, write a standard, license it to the retailers, platforms and publishers already using voluntary labels, and open membership-of-the-mark to non-union creators, crafts and small businesses. Budget ~$10M. Review at year-end for promotion to a major action.
- **Ballot-initiative preparation (public/low-key).** Draft the model "Worker AI Rights Act"; poll in ten states; file in only the two or three where 2028 qualification requires a 2027 filing. Hold the rest until after the Supreme Court ruling.
- **Building-trades understanding (private within the coalition).** The federation takes no position on datacenter or power permits. In return the building trades sign on to the Shared Gains platform and press for community-benefit agreements (local hire, prevailing wage, a community fund) on every datacenter project — which gives the rest of the coalition something to say about datacenters that isn't "no".
- **Helix contact (SECRET: the six leaders, plus outside counsel).** We do not accept documents, do not meet, and do not reply through the intermediary. We instruct independent whistleblower counsel to make themselves available to the person through a lawful channel, with the counsel — not the coalition — deciding whether anything is genuine and whether it belongs with a regulator or the House committee. Nothing the contact provides enters our campaign unless it has come through counsel and a lawful disclosure route. Rationale: if the person is a plant, the trap closes on counsel, not on the hearings; if they are genuine, they are better protected.
- **Lab-employee network (quiet).** Set up a legal-defence fund for signatories; no formal association this year.
- **Voice research (internal).** Commission a study of what members' AI assistants are telling them about work, politics and the coalition itself, given 25% of adults now treat an assistant as their primary adviser. We don't know who, if anyone, is behind that, and we need to.
- **2028 cycle.** Candidate questionnaire built around Shared Gains and Honest Answers; no primary endorsements before the Court rules and the hearings have run.

### Contingencies

- **If Helix's book fills without our conditions and allied trustees split** → publish the trustee votes and the refusal, and move the ballot-initiative rung forward: file in all ten states in Q4 regardless of the Court, framed as "the companies wouldn't answer, so the voters will ask".
- **If Meridian settles** → take the settlement public as the industry standard and present it to Helix's bookrunners as the floor.
- **If the Supreme Court ruling makes preemption of state AI law substantially more likely** → stop ballot filings; shift the political-spending plan toward a federal bill with Senate populist co-sponsors; make preemption itself the 2028 issue ("Washington took your state's protections away").
- **If the Helix contact's material reaches counsel and checks out** → it goes to the House committee or a regulator via counsel under whistleblower protection; the coalition does not touch or cite it until it is in the public record. **If it is a plant** → counsel discloses the approach to the committee first, before anyone else can.
- **If an AI firm or employer offers a single affiliate a sector deal that protects incumbents and abandons new hires** → the federation refuses to back it and says why; exception only if the affiliate's sector is collapsing, in which case we negotiate a transition fund rather than a two-tier clause.
- **If a health or education affiliate reaches a strike over AI terms** → full strike-fund and public backing for that affiliate; no coalition-wide escalation.
- **If there is sabotage or violence against datacenters, grids or AI firms** → immediate public condemnation by the federation president, and a reminder that the building trades are the people who built those sites.
- **If "reliable multi-week agents" arrive and the office/admin sectors start losing members at scale** → fast-track the training-and-transition fund plank and open a direct-membership category for displaced white-collar workers so they stay in the coalition when they leave the employer.
