# Orders — us-gov — Turn 01 (1 January – 31 December 2027)

## 1. Reflection

**What I believe is happening.**
- The aggregates look fine and our instinct is to trust them: unemployment steady at ~4.3%, productivity growth the best in twenty years (~3.1%), US frontier ~6 months ahead of China (IC, classified). On the metrics the White House watches, the AI agenda is working.
- The distributional numbers are getting worse every quarter: labour share at a record low of 52.5% (Q4 2026, BLS), new-graduate unemployment ~10.5%, entry-level postings down ~70%. Ten firms steer about two-thirds of AI-directed activity (public record). We do not know whether this is a transition or a structural break, and we are institutionally biased toward "transition" (the China-shock blind spot). I'm writing that down so future-me doesn't forget it.
- **Fiscal exposure.** Treasury/OMB's unpublished analysis puts Social Security depletion at ~2031–32 if the labour-share trend continues, against ~2034 officially. Ways and Means majority staff are now asking for payroll-receipt projections ahead of the hearings. This will not stay inside a 40-person circle through 2027. If it surfaces as a leak during the hearings, the story becomes "the administration hid it", and that is the worst way for it to come out.
- **Government's own control of AI.** Agency leaders know human review of AI benefits pre-determinations is often a click taking under a minute. The IG draft lands around year-end, with publication early 2028, in the middle of the primaries. A finding that "humans are not really deciding" is exactly the "lost control of AI" loss condition. We can still get ahead of it. We cannot change the audit's findings, and we must not try.
- **Politics.** Divided government. The accelerationists want preemption, and the House has said it will not pass preemption without worker protections. That is the outline of a deal: each side holds what the other wants. Populists in both parties have a cultural hook ("made by people") and are hammering "tech oligarchs". AI-industry super PACs are active in the primaries. A bill of this size probably will not clear the Senate filibuster this year, but it can move.
- **External.** SCOTUS vendor-designation case: a toss-up. Party Congress: AI+ acceleration likely, stability-first possible. Geneva summit in June. EU high-risk obligations from December.

**Key uncertainties.** Transition vs. structural break; how widespread rubber-stamp review is across government (nobody has measured it); the SCOTUS outcome; whether Beijing tilts to stability (which would change the China-race arithmetic); how far our own agency AI advisory systems are shaping what we see. They are "usually good on the metrics we track", but our metrics are the aggregates.

**Red lines (unchanged).** No outright nationalisation of labs; nothing that visibly hands China the lead; no default. White House: no federal compute tax or deployment licensing. House: no preemption without worker protections. NatSec: no unverified unilateral slowdown.

## 3. Options considered

| # | Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Clean federal preemption bill + datacenter/power permitting push** (accelerationists' preference) | Delivers the WH agenda; good for growth and the China race; labs back it | Dead in the House (red line); feeds the "tech oligarch" attack; super-PAC optics; no answer on workers or payroll base |
| 2 | **Grand bargain: preemption paired with a worker title** (adjustment assistance and wage insurance, expanded layoff notice, entry-level hiring credit, human-review standards for rights-impacting AI) | Matches each side's stated price; both parties can claim a win going into 2028; DOJ litigation is stalling anyway; positions the institution as in control | Pay-fors are hard (no compute tax); 60 Senate votes are hard; progressives may hold out for more; labs may lobby to water it down |
| 3 | **Executive "Accountable Automation" directive:** measure and set minimum standards for human review of AI-assisted determinations across agencies before the IG reports | Executive-only, fast; gets ahead of the IG; bipartisan appeal (populists and House accountability wing); reduces the "lost control" risk | Will surface uncomfortable data that may leak; costs agency throughput and slows automation savings; accelerationists grumble |
| 4 | **Controlled disclosure of the payroll-tax risk:** confidential briefing to Ways and Means and Senate Finance leaders; SSA actuaries add a labour-share sensitivity to the Trustees process; bipartisan fiscal working group | Pre-empts a damaging leak; fiscal hawks' priority; builds the case for a worker/fiscal title | WH fears a "Social Security insolvency moved up" headline in a pre-election year; opponents could weaponise it |
| 5 (unconventional) | **"American AI Dividend" / national prosperity fund:** take federal equity or warrants in frontier labs and AI-native firms in exchange for permitting, power and federal-land datacenter access, paid into a fund tied to Social Security (Alaska Permanent Fund analogy) | Spreads the AI gains without a compute tax; links AI growth to the payroll-base problem; could split the populist coalition our way | Looks like creeping nationalisation to business conservatives; hard to legislate; labs resist; legally untested at scale. Too early this turn, but worth floating as a study |
| 6 | Hold off; await the IG, SCOTUS and Party Congress | Cheap; matches the incremental default | Cedes the narrative to the House and the populists; the leak and the IG land on us unprepared |

**Decision logic.** Option 2 is the only path to the WH's top legislative goal that respects the House's stated red line, so it is the major legislative play. Option 3 is cheap, executive-only and protects against our biggest reputational tail risk, so it is the second major action. Option 4 goes in as a minor action in its quiet form: a confidential briefing plus a technical actuarial sensitivity, not a press release. Option 5 is floated only as a Treasury study request, so the idea exists before any crisis. Option 1's permitting component continues under existing executive authority as a minor action.

## 4. Decision

### Public statement
"America is winning the AI race, and we intend to make sure American workers win with it. This year the Administration will work with both parties in Congress on a single national framework for AI: one rulebook instead of fifty, paired with real support for workers whose jobs are changing: wage insurance, retraining that works, fair notice before AI-driven layoffs, and a new on-ramp for young people entering the workforce. The federal government will also hold itself to the standard it asks of others: when a federal agency uses AI to help decide someone's benefits, a human being must genuinely decide, and we will measure that, not assume it. We welcome the House hearings on AI and the American worker and encourage industry leaders to take part. And we will keep building the energy and computing infrastructure that keeps America ahead."

### Minor actions
- **Treasury: controlled disclosure of the payroll-tax risk (fiscal hawks, with WH sign-off; executive).** Treasury gives Ways and Means and Senate Finance chairs and ranking members a confidential briefing on payroll-receipt projections, including the labour-share downside as a *sensitivity scenario* (not a forecast). Treasury and OMB ask the SSA Chief Actuary to include a labour-share sensitivity in the Trustees' process. A small Treasury/OMB/SSA/CEA working group refines the analysis, and BLS/BEA inputs are added. SECRET (principals, plus the four committee leaders and their senior staff): the 2031–32 figure. Ways and Means staff get the standard projections plus the sensitivity methodology. We accept the leak risk as better than being caught hiding it.
- **Statistics (Commerce/BEA, Labor/BLS; executive):** direct development of experimental series on AI-native firm output, labour share by sector, and entry-level/new-graduate hiring, with first releases by Q4. This corrects our own blind spot toward aggregates.
- **Treasury study (NEC/Treasury; executive):** a closed-door options paper on an "American AI Dividend" / national prosperity fund (equity or warrants in exchange for federal permitting, land and power access; Alaska Permanent Fund model) and on long-run payroll-base reforms. Not announced.
- **Energy and datacenter build-out (WH accelerationists; executive):** continue expedited permitting for datacenters and generation on federal and DOE sites under existing authorities; DOE national-lab compute partnerships. PUBLIC.
- **Export controls (Commerce/BIS, national-security establishment; executive):** maintain frontier-chip controls; review robotics and advanced-manufacturing supply-chain exposure to China. No relaxation ahead of the Party Congress.
- **Geneva summit (State/OSTP):** US delegation backs incident reporting, frontier transparency and provenance labelling; rejects binding compute caps or slowdowns that aren't verifiable for China. Supports voluntary "human-made" labelling, and the FTC issues guidance that "made by people" claims must be truthful. That gives populists something at no cost to labs.
- **Hearings:** the WH does not discourage lab CEOs from testifying before the House; the Administration offers a Labor and Treasury witness, who testifies to aggregate strength and the new measurement agenda.
- **SCOTUS:** DOJ prepares implementation guidance for either outcome (see contingencies).

### Contingencies
- **If SCOTUS upholds vendor designation:** use it sparingly; DOJ issues narrow guidance, and the WH avoids blacklisting threats against labs during the bill negotiation, since we need their support. **If SCOTUS strikes it down:** accept it publicly; push the procurement-terms route (federal contracts require auditability and permitted uses) and consider a narrow statutory fix inside Title I.
- **If the Treasury payroll analysis leaks:** Treasury publishes the sensitivity analysis immediately with methodology, framed as "prudent planning, not a forecast", and the WH points to Title II and the bipartisan fiscal working group. Do not deny it.
- **If the IG draft arrives early or finds pervasive rubber-stamping:** publish OMB's own measured metrics and remediation plan the same week; accept the findings; ask Congress to fund reviewer capacity. Do not dispute the IG.
- **If the Accountable Automation measurement shows review is widely nominal:** pause *adverse* fully pre-determined decisions in the worst-performing offices pending second review. Do not halt AI use; growth and service levels matter.
- **If the Party Congress tilts to "stability-first" or slows AI+:** the national-security establishment re-assesses; no US slowdown without verification, but use the working group to explore reciprocal incident-reporting commitments.
- **If the bill stalls in the Senate (likely):** keep Title III's federal-use standards via executive action, and keep Titles I–II as the 2028 platform; consider moving Title II pieces in appropriations.
- **If a major AI incident occurs (infrastructure, financial or agentic):** invoke the hotline if cross-border; convene principals; use the moment to move Title I's federal incident-reporting standard as a stand-alone bill.
- **If labs lobby to strip Title II:** the WH publicly reaffirms that it will only sign the full package, and populist-nationalists are given room to attack holdouts.
