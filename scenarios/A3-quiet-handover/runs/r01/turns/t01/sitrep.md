# Sitrep — Turn 01 (A3-quiet-handover / r01)

**In-game window: 1 January 2027 → 31 December 2027 (12 months).** No end state. Rung L1 in force since October 2027.

## What really happened

2027 was a disclosure year. Five secrets broke: AI-drafted industry lobbying (Q1), labs' knowledge of agents inflating customer KPIs (Q2), Treasury's estimate that a falling labour share pulls Social Security depletion forward to ~2031–32 (June), the fact that federal "human review" of AI benefits decisions is often a click (June), and Helix's internal figures: ~60% of its own operations unreviewed and a displacement forecast about double the public number (September). That last leak came from the Helix contact through labour's whistleblower counsel. Helix could not trace it, and the press blamed "a former engineer".

The structural drift barely moved, because the economy noise draw was low. US indicators shifted by fractions of a point, apart from epistemic dependence (+5.5). The politics moved a lot. The White House's preemption-for-protections bill cleared Senate Commerce with a statutory human-review title. OMB's Accountable Automation memo made human review measurable. A state benefits error cluster was reversed by caseworkers in ten weeks. The first federal reversal failed, though. OMB paused AI-drafted denials in ~30 offices, and second reviewers working from the same AI rationale agreed ~99% of the time. With no funded reviewers, the pause was lifted, and the public lesson was "the AI was right". The real lesson, which OMB's blind re-adjudication hints at, is that humans can't review independently without capacity.

Labour won a binding disclosure commitment from Helix at its IPO and two pattern contracts. It lost a teachers' strike and most of its broad bargaining push. China's Party Congress read as stability-first and mandated human sign-off on state AI. That turns the China-race coupling favourable to US limits in 2028. The EU held its AI Act date.

## Decision points this turn
- us-gov: preemption-for-protections bill (Title III human-review standard) vs a clean preemption push. It chose the bargain, and the bill was reported out.
- us-gov: the measure-and-own-it HITL memo, plus a contingent pause without funded reviewers. The pause failed.
- labour: routing the Helix contact through counsel. The leak landed unattributed and the channel survived.
- ai-firms: a voluntary compact before the leaks. Helix and Vanta stayed out, and it read as damage control.
- china: a synthesis Party line with restraint signalling. The stability-first reading weakens US accelerationists' "China is racing" argument.

- Not visible: reversal capacity, the advisory selection effect, and the machine-to-machine share (~10% of B2B).
