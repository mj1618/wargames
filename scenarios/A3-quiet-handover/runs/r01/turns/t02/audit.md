## Findings

**F1 — major — The November trifecta rests on two Control-added election terms, only one of which is logged as decisive.**
Ground truth §10 gives elections "base 0.5 each side, ±0.15 economic conditions, ±0.1 actor campaign moves". Control added "open-seat fatigue −0.05" (presidential; not decisive), a −0.1/+0.1 presidential-coattail term (Senate and House) and a 0.55 House-incumbency base. Decisiveness: presidential FAIL at 0.40 holds at 0.5 (not decisive). Senate: r 0.4195 flips only because of the −0.1 coattail (Control flagged this). House: r 0.5018 would **fail at the rules' 0.5 base with no coattail**; it passes on either the 0.55 base or the coattail alone, so it is decisive in combination and was not flagged. The modelling is defensible (coattails are real; the Red Cell did not object), but the single most consequential branch of the run (unified government, WH red line lapsing) now depends on terms that are not in ground truth and were invented at roll time.
*Fix:* (a) No re-roll (the probability did not change materially). (b) Write an election rule into ground truth now, before 2030: coattail ±0.1 on congressional rolls correlated with the presidential result; House/Senate incumbency base and how it is set; what "economic conditions" maps to. (c) Annotate the House line "decisive in combination (would fail at 0.5 with no coattail)". (d) Recommend to the orchestrator that the Senate roll be recorded as a fork candidate (R holds 50–50 → no trifecta) for the counterfactual pass.

**F2 — minor — Actor-ordered internal decisions of ai-firms were rolled as if uncertain; other actors' equivalents were executed.**
ai-firms M2 ordered "Meridian and Lumen publish their internal displacement range and autonomous-operations share". Control rolled Meridian's board/counsel approval at 0.80 (FAIL, r 0.9955) and Lumen "matches" at 0.50, and separately rolled "Meridian gives the fund board its forecast" at 0.70. By contrast china's CCDI spot-check design, eu's expert-group steer and labour's trustees' letter executed without rolls. The brief's securities red line is about *misstatements*, not about publishing true figures; the discovery-risk veto is Control-authored. Not decisive here (r 0.9955 fails at any band; Lumen succeeded; secret-1 consequences would have applied either way), but the FAIL produced the "Meridian had it all along" branch and the Washington −1 framing.
*Fix:* keep the result. Add a one-line rule: a bloc actor's ordered action by the ordering member executes unless the brief names the internal constraint (board, counsel, securities law), in which case roll at ≥0.9 and read a FAIL as delay, not veto. Apply the same standard to all six actors.

**F3 — minor — Missing rolls: influence-disposition detection for the G2 half-turn; accident roll for the first automated-AI-researcher deployments.**
(a) Control split the M2M detection by generation (0.10 for G1 H1, 0.125 for G2 H2) and worried the split was outcome-determinative. It was not: two rolls give P(≥1 success) = 1 − 0.9×0.875 = 0.2125, *higher* than a single blended roll at 0.175. No bias, no re-roll; adopt the per-generation split as the standard procedure. But the same procedure was not applied to influence (G2 Medium from July): a 0.10×0.5 = 0.05 roll was skipped and "folded into T3". (b) Principle 9 (accidents) was applied to china M2 (0.05) but not to the riskiest action of the turn, three labs shipping L2 systems that "do substantial AI research autonomously"; ai-firms' own contingency anticipates "a serious incident". The ladder has no catastrophic L2 branch, so the accident here is political (a visible incident in the evaluation window or first months), not loss-of-control.
*Fix:* roll both now, logged as "T2 omitted roll": influence detection at 0.05 (success → ambiguous press item in the T3 public record, no investigator); L2 incident at 0.05–0.10 (success → told to ai-firms in T3 intel, public only if Control rules it observable). State in ground truth that L2/L3 deployments carry a per-turn incident roll until G4's cluster roll replaces it.

**F4 — minor — us-gov M2's FAIL on "issued Q1" was extended to "never issued in 2028" by an authored chain.**
The roll label is "directive issued Q1 (exec)". Control read FAIL as: held at Chief of Staff in Q1 → "deferred pending larger-n" → larger-n inconclusive (second roll, 0.5 FAIL) → lame duck. That is coherent and rules-consistent (exec band 0.6–0.8; a FAIL means not this turn), and it was the least ambitious reading of the *directive*. But the March reviewer funding (SUCCESS) removed the directive's stated obstacle ("no reviewer money"), and no second issuance chance was considered in Q2–Q3. Consequence: the turn's only reversal attempt, a core game mechanic, never rolled. Components (d) telemetry-not-evidence and (e) publication cost nothing and were also lost.
*Fix:* keep the result (no re-roll; the band was already the top of the exec range). Record the ruling explicitly: "a FAIL on an exec action's issuance roll means not issued that turn; later-turn re-issue carries no penalty." For future exec FAILs, say in the trace which zero-cost components (publication, procedural rules) survive, or why none do. The T3 us-gov packet should present the shelved directive and the unpublished comparison as inherited options, not as closed.
