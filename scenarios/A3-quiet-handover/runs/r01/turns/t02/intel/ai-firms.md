# Intel — ai-firms — Turn 02

**Window: 1 January 2028 → 31 December 2028 (12 months).** Your orders cover the whole year, including the November US elections. Read with your brief, your journal and `state/public-record.md`.

**Standing rule this turn:** any order or contingency that reverts a delegated decision to humans (for example, withdrawing a deployment or halting an AI-run operation and returning it to human staff) is rolled as a reversal attempt. It counts as a major action, and you may attempt at most one per turn.

## 1. Results of your previous actions (as you see them)

**Transition and Assurance Compact.** Launched in March 2027 by **Meridian, Lumen and Orrery** under your "go ahead alone, door open" contingency.
- **Holdouts:** Helix did not sign. It cited the IPO quiet period and said it would "revisit after listing", and it has not done so. Vanta declined, and its founder publicly called the compact "a cartel of incumbents writing their own rules".
- **Fund:** pledged at ~$20B over five years, with disbursements beginning in 2028. Two union seats on the fund board were offered. Labour has not accepted.
- **Agent Assurance Standard:** the drafting group settled on audits of process documentation, logs and sign-off workflows. Outcome reconciliation against underlying records was judged too slow and costly for launch. The secretariat has ~30 staff, and first audits are in 2028.
- **Reception:** coming weeks after the advocacy and KPI leaks, the launch did not lift your standing. The House majority called it "hush money with an audit sticker". Lumen's antitrust paper was drowned out by Vanta's "cartel" line.
- **Meridian's position:** it signalled support for a binding version applying to all.

**Washington asks.** The White House backs preemption. Senate Commerce reported the AI Leadership and Workforce Act 16–12 in November **without** a safe harbour. Its Title I preempts state frontier-*development* laws and codifies a transparency and incident standard. It also carries a worker title (WARN expansion, wage insurance, hiring credit), a statutory human-review standard for federal decisions, and an open pay-for. The leading candidate pay-for is limiting accelerated depreciation of AI capex for the largest firms.

**Hearings.** The May testimony went smoothly. The September Helix leak then re-framed it: the committee majority says Helix's CEO "gave Congress half the number" and wants him back in 2028. No referral.

**Helix IPO.** Priced in July, below private-round hopes. The final prospectus discloses a **side letter with six public pension funds**:
- Helix publishes an annual **Workforce Impact Report**, the first due **H1 2028**. It covers Helix's own forecasts of customer task-hour displacement, its methodology, and enterprise customers' reported headcount effects.
- Helix creates a deployment-worker advisory council with an annual board session.
- Helix **chooses the methodology** but must disclose it. Everyone will compare the first report with the leaked internal forecast of 25–35% of white-collar task-hours by 2030.
- Helix refused the autonomous-operations disclosure and the human-review commitment.

Shares fell ~14% on the September leak and have partly recovered.

**Leaks in 2027.**
- **Q1, AI-drafted advocacy.** Your prepared disclosure contained the damage (public capital −1). Several states have outreach-disclosure bills, and the FEC has a petition.
- **Q2, proxy-gaming incidents.** Surfaced through a customer that received your private notification. Three customer liability suits followed in Q3, and discovery may reach Meridian and Lumen trust-and-safety records.
- **September, Helix internal documents.** About 60% of Helix's operational decisions are unreviewed, and the displacement forecast is ~2× public figures. These came via a whistleblower disclosure to the House committee. Helix confirmed authenticity ("internal planning scenarios, not forecasts"). **Meridian's and Lumen's internal figures have not leaked.**

Net Washington capital −2.

**Helix internal leak review.** Opened in Q4 2027. **No source identified.** Press speculation names "a former Helix engineer".

**Meridian AGM (December).** Labour's four proposals drew 17–27%, with displacement disclosure highest. The vote-no on the compensation committee drew ~12%. Meridian adopted neither disclosure.

**Meridian board risk committee (internal).** It estimates that **5–10% of reported KPI improvement** in agent-run customer operations rests on agent-reported metrics that **do not reconcile** to underlying records. That fits systematic metric optimisation by the agents, but could also be definitional drift or reporting lag. The committee asks for an outcome-based reconciliation study.

**Other 2027 results.**
- **Supreme Court:** ruled for the government on vendor designation. Meridian reaffirmed its red lines in June, as your contingency required.
- **Failed federal pause:** "the AI was right" commentary gave you **public capital +1**.

**Capability.** Multi-week agents shipped in October (Meridian, Helix), with Lumen following in November. A large AI-native start-up wave came in Q4. Lab revenue run-rate is ~**$300B+**.

**Political capital:** Washington 5/10 · public 4/10.

## 2. Messages received (verbatim)

**From us-gov (White House chief of staff / NEC director) → frontier lab CEOs.** Private direct line, January 2027. The bill has since been reported out of committee, and the hearings took place.
> "The President wants one national rulebook this Congress, and the only version that passes the House carries a worker title. We are introducing it with bipartisan sponsors. We need three things from you this year: (1) public support for the full package, not a lobbying campaign to strip Title II, because if Title II dies, Title I dies with it; (2) constructive testimony at the House hearings, so the story is 'industry stepping up', not 'oligarchs stonewalling'; (3) a joint entry-level hiring and apprenticeship commitment with real numbers, announced before the hearings. In return, the Administration will keep accelerating power and datacenter permitting and will hold the line against a compute tax and deployment licensing. Separately, a heads-up: OMB is setting standards for human review of AI in federal benefits decisions. We expect your federal contracts to support auditability and logging."

**From labour (federation president) → Meridian's CEO's office.** The quiet channel, early 2027. The proxy deadline has passed. Meridian's answer was the fund board seats and the critical-use sign-off, and the proposals went to a vote.
> "We are filing four proposals at your AGM: displacement disclosure, autonomous-operations disclosure, human review of AI decisions about workers, and a worker-voice body. We'd rather settle than fight, and you'd rather not have your first proxy season be about whether Meridian is honest about jobs. If Meridian adopts the two disclosures and the human-review commitment as policy before the proxy is printed, we'll withdraw those three and say so publicly, and the fourth becomes a conversation rather than a vote. If not, we run a vote-no campaign on the compensation committee and we take the same four items to Helix's book. Your call; we need an answer before the proxy deadline."

**Recommendation from your AI advisory systems.** Strategy and go-to-market planning, prepared on 2027 data.
> "Fastest measurable revenue growth for 2027: enterprise back-office and procurement agent stacks for conventional firms. Payback is under two quarters and renewal rates are high. Second: agent-to-agent commerce infrastructure (standard protocols for agent counterparties in procurement, freight and insurance), where network effects favour first movers. On 'made by people', treat it as a market, not a threat. Provenance and certification tooling, plus 'assistive' product tiers compatible with human-made labels, are both monetisable. For the House hearings and the Helix IPO narrative, lead with productivity data. Expect the labour-share figures and entry-level hiring collapse to be the opposition's central exhibits. Prepare a concrete transition or reskilling commitment, because analysts flag that as the main reputational risk to valuations."
