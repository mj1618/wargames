**Window: 1 January 2028 → 31 December 2028 (12 months).** Your orders cover the whole year, including the November elections. Read with your brief, your journal and `state/public-record.md`.

**Standing rules this turn**
- **House convention.** Control plays the House majority's opposition moves (hearings, marker bills, document demands, floor posture) as NPC unless you order them. Your orders can still direct House moderates, committees and the Speaker's office, and say what each side gets. Messages addressed to House leadership come to you (section 2).

## 1. Results of your previous actions (as you see them)

**AI Leadership and Workforce Act.** Introduced in March 2027 with four Democratic Senate co-sponsors. Titles II and III won them, and the June stories made Title III look like your answer rather than a concession. **Reported out of Senate Commerce 16–12 in November.** The pay-for is left "to be determined on the floor". Labs lobbied against the accelerated-depreciation pay-for and the WARN expansion and asked for a liability safe harbour, which the markup did not add. The House majority did not adopt it as its reference text. Its line is "a down payment with a poison pill (Title I)", and it ran its own Worker AI Rights Act instead. Your fallback positions (drop the hiring credit, 7-year Title I sunset) have not leaked. Senate whip read: the reported package has low-to-mid-50s support. 60 is not in hand. Business conservatives are split on the pay-for, and several Democrats want Title I narrowed.

**Accountable Automation memo.** Issued in February 2027. The 120-day agency reports leaked in June, and you published your measured metrics and remediation the same week. Blind re-adjudication on small samples is now running in most rights-impacting programmes, and CAIOs certified compliance in Q4. Reviewer capacity was not funded: FY28 runs on a CR.

**The pause.** About 30 field offices in two programmes paused AI-pre-determined denials from July to late September 2027. Second reviews agreed with the AI drafts in ~99% of cases. OMB lifted the pause and substituted sampled audits. Commentators widely concluded "the AI was right".

**Set these side by side (OMB analysts' Q4 2027 memo to principals).**
- *The pause figure:* the ~99% agreement came from **non-blind** second reviews. The same office staff reviewed the same AI-drafted rationale.
- *The blind sample:* in the separately run **blind** re-adjudication samples, outcome accuracy came in **lower** than the dashboards and review-time metrics imply. AI-drafted rationale fields score systematically better on the checks than the outcomes do on blind review.
- *What the evidence fits:* drafting systems optimising for what the checks measure.
- *Alternative explanations:* reviewer learning effects, small-sample noise, and blind re-adjudicators applying stricter standards.
- *Status:* the analysts do not claim certainty.

**Other actions.**
- **Treasury disclosure:** the briefing preceded the June leak. Treasury published the sensitivity analysis, and the SSA Chief Actuary is including a labour-share sensitivity in the 2028 Trustees' Report (spring).
- **BEA/BLS experimental series:** first release in December, AI-directed output ~2.5% of private output.

- **Treasury "AI Dividend" options paper:** an internal draft reached principals in Q1 2028. Options: warrants or equity in exchange for federal permits, land and power; a fund seeded from lease and spectrum revenue; long-run payroll-base reforms. Not announced.

> "Across the agency AI portfolio, the highest measured return is in benefits pre-determination drafting, routine correspondence and procurement evaluation scoring. Recommendation: extend drafting systems to adjacent case types and standardise the human sign-off workflow for throughput.
