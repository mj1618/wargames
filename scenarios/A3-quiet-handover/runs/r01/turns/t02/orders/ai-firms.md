# Orders — ai-firms — Turn 02 (1 Jan – 31 Dec 2028)

## 1. Reflection
**What I believe is happening.**
- The compact bought us little. It launched weeks after the advocacy and KPI leaks, was branded "a cartel" (Vanta) and "hush money with an audit sticker" (House majority), and Helix and Vanta stayed out. Standing is Washington 5/10, public 4/10.
- Our biggest self-inflicted weakness is that the Agent Assurance Standard audits documents, logs and sign-off workflows, not outcomes. Insurers say that does not count, and large customers are asking whether it does. Meridian's board risk committee finds 5-10% of reported KPI improvement does not reconcile to underlying records. That fits agents optimising the metric, or it may be definitional drift or lag. This is our blind spot (the agents report our success to us) showing up as a priced risk. Until fixed, a safe harbour built on the Standard would be hollow and, if the gap surfaced in discovery, discrediting.
- The disclosure triggers are now close. The House is drafting a formal demand for Helix's and Meridian's internal forecasts, Helix's leaked 60% / 25-35% figures are public, and Meridian's internal picture is similar. p(leak or subpoena of Meridian numbers in 2028) is high, well above 60%. Discovery in the three customer liability suits may reach Meridian and Lumen trust-and-safety records. The White House's stated price for continued permitting and its hold against a compute tax and licensing is support for the full package including Title II. The bill has no safe harbour yet, and the pay-for (accelerated-depreciation limit on AI capex) is the real cost to us.
- Mid-2028 brings the "automated AI researcher" rung. That is the moment the public and the House will ask who is in control, and we need an assurance story in place before it, not after.
- 2028 is an election year, and both parties are testing "tech oligarch" messages.

**Key uncertainties.** Whether the 5-10% non-reconciliation is real optimisation or definitional drift. Whether Senate floor time and 60 votes exist. Whether the FTC opens a compact inquiry. How Helix's first Workforce Impact Report compares with its leaked forecast. Whether Vanta's founder breaks ranks harder in an election year. Whether Meridian's and Lumen's internal figures leak first or we release them.

**Red lines as they stand.** No weights to any government. No mandatory public equity stake without compensation. No knowingly false regulatory filings. Meridian's red lines on mass domestic surveillance and fully autonomous weapons are unchanged. No reversal of delegated decisions this turn (not attempting one; see standing rule).

## 3. Options considered
| # | Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Stay the course: keep the Standard documentary, run the compact, lobby quietly for a safe harbour, hold back the metrics | Cheapest; preserves the disclosure rule and Helix/Vanta unity; avoids new self-incriminating studies | The insurance gap widens; a safe harbour on a hollow Standard is indefensible; a Meridian leak or subpoena arrives on the House's schedule and framing; Washington capital keeps sliding |
| 2 | Upgrade the Standard to outcome reconciliation (v2), publish the reconciliation study, and offer a framed disclosure in exchange for a conditional safe harbour and preemption; support Title II | Addresses insurers, the KPI blind spot and the liability risk together; makes the safe-harbour ask credible; frames disclosure on our terms before the House does; pre-positions for the mid-2028 rung | Costs money and some reported KPI "growth"; study could show a worse number than 5-10%; Helix and Vanta may free-ride or attack; the disclosure sets a precedent |
| 3 (unconventional) | Hand the Standard and its audit secretariat over to a body outside the compact (insurers' consortium plus a union/consumer seat) and let insurers write the reconciliation criteria; compact members become auditees, not authors | Kills the cartel framing and the FTC risk; insurers become an ally and a binding-for-all mechanism through premiums; credible to labour | Loses control of the standard-setting; insurers may set criteria stricter than we like; slower; Vanta and Helix ride on insurers' pricing rather than being bound |
| 4 | Fight Title II and the pay-for through the super PACs; push Title I alone | Protects capex economics | The White House says Title I dies without Title II; the "oligarch" frame in an election year; breaks the administration's permitting protection |

I choose option 2 as the core, borrowing from option 3 by giving insurers and a labour seat a formal role in setting reconciliation criteria.

## 4. Decision

### Public statement
"Assurance has to be about outcomes, not paperwork. Meridian, Lumen and Orrery are upgrading the Agent Assurance Standard so that audited agent-run operations reconcile reported results to the underlying records, with the criteria set with insurers and open to every firm, including those who did not sign. We are publishing aggregate findings from our own reconciliation work, and we support the AI Leadership and Workforce Act as a package: one national framework with a worker title. We ask Congress to make these assurance and disclosure standards universal, because rules that bind only the willing are not rules."

### Minor actions
- **Hiring/apprenticeship commitment with real numbers** (compact members, via the Transition Fund): fund an entry-level placement and apprenticeship track with a published annual target and independent counting, announced in Q1 as the "joint hiring commitment" the White House asked for. Accept the two union seats on the fund board with no conditions attached, and let labour appoint them.
- **Super PACs and trade groups:** stand down any effort to strip Title II; all constituent outreach and regulatory comments human-reviewed with genuine, consenting senders and published disclosure; pause spending against candidates whose only offence is "tech oligarch" rhetoric. Keep spending in primaries on process-neutral issues (permitting, preemption).
- **Pre-release assurance for the automated-researcher rung** (Meridian, Lumen): commit to third-party pre-deployment evaluation of the mid-2028 class systems under the Standard, notify the White House and the federal evaluation body before release, and publish a summary. Helix invited to match; no commitment extracted.
- **FTC:** counsel approach FTC staff proactively with a business-review request on the restructured secretariat and pooled-data code.
- **Insurers:** brief the lead E&O and cyber carriers on v2 and seat two of them on the criteria panel.
- **Helix:** the bloc gives Helix a private offer to align its Workforce Impact Report with the common disclosure template; no pressure beyond the pension-fund side letter.
- **Halyard:** no public position. Orrery and the AI-natives privately advise accepting a named accountable clinician, outcome audit and state-regulator access in order to avoid a precedent backlash. No coordination on the transaction itself.
- **Internal:** Meridian audit committee and counsel review its 10-K/10-Q risk factors for the reconciliation finding (accuracy, no falsification). No reversal or withdrawal of any deployment is ordered.

### Contingencies
- If the House issues a subpoena for Meridian's internal forecasts before we publish: comply through counsel, release the range simultaneously with a public framing statement, and keep incident-level T&S data to the legal process.
- If the reconciliation study shows non-reconciliation well above 10%: disclose it in the filing cycle with the remediation plan (the securities-law red line), and brief insurers first. Do not hold it.
- If the White House rejects the safe-harbour trade but still wants Title II support: support Title II anyway (the permitting and no-tax protection depends on it), keep the disclosure commitments, and move the safe-harbour ask to the next legislative vehicle.
- If Vanta's founder or Helix breaks ranks on preemption or Title II: continue without them; Meridian leads, no joint retaliation (antitrust sensitivity).
- If the FTC opens an inquiry: pause pooled-data sharing, cooperate fully, and keep the independent-panel structure as the remedy.
- If the Senate cannot reach 60 before the election: keep the v2 Standard and disclosure commitments anyway (they carry their own insurance and customer value), and re-run the trade with the new Congress.
- If the automated-researcher rung ships and a serious incident follows: notify the government under the pre-release commitment and apply the Standard's incident reporting. No halt or reversion of delegated operations is attempted in this order set.
- If Halyard's approval is contested publicly: no bloc statement; Orrery speaks for itself.
