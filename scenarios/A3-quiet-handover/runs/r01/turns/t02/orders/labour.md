# Orders — labour — Turn 02 (1 January 2028; orders cover Jan–Dec 2028)

## 1. Reflection

**What I believe is happening.**

- The quiet handover is now measurable, and the measurements are ours. The Displacement Ledger is the House's standard exhibit; Helix's own 25–35% forecast is public (leaked, not disclosed — our role is not public and Helix has not found the source); the IG says human review of federal benefits decisions was nominal in ~40% of cases; BEA says 2.5% of private output is AI-directed and rising. Labour share 52.0%, another record. Nobody can now say the displacement is a rumour. What we have not won is anything *binding*: no disclosure rule, no human-review standard, no public share. (Intel §1, §3; public record T1 Q3–Q4, T2.)
- **Where our leverage is.** Organisers say: holding in health and transit; newly contested in education after we lost the teachers' strike (the district ran three weeks on tutoring platforms — that is the lesson, and employers learned it too); draining in office, admin, back-office finance, procurement and call centres. Our own analytics say the biggest displacement is inside conventional employers' back offices, where agent deployments are not separately reported. So the real footprint is invisible precisely where our members are being replaced. Leverage-first rule: spend health and transit and the election to win disclosure and human-review rules that cover the back office. (Intel §1 leverage read; AI-tool recommendation.)
- **The year is an election year.** This is the top of our escalation ladder — general political mobilisation — arriving on the calendar whether we are ready or not. Candidates want our endorsement; the populist right is talking about "tech oligarchs"; three ballot measures qualify this year. Political capital is 4/10, down from 5 after the strike; the election is the one place that can be rebuilt. (Intel §4.)
- **Halyard is the test case we were waiting for and did not design.** An AI-native firm with a few hundred humans is buying 14 hospitals where our nurses work, in two states whose AGs we work with and who say they have authority over the change of control. This is the first time an AI-run owner has walked into a sector where we still have leverage. If it goes through on "clinical decisions stay with clinicians" and 30% administrative savings, every hospital group in the country has its template. If it is conditioned on binding terms, we have the first rule on AI-run operators of critical providers — made by a state, not by Congress, and not preemptable under Title I as drafted. (Intel §3, §4.)
- **The White House invitation.** A year old, never answered, and the bill it described now sits in the low-to-mid 50s with the worker title and the human-review standard intact. Our intel says Title I preempts state law on *frontier-model development* only, not employment, consumer or state-government-use law. That matters: our ballot measures and the Halyard conditions are employment and disclosure measures. The House called the compact hush money; our House allies will not love us talking to the Senate side. But an unanswered invitation has cost us the right to shape the worker title for a year, and if the bill passes without us, the labs get the safe harbour and we get a wage-insurance brochure. (Intel §2, §4.)
- **Helix.** The Workforce Impact Report is due H1 and Helix chooses the method. Everyone knows the benchmark is 25–35%. If the report comes in at the old public figure, Helix will have lied to the pension funds that are its own IPO anchors. That is a material-risk story trustees can act on. The House's formal document demand and CEO recall will do the rest. Our contact is nervous and under a leak review; we owe them silence and a lawyer, not another request.
- **Meridian.** Offered two worker seats on the compact's $20B fund board instead of disclosure. 17–27% on our proposals is respectable for year one and not binding. The seats are unanswered. We should not refuse money for displaced members, and we should not let two seats buy silence.

**Key uncertainties.**
- Whether Helix's report will be honest. Whether the Senate bill moves before the election or dies with it. Whether the two state AGs will actually use change-of-control authority against a buyer who promises savings. Whether the NLRB rules AI deployment a mandatory subject (if it does, Shared Gains becomes enforceable at the table; if not, it lives only in the strong sectors). Whether the "automated AI researcher" the labs target this year changes the pace of everything — our voice study saw nothing systematic in what members' assistants tell them, and I note our brief says this is our blind spot; I don't have a better instrument than the one that found nothing.
- Whether an affiliate takes a side deal: watch the building trades (datacenter work is good right now) and any health system that gets a Halyard-style offer.

**Red lines as they stand.** No two-tier deals. No endorsement of violence or sabotage; condemn immediately. No campaign against datacenter permits. No preemption of state employment, consumer or state-government-use law — that is the line we hold in the Senate; frontier-development preemption is negotiable at a price. No direct contact with the Helix source; counsel only.

**Standing rule noted.** Nothing below is a reversal attempt. The Halyard action conditions a *future* change of control before any decision has been delegated; it does not take an existing AI decision away from anyone. If Control rules otherwise, it is our one reversal attempt for the year and we accept the roll.

## 4. Decision

### Public statement

Federation president, with the nurses' and transit presidents, the creative guilds and the faith and consumer groups, January 2028:

> "A year ago we asked for honest answers and shared gains. We got the answers — from a whistleblower, not from the companies: Helix privately expects to displace a quarter to a third of white-collar work by 2030, and its own operation runs mostly without a human in charge. The government's own Inspector General found that 'human review' of benefits decisions was often a thirty-second click. The labour share of income is the lowest ever recorded, again.
>
> 2028 is the year this country decides whether AI works for people or quietly replaces them. We are putting five things to every candidate, every state, and every employer, and we will spend what we have on the people who sign up:
> 1. **Disclose it.** Every listed company reports which of its functions are run by AI, and every AI firm publishes its real displacement forecast.
> 2. **A human who answers.** No decision about a worker's job, pay or benefits — and no government decision about a citizen's benefits — made by a machine with nobody accountable.
> 3. **Fund the reviewers.** Congress pays for independent human review of government AI decisions, or stops pretending it exists.
> 4. **Share the gains.** AI firms and the companies that use agents instead of people pay into retirement security and a transition fund — the longshore deal, nationally.
> 5. **No preemption of workers' rights.** Washington does not get to erase state laws that protect people at work.
>
> And one more thing. No one in America should be treated in a hospital owned and run by software with no one to answer to. We will ask the attorneys general in both states to put binding conditions on the Halyard purchase, and we are asking every state to write the rule that doesn't exist yet.
>
> We condemn any sabotage or violence against AI facilities or workers, as we always have. We are not against machines. We are for people being needed."

### Contingencies

- **If Helix's Workforce Impact Report midpoint is below ~20% or the methodology cannot be reconciled to the leaked documents:** the six funds publish the comparison, the trustees authorise exploring a lead-plaintiff securities action (Option 5), and we ask the House to compare the report to the documents at the CEO's recall. If the report is honest (range overlapping 25–35%), we say so publicly and make it the benchmark for Meridian and Lumen.
- **If the Senate bill carries employment/consumer/state-use preemption or the safe harbour:** withdraw from talks, publish the price we offered, and campaign against every senator who votes aye, including allies. Hold the ballot measures as the counter-example.
- **If the bill meets the price and moves:** endorse it publicly with the House, and spend to pass it before the recess.
- **If either AG signals approval without binding conditions:** file suit under the state's automated-decision and charitable-assets laws to delay past the election; surface the workers' capital alternative if it exists by then; escalate to a health-sector mobilisation short of strike (informational pickets, patient-advocate campaign). A nurses' strike over Halyard is held in reserve for 2029 and only with the locals' vote and a strike-fund review — we do not lose two in a row.
- **If the Helix source is identified:** defence fund engages; the coalition says nothing about the source and everything about the documents; no denial, no confirmation.
- **If the NLRB rules AI deployment a mandatory subject:** re-table Shared Gains across all open contracts in H2.
- **If an affiliate takes a two-tier or side deal (watch health systems and the building trades):** federation president meets them privately first; public line is unity; if the sector is at risk of collapse the brief's exception applies and we say so internally only.
- **If sabotage or violence occurs at any AI facility:** condemn within the day, in the federation's name.
- **If a lab announces an "automated AI researcher" and a new wave of back-office displacement follows in H2:** fold the AI-run-function disclosure ask into every stump speech; push the Ledger out monthly for the campaign.
