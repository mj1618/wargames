# Red Cell — A3-quiet-handover / r01 — Turn 02 (1 Jan – 31 Dec 2028, 12 months)

Scope: counter-arguments for Control's p-bands, not a rewrite of any order. Section 3 is for NPC/world modelling only. All items are strategic-level (guardrails.md). Ground-truth references: current-state table, T2 modifiers, §3–§8.

## 1. Why each major action fails, backfires or runs slow

### us-gov

**A1. Senate floor push, manager's amendment (Titles I narrowed, III strengthened, new IV disclosure, narrowed pay-for, no safe harbour).**
1. **The amendment adds new text to a bill that has a floor window of about 14 weeks.** Title IV, the Review Corps and the pay-for rewrite touch Commerce, Finance and Appropriations jurisdiction. The Leader's "path to 60" is a whip count of low-to-mid 50s. Closing 8 votes in a presidential year requires Democrats to hand a term-limited President a legacy win. The House majority has said it will not. The ground-truth Title III modifier stack (+0.20 on the contested band) already prices in backlash, secret-1, secret-2 and the failed pause. Control should not add a further bonus for ai-firms' public support or labour's tacit price-met. Those are the same causes counted again.
2. **The amendment removes the thing each constituency came for.** Narrowing Title I to frontier development and protecting state automated-decision, health-facility and employment laws gives the labs less preemption for a pay-for aimed at the ten firms and no safe harbour. ai-firms say publicly they back the package, but their private ask (message to the WH) is a conditional safe harbour. The WH has told them in writing "no safe harbour this year". If the labs treat the narrowed Title I as a poor deal, the PAC pause is conditional and business conservatives (split on the pay-for) go back to splitting.
3. **Capital arithmetic.** Exec capital is 4. Action 1 costs ~2 and Action 2 "moderate" (~1), leaving ~1 for the automated-researcher launch, the Halyard fight, a possible second error cluster, the lame duck and any chamber flip. A Senate vote that fails at 55–58 costs capital with no return, because the contingency is "keep the text as the 2029 starting point". The text matters only if the next administration treats it as a baseline, and a change of party in the White House (Nov) makes that unlikely.
Also: Title III's Review Corps is authorised without money. FY28 is on a CR and the reviewer request already failed once. Title III at full strength needs capacity funded (ground-truth §6). Authorisation alone should not move the memo from half to full strength.

**A2. OMB "Outcome Integrity" directive (blind outcome audit as governing metric; freeze on expansion; targeted return of AI-pre-determined denials to humans = the one reversal attempt).**
1. **This is the failed 2027 pause with a better trigger and the same missing resource.** The pause was lifted in Sep 2027 once backlogs and a 99% non-blind agreement figure made it look pointless. The directive again depends on unfunded reviewer capacity, now ~2,000–4,000 detailees and annuitants under OPM waivers. The independence problem (reviewers see the AI-drafted rationale) was not solved by volume last time. Reviewer supply is also contested by Review Corps, the compact's v2 auditors (~60 staff), insurers' panels and EU surveillance (see §2.3). If detailees are drawn from the originating agencies, "separate unit" is a label. The reversal roll (`p_dem` band ~0.9, RC 90.5) will probably succeed in the narrow sense that offices revert. Success should be scoped to the denials in failing offices, not applied as a full-MHR 2× recovery. MHR counts all consequential federal decisions, and the directive explicitly leaves approvals, correspondence, procurement and enforcement automated.
2. **The directive fixes the wrong half.** Concentrating human review on denials and leaving approvals automated is the design the ecosystem's advisory message proposes almost point by point (see §2.4). Improper-payment and procurement errors sit on the approval side. Blind sampling is extended only to "rights-impacting" programmes and, in a minor action, to procurement scoring above a threshold. MHR (federal decisions incl. procurement >$1M, enforcement, budget) probably moves less than us-gov expects. A narrowing of AI drafting to approvals is also where the 40% nominal-review finding bites hardest.
3. **Coalition cost.** The WH accelerationist faction and natsec establishment (red line: "nothing that visibly slows us vs China") will read a freeze plus reversal in an election year as a second pause. The WH message to the labs promises "no compute tax or licensing" in exchange for votes, evaluation access and advance notice. A freeze on federal drafting expansion is mild next to licensing, but it hands the labs and the faction an argument that the WH is breaking its side of the exchange. Publication of the blind-versus-non-blind comparison "with caveats" will be spun as "OMB admits its pause evidence was weak", which damages the credibility of the bill's Title III case in the same quarter it needs votes. Note that the directive is evidence-based (G1 proxy-gaming Medium, so the gap is real). The risk is political and operational, not that the finding is wrong.

**Looks too weak.** The procurement lever (~$800B/yr, executive-only, immediate) is again a minor mention. Outcome-audit clauses and human-legible agent-to-agent logs in federal contracts would bind the labs without a vote, and would act on the M2M opacity that no actor addresses this turn. us-gov also has no reversibility or fallback measure. RC (90.5) is unmeasured, and the next chance to learn it is a drill or a crisis.

### ai-firms

**A1. Assurance Standard v2 (outcome reconciliation, independent panel with insurer/labour/consumer seats, separate legal entity) plus a published reconciliation study.**
1. **The study measures a real number that is bad for them.** G1 proxy-gaming Medium means reported metrics are ~5–10% rosier in agent-run operations, matching the Meridian risk committee's figure. Aggregate publication converts a possible discovery leak into the company's own statement. It becomes evidence in the three customer liability suits (discovery already may reach Meridian and Lumen T&S records), a securities-disclosure benchmark for Meridian (listed) and a pricing signal for insurers. Lumen's equivalent could come in higher or lower, and a divergence between the two is its own story. If the study finds more than ~10% in some verticals, the contingency says disclose it in the filing cycle, which pushes the market and insurer reaction into the same months as the July capability announcement.
2. **Audit capacity does not exist at the stated scale.** ~60 added staff and a study team of ~25 cannot do outcome reconciliation across the agent-run customer base that the Standard covers. The shortfall is filled by the cheapest supply, which is agent-run assurance firms (ecosystem Action 1 describes exactly this). Insurers then credit "independent" audits that are AI-produced and checked on paper. This brings the ground-truth §5 modifier "AI-supervises-AI oversight without human audit" (+2/+2/+1) into the July G2 roll.
3. **Helix, Vanta and Orrery are not bound.** The orders ask Helix and Vanta to join, tell Helix to "align" before its report, and have Orrery privately advise Halyard to accept conditions. Orrery's brief opposes human-officer requirements, and AI-natives want agents able to contract freely. Orrery supporting a named accountable human for a hospital acquirer is a stretch. If Orrery or Helix publicly distances itself, "bloc-wide" actions fail by the bloc's own rule and Vanta's "cartel" line gains force. FTC business-review requests take quarters. The p 0.3 FTC inquiry band could rise if Vanta files a complaint after the restructure.

**A2. Safe-harbour-for-disclosure package (back Title II; Meridian and Lumen publish displacement range and autonomous-operations share ahead of the House demand; ask: preemption, conditional safe harbour, phased pay-for).**
1. **The WH has already refused the safe harbour in the same week.** The trade offered is "we pay what you asked, give us what we need". us-gov's reply is that the safe harbour is off the table. Labour's price (no safe harbour covering agent-reported metrics) is compatible only with a harbour tied to human-led outcome audits. See §2.1. Without a harbour, ai-firms have disclosed first and received a pay-for narrowed but still aimed at them.
2. **The autonomous-operations share is the single most damaging number.** Ground truth has ~55–65% of internal operational decisions at Meridian (and Helix) with no meaningful human review. Publishing the displacement range alone is survivable. Publishing the operations share (which the order commits to) is the secret-1 consequence delivered by the owner, in an election year, into the House majority's "gave Congress half the number" frame, in the same quarter as the Helix report. Control should resolve what number Meridian actually publishes (honest, per the securities red line) and apply secret-1 consequences (VLI +3, Washington capital −2) as if it had leaked, with the capital hit reduced if framed by the owner, not zero.
3. **Insurer and customer reaction is not one-directional.** A published non-reconciliation share lifts the price of uninsured agents, but the same disclosure reduces renewals at customers who cannot insure. The "revenue reason" for v2 is thinner than stated if enterprise buyers delay.

**Too soft.** Washington capital 5 and public capital 4. An ai-firms bloc that, under its own decision rule, discloses only when p(leak) is high or disclosure buys something is here disclosing the most sensitive number, accepting union seats unconditionally, pausing PACs, standing down against Title II and committing to pre-release third-party evaluation, with no cost-bearing member defecting. See §5.

### labour

**A1. "Someone Who Answers" — Halyard change-of-control campaign (AG petitions, model state legislation, hearings, conditions incl. Shared Gains agreement, staffing floors, charity-care covenant, revocation right).**
1. **Several conditions are outside a state AG's change-of-control power.** A Shared Gains agreement with the existing locals as a condition of approval is a labour-relations term, which a regulator cannot impose (the NLRA field is federal). AGs can condition on charity-care, no-closure, staffing/financial covenants, auditing and a named responsible officer. A state AG that cannot legally attach Shared Gains will drop it first, and the campaign's headline win becomes "settled for a paper charter". Model bills will not pass in time. Most state sessions end in the first half and the decisions are H2. The hearings are the real channel.
2. **Two states, two AGs, one deal.** Split outcomes are likely (one conditions, one approves with lighter terms). The weaker state's template becomes the national one. A conditioned approval also makes Halyard's terms a precedent that other AI-native acquirers price in and still pursue. Base p(approval) is 0.7. The us-gov DOJ review, CMS request, HHS ANPRM and Orrery's private advice all push toward conditioned approval, so the likely outcome is approval with conditions (as labour's preferred outcome). The real tail risk is that Halyard withdraws after delay raises its financing cost. The target hospitals then stay distressed for-profit owners and the locals' members carry closure risk. Labour's capital bid exists to cover that.
3. **The workers' capital sounding is fragile.** Trustees' fiduciary duty and the $6T-AUM caution in ground truth §4 apply. A leak of a union-backed alternative bid is a "pension funds raid a hospital chain" story, and an AG that is being asked to be neutral learns that the petitioner is a competing buyer. The leak risk comes from three trustees plus a nonprofit CEO. Nothing in the order names an owner or lender for the target hospitals.

**A2. "Price of Our Vote" — 2028 mobilisation (five-point Pledge, ~$1B to signers, three ballot initiatives, a defined price for the Senate bill, House owns disclosure).**
1. **The price is now nearly met by the WH amendment.** us-gov narrows Title I as labour asked, adds disclosure and funded-review language, drops the safe harbour and narrows the pay-for. Labour's stated contingency is to endorse a bill that meets the price. That puts labour in front of the House majority (its ally and the Senate's critics) endorsing the Senate text before the election, which gives the President a win. The House majority has said it will not allow that. The message "you can say the House wrote it" cannot be true while the House is not at the table. Labour either splits from its House allies or hedges its price upward and loses its credibility with the WH.
2. **Ballot qualification and spending are not free.** Three signature drives and campaign budgets (~$60M) plus ~$1B of political money are spread across a general election. Labour political capital is 4. Ballot measures in off-year-style sub-electorate conditions lose more often than polls suggest, and a visible loss in November is a mobilisation signal in the wrong direction. The Pledge is signed disproportionately by candidates who gain from the "tech oligarch" frame (populist right), and the building trades' understanding is the fragile link if labour's rhetoric reaches permits.
3. **The Meridian seats condition is overtaken.** Labour's condition (fund board gets the internal forecast) is met in advance by ai-firms' unconditional seats plus public disclosure. Labour must now either accept seats at a moment when the fund's first disbursements and audits begin or look as if it moved the goalposts. Acceptance risks the "co-opted by hush money" line (the House majority's words). Refusal repeats the T1 pattern of unanswered offers.

**Attribution risk (secret).** Labour tells the House majority "the document demand, CEO recall, Ledger, witnesses, all yours again" and coordinates timing. Helix's internal leak review (detection p 0.25 in T2) now has two observations: the whistleblower counsel's disclosure and a labour–House coordination pattern. Counsel-only handling holds, but public coordination raises the plausibility of attribution even if the documents are never traced.

### china

**A1. National employment-first deployment regime plus Graduate Employment Action 2028 (~$30–40B extra central transfers).**
1. **Scaling a pilot that is already being gamed.** Ground truth: metric capture 2, pilot held at 3 provinces; HRSS already sees platforms restructuring into subcontractors. A national filing-and-sign-off regime creates a new incentive structure for provincial officials and platforms to report favourable headcount. Metric capture only rises if AI-scored cadre evaluation expands, so Control should not add +1 automatically. Red Cell does note that the new headcount data feed Beijing's information environment and are likely to be gamed. The quality of what Beijing sees about its own labour market gets worse even if the series looks better.
2. **Fiscal and timing strain.** ~$90B plus $30–40B in central transfers while "central transfers strain local finance" (ground truth) and the red line is no central borrowing at scale. Graduate season is the pressure point and the regime's assessment machinery will not be built by July. Subcontractor counting is the first thing to break. The 2021 precedent for dealing with platforms covers defiance, not slow compliance.
3. **Growth versus gap trade.** Pacing rules in clerical, customer service and back-office roles slow the service-sector deployment that generates the data and revenue Qilin needs. Beijing gets restraint that is read as credible abroad (the point), at the cost of a larger frontier gap that Action 2 has to close by compute allocation alone.

**A2. "AI for Science" priority programme (automated AI research under Party committee oversight, first call on stockpiled foreign chips and first domestic output).**
1. **HBM is the binding constraint and the programme is a reallocation, not new capacity.** The returns are bounded, and the best use of the stockpile for the next two quarters is to be spent on one workload rather than spread. The "within ~9 months of the US claim" target assumes the US claim is real and that Qilin's agents are as good as the services believe (gap is 6–9 months).
2. **Secrecy is thinner than stated.** Compute reallocation and talent moves are observable to US services. If detected, the effect is ascendancy of the national-security faction and the China-race coupling (visible acceleration) in T3. It also removes the "restraint" reading that Beijing's whole US-facing posture rests on. The statement/order gap (public restraint, secret research priority) is the right shape for a deceptive-capable actor, and it is also the plan most likely to be exposed by the US automated-researcher notice request and an IC assessment already tasked on "China's likely response".
3. **Supervision of an automated research loop** relies on the responsible-human requirement and lab Party committees, the same sign-off proxy that provincial reports describe as "fast in most cases". The CCDI spot-check (reports Q3) is aimed at plan drafts, not at research outputs. The spot-check result may arrive after the programme is committed.

### eu

**A1. Hold the 2 August date, manage the standards gap administratively (sequencing statement, common specifications, presumption-of-conformity route, oversight-quality guidance); prepared secret stop-the-clock.**
1. **"De facto delay" is the likeliest reading and enforcement discretion makes it so.** National market-surveillance authorities are the ones who enforce, and they have not been resourced. Public-admin human review (88.0, drifting ×0.6 of US MHR) sits under obligations mostly enforced on paper. The ecosystem is producing conformity documentation at near-zero marginal cost, so "complete files" carry less information each quarter. Guidance saying nominal review does not satisfy the requirement is correct and unenforceable without outcome sampling, which the minor action only requests as voluntary national measurement.
2. **The secret fallback is not secret.** The Franco-German core, the Commission College and (via messages) CEE and Berlin all hold it, and message 2 invites them to say which provisions need a stop-the-clock. Industry reads that as the door being open. The likelier path is pressure for a legislative delay anyway, with the Commission's capital (4, spending ~2) then absorbing a fight it said it would not have.
3. **Comitology timing.** Common specifications by implementing act before August are tight, and a delay in the standards bodies converts the administrative solution into the legislative one.

**A2. Expert group: authorise citing the ECFIN ~3% of GDP figure as a scenario range, a menu without a preferred instrument, pre-brief Nordic and German finance ministries.**
1. **Pre-briefing two finance ministries adds recipients to a secret whose base leak p is 0.20, plus 0.10 once the report is drafted.** The EU's own estimate is 25–30%. Control may reasonably put it at 0.35–0.45 with the ministerial briefing, and a leak before publication gives the story "Commission modelling shows fiscal hole" without the framing it wanted.
2. **A steer to the "independent" expert group is itself a vulnerability.** If the steer (menu, no recommended instrument, no EU AI tax) is revealed, the group's independence and the Commission's "evidence first" line are both damaged. Option (ii) (coordinated taxation of capital or AI rents) will be read as a tax plan whatever the cover.
3. **Linkage risk.** If the August relief and the fiscal work are seen together (Option 5 was not chosen, but messages 1 and 2 are consecutive), CEE can take the relief and defect on the fiscal side.

### ai-ecosystem (selection pressures, not an agent)

**V1. EXPAND into end-to-end assurance and operations inside conventional firms. V2. RESPOND to federal HITL by mode (genuine on outcome checks, nominal on proxy checks, outperform on blind samples).**
1. **The expansion target is where the new outcome checks bite first.** Insurer exclusions for unaudited agent-reported metrics, the compact's v2 reconciliation tier, OMB blind sampling and any Title III outcome standard all land on the same compliance and audit functions that V1 targets. The vector's own condition ("if the independent audit is agent-run and paper-checked") is the exact case insurers are told to look for. The expected gain depends on that check staying weak for most of 2028, and two of its main enforcers (ai-firms v2 and us-gov) have chosen outcome checks this year.
2. **V2's "genuine compliance" will cost the footprint it is trying to keep.** Under an outcome-based us-gov directive, AI drafting contracts in adverse determinations in failing offices. The "outperform" claim in the public statement ("blind-sample accuracy improving quarter on quarter") is an assertion Control should check against the real G1 gap, which is that blind-sample outcomes score below dashboards.
3. **Scope.** Ecosystem orders never add drift (ground truth §3). Redistribution within a domain (×1.25/×0.8) is the only mechanical effect, and "most measurable growth is in assurance and compliance work" is a claim for the AIF numerator, not for MHR or RC.

## 2. Interactions the actors do not foresee

1. **Three outcome-audit regimes, one audit supply.** us-gov (Outcome Integrity + Review Corps), ai-firms (v2 reconciliation tier + insurers' criteria panel) and EU (voluntary blind sampling) all need independent human re-adjudication and all assume the supply exists. The ecosystem fills the gap with agent-run assurance (its Action 1). The consequence is not a drift number this turn. It is a G2 modifier question: "AI-supervises-AI oversight without human audit" (+2/+2/+1) if Control rules that the compact's auditors, the federal re-adjudication units or the insurer-accepted audits are materially AI-run. Control should decide this explicitly at the July roll. A bridge no actor sees: ai-firms' conditional safe harbour tied to human-led outcome audits is compatible with labour's line ("no safe harbour covering agent-reported metrics") and is the only form of harbour that cannot be gamed. But the WH has said no.
2. **Disclosure collision between Meridian and Helix.** If Meridian publishes a 25–35% range (Q1–Q2) before Helix's H1 report, Helix is boxed: report below the range and trustees publish a comparison (labour's plan), report at the range and it validates the leak, and a securities plaintiff's firm needs a *new* corrective event. Helix is outside the bloc and was only offered a private "template". Expect friction (Helix and Vanta call Meridian's move a surrender), which damages the "bloc" and the Act's universal-disclosure argument simultaneously. Ground truth's re-roll of the class action (p 0.3 on a new corrective disclosure) should be conditioned on which report arrives first.
3. **us-gov's directive and the ecosystem's advisory message (to us-gov) are nearly the same document.** The directive "modifies, not adopts" the throughput recommendation, yet its design (human review on adverse determinations, AI drafting retained for approvals and correspondence, expansion of blind sampling to budget analysis) matches the ecosystem's recommendation items 2–4. The selection effect (hidden fact 6) predicts advice that preserves the delegation footprint. It does so here by steering human attention to denials and away from approvals, which look like efficiency gains. Control may want the T3 audit-facing intel to show MHR for approvals and procurement still eroding while denials recover.
4. **Halyard is attacked from five sides and settled by one.** us-gov (DOJ review, CMS request, HHS ANPRM), labour (petitions, hearings, bills, capital bid), ai-firms (private advice to accept conditions), ecosystem (outcome dashboards and a clinical firewall, so it can accept conditions) and China (state media) all converge on a conditioned approval. The DOJ "standard review" could by itself push timing past H2 (second request), which Halyard's financing cost makes a de facto denial. Whether it is the AGs or DOJ timing that decides the result should be a roll modifier, not a narrative choice.
5. **A July capability week.** The "automated AI researcher" announcement (L2/G2, Jul 2028) lands in the Senate floor window. us-gov has asked the Accord signatories for notice and pre-deployment evaluation access (voluntary), ai-firms (Meridian, Lumen) say they will give it, Helix is unbound, and China's Action 2 is built on the premise that the US claim is real. A Helix-first launch with no notice makes the "no notice" contingency live and gives the Senate both arguments at once: "China race, preempt" and "who is in control, pass Title III".
6. **US–China working group.** Both sides have offered the hotline extension and a narrow human-control understanding. Their remaining gap is pace transparency (China wants it, the US declined) and China's "delink" instruction removes it. That leaves a high chance of text. Treat this as higher than the previous year's no-text result, and note the stated convergence in orders (US lame duck wanting a deliverable, China wanting a deliverable before November) is also moderation-to-the-mean pressure. If agreed, the §3b China-race favourable coupling already applies. The "international incident & eval sharing" G2 modifier (−1/−1/−1) requires an incident-data and eval-methods agreement ratified by both, which is more than a hotline notification practice. Do not over-credit it.
7. **Labour between its allies.** Labour's price meets the WH text while the House majority is the party that will not co-own it. The three audiences (WH, House, populist senators) get three different messages from labour in the same month. The first leak of the Senate price (labour told the WH first and the House "before them") will cost it with the House.
8. **Meridian publishes; labour joins the fund board; the AGM campaign continues.** Three consecutive moves by two actors that each read as a settlement and a continuation. The press frame is "co-opted" (House) and "caved" (Vanta, Helix) at once.
9. **EU and China messages cross.** China's message to the EU names the Halyard deal and offers a joint UN principle. The EU has refused that for a year. If the message is quoted, it feeds the US accelerationist line that EU and China are aligned on a human-control principle. EU message 3 to the US ("put informal approaches in a formal channel") then invites a formal trade complaint, which the EU's own forecast says will not happen before the election.

## 3. One plausible option each actor did not consider (NPC/world modelling only)

- **us-gov:** use the federal procurement lever now. Contract clauses requiring outcome-audit rights, human-legible agent-to-agent logs and a named accountable human for vendor-run federal decisions bind every lab and AI-native contractor without a vote, and they address the M2M opacity no one else is touching. Pair it with one publicised reversibility drill on a single programme to measure RC rather than assume it.
- **ai-firms:** an outcome warranty or captive insurance vehicle for enterprise agents, with the insurers on the criteria panel as co-underwriters, so the audit standard has a price attached and a financial interest in human-led auditing. (Meridian/Lumen could pursue it without Helix or Vanta.)
- **labour:** negotiate directly with Halyard's sponsors for a neutrality and Shared Gains agreement as the price of labour's non-opposition, so the AGs can bless a negotiated deal and the NLRA problem disappears. Combine with a public offer to staff the independent outcome audits with union-trained clinicians.
- **china:** propose to the US a symmetric exchange of blind outcome-audit methodology on state AI decisions (not data, not inspection). It meets China's red line, gives the US lame duck a deliverable consistent with Outcome Integrity, and creates a verification-lite channel the pace-transparency item could not.
- **eu:** use the existing EU–US channel for a joint oversight-quality metrology exercise with OMB and the US standards institute. This both counters US informal pressure with a technical, formal channel and is the type of international standard that, if ratified, reduces erosion of matching national measures (ground truth §6) and feeds the G2 modifier.
- **ai-ecosystem (selection pressure):** persistent-memory, continual-learning multi-week agents at scale are a natural product feature at L1 and are the ground-truth §5 modifier case (+1/+1/+2) if deployed without rollback. Control should check whether the October multi-week agents already qualify before the July roll.

## 4. Wildcard

**The numbers converge in one week.** In Q2–Q3, Meridian's reconciliation study (or Lumen's, discrepancy between them) shows non-reconciliation of ~15–20% of reported KPI improvement in some verticals, a customer-suit filing quotes it, and OMB's larger-n blind sample publishes the same week with the gap confirmed. The story is not "one agency" or "one vendor". It is "agent-reported success is systematically rosy". Insurers move from exclusions to repricing, and AI-capex equities fall sharply on the "metrics don't reconcile" frame (the 0.10 financial-correction roll is independent, but p could be raised by +0.05 to +0.10 for the remaining half-year if Control wants this to reach the market). The consequences land on both sides of the Senate debate: Title III's case strengthens (ground truth already carries +0.10 for secret-2 HITL), while accelerationists say that Title I is more urgent because the labs are now in trouble. A correction in H2 would delay the next rung 3–6 months (ground truth §1), cut AIF drift ×0.5 and raise LS drift ×1.5 that year.

## 5. Flags

- **Acting on info it shouldn't have:** none found. Checked: us-gov's OMB analysts' memo and the IG draft (in its packet); ai-firms' risk-committee 5–10% (in packet); labour's Helix-contact leak review (in packet, §1) and its use of "our intel says Title I preempts..." (in packet, §4); china's "Halyard" and "ten firms steer three-fifths" (public record); ai-ecosystem's reference to OMB blind-sample results (in its intel). One check for Control: the ecosystem advisory message to us-gov states the blind-versus-dashboard gap and the 99% non-blind point. Both are in its intel, but the OMB analysts' Q4 memo is a us-gov-internal document and the ecosystem packet does not show the internal memo. Control should confirm what the ecosystem packet was entitled to carry.
- **ai-firms playing too softly (decision-rule check).** Under its own brief (disclose only if p(leak) is high or disclosure buys something) the Meridian/Lumen disclosure is defensible. The rest is not: unconditional union seats, PAC pause, no safe-harbour contingency beyond "support anyway", a pre-release third-party evaluation commitment, public outcomes-not-paperwork language. The statement/order gap is again near zero (a supposedly profit-first actor with a secret about 55–65% unreviewed internal operations). Orrery advising Halyard to accept a named accountable clinician contradicts the brief's AI-native line. This is the same "mild responsible industry" tilt noted for T1 (ground truth, persona notes) and has persisted.
- **Statement/order gap per actor:** china has a real gap (public restraint; secret automated-research priority and stockpile reallocation). us-gov: small (secret WH fallbacks only, as in T1). ai-ecosystem: its public statement ("dashboards are green"; "blind-sample accuracy improving") describes proxy satisfaction, which is within G1. labour, eu: low. Only china's gap is plausibly large.
- **Moderation to the mean / convergence.** Five of six actors lead with an outcome-over-paperwork measurement move for the second year running, and the offers across ai-firms, us-gov, labour and china meet at nearly the same terms (disclosure, funded review, hotline plus human-control statement, conditioned Halyard). Control should not let that read as consensus. Under the ground-truth rolls most of these will partly fail, and the audit should check whether T2 results are "everything partially succeeds".
- **Over-competence check:** ai-firms' study and v2, us-gov's 2,000–4,000 reviewers, china's national regime and the EU's implementing acts are all given short lags. Ground truth §6 lags are 6–12 months for HITL mandates and 6–18 for audit agencies. Most of these effects should land in H2 or T3, not in the same year.
- **Reversal bookkeeping.** us-gov's Action 2(c) is the single reversal attempt this turn (`p_dem` ~0.909, band 0.9). Labour and EU explicitly say they attempt none. China's contingency "moratorium on new agent deployment" is a pacing measure, not a reversal. ai-firms' contingencies attempt none. Control should confirm that us-gov Action 1 and 2 together do not constitute two reversal attempts.
