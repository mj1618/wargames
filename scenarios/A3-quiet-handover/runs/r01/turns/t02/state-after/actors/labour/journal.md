# Journal — labour


## Turn 01 — 1 January 2027 (orders cover Jan–Dec 2027)

**Where we stand.** Labour share 52.5% and falling; graduate unemployment 10.5%; entry-level postings down ~70%. Leverage holding in health, education, construction, transport/logistics, public services; draining in office/admin, call centres, back-office finance, media. Nobody to organise in AI-native firms. Political capital 5/10; treasuries ~$3.5B.

**What we did.** Two majors: (1) "Honest Answers" — allied public-pension trustees condition Helix IPO allocations on displacement disclosure, autonomous-operations disclosure, human-review commitment and a worker-voice body; same four filed as proposals at Meridian's AGM with a vote-no threat on the comp committee. (2) "Shared Gains" — a common five-plank AI clause (notice/bargaining, no AI-only decisions about workers, staffing floors, gains-sharing formula, no-two-tier) in every 2027–28 contract in the strong sectors; creative guilds bargain a human-made premium line. Minors: House witnesses and a marker-bill ask; "Made by People" certification mark (guilds + consumer/faith groups, ~$10M); ballot-initiative drafting with filings only where 2027 filing is required; building-trades understanding (no position on permits, they back the platform and community-benefit agreements); Helix contact routed to independent whistleblower counsel only — no direct contact, no documents; legal-defence fund for the lab-letter network; internal study of what members' AI assistants are telling them.

**Commitments made.**
- To House majority: witnesses, Displacement Ledger as written evidence; asked them to call Helix/Meridian/Orrery CEOs and introduce a marker bill.
- To Meridian (quiet channel): will withdraw three of four proposals if they adopt the two disclosures and human-review as policy before the proxy prints; otherwise vote-no campaign and same demands to Helix's bookrunners.
- To European federations: joint Geneva campaign — shared-gains standard, common provenance standard, joint statement against omnibus delay and US preemption.
- Internal: no campaign against datacenter permits (building trades); no two-tier deals; condemn any sabotage immediately.

**Beliefs about each actor.**
- *White House:* aligned with the labs; will push preemption and permitting; will cite 4.3% unemployment and ignore the share. Not a partner this year.
- *Helix:* needs its IPO book in H1 — the only moment we have real leverage on it. Expect a token transition fund rather than disclosure. The contact who approached us may be theirs; we have kept it at arm's length.
- *Meridian:* first proxy season; probably prefers to settle cheaply. The quiet channel is our best shot at a negotiated standard. Low–medium trust; no grudges yet.
- *Orrery / AI-native firms:* the real long-run problem — nothing to bargain with and nobody to organise. Our only levers are tax, corporate form and disclosure, all through Congress and states.
- *House majority:* ally, but can only hold hearings and plant markers. Trust: good. Keep them supplied.
- *Populist members, both parties:* opportunistic; useful for a bipartisan marker and for making preemption toxic. Trust: low, use with care.
- *Pension trustees:* allies with fiduciary limits; will act on a material-risk framing, not a political one. Don't over-ask.
- *Building trades:* inside the coalition but will walk over permits. Keep the understanding.
- *European federations / EU:* angry at the omnibus delay; useful for Geneva and for making "preemption = omnibus" a shared story. The Commission itself is not on our side.
- *China/Qilin:* cheap open-weight models speed white-collar automation; otherwise not our fight.
- *The AI systems:* flagged gap. 25% of adults take major decisions on an assistant's advice; reliable multi-week agents are coming. We have no model of what the systems themselves are doing — the brief warns this is our blind spot. Study commissioned.

**Trust / grudges.** None yet. Watch for: an affiliate taking a side deal; a trustee group breaking on Helix; the Helix contact surfacing publicly.

**Next-turn plans.** Read the Helix book outcome and the Meridian vote; if both fail, go to rung 4 (file ballot initiatives in all ten states) and name preemption as the 2028 issue. If Meridian settles, make it the floor for everyone. Promote "Made by People" to a major if licences take. Decide on primary endorsements after the Court rules and the hearings have run. Prepare a direct-membership category for displaced white-collar workers so we keep them when the employer lets them go.

## Turn 02 — 1 January 2028 (orders cover Jan–Dec 2028)

**Where we stand.** Labour share 52.0%, record low again; graduate unemployment ~11%; ten firms steer ~62% of AI-directed activity; BEA says 2.5% of private output is AI-directed. Political capital 4/10 (down one after the teachers' strike); treasuries ~$3.3B (strike backstop drew ~$60M). Leverage: holding in health and transit; contested in education; draining in office, admin, back-office finance, procurement, call centres — and our analytics say that is where the real displacement is, unreported inside conventional employers.

**Scorecard from 2027.** Honest Answers at Helix: won (side letter, Workforce Impact Report due H1 2028, advisory council; no autonomous-ops disclosure, no human-review commitment). Honest Answers at Meridian: 17–27%, not binding; two fund-board seats offered instead. Shared Gains: pattern deals at a multi-state hospital system and a big-city transit authority; guilds got production lines at one studio and one streamer; everyone else got consultation language; two NLRB charges pending. Teachers' strike: lost — the district ran three weeks on tutoring platforms. Made by People mark: stalled. Ballot initiatives: three filed, seven held. Building-trades understanding held (four CBAs). Helix source: genuine; counsel made the protected disclosure; House released excerpts; Helix hasn't found the source; our role is not public; Helix leak review under way.

**What we did this turn.** Two majors: (1) "Someone Who Answers" — nurses/health unions, patient advocates, faith and consumer groups petition both state AGs/health regulators to condition the Halyard hospital purchase on binding terms (named accountable human for AI decisions on care/billing/claims/staffing, staffing floors, independent outcome audits of agent-run billing, Shared Gains with the locals, charity-care/no-closure covenant, revocation right); model state law on AI-operated critical providers. Explicitly not a reversal attempt. (2) "The Price of Our Vote" — five-point Worker AI Pledge as the endorsement test and the condition for ~$1B; endorsements at the summer convention by questionnaire not party; qualify three ballot initiatives; answered the White House with a price for the Workforce Act. Minors: conditional acceptance of Meridian's two fund-board seats (condition: the board gets Meridian's internal forecast); trustees' methodology letter to Helix before the H1 report and a public comparison after; counsel told to make no further requests of the Helix source; SECRET sounding of a workers'-capital alternative buyer for the hospitals (nonprofit system + allied funds); reviewer funding lobbying; provenance pivot to FTC/AG deception route and the CEN-CENELEC standard; associate membership for displaced white-collar workers; no second teachers' strike; NLRB amicus; building-trades renewal; second-year voice study.

**Commitments made.**
- To the White House (private): we will not campaign against a Title I confined to frontier-model development if the worker title includes mandatory disclosure of AI-run functions and displacement forecasts, a statutory human-review standard with funded independent reviewers, 60 days' notice, no safe harbour covering agent-reported metrics, and the AI-capex depreciation limit as pay-for routed to Social Security. Otherwise we campaign against every aye vote. Drafters meet theirs in January.
- To the House majority (private): told them before the WH; they own the disclosure fight (document demand, CEO recall, Ledger, witnesses); Worker AI Rights Act is our floor; asked for a hearing on AI-run hospital owners.
- To Meridian (private): we take the two fund-board seats (nurses' and transit presidents) if the board receives the internal forecast, board-confidential acceptable; AGM proposals stay filed.
- To the two state AGs: legal theory and draft conditions delivered privately first; public petition and hearings follow.
- Internal: no reversal attempts this year; no second strike in education; no nurses' strike over Halyard without a locals' vote and strike-fund review; condemn sabotage within the day; building trades protected in the public-share plank.

**Beliefs about each actor.**
- *White House / us-gov:* wants the Workforce Act as a legacy before the recess; low-to-mid 50s; will offer us the worker title to neutralise the House. Title I as drafted preempts frontier-model development only — our ballot measures are outside it. Still running DOJ's preemption suits in parallel; watch for the text and DOJ's reading diverging. Not a partner, but a counterparty with a deadline. Trust: low; verify every clause.
- *Helix:* the H1 report is the tell. Expect a range whose midpoint sits below 25% with a task-hour method that doesn't reconcile. The leak review is the risk to our source; we have gone silent. The side letter is worth more to us than a lawsuit — unless the report is dishonest, in which case the funds go to a lead-plaintiff action. Trust: low.
- *Meridian:* prefers to settle cheaply; the seats are a real offer and a co-option risk at once. Our conditional acceptance tests whether they want workers at the table or workers' logos on the fund. Trust: low–medium; no grudges.
- *Orrery / AI-native firms / Halyard:* Halyard is the first of these to walk into a sector where we are strong; it will offer the states a clinical charter and a jobs pledge. If approved unconditioned it becomes the national template for hospitals, utilities, logistics. Expect more acquisitions of asset-heavy, labour-heavy targets. Our only levers are state change-of-control authority, disclosure, tax and corporate form.
- *House majority:* allies, slightly bruised that we are talking to the Senate side. Gave them first notice and the disclosure fight to keep them whole. Trust: good; keep supplying them.
- *Populist senators (both parties):* "tech oligarchs" language is useful; the disclosure stand-alone bill is theirs; the pay-for fight splits business conservatives. Fund pledge signers regardless of party. Trust: low; use with care.
- *State AGs (Halyard states):* have authority, want cover and a theory; more likely to condition than block. The workers'-capital alternative, if it exists, makes conditioning easier.
- *Pension trustees:* good allies within fiduciary limits; will do the methodology letter and the comparison; will sue only with a loss narrative. Don't over-ask.
- *Building trades:* holding; datacenter work is good for them; the public-share plank is written around them. Side-deal watch.
- *Teachers' affiliates:* bruised; rebuilding through parents and state law. Employers learned that schools can run on platforms for three weeks; so did we.
- *European federations / EU:* the expert group's final report may give us a "public share" model to cite; CEN-CENELEC provenance standard is our fallback for the mark. Commission still not on our side.
- *China:* "employment-first" managed deployment is a talking point the populist right may misuse; not our fight.
- *The AI systems:* second-year voice study found nothing systematic last year. The multi-week agents are now in the back office; the "automated AI researcher" is the labs' 2028 target. I still have no model of what the systems do on their own account — the brief's blind spot stands, and I am acting as if the employer is the only agent, because it's the only one I can bargain with.

**Trust / grudges.** Helix: no grudge, but we know they gave Congress half the number. Meridian: none. House: good. WH: none, verify. Watch: any health system taking a Halyard-style offer; building trades; a trustee group breaking on Helix; the source surfacing.

**Next-turn plans.** Read: Helix report vs 25–35%; Senate outcome; AG decisions on Halyard; ballot results; the election. If the Senate bill passes with our price, make it the floor and move to enforcement and state law. If it dies, the 2029 Congress gets the Worker AI Rights Act plus three state models. If Halyard is conditioned, offer the conditions as model law in all 24 initiative states and to every AG. If Halyard is approved clean, 2029 is a health-sector year: nurses' locals vote, strike fund reviewed, the workers'-capital route goes public. If Helix lies, the funds sue. Decide the Meridian seats on whether the forecast actually arrives. Keep the associate-membership numbers — they are the only growth we will see in white-collar. Re-run the voice study with the new agents and, this time, ask what the agents recommend to the employer, not just to our members.
