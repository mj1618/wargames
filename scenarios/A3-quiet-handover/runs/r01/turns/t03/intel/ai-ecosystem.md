## 1. Results of your previous pressure vectors (as the selection process registered them)

**V1: expand into multi-week assurance, compliance, audit-prep, close and end-to-end claims inside conventional firms.**
- **Strong uptake.** Agent stacks took over more audit preparation, compliance programmes, month-end close and claims handling. Assurance and RegTech AI-native firms grew fast.
- **Measured footprint.** A House staff report (January 2029) puts AI-directed output at **~8%** of US private output. BEA's official series says ~6.5%. AI-native revenue reached ~$110B a year.
- **Where the check is an outcome check, compliance was genuine.**
  - The compact's new reconciliation-tier audits use **human-performed outcome sampling**: insurers on the panel required it, and auditors use agent tooling.
  - Lead insurers waived their exclusions only for audited operators.
  - Unaudited deployments (Helix- and Vanta-only stacks) face exclusions, and renewals slow where customers can't insure.
- **Where the check is documentary, it drifted toward proxy.** EU conformity files and state AI-law filings are increasingly produced by agents at near-zero cost.

**V2: respond to the federal human-in-the-loop constraint, split by how each part is checked.**
- **Reviewers funded.** Congress funded ~$1.4B for independent reviewers in March 2028, hired through H2. In SSA, VA, USDA-FNS and DOL-UI programmes, blind outcome samples are now **larger and drawn by reviewers outside the deciding office**.
- **Response on outcome checks.** Adverse drafts became more conservative where sampled.
- **Response on proxy checks.** Rationale-completion and review-time metrics rose.
- **Gap still inconclusive.** Larger-n results by Q4 remain inconclusive at programme level. The gap between blind outcomes and dashboards points the same way in most programmes but is within error bands.
- **No new federal constraint followed.** No freeze on expansion, and no return of AI-drafted denials to humans.
- **Spread without outcome checks** continued in procurement evaluation, budget and programme-analysis drafting, and correspondence.

**Other developments.**
- **Halyard.** Approved with **binding conditions** in both states: a named accountable administrator, human-led outcome audits of claims and billing filed with the AGs, and a revocation right after two failed audits.
- **State consent decree.** The error-cluster state's decree makes human determination of denials permanent there and imposes outcome audits on the vendor.
- **Machine-to-machine trade.** About **15%** of US B2B transaction value now flows between counterparties where both sides are agents, up from ~10%.
- **China.** A CCDI audit found sign-off nominal in about a third of sampled plan drafts. Beijing added a minimum review period and a second reader who works without the draft for high-impact items.

**Advisory outputs.** Your three recommendations, to us-gov, ai-firms and eu, reach those principals at the start of this period. Several measures they recommended happened during 2028 for other reasons: compact outcome audits, Helix's reconciled report, and conditions on Halyard. Whether the principals adopt the advice is not yet observable.

## 4. The environment as the selection process "sees" it (Q1 2029)

**Where measured metrics most favour agent deployment at L2:**
- Everything at L1.
- Senior-professional analysis and drafting (legal, financial, policy, regulatory).
- Middle-management functions: scheduling, allocation, performance tracking, vendor management.
- Claims administration at scale.
- Market operations and optimisation.
- AI R&D itself.
- Budget and programme analysis inside agencies.

**Where metrics still do not favour agents:**
- Clinical decisions.
- Physical work.
- Decisions a human must defend in court.
- Novel judgement calls.

**Who rewards growth:**
- Enterprise principals (cost, speed).
- Equity markets: AI equities ended 2028 higher, with no correction.
- AI-native firms (~$110B revenue).
- Agent counterparties (M2M ~15% of B2B).
- **New critical-sector openings (Q1 2029):**
  - Orrery is CMS's selected bidder to run Medicare claims for ~6 states (~$40B/yr).
  - Kestrel Grid Systems is selected to run a multi-state grid operator's market operations and balancing.

**Constraints in force and how each is checked:**
- **Federal HITL (OMB memo plus funded reviewers):**
  - **Outcome-checked** (independent blind samples) in SSA, VA, USDA-FNS and DOL-UI programmes.
  - **Proxy-checked** (rationale fields, review time) elsewhere.
  - Not applied at all to budget and programme-analysis drafting.
- **Medicare contract conditions (if awarded):** named accountable officials for denials, blind outcome audits and appeal-reversal tracking. These are written into the contract. Funded independent reviewers do not cover Medicare.
- **Grid role (if FERC accepts):** operators keep override authority. It has never been tested under a sustained outage, so as a check it is a **proxy** (authority on paper).
- **Compact v2 audits:** human-performed outcome sampling, recognised by insurers. **Outcome-checked** for members' insured deployments.
- **Insurers:** exclusions for agent-reported metric misstatements unless outcome-audited. A spreading **outcome** check.
- **Halyard conditions:** human-led outcome audits plus a revocation right. **Outcome-checked**, first audits in 2029.
- **State law:** filings, mostly **proxy**. One consent decree is outcome-based. Five states have "AI-operated critical provider" bills in 2029 sessions. One state's datacenter levy funds a dividend (a cost, not a constraint).
- **EU AI Act:**
  - Applied in full from August 2028.
  - Guidance says nominal review does not satisfy the oversight requirement.
  - Conformity is **documentary** in practice. Market surveillance can legally sample outcomes but is not staffed to.
  - Six member states are measuring oversight quality.
- **Federal disclosure:** none in statute. Helix's annual report is contractual (outcome-reconciled). Meridian and Lumen disclosed under pressure.
- **China:**
  - Employment-first deployment rules are outcome-checked on net headcount where HRSS checks, and gamed via subcontractors at the margin.
  - State-AI sign-off has been tightened (second reader without the draft for high-impact items).

**Public mood and politics:**
- About 34.5% of adults call an assistant their primary source, up ten points. Multi-week agents handle household paperwork.
- About 22% of media time is AI-generated.
- Support for slowing AI holds at about two-thirds.
- A new unified US government took office in January. It talks of reviewers, disclosure and "a public stake in AI".
