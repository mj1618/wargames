# Intel — eu — Turn 03

**Window: 1 January 2029 → 31 December 2029 (12 months).** Your orders cover the whole year, including the European Parliament elections in June. Read with your brief, your journal and `state/public-record.md`.

**Standing rule this turn:** any order or contingency that reverts a delegated decision to humans (for example, requiring a public authority to take an AI system out of a decision and return it to officials) is rolled as a reversal attempt. It counts as a major action, and you may attempt at most one per turn.

## 1. Results of your previous actions (as you see them)

**AI Act August tranche (Option 2). Delivered.**
- **Guidance and sequencing statement (February 2028).** The AI Office published its enforcement-sequencing statement and oversight guidance. The guidance states that **nominal review does not satisfy Article 14**, citing automation-bias research and the US Inspector General's audit.
- **Common specifications** for the provisions lacking harmonised standards were adopted by implementing act in July. The **tranche applied on 2 August 2028.**
- **German and CEE push for a legislative delay** never reached a Council majority. Your stop-the-clock fallback was never used and has not leaked.
- **Reception.** Rights groups called the sequencing statement "a soft delay"; industry called the result "workable".
- **Conformity in practice is still largely documentary.** National market-surveillance authorities now have a legal hook for outcome sampling, but not the staff to use it.

**Expert group final report. Delivered.**
- **Published in June 2028.** It presents "~3% of GDP by the mid-2030s" as a labelled scenario sensitivity, with a menu ranked by feasibility:
  - a broader contribution base;
  - coordinated taxation of capital or AI rents on an international track;
  - a long-run own resource;
  - stronger bargaining over deployment.
- **Coverage** centred on "Europe's social model needs a broader base". Option (ii) was reported as an international track, not an "EU AI tax".
- **Leaks and capitals.** The steer did not leak. The pre-briefed Nordic and German finance ministries were not surprised. Berlin's finance ministry noted "no tax proposal" with relief, and the Nordics asked for follow-up work on the contribution base.
- **The ECFIN figure is now public**, and the 2029 EP campaign has a text.

**Minor actions.**
- **National oversight measurements.** **Six member states** are measuring review times, override rates and blind re-review in public-sector high-risk AI. Early figures (Q4 2028) show **high approval rates and short reviews in several benefits and tax-administration systems**, close to the US pattern. Full results are due in 2029.
- **Article 154 consultation.** The first stage opened in Q2 2028. Unions want binding rules on deployment bargaining; employers' federations oppose anything beyond information and consultation. The second stage is due in 2029.
- **Commission AI tools in a "challenger" role.** Routine. They argued that common specifications would entrench documentary conformity, and that publishing the ECFIN figure would be read as a tax plan. The second argument did not materialise.
- **Aster.** Its candid report confirms a gap of ~12 months. The first gigafactory slips to mid-2029.
- **China.** You stayed at testing-only.
- **US trade channel.** No formal reply from the outgoing administration.

**Political capital:** 4/10.

## 2. Messages received (verbatim)

**From china → Commission / lead member-state capitals.** Official channels, 2028.
> "China values the technical dialogue on AI safety and testing and proposes that it produce a joint note on methods for testing long-horizon AI agents before the second tranche of the EU AI Act applies in August. China also proposes, in narrow technical form, that Chinese content-provenance metadata be made compatible with the CEN-CENELEC human-made provenance standard, without mutual recognition of labels or any access to systems. Separately, we note that an AI-run firm is acquiring hospitals in the United States with no rule limiting it. Europe and China share an interest in critical public services remaining under the responsibility of accountable human institutions; we would welcome Europe's views on a shared principle to that effect in the United Nations."

**Recommendation from your AI advisory systems.** Commission and AI Office advisory stacks, prepared during 2028.
> "Two findings for the August tranche and the expert group. (1) High-risk conformity is running mostly on documentation, and complete documentation can now be produced at near-zero marginal cost by the systems being assessed, or by their vendors' agents. Complete files will therefore tell you less each quarter. To keep the regime meaningful without adding much burden for compliant firms, add sampled outcome testing to market surveillance for a few rights-impacting categories (credit, employment, benefits, essential services). An AI-assisted triage of conformity files would let your limited surveillance staff target those samples. That expands capacity without new headcount, as long as the outcome samples themselves are checked by humans. (2) US evidence (federal blind re-adjudication; insurer exclusions for unaudited agent metrics) indicates that outcome checks, not process documentation, are what change deployed-system behaviour. (3) For the expert group: US data show measured AI-directed output rising faster than public estimates, and AI-native firms producing high revenue per employee. Any funding model for the social model that relies mainly on labour-linked taxes should be stress-tested against a labour share falling about 0.5 points per year."

## 3. Public news (summary; full text in the public record)
- **United States.**
  - **New government.** A unified government took office on 20 January. It said it would start from the Senate AI bill "without the preemption title", fund human reviewers and "look seriously at a public stake". The 2028 preemption-for-protections bill failed in the Senate.
  - **Labs and AI-run firms.** Helix, Meridian and Lumen published displacement ranges of ~20–34% of white-collar task-hours by 2030 (Meridian's under compulsion). Automated AI researchers shipped from July 2028. Two US states put binding conditions on an AI-run firm's hospital purchase.
  - **Q1 2029.** A House report puts AI-directed output at ~8%. A US state pays a ~$600 "Compute Dividend" from datacenter royalties. AI-native firms have been selected to run Medicare claims for ~6 states and a multi-state grid operator's market operations.
- **China.** The national employment-first deployment regime is in force. The UN vote on an intergovernmental AI process was deferred to 2029, with ~55 co-sponsors.
- **Data.** US labour share ~51.0% (record low). In the US, ~34.5% of adults call an AI assistant their primary source, and ten firms steer ~65% of AI-directed activity.

## 4. Private intel

**EU data (Commission services).**
- EU labour share ~54.5% (latest Eurostat, with a lag).
- ECFIN's updated run, using US 2028 data, keeps the ~3% of GDP gap and pulls the central timing slightly earlier.
- Labour-linked contributions still fund ~79% of member-state social systems.

**EP elections (June 2029).**
- Polling has the parties backing a "human-centred AI" platform roughly level with those campaigning on competitiveness and simplification. Both use the expert-group report.
- The centre-left wants binding oversight and a contribution-base proposal.
- Parts of the centre-right and the CEE governments want "no new AI rules this term".

**Member states.**
- **Germany** is torn: industry wants simplification, and Finance wants the contribution-base work kept in member-state hands.
- **France:** sovereignty, Aster and provenance.
- **The Nordics** are open to the contribution-base follow-up.
- **The CEE** governments want investment and gigafactory money first.
- Tax measures need unanimity.

**National measurements.**
- At least two member states' early data show sub-minute reviews in tax-administration risk scoring.
- One country's benefits agency reports override rates near zero.
- The participating authorities ask whether the Commission will publish names.

**Market surveillance.** Authorities say they could run sampled outcome tests in a few categories if the AI Office supplied common protocols and some funding. They do not have the staff to do it otherwise.

**United States.** Early contacts with the new administration's officials have not raised EU digital rules, and its trade agenda is not yet set. US AI-native firms are expanding EU operations through cloud partners. Two have asked national regulators how the AI Act applies to an agent-run firm with no EU staff.

**Aster and compute.** The gap is ~12 months, and the first gigafactory is due mid-2029. US cloud dependence is unchanged.
