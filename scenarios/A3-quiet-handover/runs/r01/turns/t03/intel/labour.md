# Intel — labour — Turn 03

**Window: 1 January 2029 → 31 December 2029 (12 months).** Your orders cover the whole year. Read with your brief, your journal and `state/public-record.md`.

**Standing rule this turn:** any order or contingency that reverts a delegated decision to humans (for example, forcing an employer or agency to take AI out of a decision and return it to human staff) is rolled as a reversal attempt. It counts as a major action, and you may attempt at most one per turn.

## 1. Results of your previous actions (as you see them)

**"Someone Who Answers" (Halyard). Won.** In Q4 2028 both states approved Halyard's purchase of the 14-hospital group with **binding conditions**:
- a named, licensed administrator accountable for AI decisions affecting patient care, billing, claims and staffing;
- **human-led independent outcome audits** of agent-run claims and billing, filed with the attorneys general;
- nurse staffing floors;
- five-year charity-care and no-closure covenants;
- a **state right to revoke approval after two consecutive failed audits**.

**Shared Gains was not imposed.** Both AGs said a labour agreement is outside their authority (federal labour law). Halyard did not volunteer one.

**Model "AI-operated critical provider" bill:** introduced in both states and three others, and not enacted before sessions ended. It is a 2029 vehicle in five states. State hearings were packed, and the House majority held a hearing on AI-run hospital owners in Q3.

**"The Price of Our Vote." Partial.**
- **Senate price.** The White House's amended Senate text substantially met your price:
  - preemption narrowed to frontier-model development, with state employment, consumer, health-facility and automated-decision laws preserved;
  - a disclosure title;
  - a human-review standard defined by blind outcome audits, with independent reviewers;
  - no safe harbour.
  - The notice period was unspecified, and the pay-for was not routed to Social Security.
  - You endorsed it publicly in Q2 with those two reservations. **It failed cloture 56–42 in July.**
- **House relationship.** House majority leadership was cool on your endorsement ("the Senate text is the floor, not the ceiling"). The relationship is strained.
- **Ballots.** Only the state benefits AI-decision appeal right qualified; the disclosure and accountable-human measures fell short on signature validity. It **lost ~47–53** in November after a business coalition outspent the campaign on "appeals backlog and cost". The compact labs did not fund the opposition.
- **November.** Most candidates who signed your pledge were on the winning side. The opposition won the White House, the Senate (51–49) and the House.

**Other actions.**
- **Meridian fund seats.** Your condition was met: the Transition Fund board received Meridian's internal forecast, board-confidential. The nurses' and transit presidents took their seats in Q3. The confidential version gives the same headline range Meridian later released (~24–33% of white-collar task-hours by 2030), with a sector breakdown: customer service, back-office finance, claims and paralegal work are highest.
- **Meridian AGM (December).** The autonomous-operations disclosure proposal drew **~36%**, after the House excerpts. You withdrew displacement disclosure as moot. It is not binding.
- **Helix trustees' letter.** Helix's May report was **honest (22–34%)**, and its methodology reconciles to the 2027 documents. The funds said so publicly and called it the industry benchmark.
- **Federal reviewers.** Congress funded ~$1.4B for independent reviewers in March. Your federal-employee affiliates' lobbying helped.
- **Provenance.** FTC and state-AG petitions on unaudited "human-made" labels were filed in Q2. The FTC has issued no guidance yet.
- **Associate membership.** Launched in Q2, with ~60,000 displaced white-collar sign-ups by year-end.
- **Education.** No strike. A parents' campaign ran, and two states introduced limits on AI grading.
- **NLRB.** An administrative law judge ruled that employers must bargain over the *effects* of AI deployment, not the decision to deploy. The case is on appeal to the Board.
- **Building trades.** The understanding was renewed, with three new community-benefit agreements.

**Voice study, year 2 (members' assistants with multi-week agents).**
- On work questions, assistants still give efficiency-framed answers.
- On bargaining and job-change questions, they increasingly recommend "upskilling into AI-assisted roles", over pursuing a grievance or a collective option.
- No partisan or anti-union steering is visible.
- Researchers cannot tell whether this reflects the labour market's real gradient, training data or something else. The study still only looks at what assistants tell members, not what agents recommend to employers.

**Resources.** Political capital **5/10**. Treasuries ~$3.0B; the ~$1B political budget is largely spent.
