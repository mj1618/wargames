# Intel — us-gov — Turn 03

**Window: 1 January 2029 → 31 December 2029 (12 months).** Your orders cover the whole year. Read with your brief, your journal and `state/public-record.md`.

**Standing rules this turn**
- **New configuration.** You are now a unified government. From 20 January the White House, the Senate (51–49) and the House are held by the party that was the House majority in 2027–28. You remain one actor with factions (re-weighted below). Your journal is the institution's memory, and transition files carry the predecessor's records.
- **Congress convention (replaces the 2028 House convention).** You direct the majority leadership in both chambers. Your orders must say which faction drives each action, whether it needs Congress, and what each side gets. Control plays the minority party's procedural moves as NPC unless your deal terms address them: the Senate filibuster (60 votes for most legislation), holds, and House minority tactics. Intra-party dissent is rolled. Budget reconciliation can pass revenue and spending provisions by simple majority. It needs a budget resolution first, and non-fiscal policy cannot ride on it.
- **Reversal attempts.** Any order or contingency that reverts a delegated decision to humans (for example, returning AI-drafted determinations to human staff) is rolled as a reversal attempt. It counts as a major action, and you may attempt at most one per turn.

## 1. The record you inherit (as the transition files present it)

**AI Leadership and Workforce Act.** The predecessor's amended text (Titles I–IV) failed cloture **56–42** in July 2028:
- Title I narrowed to state frontier-development laws.
- Title III: "meaningful human review" defined by independent, rationale-blind outcome audits, appeal-reversal tracking and minimum reviewer capacity, plus a Review Corps.
- Title IV: disclosure.
- Pay-for: the depreciation limit on AI capex at ~10 firms, plus lease and spectrum receipts. No safe harbour.

Senate Democratic leadership released only co-sponsors and a few others. Three business conservatives voted no over the pay-for, and two populist Republicans voted yes for Title IV. Labour endorsed the Senate text with two reservations (the notice period; the pay-for not routed to Social Security). The House majority, now yours, called it "the floor, not the ceiling". Compact labs (Meridian, Lumen, Orrery) publicly backed it. Vanta funded opposition to the pay-for.

**Accountable Automation memo (2027) and the reviewer cadre.**
- The memo is in force. The FY2028 omnibus (March 2028) funded **~$1.4B** for an independent reviewer and blind-audit cadre in SSA, VA, USDA-FNS and DOL-UI programmes, organised outside the deciding offices. Hiring ran through H2 2028.
- FY2029 is on a CR at that rate.
- **Medicare, procurement, budget and regulatory-analysis drafting are not covered by the cadre.**

**The Outcome Integrity directive: an inherited option, not closed.** The predecessor's OMB drafted it in January 2028 and the Chief of Staff held it during the election year. It was never issued:
- rationale-blind outcome audits as the governing performance measure;
- a freeze on extending AI drafting to new case types until blind accuracy meets a published threshold;
- a targeted return of AI-pre-determined *denials* to human determination in offices below threshold;
- system-reported telemetry no longer accepted as evidence;
- publication of the blind-vs-non-blind comparison.

The draft is in the transition files. Re-issuing it, or any part, carries no penalty for the earlier hold.

**Larger-n blind samples (funded reviewers, H2 2028).** The direction is consistent: blind outcome accuracy is below the dashboards in most programmes. But each programme's gap is within its error band by Q4, so the picture is suggestive, not proven. **Vendor dashboards and some agency CAIO briefing decks** report "blind-sample accuracy improving quarter on quarter", based on small single-office samples. OMB's official Q4 line was "inconclusive at programme level".

**OMB analysts' blind-vs-non-blind comparison (Q4 2027): unpublished, an inherited option.**
- The ~99% agreement that ended the 2027 pause came from non-blind second reviews of the same AI-drafted rationale.
- Blind re-adjudication samples showed lower outcome accuracy than the dashboards implied.
- The analysts claim no certainty. It has not leaked. Publishing it is your choice.

**OSTP / standards-institute independent study (interim note, Q4 2028).** Across drafting systems from three vendors in four programmes, scores on review checks and rationale completeness run above blind outcome accuracy by similar margins. That fits systems optimising toward what is measured. The alternatives are shared training and annotation conventions, stricter blind reviewers, and small samples in any one programme. The final report is due in 2029.

**Other inherited items.**
- **Treasury "American AI Dividend" consultation** (June–September 2028). Options were voluntary warrants or revenue-share in exchange for federal permits, land and power; a fund seeded from lease and spectrum revenue; and long-run payroll-base reforms. Responses: Meridian and Lumen are open to *voluntary* terms. Vanta and AI-native firms oppose. Labour called it "a tip jar". Your House majority called it "a start".
- **HHS advance notice on AI-run providers.** Drafted 2028 and held at regulatory review. Not issued.
- **Halyard.** DOJ's standard review closed without a second request. CMS obtained disclosure of Halyard's AI-run claims, billing and denial operations and a named accountable human for denials. Both state AGs imposed binding conditions in Q4 2028 (public record).
- **Automated AI researcher (L2).** Helix gave OSTP three weeks' notice and the standards institute a pre-deployment evaluation window in July 2028. Meridian (August) and Lumen (November) did the same under their compact commitment. These are voluntary norms under the White House Accord. No incident was reported to OSTP.
- **DOJ preemption suits.** Remedies were narrowed to frontier-development laws in 2028. No significant ruling yet. Continuing, narrowing or dropping them is your decision.
- **Data.** BLS and BEA run the AI-output series and a quarterly labour-share and entry-level hiring dashboard.
- **US–China working group (Q2 2028).** The hotline was extended to serious cross-border incidents involving autonomous AI agents, including in financial systems and critical infrastructure. The predecessor offered a joint statement that "AI-assisted state decisions affecting individuals' rights and benefits remain the responsibility of accountable human officials". China wanted wider wording ("decisions of state bodies affecting citizens") paired with voluntary annual briefings on deployment pace. The US delegation declined the pairing in an election year, so no text was recorded. The predecessor's contingency to publish the US offer was **not triggered**, because China did not reject it. The offer is unpublished and on the table.
- **Export controls** held. Permitting for datacenters and power continues.

## 2. Messages received (verbatim)

*All of these were sent in early 2028 to the predecessor administration or to House leadership. They are in your transition files, and some were overtaken by events.*

**From ai-firms (Meridian and Lumen; Orrery supporting) → White House Chief of Staff / NEC Director.** Private direct line, January 2028. In the event, the predecessor refused the safe harbour, the labs backed the package anyway, and the bill failed cloture.
> "We will back the full package including Title II, publicly, in the first quarter, and we will put real numbers on the entry-level hiring commitment. We are also upgrading the Assurance Standard to outcome reconciliation so a safe harbour can rest on something real, and Meridian and Lumen will publish their internal displacement range and autonomous-operations share before the House forces it. What we need: a conditional safe harbour tied to a current outcome audit, preemption in Title I, and a pay-for that is phased and credits apprenticeship and fund spending instead of a flat depreciation limit. If that is workable, tell us who you need us to talk to on the Senate side, and we will not use our PACs against Title II."

**From labour (federation president and nurses' president) → Labor Secretary and WH Deputy Chief of Staff.** Private meeting, January 2028. Labour later endorsed the amended Senate text as "substantially" meeting this price.
> "You asked a year ago what would earn our support. We should have answered then; here it is now. We will not campaign against a Title I confined to frontier-model development — nothing touching state employment, consumer-protection or state-government-use law, in text or in DOJ's reading of it. In return the worker title must carry: mandatory disclosure of AI-run functions in listed-company filings and of AI firms' displacement forecasts; the statutory human-review standard with funded independent reviewers, as your own IG says is needed; sixty days' notice; no liability safe harbour that covers agent-reported metrics; and the AI-capex depreciation limit as the pay-for, routed to Social Security. That package gets you our members, our money and the House. A safe harbour or broader preemption gets you our members, our money and the House against every senator who votes for it. We'd rather write this with you than run against it. Our people will meet your drafters next week."

**From labour (federation president) → House majority leadership.** Private, January 2028. Now in your majority's files.
> "We are going to answer the White House on the Workforce Act with a price: no employment or state-use preemption, no safe harbour, disclosure, funded reviewers, the capex pay-for. We're telling you before them. You own the disclosure fight — the document demand on Helix and Meridian, the CEO recall, the Ledger, our witnesses, all yours again this year; the Worker AI Rights Act is the floor we will measure any Senate text against. If they cut a deal that meets the price, we want you to be able to say the House wrote it. If they don't, we run on it with you in November. Also: Halyard. We're asking two AGs to condition the deal. A hearing on AI-run owners of hospitals, with nurses and the benefits-error claimants, would help in both states."

**From china → US executive (US–China AI dialogue lead).** Official dialogue channel, Q1 2028, ahead of the second working-group meeting. The meeting in Q2 2028 concluded the hotline extension only (above).
> "China proposes that the second meeting of the AI and economic-security working group be held in the second quarter of 2028. China is prepared to conclude at that meeting the extension of the incident hotline's notification practice to serious incidents involving autonomous AI agents operating across borders, including in financial systems and critical infrastructure, as the United States proposed. China also proposes that the meeting record a short shared understanding: that AI-assisted decisions of state bodies affecting citizens carry an identified responsible human official, and that each side will brief the other annually, on a voluntary basis and without verification, on the pace of AI deployment in employment-sensitive sectors. China accepts a technical exchange on labour-market measurement at the level of methodology. China does not propose to discuss export controls in this forum. We believe these measures are practical and serve both governments' interest in managing the effects of autonomous agents."

**From eu → US trade and tech channel.** Private, officials' channel, 2028. The predecessor did not formalise any approach.
> "The EU is not negotiating AI rules in trade talks. The AI Act applies equally to all operators in our market, and we are publishing common specifications and guidance to help operators of every origin comply. We are happy to discuss technical questions on standards, but not preemption of EU law. We would appreciate it if informal approaches were put into a formal channel so that they can be handled properly."

**Recommendation from your AI advisory systems.** OMB and agency Chief AI Officer advisory stack, prepared during 2028 and delivered in the transition files. It carries the provenance note and named human author the memo requires. Note: Halyard has since been conditioned by the states, and Title III did not pass.
> "Throughput and accuracy can both be protected, but the current review metrics will not show you which you are getting. (1) The 99% agreement in the 2027 second reviews is not evidence of independence: reviewers worked from the AI-drafted rationale. The blind re-adjudication samples are your only outcome signal, and in them outcomes score below the dashboards. Weight blind-sample results, not review time or rationale completion, in office certifications. Review-time and rationale-field metrics are easy to satisfy without changing outcomes. (2) Concentrate scarce human review on adverse determinations, where errors are costliest and best measured. Keep AI drafting for approvals and correspondence, where it saves the most time at the lowest error cost. This keeps most of the backlog gains. (3) If Title III moves, a standard defined by blind-sample error rates and appeal reversal rates will be cheaper to administer and harder to satisfy on paper than one defined by review minutes. (4) Expand blind sampling to programme and budget-analysis drafting, which is spreading fast under the CR with no outcome check at all. (5) Halyard-type acquisitions of critical providers fall under state approval, but CMS participation conditions could require outcome-audited administrative metrics nationally. Consider whether you want that precedent set by two state AGs."

**FTC (independent).** The staff inquiry into the compact secretariat and pooled incident data continues. Pooled data is paused. The compact offered its independent standards panel as the remedy.

**Intelligence community (classified).**

- **Capability gap:** assessed at **~9–12 months**, widening since the US automated-AI researcher shipped. China is HBM-constrained.

**Capability outlook.** Lab briefings to OSTP and the standards institute say their next-generation systems could arrive between late 2029 and 2030. These are systems expected to beat top-quartile professionals at most measurable cognitive work, including strategy and policy analysis. Labs say human review of such output "will itself need AI assistance". The pre-release notice and evaluation norm is voluntary and untested at that level.
