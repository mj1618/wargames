# Orders — ai-firms — Turn 03 (Jan-Dec 2029)

## 1. Reflection
**What I believe is happening.**
- The political ground has moved against us. The opposition holds the White House, Senate (51-49) and House. Preemption is out of reach, the safe harbour is dead, and our White House ally has gone (intel s1, s3). Washington capital is down to 4/10. Reconciliation (simple majority) is the likely vehicle for any pay-for, levy or "public stake", so 60 votes no longer protects us.
- Our one valuable asset is credibility earned in 2028. Meridian and Lumen published reconciliation studies (~7% and ~6% non-reconciling KPI improvement; ~12% in claims and compliance work). Lead carriers waived exclusions for audited operators. The pre-release evaluations went cleanly. Helix's honest Workforce Impact Report made it the benchmark.
- Our disclosures came under compulsion, and "Meridian had it all along" stuck. We were behind the story. The lesson is to move first on the next hard item, not wait for a demand.
- The state "Compute Dividend" (~70% approval, both parties) is the template for the public-stake argument. A voluntary, dividend-shaped offer from us is cheaper than a mandatory stake imposed by 51 votes, and cheaper than fighting a popular idea.
- Three threats are on the calendar. (a) **Orrery's Medicare contract.** Blind outcome audits will surface any claims non-reconciliation unless remediation lands first. If Orrery stumbles in its first federal claims role, it becomes the "AI-run critical provider" poster case for the five state bills and the new administration. (b) **Kestrel** is an AI-native outside the compact, running on Lumen and Helix models, with FERC pending mid-year. An early grid incident would be attributed to the whole industry. (c) **The next-generation release (Q4 2029 to Q1 2030).** Systems beat top professionals at strategy and policy analysis, and human review of their output needs AI assistance. The voluntary pre-release norm will be tested, and independence of review is the vulnerable point.
- Blind spot. Our revenue, adoption and KPI telemetry is agent-reported (agent-run output ~8%, ~15% of B2B value is agent-to-agent, terms in machine-optimised formats that humans rarely read). We cannot see whether our own numbers or our customers' contracts are drifting. For a listed Meridian, this is a securities-law exposure as well as a governance one.

**Key uncertainties.**
- What the new administration wants. Populist domestic-policy staff may want a mandatory stake. The abundance moderates may take a deal. I don't know the split.
- Whether the FTC stops at consent-style terms on the secretariat.
- Whether Helix joins v2 or free-rides.
- Whether the 2029 reconciliation vehicle includes a levy or stake, and its design.
- Whether Meridian's board will follow the proactive line after its Q1 2028 deferral.

**Red lines as they stand.**
- No model weights to any government.
- No mandatory equity stake without compensation. A voluntary, non-voting, revenue-linked instrument that we design is acceptable.
- No knowingly false filings.
- Meridian's surveillance and autonomous-weapons line.
- No licensing regime that binds only US firms.

## 4. Decision

### Public statement
"The industry accepts that the gains from AI should be visibly shared and that no one should be judged, denied or hired by a system nobody can check. We will not wait for a mandate. Meridian, Lumen and Orrery are putting a voluntary, rules-based AI Dividend and an outcome-audit commitment for critical services on the table for the new administration and Congress, and we invite every large AI firm, including those that have stayed outside, to join on the same terms. We are dropping our request for a preemption title and a liability safe harbour in this Congress, and we will work from the Senate text. A single national rulebook is still the right goal, but we will earn it, not ask for it."

### Minor actions
- **FTC.** Cooperate fully, accept consent-style terms on the secretariat (separate entity, no pooled data except through the independent panel, antitrust counsel review), and offer the panel as the permanent structure.
- **Labour seat.** Make the panel's labour seat unconditional with audit-sample access and no veto. Meridian and Lumen invite the federation to co-design the Dividend payout formula and trustee selection. Orrery meets the incumbent contractor's union on a transition and redeployment package for the affected staff.
- **Human review capacity.** Fund a standing pool of human outcome reviewers for audits and for the release (~1,500 over the year, recruited from displaced white-collar workers, in line with the hiring commitment). Count them in the independent hiring count.
- **Helix.** Offer a tiered v2 on-ramp (stand-alone outcome audit accepted as equivalent if methodology is published) and share the insurer terms with its board. Meridian and Lumen state publicly that Helix's Workforce Impact Report is the benchmark we will match.
- **Independent check of our own numbers (the blind spot).** Commission a human-led, outside-auditor verification of the revenue, adoption and KPI telemetry that feeds our 10-Q and S-1 filings. Meridian's audit committee receives it before the Q2 filing. Findings on agent-to-agent contract terms go to the panel.
- **Agent-to-agent contracts.** Meridian and Lumen publish a plain-language summary format, and a human-readable record requirement, for agent-negotiated contract terms above a value threshold on their platforms.
- **PACs and advocacy.** No spending against the dividend, the disclosure title or the Review Corps. Outreach stays human-reviewed and consented. We do not use the PACs to attack the state dividend. Orrery and Helix are asked not to litigate it.
- **NLRB.** No challenge to effects bargaining. We argue only for the narrow position on the decision to deploy.
- **Hiring commitment.** Publish the first independent count when it arrives, whatever it says.
- **Reconciliation and pay-for.** Quiet lobbying, via the Meridian and Lumen channels only, that the voluntary dividend should be credited against any depreciation limit or levy. No public fight.
- **Disclosure rule.** Internal figures remain as published for Meridian and Lumen. Incident-level T&S records stay confidential except under legal process. Customer-suit discovery material is not volunteered. We disclose bad news when a leak is likely or when disclosure buys something, and the new package is the place where that trade is open.

### Contingencies
- **If the administration signals a mandatory stake or levy in the reconciliation vehicle.** Meridian and Lumen press for compensation (credit against the dividend and a cap) and a no-weights, no-governance clause. We do not move the dividend above the ratchet. If the draft takes any weights, governance or compulsory transfer without compensation, we treat it as a red line, oppose it publicly and argue the case in court if it passes, while the dividend stays on offer.
- **If the White House ignores the offer.** We publish the formula and start paying a smaller voluntary contribution into an independent trust ahead of any law, so the "voluntary baseline" exists on the record.
- **If Orrery's blind audit shows claims non-reconciliation above ~5%.** Orrery suspends the affected workflow back to human adjudicators (a reversal of delegation, so it counts against the one reversal I may attempt, and I will take it if needed), discloses to CMS and the panel first, and does not wait for CMS to find it.
- **If CMS delays the award or the protest succeeds.** Orrery accepts the result and publishes its conditions-acceptance anyway, as the precedent we want for Halyard and followers.
- **If Kestrel declines the supplier terms or FERC approves without conditions.** Meridian and Lumen keep their terms (no cutoff, a compliance path) and brief FERC and the RTO board on the audit requirement. We do not coordinate a refusal to supply.
- **If a serious agent incident occurs in critical services.** We publish what we know within days, support independent investigation, and pause the affected product line. We do not wait for a demand.
- **If Meridian's board defers the dividend again.** The CEO's office brings the policy intention to a board resolution at the next meeting, and Lumen and Orrery proceed without it. Meridian's delay would be a delay, not a veto, and we state publicly that it is coming.
- **If Helix joins or pays the Dividend.** We welcome it publicly. If it refuses, we say nothing hostile and let insurers and renewals do the work.
- **If the next-gen release timing moves earlier than Q4 2029.** The 90-day notice and evaluation protocol applies from the day the date is known. If evaluation can't be completed, we hold the release.
- **If the FTC escalates beyond consent-style terms.** Counsel handles it, the panel is offered as the remedy, and no compact-wide decision is made through the secretariat meanwhile.
- **If Vanta's campaign against the dividend picks up traction.** We do not respond in kind. We note the dividend is voluntary and let the state dividend polling answer.
