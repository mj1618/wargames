# Orders — china — Turn 03 (1 January 2029; orders cover Jan–Dec 2029)

## 1. Reflection

**What I believe is happening.**

- *The gap is the problem of the year.* Services put the frontier gap at ~10–12 months (was 6–9). The US shipped automated AI researchers from July 2028 and AI for Science could not keep pace under the HBM constraint. Private intel says the next US generation (late 2029–2030) pushes the gap past 12 months during 2030 *unless AI for Science output scales*. My trigger (>12 months → catch-up shift) is at the edge but not crossed. The correct response is not to wait for the trigger: by the time it is clearly crossed the shift is a year late. But the shift must happen in the research layer and in compute, not in service-sector deployment — relaxing pacing in tier-1 services does nothing for the research gap and everything for graduate-season risk.
- *Stability is holding, and the numbers are being gamed.* True youth unemployment ~20.5–21% (NBS household survey, matches internal); published ~16.5%. The new regime's provincial net-headcount series runs 1–2 points rosier than the cross-check. The reporting chain is the problem I flagged last year; I now have an independent instrument (the six-city survey) that works. The platforms comply where HRSS counts heads and route around via subcontractors elsewhere. No unrest. Provinces want more money before summer. Political capital 8/10.
- *The responsible-official rule was nominal in a third of cases* — the same thing the US IG found. The CCDI fix (minimum review period, second reader without the draft) has raised override rates from near zero on high-impact items. NDRC and provincial planners want exemptions. I read the rise in overrides as the rule starting to work, not as a cost. I will not loosen it.
- *Washington has changed.* The new administration is labour-wing at home, undecided between dialogue and chip hawks on China, and has "said little about China". Export controls unchanged. The predecessor's offer (narrower principle text + statistics exchange + hotline) was never recorded; I hold the text. My restraint posture no longer needs to weaken US accelerationists — they lost. Its new function is to deny the chip hawks an argument while the new team is choosing. Visible acceleration now is the one thing that could unify the new administration against me. So: public steadiness, private compute mobilisation — the same lever, new target.
- *AI for Science is probably not identified* (moderate confidence; compute reallocation is observable in principle). A larger pooling of platform compute this year raises that exposure. I accept that, with a cover story ready.
- *UN/Global South* is my best external asset: 55 co-sponsors, 15 Qilin localisation deals, vote in autumn. The US and EU will push the multistakeholder alternative again; I can probably absorb that language rather than lose the vote.

**Belief probe.**
- *us-gov (new):* Knows our published labour statistics are rosier than reality (their own analysts will have seen the pattern) but not by how much. Does not know about AI for Science (moderate confidence). Intends to prioritise its domestic programme (reviewers, public stake, Senate bill without preemption) and will treat China as a second-tier question for H1 2029; the chip decision will be made by whichever faction has the better story by mid-year. Its "public stake in AI" and anti-"tech oligarch" framing is ideologically closer to state direction than any predecessor — an opening I can use rhetorically without expecting policy concessions.
- *eu:* Knows only what the testing dialogue shows and what we publish. Intends to stay testing-only, hold with the US on the UN vote, and spend 2029 on its own elections and the ~3% fiscal-gap debate. Will not break with Washington; may abstain on a resolution that includes multistakeholder language if the US does not object loudly.

**Key uncertainties.** Whether HBM milestones land (the whole catch-up logic depends on it); whether the US next generation arrives on time; which way the new US team leans on chips; whether this year's graduate season passes as quietly as 2028 with the published rate now diverging ~4 points from truth.

**Red lines as they stand.** No foreign inspection inside Chinese systems (reciprocal, non-intrusive exchange on published statistics and methods is fine). No platform or AI system becomes an independent power centre — the compute pooling this year is also a reminder of who directs whom. Taiwan quiet; no accepted independence. No reversal of the responsible-official rule.

## 3. Options considered

| 4 (unconventional) | **Make the independent NBS survey the yardstick**: expand to 20 cities, make it the basis for provincial sign-off compliance and CCDI enforcement, close the subcontractor loophole | Fixes the blind spot I identified; Party control over provinces, not over AI; cheap relative to transfers | Provinces resist; produces a worse number I then have to act on; the published series becomes harder to defend |
| 5 (unconventional) | **Early principals-level offer to the new US administration**: accept the predecessor's narrower text, add a statistics exchange, propose a shared "AI serves people, not a few companies" line, ask for export-control predictability | Denies hawks an argument; cheap; ideological fit with their populism | Could read as eagerness (weakness); the text was theirs, so accepting it costs them nothing; they may not answer in H1 |
| 6 (unconventional) | **Release a near-frontier Qilin open-weight model to the Global South before the UNGA vote** | Bloc-building; undercuts US labs commercially | Gives away capability; reads in Washington as acceleration; the Global South is already with me for the vote |

**Decision:** Option 3 and Option 4 as the two majors; Option 5 executed through messages (no action cost); Option 6 held back. Option 1 is the contingency if the gap is assessed >12 months during the year.

## 4. Decision

### Public statement
(NPC session, March; State Council work report; MFA.)

"China will continue steady AI+ under Party leadership. In 2029 the priority is AI for Science and industrial and technological self-reliance — advanced computing, new materials, robotics — and the employment-first management of AI in services continues. The Graduate Employment Action is renewed and expanded for 2029. AI advises; people decide: the responsible-official requirement for state AI is being strengthened, not relaxed. We note that the new United States administration has placed workers, accountable human decisions and the concentration of AI power in a few companies at the centre of its programme; these are concerns China has raised since 2027. The two largest AI economies share a responsibility to ensure AI serves people. China proposes that the AI dialogue resume at a senior level this year, and will bring the intergovernmental AI governance process to a vote at the General Assembly in the autumn, together with more than fifty co-sponsors."

### Minor actions
- Tianshu review: keep the minimum review period and the second reader for high-impact items; grant NDRC a narrow exemption for routine low-impact drafts only, with the CCDI auditing the exemption list in Q3. Report override rates to the Standing Committee quarterly.
- Security services: quarterly gap assessment; a specific watch on whether AI for Science or the compute pooling has been identified in Washington (markers: US commentary shifting from "steady" to "mobilisation"; chip-control drafts naming HBM/packaging).
- Cover story prepared, not used: an "AI for Science" white paper (civilian science applications, named flagship projects) ready to publish if the programme is identified.
- Qilin: release cadence unchanged, "steady" framing; localisation deals extended toward ~25 states; no "beating the US" claims; no near-frontier open-weight release this year.
- State media: welcome the new US administration's worker and accountability agenda; present the Compute Dividend and the Medicare/grid awards as "AI-run firms now run American public services" — the exhibit for "AI serves a few companies"; no personal attacks on US leaders.
- Taiwan: routine posture; no exercises timed to US decisions; nothing that gives the chip hawks a story.
- Second capacity-building tranche disbursed in Q2.

### Contingencies
- **If services assess the gap >12 months at any quarterly review** → execute the full catch-up shift (Option 1): relax pacing in tier-2 services adjacent to industry, commit the remaining reserve, accept the stability cost; publish the AI for Science white paper to frame it.
- **If the US tightens HBM/advanced-packaging controls** → accelerate the domestic campaign; a proportionate, deferred response through critical-minerals licensing *after* the dialogue window closes, not during it; no linkage to Taiwan.
- **If the new administration accepts the principle text** → sign at principals level in Q2, pair with the statistics exchange and the voluntary pace briefings; hold the UNGA language concession for a US abstention.
- **If AI for Science or the compute pooling is identified in Washington** → publish the white paper the same month; state media frame it as civilian science and self-reliance; do not deny the compute.
- **If unrest in more than one city during graduate season** → campaign-style mobilisation: moratorium on new agent deployments in tier-1/2 services, emergency SOE intake, hukou-linked placement; the compute programme is not touched.
- **If the 20-city survey shows youth unemployment above ~22%** → the package is undersized: a third tranche, public-sector hiring mandate, and a Standing Committee review of the published series.
- **If a platform games the compute pooling or routes around the regime after being named** → 2021 precedent on one platform, publicly, as an example.
- **If the UNGA resolution looks short in September** → accept the multistakeholder track in full and defer the human-control principle to a follow-on resolution; a deferred vote is worse than a diluted one.
