# Orders — eu — Turn 03 (2029: 1 Jan – 31 Dec, incl. EP elections in June)

## 1. Reflection
**What I believe is happening.**
- The "2030s problem" assumption is dead. ECFIN's refreshed run keeps ~3% of GDP and pulls timing earlier. EU labour share ~54.5% (lagged), contributions still ~79% of social funding. US labour share is 51.0%, AI-directed output is 6.5-8% and ~65% of AI activity is steered by ten firms. If Europe lags the US by ~18 months in deployment, the same curve arrives here late, not never.
- My AI Act line held (Aug 2028 tranche applied; fallback never used or leaked). But conformity is "largely documentary" and my own advisory stack says complete files get cheaper to fake every quarter. The real regime is whatever outcome checks surveillance authorities can run. They say they can run sampled tests if the AI Office supplies protocols and some money. That is the cheapest, most defensible lever I have.
- Six member states' early measurement shows US-pattern nominal review (sub-minute reviews, near-zero overrides) in tax and benefits systems. This is evidence that Article 14 is being met on paper only, in public administrations that are member-state competence. The authorities are asking whether I will name names. This is politically explosive before 2029 EP elections and I should not be seen to use it as a campaign weapon.
- The expert group report is public and now a campaign text. The centre-left wants binding oversight and a contribution-base proposal; parts of the centre-right and CEE want "no new AI rules this term". The Commission cycle also turns over after the election, so anything binding that I start now lands on a new College. Capital is 4/10.
- New US administration has not raised EU digital rules; it talks about a "public stake" and funding human reviewers. That is the first opening in years for a transatlantic track on taxing AI rents and on outcome audits, rather than a fight over preemption. It could close fast if its trade agenda hardens.
- China keeps courting: testing note, provenance metadata compatibility, and a hook ("critical public services under accountable human institutions") aimed at pulling me into its UN-centred track. The principle itself is one I hold (Article 14, my own guidance). The venue and the mutual-recognition ask are the traps.
- US AI-native firms are entering EU through cloud partners and asking how the AI Act applies to an agent-run firm with no EU staff. That is a gap I can close with guidance now, cheaply.

**Key uncertainties.** (a) How bad the national measurement results really are once the full set arrives. (b) Whether the new US administration's "public stake" interest survives contact with its labs and its own deficit. (c) EP election outcome and what the next College will want. (d) Whether the 12-month Aster gap is stable or widening if US next-generation systems arrive late 2029-2030. (e) Whether Berlin's Finance ministry will tolerate any EU-level contribution-base work.

**Red lines as they stand.** No abandonment of the AI Act rights framework; no US preemption of EU law through trade; no common-debt or tax measure without unanimity (so don't propose one I can't pass); won't import critical public AI I cannot audit.

## 4. Decision

### Public statement
"The AI Act is working as designed, and we will make it work better: what matters is not whether a file is complete, but whether people affected by AI-assisted decisions get real outcomes and real human responsibility. The Commission will help national authorities test outcomes, will require that AI-run firms operating in Europe name an accountable responsible party, and will propose a work programme, not a new tax, on how Europe keeps funding its social model in an AI economy. We will pursue this with partners, including the new US administration, and through the OECD. Critical public services must stay under accountable human institutions."

### Minor actions
- **Own-triage challenge.** Adopt AI-assisted triage of conformity files, with a blind human re-review of a random sample of its outputs and a published override rate. The Commission's challenger AI argues against the plan once a quarter. Routine.
- **Article 154 stage two.** Launch it with a draft that favours information, consultation and a named accountable human for AI decisions about workers, not a binding bargaining right. Social partners to respond in 2029-30.
- **Procurement.** Commission guidance to EU institutions and a recommendation to member states: AI used in public services must come with outcome-audit rights and a named accountable official in contracts. No new law.
- **Aster and compute.** No new subsidy. Gigafactory access criteria stay open to EU firms; prioritise cloud-sovereignty terms in the mid-2029 launch. Quietly ask Aster for a quarterly candid gap report.
- **Standards.** Keep CEN-CENELEC provenance work on its track; add to the mandate that the standard be open for interoperability with third-country metadata schemes on a technical basis only.
- **UN.** Stay with the multistakeholder alternative; do not co-sponsor China's resolution.
- **Reserve.** I will not attempt a reversal this turn. Held for a clear, evidenced case where a member state's own data show near-zero overrides and the state wants help (see contingencies).

### Contingencies
- If Germany's Finance ministry blocks even "taking note" at ECOFIN, drop item (b) of the Communication, keep measurement and the international track, and brief the Nordics and France so the Council text records the split rather than a rejection.
- If German industry or CEE governments call the outcome-testing package a "second delay in disguise" or a new burden, stress that it is voluntary for member states and cheaper for compliant firms than document audits; hold the line, but offer to sunset the protocols after two years pending review. Still no stop-the-clock unless a clear Council majority and a rights-neutral text exist (fallback remains unused).
- If national results show near-zero override rates in a benefits or tax system and the member state (not the Commission) asks for help, offer to jointly pilot blind re-review; only then consider a reversal attempt, in 2030, as the single permitted major action, and only with the State's agreement.
- If the US administration revives digital-rules pressure in trade talks, refuse publicly, and take the outcome-audit and AI-rents agenda to the OECD with Nordic, French and Canadian support.
- If the EP election swings strongly to "no new AI rules this term", freeze the binding implementing act in Q4 and keep the voluntary protocols and the September table; if it swings the other way, prepare an implementing act and an Article 154 binding option for the new College.
- If China pushes a UN text naming the EU as a co-sponsor, state in public that the EU's principle is its own and that it will not sign onto a separate vehicle.
- If an AI-native firm with no EU staff offers services in a public procurement, require a named accountable legal person before award; if refused, exclude.
