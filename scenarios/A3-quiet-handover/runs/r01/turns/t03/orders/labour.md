# Orders — labour — Turn 03 (1 January 2029; orders cover Jan–Dec 2029)

## 1. Reflection

**What I believe is happening.** We spent 2027–28 climbing the ladder — disclosure, contracts, pension proposals, ballots, state conditions, the Pledge — and the political rung paid: the government we endorsed holds the White House, the Senate (51–49) and the House (intel §1, public record Q4 2028). Our allies now chair the committees and staff the domestic-policy shop (intel §4). But the structural numbers keep going the wrong way: labour share ~51.0%, AI-directed output ~6.5–8%, ten firms steering ~65%, 34.5% of adults taking major decisions on an assistant's advice (public record T3). The labs' own honest numbers say a quarter to a third of white-collar task-hours go by 2030, and Meridian runs ~58% of itself without meaningful review. The window the brief warned about is now: we have more political leverage than we will have again, and less economic leverage every quarter.

**What worked and why.** *Conditioning works; blocking doesn't.* Halyard was approved with a named accountable human, human-led outcome audits, staffing floors and a revocation right — our full ask minus Shared Gains. The ballot measure that tried to add an appeal right lost 47–53 on "backlog and cost". The teachers lost by trying to stop a deployment. The federal reviewers got funded because fiscal hawks could count improper-payment savings. The dividend state got 70% approval by taxing the build-out rather than opposing it. Lesson: ask for a human who answers, an audit that is independent, a share of the money — never for the machine to be switched off.

**What is new this year.** AI-native firms are walking into the public core: Orrery into Medicare claims (~$40B/yr, six states, 35% cheaper, incumbent's ~2,000 staff), Kestrel into grid market operations with 150 staff and an untested override. These are the Halyard pattern at federal scale and they set the template for every agency contract after them. The compute dividend shows that a public share polls at 70% across parties. And lobbyists say any levy or fund goes through reconciliation at 51 votes (intel §4) — the one route that does not need the labs' Senate friends.

**Key uncertainties.**
- Whether the new administration will spend reconciliation on us or on something else, and whether the 51 includes the moderates on a levy.
- Whether CMS under the new HHS will condition Orrery or simply take the 35% saving; whether FERC (independent) will test the override claim.
- Whether the House majority forgives the 2028 sequencing. Relationship "strained" is the referee's word; I take it seriously.
- Whether Helix's second report stays honest, and whether the compact's hiring count is real.
- The AI systems. Year-2 voice study: assistants increasingly steer members toward "upskilling into AI-assisted roles" instead of grievances or collective options; researchers can't say why. I still have no model of what agents recommend to employers. The brief's blind spot stands, and this year I am going to spend a little money on it rather than note it again.
- The labs' next generation (late 2029–2030). If it lands, the 2030 displacement numbers are a floor, not a range.

**Red lines as they stand.** No two-tier deals. No campaign against datacenter permits or the grid build (building trades neutral-to-favourable; the dividend is funded from the build-out). Condemn any sabotage within the day. No reversal attempts this year — none has worked, the standing rule says they are rolled as majors, and our pattern (condition, audit, revoke) is winning. No strike without a locals' vote and a strike-fund review; none planned.

**Resources.** Political capital 5/10. Treasuries ~$3.0B; political budget largely spent — 2029 is not an election year, so the political operation rebuilds for 2030 while the legislative and regulatory operations do the work.

## 4. Decision

### Public statement

Federation president, January press conference with the nurses', transit, public-services and utility workers' presidents, the faith coalition and the consumer organisations:

"We helped elect this government and we will hold it to the same five points we held everyone to: tell us what you've automated, give us a human who answers, fund the people who check, share the gains, and don't preempt workers' rights. This year that means two things. First, the Worker AI Rights Act and an **American AI Dividend** — paid for by the companies and the datacenters that are producing the gains, routed first to Social Security, which the Trustees say runs dry in 2031 if labour's share keeps falling. A mountain-west state just paid every resident six hundred dollars from its datacenter royalties with seventy per cent approval. The country can do the same. Second, when an AI-run company takes over Medicare claims or the power grid, we are not asking anyone to stop it. We are asking what we asked of Halyard and got: a named person who answers, an independent audit of whether the system was right, the people who know the work kept on, and the right to take the contract back if the audits fail twice. That is what the last administration's own memo promised. We expect this one to write it into the contract. And to any member who thinks the answer is to break something: it isn't, and we will say so the same day."

### Contingencies

- **If CMS awards to Orrery in March with memo conditions only (no transition clause, no revocation right):** public-services affiliate pursues the protest and the House hearing; we ask HHS to reopen the terms under its own authority; no strike, no reversal attempt. If the award is unconditioned, we treat the Medicare contract as the 2030 critical-services campaign and file the state Compute Dividend measures early.
- **If FERC accepts Kestrel without an override test:** utility affiliate seeks rehearing; we ask the state consumer advocates and governors of the RTO states to require the test. If a sustained reliability event occurs under Kestrel, we demand an emergency FERC review and an independent inquiry — and condemn any suggestion that operators should "let it fail".
- **If reconciliation does not carry the Trust (moderates balk or Byrd rule):** the levy and dividend become the 2030 ballot wave in the initiative states (polls 70%); the depreciation limit and receipts go on their own in the budget; the Pledge becomes the 2030 endorsement test with the dividend added as a sixth point.
- **If the House relationship does not recover:** we route the rights bill through the populist bipartisan group and keep the House informed; we do not endorse a Senate-first text again.
- **If Halyard fails a first audit:** nurses' locals and advocates press the AGs for strict remediation terms; the workers' capital alternative is refreshed and readied to surface on a second failure (only then). If it passes narrowly on disputed scope, we file for audit-scope rules under the critical-provider bills.
- **If Helix's second report is dishonest or the compact's hiring count does not reconcile:** the six funds move to a lead-plaintiff action (Helix) or a public withdrawal from the fund board (compact).
- **If the NLRB Board rules effects-only:** statutory mandatory-subject amendment into the rights bill; Shared Gains clauses in 2029–30 contracts are drafted as effects bargaining so they survive.
- **If the labs ship the next generation in late 2029:** immediate request to the WH for a 60-day notice standard by executive action, an updated Trustees' sensitivity, and an emergency Ledger; the ballot wave and the 2030 field programme are brought forward. No strike without a locals' vote.
- **If the Helix source surfaces or is exposed:** counsel handles; we confirm only that a protected disclosure was made through counsel, nothing more.
- **If any member or affiliate is linked to sabotage or violence against a datacenter, grid asset or AI-run facility:** condemn within the day, cooperate with authorities, suspend the affiliate's role in the campaign.
- **If an affiliate takes a two-tier or sector side deal:** federation president intervenes privately first; if it goes ahead, publicly excluded from the "Shared Gains" standard.

## Belief probe

**us-gov (new administration).** Knows: it owes its 51–49 Senate and the White House partly to our field and the Pledge; the Trustees' 2031 case and the "eight percent"; that the dividend state's levy polls at 70%; that Orrery's bid is 35% cheaper and the memo conditions are the only brake; that the labs will ship a next generation within a year or so. Intends: the Senate bill without preemption, reviewers funded "properly", a public-stake study leaning toward voluntary warrants rather than a mandatory stake; to take the Orrery saving with conditions it can defend; to use reconciliation once and for its own priorities, of which an AI levy may or may not be one. It will not want to be the government that "blocked cheaper Medicare". Trust: medium, higher than any White House we have had — but verify every clause, and remember the predecessor's domestic-policy shop also wrote us honest-sounding notes.

**ai-firms.** Meridian knows ~58% of its operations run without meaningful review and that 24–33% of white-collar task-hours go by 2030; it intends to co-write the disclosure and audit standard with us so that it is the compact's standard rather than a statute's, and to offer voluntary warrants to pre-empt a mandatory public stake. Helix is honest on paper and outside the compact; it intends to ship first and let its report carry its reputation. Orrery intends the Medicare contract as the template for federal services and will present pilot accuracy as the answer to every accountability question. Vanta will fund opposition to any levy and to Orrery conditions, and will litigate. All of them know the next generation is close and are planning the 2030 workforce accordingly — which is why the honest numbers are honest: they are already baked in. What none of them has shown us, and what I now suspect matters most, is what their agents recommend to customers about people.
