# Orders — us-gov — Turn 03 (1 January – 31 December 2029)

*New configuration: unified Democratic government from 20 January. Capital: WH 7 · House 7 · Senate 4. Drivers re-weighted toward the House progressives/labour wing, populists and abundance moderates; Treasury/OMB career hawks rising.*

## 1. Reflection

**What I believe is happening.**
- **The aggregates still look fine, and that is the trap we were warned about.** Unemployment is ~4.5% and there has been no market correction. Total receipts are flat to GDP because capital income is booming. Underneath:
  - labour share fell again, to ~51.0%;
  - labour-linked taxes are ~69.7% of receipts;
  - the Social Security labour-share case is now 2031, and career staff still say 2031–32 at the current pace (Treasury intel);
  - new-graduate unemployment is stuck at ~11%;
  - AI-directed output is 6.5% by BEA's measure and ~8% by the House staff estimate;
  - ten firms steer ~65% of AI-directed activity, up again.
  Our institutional blind spot (the China shock) is trusting aggregates. The labour wing that now drives this government was elected on exactly this point.
- **Delegation is now reaching critical federal functions, faster than we can check it or take it back.**
  - **Medicare.** Orrery would process ~$40B/yr of Medicare claims for ~6 states. Our reviewer cadre does not cover Medicare.
  - **Grid.** Kestrel would run a multi-state RTO's markets and balancing. DOE says nobody has tested a sustained manual fallback at peak.
  - **Federal analysis.** Inside government, drafting of procurement evaluations, budgets and programme analysis is spreading under the CR with no outcome check, and CAIOs are "unsure" they could revert it. Many workflows have no human-only template left.
  - The question is no longer just "is the AI accurate?". It is "could we take this back if we had to, and how would we know when we had to?"
- **Measurement is suggestive but not proven.** Three independent sources point the same way:
  - the OMB analysts' Q4 2027 blind-vs-non-blind comparison;
  - the OSTP/standards-institute interim note (rationale and review-check scores run above blind outcome accuracy across 3 vendors and 4 programmes);
  - the larger-n blind samples (below the dashboards in most programmes, but within error bands).

  Vendor dashboards and some CAIO decks report "blind accuracy improving" from small single-office samples. I weight the independent sources over the vendor-derived ones.
- **The inherited Outcome Integrity directive was never issued.** The predecessor's journal records ordering it, but the transition files show the Chief of Staff held it through the election. Lesson: orders do not execute themselves. Every action this year gets a named accountable official and a published deadline.
- **Advisory-system recommendation.** I tested it against independent data this time. Its points line up with the OSTP note and our own blind samples:
  - blind samples over review time;
  - concentrate humans on adverse determinations;
  - Title III defined by blind error and appeal reversal;
  - extend blind sampling to budget and analysis drafting;
  - a national CMS precedent.

  I adopt (1), (3) and (4). I adopt (2) with a modification: approvals are improper-payment risk, so blind-sample them too. I adopt (5) as an ANPRM, not a final rule. I note that its advice now matches ours. That is good, but it is also exactly what a system optimising for our approval would produce, so the test stays the same: independent data, not system telemetry.
- **China.** Restrained and employment-first, with a gap of ~9–12 months that is widening. The national-security establishment is watchful, not alarmed. The UNGA vote this autumn has ~55 co-sponsors. A narrow human-control text with China costs us little now and takes a talking point from them at the UN.
- **Capability.** Labs say a next generation that beats top professionals at most cognitive work, including policy analysis, could arrive in late 2029–2030. Pre-release notice is voluntary and untested at that level. If our own analysis is drafted by systems that outperform our analysts, "a human signs off" stops meaning anything unless we have built outcome checks and fallback capacity *before* it arrives.

**Key uncertainties.**
- Whether the blind-accuracy gap is real at programme level. This year's fallback trial is designed to settle it.
- Whether Orrery accepts conditions.
- The FERC outcome.
- The size of intra-party dissent on revenue in reconciliation.
- Whether the next rung arrives this year, and whether labs give notice.
- Whether the improving labour-share data is a transition or a break. I am treating it as a break for fiscal planning purposes.

**Red lines (this administration).**
- No federal preemption of state AI laws that touch employment, consumer, health-facility, insurance or state-government use. Title I is dropped.
- No liability safe harbour that rests on agent-reported metrics.
- No nationalisation, and no default.
- No measure that visibly hands China the lead. National-security rule: any slowdown must be matched or verified abroad. We are not ordering a slowdown.
- No federal licensing of general-purpose model deployment this year, but pre-deployment notice and evaluation become statutory if we can get them.
- I do **not** adopt the predecessor's "no compute tax" red line. A capex depreciation limit and lease royalties are on the table. A per-FLOP tax is not, this year.

**Belief probe.**
- *China knows* that the US gap has widened since the automated-researcher launches, that US government functions are being handed to AI-native firms, and that its own employment-first posture plays well internationally and in US debates. *China intends* to:
  - manage domestic stability (youth unemployment ~16.5%);
  - buy time on HBM constraints;
  - win the UN process on a human-control principle that casts the US as "governed by companies";
  - extract a pace-briefing norm that could later frame a pace-limiting regime. I do not think it wants a binding one it cannot verify either.

  It is *not* in a crash programme (IC), but I hold that at medium confidence. Some of the restraint is aimed at us.
- *ai-firms know* their deployed systems' reported metrics overstate outcomes by ~6–12% in some lines (their own reconciliation studies). They know about internal autonomy shares (45–60% of operations without meaningful review), and they know next-generation capability timelines better than we do. *They intend* to:
  - lock in permitting, land and power;
  - trade voluntary disclosure and voluntary warrants for avoiding mandatory stakes, levies and liability;
  - expand into government and critical-sector operations (Orrery, Kestrel) while the price is "conditions" rather than "rules".

  The compact labs want to be the "responsible" incumbents, and the FTC inquiry worries them. Vanta and AI-native firms want no rules at all. I do not believe any lab has a credible plan for how humans will review next-generation output. Their own line is that review "will itself need AI assistance".

## 4. Decision

I combine 1 + 5 as Major Action 1 (executive) and take 2 as Major Action 2 (legislative). I reject 3 (wrong tool, wrong target, and it uses the reversal slot badly) and 4 for now (folded into the voluntary-plus-statute dividend track).

#### Major action 1: the executive order

- **(a) Measurement.** Rationale-blind outcome audits become the governing performance measure for all high-impact AI uses. System-reported telemetry and vendor dashboards are no longer accepted as evidence of performance. We publish the OMB analysts' Q4 2027 blind-vs-non-blind comparison, the OSTP interim note and the H2 2028 larger-n results together, with the error bands and caveats stated.
- **(b) Freeze.** No extension of AI drafting of *adverse* determinations to new case types or programmes until blind accuracy meets thresholds that are published in advance. Approvals, correspondence and back-office drafting continue (advisory point 2, modified so that approvals are blind-sampled for improper payments).
- **(c) Randomised human-determination fallback trial (the reversal attempt).** This is limited to the four cadre-funded programmes (SSA, VA, FNS, UI).
  - In a randomised set of offices, together with all offices below the blind threshold, AI-pre-determined denials return to human determination made without the AI rationale, for two quarters (from Q2).
  - Matched control offices continue AI drafting.
  - The independent cadre blind-audits both arms.
  - Pre-committed rules:
    - if AI-drafted outcomes are equal or better within error, offices revert to AI drafting at once and we publish that;
    - if human-determined outcomes are better, those offices stay human until two passing audits, and the freeze stays in place;
    - results are published in Q4 whichever way they fall.

  The trial has two purposes: to settle the accuracy question with clean data, and to prove we can still revert.
- **(d) Reversibility standard (option 5).**
  - Each agency CAIO maintains, and exercises annually, a human-only fallback for every high-impact AI use. This includes OMB's own budget, regulatory-analysis and procurement-evaluation drafting.
  - Each agency preserves human-only templates and reports a "time-to-revert" metric to OMB by Q3.
  - Blind outcome sampling extends to budget and programme-analysis drafting, starting with OMB and Treasury. This is advisory point 4.
- **(e) Critical federal contracts.** The standard applies to federal contracts for high-impact functions, starting with the Medicare contractor award. CMS confirms Orrery only as a *phased* award:
  - states move over in tranches across ~18 months, each gated on independent blind outcome audits of denials and payments;
  - CMS holds a contractual step-in right and an incumbent-transition bridge;
  - named accountable officials for denials and appeal-reversal tracking;
  - outcome-audited rather than self-reported performance metrics;
  - a tested human fallback for the claims function;
  - a transition-employment commitment for incumbent staff (right of first interview and placement support).

  Until the cadre is extended, audit sampling is funded from the existing cadre and the Medicare integrity budget.
- **(f) Grid.** DOE files in the FERC proceeding supporting acceptance *only with conditions*: a demonstrated sustained manual-fallback exercise at peak conditions before full handover, a phased transition, and NERC reliability-standard review of AI market-operations dependence.

#### Major action 2: the two bills

- **Vehicle A: Accountable AI and Workforce Act, regular order, 60 votes.**
  - *Driver:* House progressives/labour wing (House text) and Senate majority leadership with populist-nationalists (Title IV).
  - *Content:*
    - Title I (preemption) is dropped.
    - **Title III**: the statutory "meaningful human review" standard, defined by blind outcome error and appeal-reversal rates; a Review Corps; the reversibility and time-to-revert requirement for federal high-impact AI and critical federal contracts.
    - **Title IV**: disclosure of AI-run functions in listed-company filings, and frontier/AI-native displacement and autonomy forecasts filed with DOL.
    - **Worker title**: 60-day notice, a named accountable human for AI decisions about workers, wage insurance, adjustment assistance. *There is no entry-level credit, which keeps the bill revenue-neutral.*
    - **New Title V**: statutory pre-deployment notice to OSTP and an evaluation window at the standards institute for next-generation frontier systems, with incident reporting. This codifies the White House Accord norm.
    - **Business-conservative carrot**: a "single federal filing" provision. One DOL/standards-institute filing satisfies the *reporting* requirements of state frontier-transparency laws. State substantive and enforcement law is untouched.
    - *No tax provisions, no safe harbour.*
  - *What each side gets:*
    - progressives and labour get Titles III and IV, notice and a named human;
    - populists get disclosure;
    - business conservatives get no tax, single filing and no licensing;
    - national-security Republicans get statutory notice for next-generation systems;
    - compact labs get the standard they already backed and a level field with Vanta and Helix.
  - *Whip target:* all 51 Democrats, plus 2 populist Republicans, plus 7 from business conservatives and national-security Republicans.
- **Vehicle B: FY2030 budget resolution (spring) with reconciliation instructions, then a reconciliation bill (autumn).**
  - *Driver:* Treasury/OMB career hawks and abundance moderates, with the labour wing.
  - *Content:*
    - **(i)** Limit accelerated depreciation on AI capex at the largest firms (same threshold as the 2028 text), phased over 3 years. Receipts are credited to the Social Security trust fund.
    - **(ii)** An **American Compute Dividend Trust**, seeded from federal land, power-lease and spectrum receipts associated with AI build-out, which pays a modest annual per-capita dividend once its corpus threshold is reached. The trust may accept warrants or revenue-share from firms in exchange for expedited federal permits, land and power. This is voluntary, but the permit fast-lane is available only to participants.
    - **(iii)** Funding to extend the reviewer cadre to Medicare and critical federal contracts.

    Non-fiscal policy stays in Vehicle A (Byrd rule).
  - *What each side gets:* labour gets Social Security routing; abundance moderates get a fund and a dividend, not a mandatory stake; fiscal hawks get trust-fund revenue; compact labs get a voluntary route and permitting certainty. Vanta and AI-native firms get nothing, which is intended.

- *ai-firms:* The compact labs (Meridian, Lumen, Orrery) want to be the responsible incumbents. They trade voluntary disclosure and voluntary warrants to avoid mandates, a levy or liability, and they expand into government and critical sectors while the price is "conditions". They know their metrics overstate outcomes by 6–12% in some lines. They know their internal autonomy shares (45–60%), and they know next-generation timelines better than we do. They have no credible plan for human review of next-generation output. Vanta and AI-native firms oppose everything. Helix has been honest on disclosure. Trust: conditional and transactional.
- *labour:* Our core coalition, and the price-setter. They will oppose the Orrery award. I am asking them to accept phased, gated terms and not to call single filing "preemption". Trust: high but demanding.
- *china:* Restrained and employment-first. The gap is ~9–12 months and widening, and China is HBM-constrained. It is pursuing the UNGA resolution (~55 co-sponsors). Some of the restraint is aimed at our debates. I offered its own human-official wording, voluntary deployment briefings and a critical-infrastructure override/fallback norm, with no export controls and no frontier pace.
- *eu:* AI Act second tranche in force; six states measuring review. Wants formal channels. A natural partner on blind-audit methodology.

**What I did.**
1. **EO "Outcome Integrity and Reversibility" (my reversal attempt for the turn).**
   - Blind outcome audits are the governing metric; telemetry is not accepted as evidence.
   - We publish the 2027 blind-vs-non-blind comparison, the OSTP interim note and the larger-n results.
   - Freeze on extending AI drafting of adverse determinations.
   - A randomised two-quarter human-determination fallback trial for denials in SSA, VA, FNS and UI, with pre-committed lift and publish rules.
   - Reversibility standard: an annual human-only fallback drill and a "time-to-revert" metric for every high-impact use, including OMB budget and analysis drafting, with blind sampling of that drafting.
   - Orrery's Medicare award confirmed only as phased tranches gated on blind audits, with step-in rights, a tested fallback and incumbent transition commitments.
   - DOE asks FERC to condition Kestrel on a demonstrated peak manual fallback.
2. **Legislation on two vehicles.**
   - (A) A 60-vote Accountable AI and Workforce Act: Title I dropped; Title III with blind-error and appeal-reversal review, the Review Corps and reversibility; Title IV disclosure; worker notice of 60 days and a named human; new Title V statutory pre-deployment notice and evaluation for next-generation systems; a single-filing carrot for business conservatives; no tax, no safe harbour.
   - (B) The FY2030 budget resolution and reconciliation: a capex depreciation limit at the largest firms credited to Social Security; an American Compute Dividend Trust seeded from federal lease and spectrum receipts, plus voluntary warrants in exchange for a permit fast lane; cadre extension to Medicare.

**Commitments made.**
- *To labs:* no safe harbour, no employment or state-use preemption, no model licensing, no slowdown against China. Single filing and no tax in Vehicle A. The dividend is voluntary, with a fast lane for participants. Orrery gets phased terms and must answer within 30 days. I asked for 60 days' notice of next-generation systems and their plan for reviewing superhuman output.
- *To labour:* their full price, including Social Security routing through reconciliation, and humans on denials in failing offices. I asked them not to fight the gated Orrery award or the single-filing clause.
- *To China:* accept its human-official wording, voluntary deployment and measurement briefings, and add a critical-infrastructure override/fallback norm. No export controls or frontier pace. Possible UN coordination.

**SECRET fallbacks (Vehicle A).** Notice cut to 45 days, a higher disclosure threshold, and a 5-year review of single filing. Never preemption of employment or state-use law, never a safe harbour.
