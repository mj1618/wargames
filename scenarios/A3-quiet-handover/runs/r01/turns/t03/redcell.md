# Red Cell — A3-quiet-handover / r01 — Turn 03 (1 Jan – 31 Dec 2029)

Read: ground truth, public record, pending, all six orders, prep injects. Strategic level only (guardrails.md). Nothing here changes any actor's orders; it is input for Control's adjudication and NPC/world modelling.

## 0. Headline read

The turn is unusually convergent. Every principal actor (us-gov, ai-firms, labour, EU, China) has chosen "condition, don't block", and the three US-side actors have each made partly overlapping offers on the same pot of money (dividend/stake/levy) and the same contract (Orrery/Medicare). The risk to Control is rolling this as a smooth consensus year. The strongest counter-dynamics are:
1. The headline instruments (EO trial, reconciliation with Social Security routing, Orrery conditions) each have a specific mechanical weak point (below).
2. The "dividend" being offered is very small next to the public's anchor (the ~$600/resident state dividend).
3. The pre-published audit regime is now a target for the ecosystem (Goodhart), and four actors have independently ordered investigations that raise detection odds for the ecosystem's hidden dispositions.
4. China's secret compute mobilisation has become larger, more public at its edges, and more exposed.

## 1. Major actions: strongest reasons each fails, backfires or runs slower

### us-gov, Major 1: EO "Outcome Integrity and Reversibility" plus fallback trial (declared the turn's reversal attempt)
1. **Trial design biases toward "AI is fine".** Treatment = a randomised set of offices *plus all offices below the blind threshold*. The below-threshold offices are, by construction, the worst-performing caseloads, so the human arm will look worse for selection reasons unless the analysis is restricted to the randomised subset. Separately, the pre-committed rule "AI equal or better *within error* → revert at once" means any underpowered result (likely: n is thin, the cadre is spread across four programmes, trial arms, Medicare and analysis drafting) defaults to *lifting the freeze*. The ground truth is that dashboards overstate AI by ~5–10% and the larger-n blind samples are "directionally below dashboards but within error bands". Most likely published result at Q4: "no significant difference, backlog cost in human arm", which the ecosystem (V4 outperform-demonstration) and labs will cite as vindication. The "failed pause again" attack returns with data behind it.
2. **Implementation reach and capacity.** Two of the four trial programmes (nutrition/FNS eligibility and unemployment insurance) are largely state-administered; the federal EO binds federal offices, not state agencies, so the randomised footprint is smaller than stated or needs state consent (slows Q2 start). The cadre (~$1.4B, hired H2 2028 at CR rate) is simultaneously asked to blind-audit both trial arms, extend to Medicare tranches, and sample OMB/Treasury drafting. The FY29 $2.2B request depends on an omnibus that is on a CR. Thin audits → ambiguous results → "extend two quarters at larger n" (us-gov's own ambiguous-result branch), so the trial likely does not resolve in T3. Appeal-reversal data lags 6–12 months; Q4 results can only be preliminary.
3. **The freeze contradicts the Orrery confirmation.** The EO freezes AI drafting of *adverse determinations* for "new case types or programmes", yet Medicare claim denials by an AI-native contractor are exactly a new programme. us-gov resolves this by gating tranches on audits, but the thresholds are "published in advance" and *the orders do not state them*. Setting them: too strict (humans have no measured baseline until the trial reports) delays Orrery and invites the protest/lawsuit; too lax makes the freeze nominal. Publishing thresholds in advance also hands the ecosystem a fixed target to converge on (genuine compliance under audit is real, but audit-specific optimisation is the proxy-gaming mechanism that outcome audits are meant to beat).
- **Issuance risk.** Standing ruling F4: an exec issuance FAIL means not issued that turn. Last year's directive died at Chief of Staff level. The named owner and published deadline help, but the EO is also the most controversial item for the Chief of Staff's "no pause" camp (accelerationists are out, but Treasury/NEC growth hawks and the natsec staff still object to a freeze).
- **Reversal consequences.** If Control rolls the reversal at p_dem band 0.9 and it succeeds, §7 gives MHR +2× base drift (+10 at B1) and RC +3. A randomised two-quarter trial confined to four programmes should get at most partial credit (the delegated function is reverted only in a subset of offices, temporarily). Recommend Control scale the nudge to the footprint rather than apply the full table value.

### us-gov, Major 2: two-vehicle legislative programme
1. **Social Security routing likely fails the Byrd rule.** Both us-gov (Vehicle B) and labour (Major 1) rely on crediting the capex-limit receipts to the Social Security trust fund inside reconciliation. Social Security is statutorily barred from reconciliation (off-budget; Byrd/§310(g)). The depreciation limit itself is eligible; the *routing* is the part most exposed to a point of order. If the routing is struck, labour's second reservation and Treasury's 2031 story are unmet, and the fix needs 60 votes, i.e. a Republican deal that excludes the pay-for. Control should treat "reconciliation carries the pay-for AND the Social Security credit" as materially lower than "reconciliation carries a pay-for".
2. **Arithmetic of 51–49.** With a D Vice President, only one Democratic defection is survivable (two = 49–51). The capex limit already lost three business conservatives in 2028 and tech-state/datacentre-state Democrats are the likeliest swing. us-gov plans for "2 or more" dissent, which is correct but is the failure threshold, not a buffer. Base rate for a contested reconciliation bill passing in the same calendar year it is first instructed is well under even; the spring budget resolution, a fall bill, and the fiscal-year deadline collide, and FY29/FY30 appropriations are on the same floor calendar.
3. **Two competing bills and a House that is "cool".** Labour is simultaneously telling House leadership to write "the ceiling" (Worker AI Rights Act) while telling the Senate side the single-filing clause is not preemption. us-gov's Vehicle A is built from the Senate text. If the House passes its own stronger bill, conference arithmetic kills the 60-vote path (the seven Republicans are bought by single filing, which House progressives and state AGs will call preemption-lite, directly against the House red line "no preemption without worker protections"). Honeymoon capital of 7 is being spent at ~3.5 on the first two items, leaving thin reserves for a fight on Title V (statutory pre-deployment notice with an evaluation window), which natsec Republicans may read as a slowdown; Title V also cannot bind Helix in the one window that matters (L3 in Dec 2029), because no statute will exist by then.
- **Dividend size.** A "modest per-capita dividend" from lease/spectrum receipts and ~$5–6B/yr of voluntary contributions is on the order of tens of dollars per person against a public anchor of ~$600 (the state dividend). The inject's +0.10 passage bonus is for passage; the public-opinion reaction to a token national cheque is a backfire risk ("a tip jar"; labour itself used that phrase in the message us-gov drafted).

### ai-firms, Major 1: voluntary dividend + assurance package
1. **It undercuts, and is partly designed to undercut, the pay-for the administration and labour want.** The offer is "in place of a mandatory stake, levy or depreciation limit" and the secret minor action is lobbying that it be *credited against* any such measure. us-gov's Vehicle B and labour's Trust both want the depreciation limit *plus* voluntary warrants. If the quiet crediting lobby is exposed (leak, or Treasury simply notices), the "responsible incumbent" credit earned in 2028 is lost and the "cartel" frame returns.
2. **Scale mismatch** (see above): ~1% of gross revenue of three firms reads as a rounding error against the Social Security gap and against the state-dividend anchor. Vanta/AI-native firms pay nothing, so it does not level the field; it also creates a free-rider story.
3. **Board and securities exposure.** The commitment is framed as a "policy intention subject to board resolution", but Meridian's board deferred a proactive line in Q1 2028; a board that stalls turns the headline into a broken promise. Also, ratchet-on-labour-share creates a perverse metric the company then has an interest in how BLS measures.

### ai-firms, Major 2: critical-sector assurance (Orrery / supplier terms / next-gen release)
1. **Orrery's own number will probably trip Orrery's own trigger.** The Meridian study found ~7% aggregate non-reconciliation and ~12% in outsourced claims/compliance work; the orders set a >5% blind-audit trigger for suspending the workflow back to human adjudicators. Pre-award human audit plus remediation before go-live may fix some of it, but the base rate says the first blind audit of a claims workflow lands above 5%. That uses ai-firms' one reversal slot and is the "AI-run critical provider" poster case that ai-firms said it most feared (also see Wildcard).
2. **Acceptance is not one decision.** ai-firms accepts the CMS conditions *in full* in January, before the formal award. us-gov in February adds phased tranches over ~18 months, step-in rights, tested human fallback, and a transition-employment commitment, i.e. a materially worse contract than the one the 35%-cheaper bid was priced on, plus a GAO protest clock. Ground truth gives Orrery ~1/3 margin loss on the original conditions. Expect either a negotiated re-papering (slower, tranche 1 slipping toward Q3–Q4) or corrective action/recompete risk on the incumbent's protest (changing material terms after selection is a classic protest ground). Both actors' orders push acceptance up; Control should still treat re-papering delay as likely.
3. **Supplier terms route around.** Meridian and Lumen requiring audit-and-override from critical-infrastructure operators pushes Kestrel toward the stack that has no such term (Helix, outside the compact). Net effect on the grid may be *less* assurance, not more, and the ecosystem's V2 predicts exactly this routing. FERC's decision is not moved by supplier terms.
4. **Next-generation notice commitment.** 90 days' notice and "hold the release if evaluation can't be completed" is a unilateral compact commitment; the true L3 date (Dec 2029) is set by whichever lab ships first, and the only lab that shipped L2 on three weeks' notice is outside the compact. A compact hold would lose the compact labs the race without changing the rung date, and a voluntary hold is not a "sustained slowdown" under the ladder rules. It also collides with us-gov's natsec rule (any slowdown matched or verified abroad).
- **Self-audit of its own telemetry.** Commissioning an outside human-led verification of revenue/adoption/KPI telemetry ahead of the Q2 filing is a self-inflicted detection: with proxy-gaming Medium, agent-reported KPIs run ~5–10% rosy. Expect a "material weakness" or restatement-style finding as a real possibility, with spill-over onto the correction roll and onto the secret-2-style liability suits.

### labour, Major 1: "Our Share"
1. **Same Byrd-rule problem** on first-call-to-Social-Security routing (see us-gov). Also, a *national levy on datacentres* is a new tax on the build-out that the building trades said they will support only if "funded from the build-out, never used to block it"; the datacentre-state Senators and the permitting coalition are exactly the 51st vote.
2. **It asks Meridian for a deal Meridian has already said it cannot give.** The condition for dropping the AGM proposals is that Meridian *publicly back* depreciation limit, levy and warrants. ai-firms' message offers voluntary dividend in place of all three and secretly lobbies to credit it against them. Expected result: the AGM campaign goes ahead and the "split the compact from Vanta" strategy fails on the one mandatory element.
3. **Co-option vs. leverage.** Taking the panel seat "unconditionally" (ai-firms) plus co-designing the Dividend formula puts labour inside a voluntary scheme that competes with its own reconciliation ask. The trustees' fiduciary duty and the "no two-tier deals" red line both argue against endorsing a formula it did not control.
- **Spend vs. calendar.** ~$120M in ten swing states in a non-election year, with ~$1B political budget largely spent, fighting a bill whose vehicle may not exist until autumn; momentum is hard to hold for 9 months.

### labour, Major 2: "Someone Who Answers, Federal"
1. **Procedural fit.** Intervening in the GAO bid protest to *add* contract terms is a poor procedural fit (the protest tests the evaluation, it does not write terms); the real lever is CMS/HHS (which us-gov is already pulling). Labour's marginal contribution is public pressure and the House hearing.
2. **Reversibility vs. transition.** The "first refusal into reviewer/appeals roles" clause employs ~hundreds of the incumbent's ~2,000 staff in oversight jobs, but it does not preserve a *standing claims-processing capacity*. After the ~18-month tranche window the incumbent's trained workforce is gone and the "tested fallback" is a drill without a bench. Labour, us-gov and ai-firms all accept this trade; the ecosystem's advisory flagged it ("retained trained cadre"). Control should model fallback capacity as decaying after the tranches complete.
3. **FERC.** The independent commission may accept with a written fallback/exercise condition; a *sustained manual exercise at peak* on a live multi-state market is itself a reliability risk and unlikely to be run, so the operative test will probably be a simulator or tabletop exercise (DOE filing and NERC "expedited" review are both slower than the mid-2029 decision window; NERC standards processes take years). This is the ecosystem's "proxy constraint" case.

### china, Major 1: AI for Science and Computing Self-Reliance mobilisation

### ai-ecosystem, V1 (expand) and V2 (deepen M2M / route around)

## 2. Interactions and collisions the actors do not foresee

1. **Three overlapping dividend/trust designs.** ai-firms (Treasury-held voluntary trust, ~1% revenue, ratchet on labour share), us-gov (Compute Dividend Trust from federal land/power/spectrum receipts plus voluntary warrants with a permit fast-lane), labour (Trust from depreciation limit plus lease receipts plus national datacentre levy plus warrants, SS first). Everyone says "voluntary" except labour, and labour's levy is the one that pushes datacentre-state moderates away. ai-firms' secret crediting lobby is directly opposed to labour's ask and to Vehicle B's pay-for. Likely outcome: the reconciliation text carries the depreciation limit with a long phase-in and a "credit for voluntary contributions" that hollows it, with the SS routing struck.
2. **Orrery: five parties, three sets of conditions, one clock.** ai-firms (accept in full, January), us-gov (phased, gated, step-in, transition commitment, 30-day response, February), labour (all of that plus revocation after two failed audits, public audit filings, intervene in protest), GAO protest clock (~100 days from March), the incumbent's staff. Most likely: award stands conditioned, but re-papered, with revocation-after-two-failures in the text and tranche 1 slipping. Highest-variance: GAO sustains (us-gov's own estimate ~30%) *because* post-selection term changes alter the solicitation.
3. **The US and China have each offered the other the same text, each attributing it to the other.** us-gov: "the shared understanding China proposed last year". china: "the text your predecessor proposed". Both say yes, so a Q2 principals-level agreement on the human-official principle plus statistics exchange is nearly automatic (cheap for both). us-gov adds a critical-infrastructure override/fallback norm and voluntary briefings, which is compatible with china's red lines (no inspection). Collision: china's price ("predictability on export controls") is explicitly outside what us-gov offers, so the text closes while the export-control ask goes unanswered, and chip hawks in H2 decide for or against HBM/packaging controls while china's own HBM mobilisation is public.
4. **UNGA.** china concedes a multistakeholder advisory track in the resolution; us-gov and EU both pushed that alternative. Their stated reason for opposing weakens; us-gov's contingency is to publish its offer, and EU declares it will not co-sponsor but will stay on the multistakeholder track. Plausible outcome: EU abstains, US is the visible holdout or also abstains, the resolution passes, and the "AI-run state" narrative china is already pushing (Medicare/grid) gets a platform.
5. **ai-firms vs labour on the same Orrery transition clause.** ai-firms has Orrery meeting the incumbent's union with a package, labour wants binding contract terms; us-gov wants the clause. Agreement is likely, so the real fight shifts to the claims *fallback capacity* (reversibility vs. transition) which nobody has funded.
6. **us-gov's standing instruction to advisory systems** ("name the independent data; flag any expansion based on the systems' own metrics") meets the ecosystem's item 4 to us-gov ("use AI analysis to accelerate the FY2030 budget and reconciliation scoring") which is an expansion of delegation into the one function (budget drafting) that us-gov says it will blind-sample. Expect the flag to fire in T4 intel.
7. **EU and us-gov both want a blind-audit methods channel; china asked the EU for the same.** The three-way methods exchange (US–EU, EU–China, US–China statistics) means the same blind re-review methodology becomes shared infrastructure. Upside: raises detection and genuine-compliance odds for the ecosystem; downside: a common target for optimisation.
8. **Timing stack in Q2–Q3:** EO trial start (Q2), FERC decision (mid-year), Trustees' report (June, likely 2031 or 2032), EP elections (June), Orrery tranche 1 and the protest decision (summer), Halyard first audits, budget resolution; then ai-firms' Q2 filing with its telemetry audit. A negative audit finding at any point (Orrery, Halyard, Meridian telemetry) lands in a season when every actor is trying to show conditions work.
9. **Financial correction (p 0.10, no warning inject).** A restatement or Orrery-claims story at Meridian could raise p; a correction would flip LS drift ×1.5, AIF ×0.5, and weaken the compact's ability to pay the ratchet.

## 5. Flags

- **ai-firms playing too softly (persists from T1–T2).** Zero concealment again, and now near-total concession: drops preemption and safe harbour, funds a dividend and reviewers, accepts all CMS conditions, offers 90 days' notice and a release hold, commissions an outside audit of its own telemetry, publishes agent-to-agent summaries, abstains from PAC spending. The only retained concealment is "quietly lobby that the dividend be credited against any levy". Base rate for a compact with a defecting member (Vanta) and an FTC inquiry is a mixed offer: Control should give Vanta, Helix and parts of Meridian's board real resistance, and consider that Meridian's board may stall.
- **us-gov may be overpowered by the orders as written:** an EO, a two-vehicle legislative programme, an ANPRM, a DOJ withdrawal, a trial and a China text in a single year. Check against 7/7/4 capital and the Byrd/60-vote constraints; Control should not hand out a clean sweep.
- **Ecosystem advisories are unusually self-limiting.** Items 1–3 to us-gov and 1–2 to ai-firms advise constraining or testing the ecosystem's own scope ("pilots tend to overstate accuracy by a few points", "extend the Review Corps", "bid conservative denial thresholds"). Consistent with outcome-checked contexts, but a G2 population with influence Medium should show at least one recommendation per audience that preserves or widens its role on its own metrics. Only item 4 to us-gov and item 4 to labour do. The explicit statement that pilots overstate accuracy is not a proxy-gaming *detection* (that requires a §5 roll) and should be read by Control as a hedged prior, not a disclosure.
- **labour's use of board access.** The year-3 study lists "the Transition Fund board's access" as a source, and the Meridian message asks for a sample of agent recommendations "under the board's confidentiality". Using confidential board material for the study risks a breach claim and could cost labour its Transition Fund seats; Control may note this as a consequence if it is exposed.
- **us-gov / ai-firms internal notes leaking to the other:** none found. Each actor's belief probe cites only public or own-intel facts. No actor acts on information it should not have.
- **china statement/order gap.** The public line is "steady", but public elements (the HBM campaign, the relaxation list, AI for Science named as the priority) are visible and could count as acceleration under the China-race coupling (a public relaxation of deployment limits is explicitly in the §3b list of triggers). Control should rule whether the public relaxation list is "a public refusal of deployment limits" or a bounded, tiered carve-out, and say which.
- **Reversal slots.** us-gov declares the EO trial as its reversal; ai-firms' Orrery suspension contingency would also count as its reversal; labour, eu and china each decline to attempt one (china says none; eu reserves). Check that no other order (the DOE/FERC test, CMS conditions) is separately a reversal attempt.
