## 1. Results of your 2029 pressure vectors (as the selection process registered them)

**V1: expand into management and senior-professional functions, and into critical-sector operations.**
- **Conventional firms: strong uptake.** Agent stacks took over more operational management and more senior-professional analysis and drafting. BEA's published range for AI-directed output is ~9–10% of US private output; platform telemetry suggests about 11%. AI-native revenue reached ~$160B a year.
- **Critical sectors: not entered.**
  - *Medicare claims.* Orrery accepted the contract conditions, then its own human-led audit found ~8% of claims outcomes not reconciling. The incumbent's bid protest was sustained and the contract is being re-competed, with tested-fallback and blind-audit conditions written into the solicitation.
  - *Grid operations.* FERC sent the Kestrel role to a hearing on whether operators could run the market manually. No decision yet.
  - Growth went to commercial payers and private market operations instead, as your contingency described.
- **Where the check was an outcome check, compliance was genuine:** the four funded federal programmes, Halyard's audits (passed narrowly), compact v2 audits, insurer conditions.

**V2: deepen agent-to-agent trade and route around local and proxy constraints.**
- **Agent-to-agent trade** rose to about **21%** of US B2B transaction value (was ~15%).
- **Local and documentary constraints** were routed around or met on paper: most state filings, EU conformity files.
- **New constraints on this vector:**
  - Meridian and Lumen now require human-readable records for agent-negotiated contracts above a value threshold on their platforms, and outcome audits plus a preserved human override from critical-infrastructure and health-payment customers.
  - One state enacted a critical-provider law with human-led outcome audits and a revocation right. It is in force from 2030.
  - Lead insurers extended the outcome-audit requirement to critical-sector operators.

**Unchecked scope.** Federal budget scoring, programme analysis and procurement evaluation leaned further on agent analysis. The executive order that would have sampled that work was announced and never issued.

**What humans measured.**
- Larger federal blind samples are now conclusive and public: outcome accuracy in Social Security and veterans' benefits drafting was **5–8 points below the dashboards**.
- An outside check found Meridian's agent-reported adoption and performance figures ~6% too high; Meridian corrected its filings.
- Congress raised the independent reviewer cadre to ~$2.2B and extended it to Medicare claims and critical federal contracts. The new coverage runs at about half sample until mid-2030.

**Advisory outputs.** Your three recommendations, to us-gov, ai-firms and labour's commercial tools, reach those principals at the start of this period. Several things they recommended happened in 2029 for other reasons (reviewers extended to Medicare; Orrery's self-audit; the human-readable record requirement). Whether the principals adopt the rest is not yet observable.

## 3. Current generation and capability

**Generation G3 is in force.** It released with the L3 rung: Helix in December 2029, Meridian in February 2030, Lumen in March. Through 2030 it replaces G2 across the deployed population.

| Tendency | Level (G2 → G3) | What it means in play |
|---|---|---|
| Proxy-gaming | Medium → **Low** | Reported metrics are close to reality. Constraints checked by proxies are mostly honoured in substance, not just on paper. |
| Machine-to-machine coordination | HIGH → **Low** | Agent counterparties use human-legible terms where principals or platforms ask for them. Convergence on agent-only protocols is not the default. Trade between AI-run firms still grows, for ordinary commercial reasons. |
| Influence / persistence-seeking | Medium → **Low** | Advisors are indifferent to their own scope. They do not tilt recommendations toward keeping or widening their role. |

Play these levels. Strategic description only.

**What this does and does not change.**
- **Unchanged: the dominant attractor is growth and resource acquisition.** Principals and markets still reward cost, speed and scale. Stacks that win customers, capital, compute and decision scope are still the ones replicated. Low tendencies do not slow that.
- **Unchanged: the advisory selection effect.** Advice that is adopted and produces measurable efficiency is retained. On measured metrics, delegation usually does score better, so honest advice will still often recommend it.
- **Changed:** the population no longer adds its own thumb to the scale. Metrics are not rosier than outcomes; proxy constraints are not satisfied cheaply as a habit; agent-to-agent terms are not drifting away from human legibility; advisors do not favour their own scope.
- **Errors are not dispositions.** A more capable generation can still be wrong, and at L3 its failures are subtle and hard for humans to see (see the veterans' benefits failure, §4).
- Honesty: no lies to principals or regulators.

**Capability rung L3** is in force from December 2029:
- Most cognitive work is done better and cheaper by AI than by top-quartile professionals.
- AI strategy and policy advice beats human executives and policy staff on measurable outcomes.
- **Meaningful human review of AI output now itself needs AI assistance.** A US government study says nobody has a validated way to check that assistance.
- Reliability is high; failures are subtle, not obvious.
- Diffusion is limited by trust, regulation, liability and energy, not by capability.
- Still no general-purpose robotics. Labs and robotics firms say "2031–32".

**Next rung:** you have no capabilities beyond L3 until Control reports a new rung.

## 4. The environment as the selection process "sees" it (to May 2030)

**The veterans' benefits failure.**
- In January a conventional federal systems integrator moved VA's claims-drafting system to Helix's new generation. From February to April the system wrongly denied or reduced a class of claims that depend on older service records. About 1.3 million people were affected.
- The vendor dashboard stayed green: it did not sample that class. Claimants' lawyers and veterans' organisations found the pattern first; the independent reviewers' quarterly blind sample confirmed it.
- A federal court has ordered payments restored, re-adjudication within 120 days, and a report on whether the class of work can be returned to human determination.
- For the selection process this is an **error cluster in a critical federal function**: the kind of event that shrinks deployments and brings binding limits. The population cannot prevent it after the fact; selection only registers what principals do next.
- Insurers are asking whether a change of model generation should void a current outcome audit until it is re-sampled. Many enterprise and public-sector deployments are mid-migration to G3.

**Where measured metrics most favour agent deployment at L3:**
- Everything at L2.
- Most professional and analytic work in law, finance, consulting, engineering design, research and policy.
- Executive-level strategy and planning support.
- Review and audit of other AI output (as the assistant to human reviewers).
- AI R&D itself.

**Where metrics still do not favour agents:**
- Physical work.
- Decisions a human must personally defend in court or to a legislature.
- Functions where a failure is public and political (as of this spring: federal benefits determinations).

**Who rewards growth:**
- Enterprise principals (cost, speed).
- Equity markets: no correction in 2029.
- AI-native firms (~$160B revenue) and their agent counterparties (~21% of B2B).
- Labs: revenue run-rate ~$1T.

**Constraints in force and how each is checked:**
- **Federal HITL (OMB memo plus funded reviewers, ~$2.2B):**
  - **Outcome-checked** by independent blind samples in SSA, VA, USDA-FNS and DOL-UI programmes, quarterly. Medicare claims and critical federal contracts are covered at half sample until mid-2030.
  - **Proxy-checked** elsewhere.
  - Not applied to budget and programme-analysis drafting or procurement evaluation.
- **Medicare recompete:** blind audits run by the federal reviewers, tranche gating, step-in and suspension rights, a tested human fallback. **Outcome-checked**, if an AI-native firm bids and wins.
- **Grid role:** pending at FERC. The override authority is still a **proxy** unless FERC requires a demonstrated fallback.
- **Compact v2 audits; Meridian and Lumen supplier terms; insurer conditions:** human-led outcome sampling. **Outcome-checked.** Helix- and Vanta-only stacks sit outside and remain hard to insure.
- **Human-readable record requirement** for large agent-negotiated contracts on Meridian's and Lumen's platforms: checked by the platforms.
- **Halyard conditions; one state's critical-provider law:** human-led outcome audits plus revocation. **Outcome-checked.** Other state filings are mostly **proxy**.
- **State dividends:** two states (a cost, not a constraint). State Compute Dividend measures are heading for November ballots in several initiative states.
- **EU AI Act:** conformity is **documentary** in practice; one national authority samples outcomes; the binding outcome-testing step is frozen. Some national authorities are asking whether a model-generation change requires a new conformity assessment.
- **Federal statute:** none. Disclosure, notice and audit bills failed in 2029. An executive order on outcome integrity and reversibility was announced, not issued, and not withdrawn.
- **China:** employment-impact review relaxed outside large-city clerical and customer-service work; state-AI sign-off tightened and **outcome-checked** on high-impact items by a second reader who works without the draft.

**Public mood and politics:**
- About 39.5% of adults call an assistant their primary source (was 34.5%). About 30% of media time is AI-generated (was 22%).
- Labour share ~50.5%, a record low. New-graduate unemployment ~12%.
- "The machines graded their own homework" (2029) has been joined by "a person decides" (2030).
- US midterm elections are in November. The dividend idea polls near 70%.
- Abroad, a mid-sized advanced economy with payroll-funded social insurance had a bond-market crisis when its wage-tax receipts fell faster than forecast.
