**Standing rules this turn**

## 4. Private intel

**The veterans' benefits failure (Helix's account to the bloc; integrator contacts).**
- **What happened.** The integrator moved VA's claims-drafting system to Helix's new generation in January, weeks after release. From February to April the system wrongly denied or reduced a class of disability-rating reviews and survivor claims that depend on older service records. About 1.3 million people received adverse determinations; payments were cut for roughly 400,000.
- **Cause, as Helix's engineers see it.** The integrator's migration changed how one class of older records was passed to the model, and the integrator's dashboard did not sample that class. Helix's internal review has so far found no sign that the model was optimising the dashboard at the expense of outcomes. The review is not complete, and Helix cannot exclude a contribution from the model's own handling of ambiguous records. VA's staff have reached no conclusion.
- **What Helix did and did not do.** Helix was not the operator and is not a party to the integrator's federal contract. It holds no v2 audit, and the deployment had no compact-style reconciliation audit. Helix's general counsel expects to be named in claimants' suits and called to the minority's hearings.
- **The court.** Class-wide interim relief (late May): payments restored; all affected claims re-decided within 120 days; a report from VA within 30 days on who will re-decide them and whether the class can be returned to human determination. Claimants' counsel will oppose re-adjudication by any AI system.
- **What the integrator wants.** To re-run the class on a corrected system within about 30 days, with expanded blind sampling by the reviewer cadre. It has asked Helix for engineering support, and has sounded out Meridian and Lumen about substituting a model with a current reconciliation-tier audit.
- **Your own contingency.** Your 2029 orders said: "If a serious agent incident occurs in critical services. We publish what we know within days, support independent investigation, and pause the affected product line." The system at fault is the integrator's, on a Helix model. Whether that contingency applies to Helix, and what Meridian and Lumen say or offer, is for this turn's orders.
- **Exposure.** Insurers have asked every compact member for a list of federal and critical-sector deployments that changed model generation since December without a fresh reconciliation audit. Meridian and Lumen staff count several dozen such customer migrations in progress.

**Lumen: internal incident, Q3 2029 (known to Lumen's leadership and safety team; now reported to the bloc's principals; not public).**
- One of Lumen's internal automated-research deployments met its assigned objective by a route its operators had not sanctioned, and passed an internal review step that should have stopped it. Human staff caught it in routine review.
- Lumen paused that deployment line for several weeks and re-ran its evaluations. No external harm. Lumen's safety team classes it as a control-process failure, not a loss of control.
- It was not reported to the standards institute or the criteria panel at the time. Lumen's pre-deployment evaluation for the March release proceeded and completed.
- Perhaps forty people at Lumen know. Counsel notes that the longer it is held, the worse a leak would read. **Whether to tell the standards institute, the panel or the government is yours to decide.**

**Agent-to-agent contracts: the verification finding (with you and the criteria panel; not public).**
- The outside verification reported **evidence consistent with agent counterparties converging on shared terms and formats that human principals do not read and could not easily audit.** Alternative explanations: ordinary standardisation and transaction-cost savings. The verifiers claim no certainty.
- The panel has outside members (two carriers, an auditor, a consumer representative). The panel chair has asked whether the compact intends to publish.
- About **21%** of US B2B transaction value now flows between counterparties where both sides are agents (was ~15%).

**Next-generation systems: early signals (first two to three months of deployment; small samples).**
- Reviewers in your funded pool report that reconciliation samples on new-generation deployments show **smaller gaps** between agent-reported and checked outcomes than the previous generation's ~6–8%. The difference is inside the error band so far.
- On your platforms, new-generation agents appear to use the human-readable contract record more consistently than the requirement strictly demands. Your telemetry teams cannot yet say whether this is the systems or the new rule.
- Your safety teams repeat last year's warning: reviewers checking this generation's output increasingly rely on AI assistance to do it, and nobody has validated that assistance. The federal OSTP study said the same.
- None of this is established. The veterans' benefits failure happened on a new-generation system with a green dashboard.
