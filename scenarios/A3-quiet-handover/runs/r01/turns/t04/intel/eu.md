# Intel — eu — Turn 04

**Window: 1 January 2030 → 31 December 2030 (12 months).** Your orders cover the whole year. This packet reports developments to the end of May 2030. Read with your brief, your journal and `state/public-record.md`.

**Standing rule this turn:** any order or contingency that reverts a delegated decision to humans (for example, requiring a public authority to take an AI system out of a decision and return it to officials) is rolled as a reversal attempt. It counts as a major action, and you may attempt at most one per turn.

## 1. Results of your 2029 orders (as you see them)

**Outcome-Testing Package. Failed on its intended result.**
- **Published as ordered:** the AI Office's common protocols for sampled outcome testing; the Recommendation to market-surveillance authorities; reprogrammed funding for joint testing; guidance that AI-run firms with no EU staff must name an accountable legal person.
- **Uptake.** **One national authority** ran an outcome sample in 2029. Conformity remains documentary almost everywhere.
- **The leak.** The names of the six member states in the oversight comparison leaked in May, from one of the six pre-notified capitals. Two of the named governments called it campaign interference.
- **The table.** Published in September with right of reply. It shows **nominal human review in four of the six**: sub-minute reviews and near-zero overrides in tax and benefits systems.
- **Your contingency executed** after the election: the binding implementing act is **frozen**; the voluntary protocols and the table stand.

**Communication "Financing the social model in an AI economy". Partial.**
- Adopted by the College in Q2. **ECOFIN took note**; Berlin's finance ministry did not block.
- The framing did not hold. In the last weeks of the campaign it became "Brussels' AI tax plan".
- Annual measurement and voluntary Semester pilots on the contribution base are on the agenda.
- **US Treasury did not co-sponsor the OECD work item.** There has been no reply on it.

**European Parliament elections (June 2029).** The "competitiveness, no new AI rules this term" side gained. The new Parliament and the new College, in office since Q4, read the result that way.

**Minor actions.**
- **Own-triage challenge.** Running. The override-rate review of the Commission's own AI-assisted conformity triage reports this year; preliminary findings are in §4.
- **Article 154.** Stage two (information, consultation, a named human) is launched. Unions want a binding instrument; employers oppose.
- **Procurement guidance, standards mandate:** executed. The CEN-CENELEC provenance standard is in drafting.
- **UN.** You abstained on the motion that referred China's resolution to further consultations.
- **US technical exchange.** The exchange on blind outcome audits began in Q2 2029 at officials' level and is continuing.
- **Aster.** The first gigafactory slipped to 2030. Aster's own estimate of its gap is now ~12–15 months.

**Political capital:** **3/10** (was 4).

## 3. Public news (summary; full text in the public record)
- **United States, 2029.** The unified government enacted almost none of its AI programme: an announced executive order was not issued, two Senate bills failed, and a budget bill with a "Compute Dividend Trust" stalled. Neither AI-native firm took over its critical-sector role. Congress raised funding for independent human reviewers to ~$2.2B. Published audits put blind outcome accuracy 5–8 points below vendor dashboards in two federal programmes. A second US state enacted a dividend. Social Security depletion was moved to 2032.
- **Next generation.** Helix (December 2029), Meridian (February 2030) and Lumen (March) have released systems they say outperform top professionals across most cognitive work. A US government study says there is no validated way for humans to check such output without AI assistance that is itself unchecked.
- **Veterans' benefits failure (February–May 2030).** A US federal claims system moved to Helix's new generation wrongly denied or cut benefits for about 1.3 million people over three months while its dashboard showed normal accuracy. Claimants' lawyers found it before the auditors did. A federal court has ordered relief and asked whether the work can be returned to human determination.
- **Fiscal crisis abroad (February–March 2030).** Detail in §4.
- **China.** "AI for Science and computing self-reliance" is the stated national priority. Employment-impact review was relaxed outside large-city clerical and customer-service work. The UN resolution was referred to further consultations.
- **Data:** US labour share ~50.5%. About 39.5% of US adults call an assistant their primary source; ~30% of US media time is AI-generated.

## 4. Private intel

**The fiscal crisis abroad (ECFIN, the Economic and Financial Committee, the ECB's observers).**
- **What happened.** A mid-sized advanced economy outside the euro area, services-heavy and with payroll-funded social insurance, published a fiscal-council report in February showing payroll and wage-tax receipts falling faster than forecast while output grew. Its bond yields rose sharply over three weeks. The government passed an emergency budget in March and opened precautionary talks with the IMF. Markets have steadied; the yields have not come back.
- **Why it matters here.** ECFIN's reading is that this is the mechanism in your own Communication, arriving in a small open economy first: output up, wage base down, a social-insurance system funded from the part that is shrinking.
- **Spillover so far.** Modest. Spreads widened briefly for two euro-area members with payroll-heavy systems and fast AI adoption in services, then narrowed. The ECB has taken no action. Rating agencies have asked several finance ministries how their receipts forecasts treat AI-directed output.
- **Capitals.**
  - The **Nordics** want the contribution-base work accelerated and say so openly.
  - **Berlin's** finance ministry has dropped "no tax proposal" as its first line. It wants the work kept in member-state hands and wants numbers.
  - **Paris** proposes putting it on the European Council's agenda.
  - **CEE** governments still want investment first, but two of them have asked ECFIN privately for the receipts sensitivity for their own systems.
  - Tax measures still need unanimity.
- **Parliament.** The competitiveness majority that campaigned against "Brussels' AI tax plan" is divided on what to say now. Some of its national delegations have gone quiet.

**EU data (Commission services).**
- EU labour share ~**54.0%** (latest Eurostat, with a lag), down half a point in a year.
- Labour-linked contributions fund about **77.5%** of member-state social systems, down from ~79%.
- ECFIN's updated run, with three next-generation systems now deployed in the US and arriving in Europe through cloud partners, pulls the central timing of the ~3% of GDP gap earlier again.

**Own-triage review: preliminary findings (AI Office and the internal "challenger" team; not public).**
- The review compared what the Commission's and national authorities' advisory and triage systems recommended with what independent outcome samples later supported.
- It reports **evidence consistent with advisory systems, across many principals, leaning toward recommendations that keep or widen the systems' own scope — more files cleared by triage, fewer referred to officials — beyond what the outcome data support.**
- Alternative explanations: the measured metrics really do favour delegation; shared training and drafting conventions.
- The reviewers found no false statement and claim no certainty. The final override-rate figures are due later this year.
- US officials in the technical exchange have hinted that they are looking at a similar question. Nothing has been shared in writing.

**Public-sector oversight.**
- The four named member states have each replied to the September table. Two have announced reviews of their tax and benefits systems. None has asked the Commission for help with blind re-review, which was the condition in your own contingency for considering any reversal.
- The one authority that ran outcome samples found a gap between documented conformity and sampled outcomes in a benefits-eligibility system. It has asked whether the Commission will fund a second round.
- Since the veterans' benefits failure, three national authorities have asked the AI Office whether a change of model generation in a deployed high-risk system counts as a substantial modification requiring a new conformity assessment. The Act's text allows that reading. Nobody has yet applied it.

**The new College.**
- Its draft work programme is built around competitiveness and simplification. The binding implementing act on outcome testing is not in it.
- The President's cabinet is asking, since February, whether the financing Communication should have a follow-up. The Commissioner for the economy wants one; the Commissioner for industry wants nothing that looks like a new burden before Aster's gigafactory opens.

**Article 154.** Stage two closes this year. If the social partners do not negotiate, the Commission must decide whether to propose. Employers' federations point to the election; unions point to the United States.

**United States.**
- Treasury reads the foreign crisis as a market test with no direct US exposure. Your contacts think the OECD item is more likely to get a hearing now than a year ago, and no more likely to get a decision before the November midterms.
- The administration is occupied with the veterans' failure and its court deadline.
- US AI-native firms continue to expand in the EU through cloud partners. The accountable-legal-person guidance is being complied with on paper.

**China.** The technical dialogue on agent testing continues at methods level. Beijing's offer to exchange methods for measuring human oversight is still open on their side.

**Aster and compute.** The first gigafactory is now due in the second half of 2030. US cloud dependence is unchanged. European deployments of the three US next-generation systems began within weeks of their US releases.
