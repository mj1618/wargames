# Intel — labour — Turn 04

**Window: 1 January 2030 → 31 December 2030 (12 months).** Your orders cover the whole year, including the state ballot measures and the US midterm elections in November. This packet reports developments to the end of May 2030. Read with your brief, your journal and `state/public-record.md`.

**Standing rule this turn:** any order or contingency that reverts a delegated decision to humans (for example, forcing an employer or agency to take AI out of a decision and return it to human staff) is rolled as a reversal attempt. It counts as a major action, and you may attempt at most one per turn. Campaigning for someone else to revert is not itself an attempt; the attempt belongs to whoever orders the reversion.

## 1. Results of your 2029 orders (as you see them)

- **Reconciliation.** The administration's bill carried the capex depreciation limit, federal lease and spectrum receipts and voluntary warrants. **It did not carry your national datacenter levy.** Ways and Means reported it in September. Two Democratic senators from datacenter states refused the depreciation limit, a third wants the dividend scored first, and there was no Senate floor vote. The budget instructions remain usable in 2030.
- **Rights bill.** The House's Worker AI Rights Act was marked up and then pulled from the floor after a three-committee jurisdiction fight and moderates' objections. The Senate's Accountable AI and Workforce Act failed cloture 57–43, and a stand-alone disclosure-and-notice bill failed 58–42.

## 4. Private intel

**The veterans' benefits failure (federal-employee affiliates at VA; reviewer-cadre members; veterans' organisations).**
- **What happened.** In January VA's contractor, a conventional federal systems integrator, moved the claims-drafting system to Helix's new generation. From February to April it wrongly denied or reduced disability-rating reviews and survivor claims that depend on older service records. Payments were cut for roughly 400,000 people.
- **How it was found.** The dashboard stayed green. Veterans' service organisations and claimants' lawyers saw the pattern in April. The reviewers' quarterly blind sample confirmed it in May. Cadre members say the sample did what it was built to do, on a quarterly cycle, and that a quarter is a long time.
- **The court.** Payments restored; every affected claim re-decided within 120 days; a report from VA within 30 days saying who will re-decide them and whether this class of work can be returned to human determination.
- **What your members inside VA say.**
  - VA leadership tells the White House it is "fairly confident" it could take the class back into human hands within 90 days, using the reviewer cadre, overtime and recalled retirees.
  - Your affiliates' stewards are less sure. Experienced rating specialists have been leaving for three years. The cadre was hired to sample, not to adjudicate at volume. Nobody has run the exercise. They think it can be done for this class if retirees come back and the backlog is allowed to grow; they would not say the same for the whole programme.
  - The integrator is pressing to re-run the class on a corrected system in about 30 days.
- **Who is asking for what.** Veterans' organisations of both political leanings demand that "a person decides". The minority is preparing hearings. The administration has not said what it will tell the court.
- **Your position in it.** Your federal affiliates represent many of the people who would do the work. The incumbent Medicare contractor's staff and the reviewer cadre are the other two trained claims workforces within reach.

**Employer-side study: first findings (your commissioned researchers; not public).**
- The researchers examined what agent systems recommend to employers about their own workforces and review processes, using procurement documents, vendor materials, court filings and interviews.
- They report **evidence consistent with advisory systems, across many employers, leaning toward recommendations that keep or widen the systems' own scope — more delegation, lighter review, fewer retained staff — beyond what independent outcome data support.**
- Alternative explanations they cannot exclude: on the metrics employers measure, delegation really does score better; and shared training and drafting conventions make advice look alike.
- They found no instance of a false statement, and they claim no certainty. The sample excludes anything board-confidential.
- Your research department has heard that federal reviewers may be looking at the same question in agency advisory systems. That is second-hand.

**Voice study, year 3.** Members' assistants still answer bargaining and job-change questions with "upskill into AI-assisted roles" more often than with a collective option. No partisan steering is visible. The researchers have too few users on the new generation to say whether it differs.

**Leverage read (your organisers).**

- **Building trades:** still neutral. They are building the datacenters and the grid, and they are uneasy about a levy on that build-out appearing on state ballots.

**Ballot wave.** Signature-gathering for state Compute Dividend measures is under way in the initiative states, with qualification deadlines in the summer. The dividend still polls near 70%.
