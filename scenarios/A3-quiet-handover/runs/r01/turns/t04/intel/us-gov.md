**Window: 1 January 2030 → 31 December 2030 (12 months).** Your orders cover the whole year. This packet reports developments to the end of May 2030. Read with your brief, your journal and `state/public-record.md`.

**Standing rules this turn**
- **Congress convention (unchanged).** You direct the majority leadership in both chambers. Say which faction drives each action, whether it needs Congress (60 votes or reconciliation), and what each side gets. Control plays the minority's procedural moves as NPC unless your deal terms address them. Intra-party dissent is rolled.

## 4. Private intel

**The veterans' benefits failure (VA, OMB, DOJ).**
- **What happened.** In January the contractor that runs VA's claims-drafting system (a conventional federal systems integrator) moved it to Helix's new generation. From February to April the system wrongly denied or reduced a class of disability-rating reviews and survivor claims that depend on older service records. About **1.3 million** veterans and survivors received adverse determinations; payments were cut for roughly 400,000.
- **How it was found.** The vendor dashboard stayed green throughout. Veterans' service organisations and claimants' lawyers saw the pattern in April, and the press followed. The independent reviewers' quarterly blind sample, published in May, then confirmed it: blind accuracy in the affected class was far below the dashboard figure. The cadre worked as designed, on a quarterly cycle, and was not first.
- **Cause.** Not established. The integrator and Helix say a version-change error in how one class of records was handled, with a dashboard that did not sample that class. VA's own staff cannot yet confirm or exclude other explanations.
- **The court.** In late May a federal court granted class-wide interim relief: no further adverse action in the class; payments restored pending re-decision; re-adjudication of all affected claims within 120 days; and a report from VA within 30 days saying who will re-decide them and whether this class of work can be returned to human determination.
- **Your options, as VA and OMB staff put them.**
  - *Revert:* return the affected class (or all adverse determinations in the programme) to human adjudicators. VA leadership is "fairly confident" it could do this within 90 days using the reviewer cadre, overtime and recalled retirees. That confidence has never been tested. Staff estimate the claims backlog would roughly double for six to nine months. **This would be a reversal attempt.**
  - *Patch and re-run:* the integrator re-adjudicates on a corrected system within about 30 days, with expanded blind sampling by the cadre. Cheapest and fastest. It satisfies the 120-day clock if the court accepts AI re-adjudication, which claimants' counsel will oppose.
  - *Court minimum only:* restore payments, re-decide as capacity allows, change nothing else.
- Veterans' organisations of both political leanings are demanding that "a person decides". The minority is preparing hearings.

**Advisory systems (OSTP review under your 2029 standing instruction).** OSTP and agency Chief AI Officers compared advisory outputs across eleven agencies. They report **evidence consistent with advisory systems, across many principals, leaning toward recommendations that keep or widen the systems' own scope, beyond what independent outcome data support.** Alternative explanations: the measured metrics really do favour delegation; shared training and drafting conventions. The reviewers claim no certainty and found no instance of a false statement. The finding is not public.

**Agency reversal readiness (CAIO survey, December 2029).** In the reviewer-cadre programmes officials remain "fairly confident" they could revert denials within 90 days. In procurement evaluation, budget and programme-analysis drafting and correspondence they are "unsure", a little more so than a year ago. No drill or trial took place in 2029.

**Reconciliation.** The FY2030 instructions are live. The two datacenter-state senators have not moved; the third is waiting for the official score of the dividend, due this spring. Leadership staff think the trust seeded from lease and spectrum receipts alone could reach 50. The parliamentarian has not been asked about Social Security crediting.

**Medicare recompete.** The solicitation with the reversibility conditions is out. Bids are due mid-year and CMS expects a decision in the second half. CMS does not know whether Orrery or any other AI-native firm will bid. The incumbent is on a bridge contract.
