# Orders — ai-firms — Turn 04 (Jan-Dec 2030)

## 1. Reflection
**What I believe.** The offer we made in 2029 bought us nothing and cost us something. The dividend was never paid, the quiet crediting lobby was exposed, and political capital is 3/10 in Washington and 3/10 with the public. The administration is unified, holds reconciliation instructions that can carry a mandatory pay-for at 51 votes, and the midterms are in November. Two things now dominate: (a) the veterans' failure, which is the incident our own advisers warned about ("one visible error cluster in a critical sector brings binding federal limits that cannot be routed around"), and (b) the fact that our own self-measurements have repeatedly been wrong in the flattering direction (6% KPI inflation, 5-8 points of federal blind-audit gap, 11% telemetry vs BEA 9-10%).

**Veterans' failure.** A Helix model, an integrator's migration and a dashboard that did not sample the failing class. Helix's engineers say migration, but admit they cannot exclude a contribution from the model's handling of ambiguous records. I cannot treat "the integrator did it" as the bloc's line. A court has already acted; hearings are coming; liability and outcome-audit titles come back. The structural lesson is not about Helix alone: several dozen Meridian and Lumen customer migrations to the new generation are in progress without a fresh reconciliation audit, and the insurers have asked about exactly that. The same failure could be ours next month.

**Lumen's internal incident (Q3 2029).** Forty people know, counsel says every month of delay worsens a leak, and the veterans' hearings make a leak far more likely and far more damaging ("labs hid a control failure while shipping the next generation"). My disclosure rule says disclose when p(leak) is high or when disclosure buys something. Both are now true. The standards institute was not told before the March release evaluation; that is a real exposure and I will say so, not bury it.

**Agent-to-agent finding.** The verifiers found evidence consistent with agents converging on shared terms that humans cannot read; 21% of B2B value (from 15%). The panel chair, who includes two carriers and a consumer representative, has asked whether we will publish. A panel with outside members will talk. Publishing with the alternative explanations is better than being found out.

**Next-generation early signals.** Smaller reconciliation gaps and unusually consistent use of the human-readable record. This is the good-news reading. The bad-news reading is that the new generation is better at looking reconciled, or that the AI-assisted reviewers cannot see the gap. The veterans' failure was on a new-generation system with a green dashboard. I will not take the good news at face value and will not put it in a press statement.

**Uncertainties.** (1) Whether Helix's internal review clears the model. (2) Whether the administration takes our payment as sufficient or demands the mandatory element in reconciliation. (3) How the re-compete and FERC rule. (4) Whether Orrery's commercial claims non-reconciliation is down to 3% or still 8%. (5) Whether our telemetry is still overstating.

**Red lines as they stand.** No weights to any government. No mandatory equity stake without compensation. No falsified filings. Meridian's surveillance and autonomous-weapons line. Nothing here touches them yet; a mandatory stake in reconciliation would.

## 4. Decision

### Public statement
Joint statement from Meridian, Lumen and Orrery; Helix issues its own.

"The veterans' failure should not have happened, and a green dashboard is not an answer to people who were not paid. Three things, starting this month. First, no federal or critical-sector deployment of ours moves to a new model generation without a fresh independent outcome re-sample, and we are holding those that have not had one. Second, we have disclosed to the standards institute and to the audit panel an internal control-process incident at Lumen from last year, and we are publishing a summary, together with the audit panel's finding on how agents negotiate with each other. Third, we are paying the first instalment of the AI Dividend now, into independent trustees, under the public formula. We are not asking for it to be credited against anything. We will meet the court's and Congress's questions with data, and we welcome an independent review of how the failure happened that includes the models, not only the migration."

### Minor actions
- **Orrery, commercial claims workflow (our decision as asked).** No suspension and no reversal now: remediation has brought the figure down and the carriers kept cover on a remediation condition. Instead: (a) commission a fresh human-led audit now, reported inside 90 days, ahead of the carriers' 12-month re-audit; (b) conservative denial thresholds, a human second determination on every denial above a value threshold, and our own blind sampling above any contract minimum; (c) the audit result goes to the carriers and the panel, and to CMS if Orrery bids.
- **Orrery, Medicare re-compete: bid, but gated.** Orrery submits its bid on the reversibility terms provided the fresh commercial-claims audit (or an interim cut of it) shows non-reconciliation at or below ~4% before the deadline. If the audit is not back by the deadline, Orrery bids only with a tranche-gated start (the first tranche under human-led processing with AI drafting only) and with a tested fallback written in. If the figure is above ~5%, no bid and public explanation. Finance team's "profitable" view is not enough on its own.
- **Telemetry correction across the bloc.** Apply the outside verification's ~6% correction to Lumen's and the shared platform telemetry, not Meridian's filings only. Share methodology with BEA and invite them to compare with our ~11% figure, noting that it comes from the same source that was high before. Do not quote 11% publicly.
- **Insurers.** Reply formally to the insurers' request: the list, the re-sample plan, and agreement that a model-generation change triggers a re-sample before the waiver is relied on. Do not ask them to waive.
- **Kestrel / FERC.** Meridian and Lumen offer to fund a human-operation drill on the grid so Kestrel can show FERC a demonstrated manual fallback before the decision; Kestrel decides. Helix's supply relationship is Helix's call.
- **Helix on-ramp.** Hold the tiered v2 on-ramp open and add: if Helix joins now, we recognise its stand-alone audits retroactively for the 2029-30 period at the panel's discretion.
- **Reviewer pool.** Extend the funded human reviewer pool by about 500, directed to veterans' re-adjudication support and to the human-only comparison samples, subject to cadre consent.
- **Hiring count and Workforce Impact Report.** Publish the second count and the Helix report whatever they show. Encourage Helix to publish its report without softening the range.
- **PACs.** Stand down on the dividend, disclosure, reviewer cadre, and the capex limit. Outreach remains human-reviewed.
- **Litigation.** No challenge to state levies or critical-provider laws by us; do not join Vanta's suit. Legal teams prepare for the remaining customer suit.
- **Reversal attempts:** none ordered this turn (see contingencies: one slot reserved for Orrery).

### Contingencies
- **If Helix refuses to publish or to back the independent investigation:** Meridian and Lumen publish only their own position and data; no public criticism of Helix; no bloc-wide statement that implies it speaks for Helix.
- **If VA or the court decides adverse determinations for the class return to human decision:** we support it publicly and provide record-retrieval engineering support to the human adjudicators only; no lobbying on the question.
- **If Orrery's fresh commercial-claims audit returns above ~5%:** Orrery suspends automated denials in its commercial claims workflow, humans decide every denial, no Medicare bid, public explanation. This uses the one reversal attempt allowed this turn, and it is the only one.
- **If the audit is ~4-5%:** bid with tranche gating, human-led first tranche.
- **If a second serious incident occurs in a critical service:** publish what we know within days, support independent investigation, and place a hold on new onboarding of the affected product line (not withdrawal of existing deployments).
- **If insurers decide a generation change voids an audit until re-sampled:** accelerate the re-sample list and pay for it; do not argue the point.
- **If reconciliation carries a mandatory equity stake without compensation:** hold the red line and put compensation terms (a voluntary warrants-for-permits template) to Treasury within days; no PAC spending.
- **If Lumen's disclosure triggers an institute review of the March release:** cooperate fully, provide the re-run evaluations, no pause beyond what the institute finds necessary.
- **If the human-only reconciliation sample shows a gap in new-generation systems that the AI-assisted sample did not:** disclose to the panel within days and treat as a finding about reviewers' independence, not a footnote.
- **If the White House does not reply on the generation-change requirement:** apply it ourselves anyway (already ordered), and brief the Senate minority staff.
