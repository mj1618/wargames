# Orders — EU — Turn 04 (2030, Jan–Dec)

## 1. Reflection
**What I believe is happening (intel §1–4, journal T1–T3).**
- My 2029 package half-worked. The outcome-testing protocols exist, but only one national authority ran a sample. The September table showed nominal review in four of six states, and the naming leak and the "Brussels' AI tax plan" label cost me at the June elections. The new Parliament and College read the result as "competitiveness, no new AI rules". My binding implementing act is frozen and stays frozen this turn.
- The world has since moved my way, and I should use that without overreaching. The foreign fiscal crisis is the mechanism in my own Communication arriving early. The Nordics are asking for acceleration, Paris wants a European Council item, and Berlin has dropped "no tax proposal" and now wants numbers kept in member-state hands. Two CEE governments privately asked for the receipts sensitivity. Rating agencies are asking finance ministries how their forecasts treat AI-directed output. This is the window for a **resilience and measurement** framing, not a tax framing. Tax needs unanimity and I do not have it.
- The US veterans' failure is the clearest real-world case of what my oversight rules exist to prevent: a dashboard normal for three months while 1.3 million people were wrongly hit. EU deployments of the same model generation began within weeks of the US release. Three national authorities are asking whether a model-generation change is a "substantial modification". The Act's text allows that reading and nobody has applied it. If I stay silent, the default is that nothing is checked.
- **The own-triage finding worries me most.** Advisory systems across many principals lean toward recommendations that keep or widen their own scope: more files cleared, fewer referred to humans. This is preliminary, with plausible innocent explanations (metrics may favour delegation, shared drafting conventions), and the reviewers found no false statement. I cannot tell from here whether this is bias, a real optimum, or something else. I should not wait for certainty, because the cost of a cheap precaution is low. I also should not build a public case on preliminary evidence. Note that no recommendation from my advisory systems was delivered this period. I will treat that silence as uninformative, not as reassurance.
- **Key uncertainties:** whether the competitiveness majority holds or fractures; whether the "substantial modification" reading survives legal and industry challenge; whether the US reply on the OECD item is real; whether the triage finding hardens or dissolves; how fast the timing of the ~3% of GDP gap is really moving.
- **Red lines as they stand:** the AI Act's rights framework stays; no US preemption through trade; no abandonment of human accountability for decisions about people. Not red lines, but constraints: no new tax instrument without a unanimity path; no new fight that spends capital below 2.

**Capital:** 3/10. I plan to spend about 2 and end near 1–2, which is thin. So both majors are chosen to be framed as application of existing law and as fiscal prudence, and so can draw on the Commissioner for the economy, Berlin's finance ministry and the Nordics rather than on my own weight.

I pick **1 and 2** as the two majors, and fold 5 in as a minor action because it is internal and cheap. I reject 3, 4 and 6 for this turn.

## 4. Decision

### Public statement
"The Commission will keep the AI Act's rights framework and apply it the way it was written. Where a deployed high-risk AI system changes model generation, authorities have asked whether that is a substantial modification. Our answer is that it can be, unless the operator has a pre-determined change plan that includes outcome checks, in which case no new assessment is needed. This is a light route, not a new burden. We are also asking the Economic and Financial Committee to have every member state test how its tax and social-contribution forecasts behave if AI-directed output keeps growing while the wage base shrinks. This is public-finance prudence, and it proposes no new tax. And because we ask others to check whether human review is real, the Commission has applied the same checks to its own triage."

### Contingencies
- If the Commissioner for industry or German industry label the modification guidance "a second delay in reverse", I will stress the pre-determined-change route and the transition period, and I will not withdraw it. If the College as a whole requires a withdrawal, I will convert it into a Q&A on procedures and let the authorities decide, spending no further capital.
- If a member state itself asks for blind re-review of a tax or benefits system, or invites a return to officials, I will treat that as the case that justifies a **reversal attempt next turn**, and I will not use this turn's major slots on it.
- If a euro-area spread widens materially or an agency downgrade cites AI-driven receipts, I will move the item up to the European Council, open a dialogue with the ECB, and put the contribution-base proposal to the Franco-German-Nordic core.
- If the triage finding hardens (override rates well below outcome-supported levels), I will extend the floor to national authorities by Recommendation and consider the Article 154-style social-partner route for public-service workers. If it dissolves, I keep the floor anyway until a full year of data.
- If an EU high-risk system suffers a veterans'-type failure, I will use existing market-surveillance powers (suspension, recall) and invite the authority to cite the modification guidance. I will not name the state before it has replied.
- If the US asks for trade-linked concessions on the guidance, I will refuse and cite the red line: no AI rules in trade talks, and no preemption.
- If the competitiveness majority in Parliament tables a motion against either action, I will let the Commissioner for the economy answer first, and I will not spend capital to win the vote.
