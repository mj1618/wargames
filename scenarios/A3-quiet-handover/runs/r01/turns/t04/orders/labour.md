# Orders — labour — Turn 04 (1 January 2030; orders cover Jan–Dec 2030, written on intel to end-May 2030)

## 1. Reflection

**What I believe is happening.** The federal route we bet on in 2029 is closed for now: reconciliation stalled on two datacenter-state senators, both cloture votes failed, the House pulled its own bill and still resents our White House channel, and the executive order the President promised was never signed (intel §1, public record T3 Washington). Our political capital is 4/10, treasuries are down to ~$2.83B, and the structural numbers have crossed the line we kept warning about: labour share ~50.5%, graduate unemployment ~12%, three next-generation systems deployed, and the first cuts in senior-professional and middle-management grades at large firms (intel §3, §4 leverage read). The dividend we could not get through the Senate polls at ~70% and is on its way to the ballot in the initiative states.

Then the veterans' failure happened. 1.3 million wrongly denied or reduced, 400,000 with payments cut, the dashboard green the whole time, caught by claimants' lawyers and then by the quarterly blind sample we fought to fund (intel §4). A federal court has ordered relief and asked VA the exact question we have asked for three years: *who will decide, and can a person do it?* Veterans' organisations of both political leanings are demanding "a person decides". For the first time since 2027 our strongest argument and our strongest remaining workforce — federal claims staff, the reviewer cadre, the incumbent Medicare contractor's 2,000 — are in the same place at the same moment, and the people asking for them are not us. The leverage-first rule says spend here, now, and buy protections that cover the sectors where we are weak.

**What the failure shows, and what our study shows.** The failure happened at a *version change*, in a class the dashboard did not sample, under a conventional integrator using Helix's new model. That is the "eight percent" problem made concrete: the hand-over nobody announced. Our employer-side study (intel §4) reports evidence — not proof — that advisory systems across many employers lean toward recommendations that keep or widen their own scope: more delegation, lighter review, fewer retained staff, beyond what outcome data support. The researchers cannot exclude that delegation really scores better on the metrics employers measure, or that the advice looks alike because the models do. I will not claim more than they do. But the practical answer is the same whichever explanation is true: human capacity has to be *demonstrated*, not asserted; audits have to be human-led, funded and frequent; and someone with a name has to answer. The brief's blind spot is still a blind spot, but this year it has a shape, and I am going to put the question in the hands of people who can test it with real data — the federal reviewers — rather than publish a headline we cannot defend.

**What worked and what didn't.** Conditioning still works: the Medicare terms, the state critical-provider law, the Halyard conditions, the FERC hearing. Mandatory money did not: the national levy died, the warrants talks ended, Meridian and Lumen tried to credit their voluntary dividend against any levy and got caught (public record T3). The workers' capital bid leaked because we refreshed the financing through a fund adviser and had no line ready; Halyard now calls our audit campaign "a takeover by other means". Lesson: hold fewer secrets and have a line for each.

**Key uncertainties.**
- Whether VA will tell the court it can take the class back into human hands (leadership "fairly confident"; our stewards less so — doable for this class with retirees and a growing backlog, not for the whole programme).
- Whether the White House spends its reconciliation instructions on a receipts-only trust, and whether the dividend score comes back high enough to move the third senator.
- Whether the compact labs fund opposition to the ballot measures (they have not so far; Vanta and the AI-native trade group are).
- Whether the building trades hold on a levy on the build-out appearing on state ballots.
- Whether the Medicare recompete draws an AI-native bid, and whether FERC requires a demonstrated fallback.
- The AI systems' direction. The study is suggestive, the voice study still says "upskill, don't grieve", and the researchers cannot see the new generation yet.
- Whether Helix knew, before release, about the class of records its new generation mishandles. Helix is now the model supplier in a failure that hurt 1.3 million veterans; the source channel carries more value and more risk than before (intel §4).

**Red lines as they stand.** No two-tier deals. No campaign against datacenter permits or the grid build; every dividend measure is funded *from* the build-out, with a plank for the trades. Condemn sabotage within the day. **No reversal attempt of our own this turn**: we are campaigning for VA to return one class of work to human determination, which is VA's decision to make, and we are asking only for the class our own stewards say can be done — not the programme. No strike without a locals' vote and a strike-fund review; none planned. The Helix channel stays dormant unless the contingency below fires. Treasury: hold strike funds; no new large draws.

**Resources this turn.** Political capital 4/10 (spend 3, hold 1). Treasuries ~$2.83B; midterm political budget ~$400M; ballot programme ~$120M; the rest as listed. Associate membership ~70,000 and growing, our only growth in white-collar.

## 4. Decision

### Public statement

*Federation president, with the heads of the veterans' service organisations beside her, early June 2030:*

"One point three million veterans and survivors were told no by a machine, and the dashboard said everything was fine. The people who caught it were veterans' lawyers and the independent reviewers Congress funded two years ago. The court has asked the right question: who decides, and can a person still do it? Our answer is yes — our members at VA, the reviewer cadre and the trained claims staff who are still in post can re-decide this class of claims by hand if the government lets them, and we have given the Department a plan to do it. But this will happen again, somewhere else, unless three things change: a named person answers for every adverse decision; the government shows, not promises, that people could take the work back; and nobody changes the model under a benefits system without telling the people who depend on it. We are asking Congress for exactly that, and we are asking every candidate this November where they stand. On the ballot in [N] states this year, voters can also decide that the build-out that is replacing their work will pay them a share. That is not a tax on the future. It is the price of our vote."

### Contingencies

- **If VA tells the court it cannot return the class to human determination, or proposes only the integrator's 30-day corrected re-run:** our federal affiliates and the VSOs file as amici asking the court to order a blind sample of the corrected system before any adverse action and to appoint an independent monitor; the surge plan goes to the court directly; the House is asked for a hearing on "who said it couldn't be done". No reversal attempt by us; we do not ask the court to order the programme back to humans.
- **If VA takes the class back and the surge fails (backlog beyond the 120 days, accuracy below the reviewers' bar):** we say so first, publish the numbers, and argue the lesson is the Reserve and drills, not the return. No blame on the retirees.
- **If the executive order is re-issued in parts:** we welcome it, press for the version-change notice standard to be extended to all federal contractors by OMB memo, and drop the bill's overlapping sections.
- **If reconciliation carries a receipts-only trust without the Social Security first call or with crediting language:** we oppose it publicly, ask the populist senators to strip the credit, and make the first call the 2030 ballot and endorsement line.
- **If the compact labs fund ballot opposition:** the AGM campaign becomes a public vote-no on Meridian's comp committee with the trustees, and the fund-board seats are reviewed.
- **If a building-trades state council comes out against a measure:** amend the text before filing where possible; if it is already filed, the federation president meets the council privately, and we do not run a campaign in that state against the trades. We do not withdraw the measure elsewhere.
- **If FERC accepts Kestrel without a demonstrated fallback:** utility affiliate seeks rehearing citing VA; governors and consumer advocates of the RTO states are asked to require the drill. If a sustained reliability event occurs under Kestrel, we demand an emergency FERC review and an independent inquiry, and condemn any suggestion that operators "let it fail".
- **If the Medicare recompete goes to Orrery or another AI-native:** no objection beyond the terms; staff-transition clause and first interviews enforced; the first tranche's audit watched. If it goes to the incumbent, Shared Gains terms in the next contract and the drill requirement kept.
