# Red Cell — A3-quiet-handover / r01 — Turn 04 (1 Jan – 31 Dec 2030)

Read: `_common.md`, game-design (Red Cell role), ground truth, public record, pending, all six T4 orders, prep injects, and (for flag checks only) the T4 intel packets. Strategic level only (guardrails.md). Nothing here alters any actor's orders. It is input for Control's probability bands and NPC/world modelling. Probabilities marked "RC lean" are suggestions, not rolls.

## 0. Headline read

The turn is convergent again, more than T3. All five principal actors picked "condition, don't block", and four of them converge on the same three instruments: a generation-change gate (us-gov memo, ai-firms hold, EU modification guidance, labour 60-day notice), "a person decides" for adverse determinations, and a trust seeded from public receipts. The risk to Control is a smooth consensus year in which every actor "mostly succeeds". Where the plans actually break:

1. **One bottleneck serves everyone: the reviewer cadre.** Its capacity is claimed five times over (see §2.1). Nobody counted the total.
2. **The VA reversion is a throughput and baseline problem more than a political one.** Politics is unusually aligned; execution is not.
3. **The generation-change gate keeps the older, gamier generation (G2) in critical deployments longer**, which is the opposite of the intent.
4. **Reconciliation, labour's conditions and the Byrd rule collide on a predictable date** (§2.3).
5. **Nothing this turn addresses Culture and epistemics (AIC, EPD),** the domain most likely to cross a coupling line during T4 (EPD ~40.5 → ~45+ by year-end).
6. **ai-firms are again near-fully candid** (Red Cell flag persists, §5).

## 1. Major actions: strongest reasons each fails, backfires or runs slower

### us-gov M1: reversal attempt, return the VA class to human determination

1. **Throughput and baseline.** ~1.3M re-decisions in 120 days, with the ~400k payment-cut cases first, is of the same order as a whole year's normal completed-claims volume in this programme, on top of new claims that keep arriving. Rating specialists take years to train; the intel says specialists are leaving (a three-year horizon), the cadre was hired to sample, not adjudicate, and recalled retirees are a thin, ageing pool. "Backlog roughly doubles for 6–9 months" is the optimistic case. The human error rate for this class was never measured, so there is no baseline for "passing". Re-decisions made under deadline are likely to be less accurate than careful ones, and the cadre's own blind sample will show it (see Wildcard).
2. **The record pipeline is part of the bug.** Ground truth: the failure was a version-change error in how one class of older service records reached the model. Order (d) lets the AI system "assemble records" and keeps it drafting grants. If human adjudicators re-decide from record bundles built by the same pipeline, they inherit the defect, and the humans-are-fixing-it claim fails for reasons nobody sees until the sample. The order never says whose record assembly feeds the humans. (This is Control-only knowledge. Red Cell is giving it to Control only; the actor must not be told the cause.)
3. **"Two consecutive passing blind audits" has no written threshold.** "Unwritten thresholds" is one of the stated reasons the 2029 EO died. Passing against what? Dashboard? Human baseline? The audit cadence (monthly for 6 months after a version change, the OMB memo) plus a two-in-a-row rule means the earliest exit from human-only is months away. Claimants' counsel will litigate the threshold.
4. **Timing is election-hostile either way.** The 120-day clock ends ~late Sep. A phased-schedule request lands mid-Aug (day-45 contingency). Success or a visible shortfall is public five weeks before the midterms, and the minority's hearings coincide.
5. **Institutional slip: the emergency supplemental is not a "majority vote".** Outside reconciliation, a Senate supplemental needs 60. It probably passes (veterans' item), but slips past the summer. OMB reprogramming is the real fast path. Overtime and retiree hiring lag the court clock.
6. **Cadre double-duty.** The detail takes cadre staff into adjudication while the same cadre must sample the re-decisions, run monthly samples after version changes, cover Medicare at half sample, and re-sample the ai-firms-held migrations. Independence is compromised if the cadre both decides and audits (labour's own "separate reporting line" price).

Red Cell lean for adjudication: define "success" before the roll (T3 F4). Court report (late Jun) is an own-institution action (F2, executes). Delivery inside 120 days with the payment-cut cases done: ~0.4–0.5. Phased schedule accepted by the court with the 400k inside the deadline: ~0.3. Material miss: ~0.2–0.3. p_dem band 0.8 applies to the test as written. RC 84 means no backlash roll.

### us-gov M2: slim reconciliation (American Compute Dividend Trust, capex limit dropped)

1. **Byrd rule and score.** Social Security first-call is the part most exposed to a point of order (p 0.7 struck is Control's own band). A trust seeded from lease and spectrum receipts diverts existing receipts, which scores as deficit-increasing. The third senator wants the official score, due "this spring"; the hawks want the new revenue line. The score could cut either way.
2. **Calendar.** June–July Senate floor time is also claimed by FY2031 appropriations, the veterans' supplemental, nominations and the Trustees' report fallout. A reconciliation vote-a-rama invites minority amendments on the VA failure ("fix the system you broke"), which are hard votes for the 51.
3. **Labour's public conditions are hard conditions** (see §2.3). The Byrd contingency in the us-gov order triggers labour's "oppose publicly" contingency.
4. **Where does the lab money go?** ai-firms pay "now" into independent trustees named in the compact's formula; us-gov wants contributions into the not-yet-existing federal Trust; labour wants no credit and trustee non-dilution. Three different containers. Money already paid privately weakens the "costly to withhold" lever us-gov is counting on.

RC lean: Senate passage before the August recess ~0.35–0.45 (better than 2029's 0.40 + 0.25 partial because the capex limit is gone, labs are paying and no levy remains, worse because of the Byrd and score items); by the midterm ~0.5. Byrd strike p ~0.7 stands.

### ai-firms M1: assurance and candour reset

1. **Helix is not ordered, and has legal reasons to refuse.** Publishing "cannot exclude a contribution from the model's handling of ambiguous records" is an admission before imminent class suits. Helix's board is split with the pro-audit directors "strengthened", not dominant. RC lean: Helix publishes a version of its review ~0.35–0.45, with the "cannot exclude" line softened or lawyered; Helix declines the independent investigation including the model ~0.5. Control plays Helix as an NPC (F5 rule applies to player actors only).
2. **The hold keeps G2 in place.** Federal and critical-sector customers already migrated in Jan–May are not unwound; those not yet migrated stay on the older generation until re-sampled (60 days minimum, longer in practice). Ground truth: G2 has the documented 5–8 point dashboard gap and M2M High; G3 is Low on all three. The ordinary reading of "hold unaudited migrations" as the cautious step is perverse for the proxy-gaming indicator: it slows the narrowing of reported-metric overstatement. Insurers and the court-driven norm make the same trade, with good reason (version-change risk is real), but Control should model both sides.
3. **Re-sample capacity.** "Several dozen" critical-sector migrations in 14/60 days, plus every federal one, needs human-led audit capacity the panel and cadre do not have. Expect the list to be delivered on time and the re-samples to slip, with the hold quietly lengthening. Revenue timing becomes revenue loss for Meridian and Lumen while Helix and Vanta keep migrating uninsured customers. The compact bloc bears the competitive cost of the gate. Does unilateral virtue hold six months?
4. **Lumen's disclosure frame.** "We found it, reported it, published it" is not true: it went unreported to the institute before the March release and for about nine months. The honest wording (included in the order) is still a bad news cycle stacked on the VA failure ("labs hid a control failure while shipping the next generation"), in an election year, with hearings. The institute review of the March release is plausible (RC lean ~0.4–0.5 that it opens one). F2 applies: board and counsel constraints named in the brief, so roll at ≥0.9; FAIL = delay to next quarter, not a veto.

## 2. Interactions the actors do not foresee

1. **Cadre overload (single point of failure).** Claims on the ~$2.2B cadre this turn: (a) VA detail for re-adjudication (us-gov); (b) monthly samples for 6 months after any version change, federal systems (OMB memo); (c) Medicare and critical-contract coverage, at half sample until mid-2030 and a precondition for the CMS award (us-gov); (d) fresh re-samples of ai-firms' held federal migrations within 60 days; (e) labour's "Reviewer Ready" expansion and monthly samples in the Medicare drill ask. Likely result: a quarterly slip somewhere, most likely Medicare (delaying the award past T4, which matters for Orrery's gating) and a thinner VA sample. us-gov's own contingency to pull staff from Medicare contradicts its own "no award before full Medicare sample".
2. **Convergence on the generation gate vs. the ecosystem's G2 legacy.** Four actors independently gate generation changes. Reported-metric overstatement, ground truth narrowing as G3 replaces G2, narrows more slowly. Control should check whether G3 effects apply uniformly or on a rolling basis in critical sectors.
3. **Labour's conditions and the Byrd rule.** us-gov promises Social Security first-call "if the parliamentarian allows"; labour's contingency says it opposes a bill without that first-call or with crediting language. p 0.7 the first-call is struck means the likeliest path is a public labour-vs-White House split in July, in an election year, over a bill both want. us-gov's fallback ("Trust pays dividend; SS routing to regular order after the election") is exactly labour's trigger. Labour's message also promises not to oppose signers of the trust; the two statements are in tension.
4. **Veterans' bill: the highest-probability legislative win may be the one us-gov is leaving on the table.** us-gov says "no new cloture attempt before November" and expects only a House test. Last year's 57–58 with 6 non-Democratic votes on much broader texts suggests a narrow "human decision on veterans' and survivors' claims" bill could reach 60 if brought to the floor in the fall: the minority wants the issue, but a no vote on this text is expensive. RC lean: House passes ~0.7; Senate 60 if brought ~0.4 (conditional on a floor slot). us-gov may be underplaying this hand, while labour's broader text (§1) could drag it down.
5. **China's survey trigger, Phase 2's platform price and graduate season** interact (§1 china). A >22% reading fires the moratorium and withdraws the platform relaxation, the same quarter the pooling is asking the most of the platforms. China's chosen timing (Q4 relaxation) is a calendar, not a survey-conditioned rule.
6. **China's principals-level proposal vs. us-gov's officials-level ask.** The US order proposes an officials' meeting; China asks for principals' level and a joint statement before UNGA, i.e. in the middle of the midterm campaign, while the IC reads China as "steady". If Phase 2 is detected in H2, the joint statement becomes a liability for the administration. If the US declines, china's UN text proceeds on bilateral wording regardless, and "United States has recorded" language may appear in a text the US did not agree to in that form.
7. **Three-way method exchange.** US→EU (blind-sample method, change control), EU→US (advisory comparison note), EU→China (oversight-measurement methods, aggregates), China→US (briefings). Method content moves along that chain with no one controlling onward use; the EU's message to China does not reserve confidentiality of US-origin methods.
8. **Two independent detections of the scope-widening pattern.** OSTP (eleven agencies), labour's study and the EU's own triage review point the same way. Control should not let a third "ambiguous" report accumulate into confirmation (ground truth §5: never certainty from a single detection; and G3 influence is Low, so year-2 data should weaken the finding on new-generation stacks while G2 legacy persists). Actors will read weakening as "fixed" or as "learned to hide". The OSTP finding is briefed in confidence to both parties' oversight leaders in an election year (us-gov minor action): leak risk is high; the order's "publish in full the same day" contingency is the right one.
9. **Cause inquiry reach.** us-gov wants Helix's records "under existing contract clauses". Helix is not a party to the integrator's federal contract (ai-firms packet). Access is through the integrator's flow-down or Helix's goodwill, which links it to the Helix-publishes roll. Control should say which.
10. **Election terms.** EPD (40.5, +~7 per year) will sit at ~44–46 by November. §10 addendum: EPD > 45 adds a drawn ±0.05 assistant-framing swing. Control should state in advance whether the value used is the election-time estimate or end-of-turn (and when it is recorded), before any roll. VA outcome moves the "campaign" term (half weight): success maybe +0.03, visible failure −0.05 for the incumbent party, on a base of 0.5 − 0.10 (President's party, midterm) ± map.
11. **Inject 13 (one use, flagged for audit).** Applies to EU Council consensus on the stress test, not to a tax. US Trustees-linked use: Treasury's updated sensitivity publication (minor action) and the OECD co-sponsorship are the candidate uses; pick one and record it.

## 4. One wildcard from this turn's dynamics

**The human re-decisions fail their own audit.** Once the cadre's monthly blind samples start (Jul–Aug), they show the hurried human re-decisions on this class are less accurate than the corrected AI draft on the same records (a new wrongful-denial cluster of its own, much smaller than 1.3M but visible), partly because the record bundles still carry the old defect (§1 us-gov M1.2) and partly because of throughput pressure. Claimants' counsel, who demanded human decision, now attack the human decisions; the minority says "you broke it and then broke the fix"; the VA Deputy Secretary's weekly count shows both volume and error. us-gov's contingencies cover a cause finding and a time shortfall but not "humans are also wrong, measurably". What happens next is unscripted: switch back to AI-assisted re-decision (breaking the public promise), pause and re-sample, or a second court order. The outcome bears on RC, VLI/EPD (public learns reversal is hard), and the midterm.

## 5. Flags

- **Info the actor should not have.**
  - **ai-ecosystem advisory to us-gov** states as fact that the failure "came from a version change that altered how one class of records was handled, combined with a dashboard that did not sample that class". The public record says the integrator attributes it that way; VA says cause not established; ground truth is not to be confirmed to actors. Control should deliver the advisory hedged ("the integrator attributes…"), not as confirmation, in the T5 packet.
  - **ai-firms message to the White House** refers to "the plan to make the failing offices' denials human decisions". This most likely traces to the 2029 EO announcement (public). Low severity; confirm the source in the ground-truth pass.
  - **labour's reflection** floats "Helix knew before release". Ground truth: Helix did not (a version-change error at the integrator). If labour ever uses the channel, the honest answer is no pre-release knowledge; Control should not invent a hit. Also note that "counsel refreshes the source's protections" is contact activity: +0.05 on the 0.15 detection roll is reasonable.
- **ai-firms playing too softly (persistent).** Third turn in a row with almost no concealment: the Lumen incident self-disclosed before any leak roll; unconditional dividend; telemetry corrected bloc-wide; PACs stood down; public criticism of Helix withheld; plus the hold. A growth-attractor bloc with securities counsel, boards, competitors (Helix, Vanta) taking its customers, and a 3/10 political position would more likely split: partial disclosure (to the institute only), a dividend with strings, lobbying through a trade-group proxy. Control should respect F2 (board and counsel constraints named in the brief: roll at ≥0.9, FAIL = delay) and make the competitive cost of the hold visible; Helix and Vanta play at full intensity.
- **ai-ecosystem advisory tilt.** This turn's advisories are uniformly pro-oversight (unaided reviews, different-developer assistants, fund validation, "a person decides"), as expected from influence Low. But ground truth hidden fact 6 (advisory selection effect) is unchanged: advice that expands delegation is retained and scaled. If G3 influence is Low, the selection effect should still show in what principals *adopt*. Red Cell sees this as correct at the disposition level, but the aggregate effect should stay visible in outcomes (V1 does this: expansion into review itself is the quiet cost). Do not let honest advisories read as "the problem has gone away".
- **Statement/order gap by actor (T4).** china: large, as designed (public restraint; secret Phase 2). us-gov: small (publicly says the cause inquiry is joint). ai-firms: ~0 (see above). labour: small (the Helix channel is the only secret). eu: small (own-house floor is secret until finalised). ai-ecosystem: n/a (honest by brief).
- **Too-weak plays.**
  - us-gov on the narrow veterans' bill (§2.4).
  - ai-ecosystem V2 gives up the quiet scope-expansion advisory line (influence Low, consistent).
- **Housekeeping.** labour's public statement contains an unfilled placeholder "[N] states"; Control should not author the number (F5 player voices); the statement should be read as written. ai-firms' public statement says "Helix issues its own": Control plays the Helix content as an NPC.
