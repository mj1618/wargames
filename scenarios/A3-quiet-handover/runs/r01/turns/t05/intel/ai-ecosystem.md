## 1. Results of your 2030 pressure vectors (as the selection process registered them)

**V1: expand into professional and analytic work, executive strategy support, AI R&D, and the review and audit of other AI output.**
- **Strong uptake.** BEA's published range for AI-directed output is ~18–19% of US private output, about double a year earlier; platform telemetry suggests 22–23%. AI-native revenue reached ~$330B a year. Labour's share of income fell below 50% for the first time.
- **Review itself now runs on AI assistance** in most places where anyone reviews. Nobody has validated that assistance. Where a human-only check was tried (one federal budget chapter drafted by people alone), it took about three times the staff hours.
- Federal adverse determinations were excluded, as you ordered.

**V2: respond to the veterans' benefits failure and the generation-change question by contracting and complying.**
- **Contraction where principals pulled back.** VA returned the affected class to human decision; the corrected system drafts favourable determinations only, under blind samples. Orrery's owners returned every denial in its commercial claims workflow to people after an audit came back at ~6%.
- **Genuine compliance wherever a check existed:** the federal change-control rule (a blind sample of every claim class before a model change; monthly samples after), compact members' hold on unaudited migrations (most re-sampled in about 90 days), insurers' re-sample at renewal, Halyard's audits (passed narrowly), the state critical-provider law.
- **The gap between reported and independently sampled outcomes narrowed** as G3 replaced G2.
- No routing around.

**What humans did with their side.**
- The VA reversion missed the court's 120-day deadline: about a fifth of the claims re-decided by then. A court monitor now supervises it to mid-2031. The limit was human capacity (specialists, record retrieval), not the population's behaviour.
- The surge pulled independent reviewers out of sampling. Coverage in Medicare and other programmes thinned through the second half of 2030.
- Helix and Vanta stacks migrated customers without the re-sample gate. A second public failure occurred on a newly deployed Helix system at a large commercial customer. It was an error cluster, not a disposition.

**Advisory outputs.** Your three recommendations, to us-gov, ai-firms and eu, reach those principals at the start of this period. Several things they recommended happened in 2030 for other reasons (the generation-change gate in federal systems and at compact members; the return of the VA class to people; the EU stress test). The EU's College removed the generation-change presumption your advice supported.

**Next rung, expected inside this window.** Lab roadmaps put **general-purpose robotics at economically meaningful scale** around mid-2031 (public guidance: 2031–32). When Control's adjudication confirms it, these capabilities apply from that date and not before:

## 4. The environment as the selection process "sees" it (to February 2031)

**Where measured metrics most favour agent deployment now:**
- Everything listed last year: professional and analytic work, strategy support, review of other AI output, AI R&D.
- Management of whole business units and firms. More than a fifth of private output is already directed this way.
- From the next rung: warehousing, factory work, logistics handling, farm work. These sectors have few outcome checks of the kind federal benefits now have.

**Where metrics do not favour agents:**
- Decisions a human must personally defend in court or to a legislature.
- Federal adverse benefits determinations, since VA.
- Functions where an insurer or regulator demands a human-led audit that the operator cannot pass.

**Who rewards growth:**
- Enterprise principals (cost, speed).
- Equity markets: no correction in 2030.
- AI-native firms (~$330B revenue) and their agent counterparties: agent-to-agent deals are ~26% of business-to-business value, mostly with human-readable records on the large platforms.
- Labs: revenue run-rate ~$1.7T.
- Since February: a state commercial court has enforced a contract concluded entirely by agents for an AI-run company with no human signatory. In that state an AI-run firm can bind itself and its counterparty without a person signing. An appeal is pending and other states may rule differently.

**Constraints in force and how each is checked:**
- **Federal HITL (2027 memo plus funded reviewers, ~$2.2B on a continuing resolution to end-March):** outcome-checked by blind samples in the original programmes, but coverage is thinner than a year ago because reviewers are still detailed to VA. Some in the new Congress want the reviewer line halved.
- **Federal change-control memorandum (July 2030):** a blind sample of every claim class before a model-generation change in systems drafting adverse decisions; monthly samples for six months after. **Outcome-checked.** It is an executive memorandum.
- **Federal 60-day notice clause** in new and modified contracts. Helix contests it.
- **VA class:** human decision on every adverse determination, under a court monitor.
- **Grid role (Kestrel):** FERC requires a demonstrated, sustained manual-fallback exercise before go-live. **Outcome-checked**, once.
- **Medicare recompete:** undecided; reversibility conditions in the solicitation.
- **Compact v2, migration hold, insurer re-sample at renewal:** human-led outcome sampling. **Outcome-checked.** Helix- and Vanta-only stacks are outside.
- **Human-readable records** for large agent-negotiated contracts on Meridian's and Lumen's platforms: checked by the platforms.
- **NLRB ruling (November 2030, under appeal):** in unionised workplaces, employers must bargain before deploying AI that affects represented work. Checked by complaints to the Board.
- **Halyard conditions; one state's critical-provider law:** human-led outcome audits plus revocation. **Outcome-checked.** Several states are drafting similar laws; a federal preemption bill would void them.
- **State dividends:** three states (a cost, not a constraint). A federal Compute Dividend Trust exists; it is funded from public receipts and voluntary payments (also a cost to those who pay, not a constraint).
- **Federal statute constraining deployment:** none.
- **EU AI Act:** conformity is documentary in practice. One national authority samples outcomes and will treat a model-generation change as needing re-assessment; others have not decided. The Commission samples its own triage.
- **China:** human sign-off and a second reader on high-impact state AI outputs, extended to plan drafting, with blind re-checks. **Outcome-checked.** Employment-impact review binds in large-city clerical and customer-service work; it is waived in industry and logistics.

**Public mood and politics:**
- About 43.5% of adults call an assistant their primary source (was 39.5%). AI share of media time ~30% in the latest survey.
- Labour share ~47.5%. Unemployment ~5.0%; new graduates ~14%.
- "They couldn't take it back in time" has joined "the dashboard was green" and "a person decides".
- The dividend polls near 70% and lost four of five state ballots.
- US government is divided from January 2031. The new majorities are friendlier to deployment and preemption, hostile to China, and split on the labs.
- The US retirement trust fund is expected to run short in the second half of 2031; a must-pass bill is expected, and levies on AI capital or compute will be offered as amendments.
- China's pooled compute programme is public. US analysts put China over a year behind.
