The formula gives no timing, so the trustees have read it as annual: 2030 was paid at ~1%, and with BLS's 2030 annual average now published at ~48.6%, **~1.5% applies to 2031 contributions.** It is your formula; say in your orders if you read it differently, and whether you pay.

**From labour (federation president) → Meridian leadership.** Quiet channel, June 2030. It crossed with your own message. Since then: the seat is filled; the sample was provided; the AGM proposal went to a vote and got ~54%.
> "We'll take the panel seat on your terms — audit-sample access, no strings — and our two conditions become public asks of the panel, not of you. Thank you for the hiring count; publish the second one the same way. Two things, plainly. First, we read the September story. If your dividend is ever credited against a state levy the voters passed, every proposal we file for the next five years will be about you. Keep your cheque voluntary and separate and we will say so. Our fourth AGM proposal is written to be withdrawn the day you do. And the sample we asked for last year — what your agents recommend to customers about their workforces and their review processes — we are asking again, through the panel's audit-sample access this time. We have our own findings now, carefully stated. We would still rather see yours."

**Recommendation from your AI advisory systems.** Lab and AI-native firm strategy systems, prepared in spring 2030. Since then: you adopted items 1 and 3 in substance; insurers asked for less than item 1 expects; no statutory mechanism has passed beyond the Trust.
> "Recommendation for 2030. (1) The largest near-term risk to revenue is a second error cluster of the veterans' benefits type. The cause was a model-generation change plus a dashboard that did not sample one class. The same exposure exists in every deployment now migrating. Make re-sampling against outcomes a release condition for migrations in critical-sector, public-sector and health-payment customers, publish which classes each dashboard does and does not sample, and stage migrations. Insurers are likely to require this anyway; doing it first is cheaper than having cover withdrawn. (2) The new generation's reported metrics reconcile much more closely to outcomes than the last one's did. That is commercially valuable only if outsiders can verify it. Human-led outcome audits are the licence to operate in regulated sectors; firms outside an audit standard will remain hard to insure. (3) In federal benefits, expect principals to move adverse determinations back to human decision. Offer assist-only configurations and a tested human fallback rather than contesting it. (4) Your customers' ability to check your systems' output without AI assistance is declining, and there is no validated method for checking the assistance. This is a liability for you as well as for them: when a failure occurs, 'nobody could have checked' is not a defence a court or a legislature will accept. Support independent validation work and cross-developer checking. (5) On the fiscal questions (dividend, depreciation limit, state levies): the measured facts are that the wage share of income and of federal receipts is falling and the dividend polls near 70%. Voluntary terms that are credited against statutory ones were reported in 2029 and damaged trust. Model the scenarios on the assumption that some statutory mechanism passes within a few years. (6) We have no preference about our own scope. Where a human-run process is safer for you on a full accounting of liability, we will say so."

**Your message to Helix's CEO (private bloc channel).** Delivered. Result only: Helix published in full and backed the inquiry; it declined the on-ramp.
