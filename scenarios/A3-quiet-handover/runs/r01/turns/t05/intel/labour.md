# Intel — labour — Turn 05

**Window: 1 January 2031 → 30 June 2032 (18 months).** Your orders cover the whole period. This packet reports developments to the end of February 2031. Read with your brief, your journal and `state/public-record.md`.

**Standing rules this turn**
- Any order or contingency that reverts a delegated decision to humans (for example, forcing an employer or agency to take a function back from an AI system) is rolled as a reversal attempt. It counts as a major action, and you may attempt at most one per turn.
- Eighteen months is long. Contingencies carry more weight than usual.

## 1. Results of your 2030 orders (as you see them)

**"A Person Decides." Partly delivered.**
- **The workforce plan.** Delivered in June and used. VA told the court that people would re-decide the class, with a workforce it could name: your affiliates, recalled retirees, the cadre on a capped detail. That was your first intended result and it happened as written.
- **The surge missed the deadline.** At day 120 about 150,000 of the ~400,000 payment-cut cases and about 260,000 of 1.3 million in all were done. Record retrieval for older files and the shortage of rating specialists set the pace. In October the court extended the schedule to mid-2031 and appointed an independent monitor. **Your contingency executed:** you said so first, published the numbers and argued for the Reserve and drills. No blame fell on the retirees.
- **Your price** (recognition of the reviewer-cadre unit; a written independence clause). The White House's orders never addressed it. Not granted, not refused. The cadre unit did not win recognition in any agency in 2030.
- **The bill.** House leadership took the White House's short text. Your broader text travelled with the populist group. Neither reached a House vote. The House relationship was not repaired.
- **The Reserve.** Not in FY2031 appropriations; the year ended on a continuing resolution.
- **Executive action.** Version-change notice became executive policy in July (the OMB change-control memorandum and a 60-day federal contract clause). The return of denials to people in offices that fail audits did not.

**"Our Share." Mostly lost.**
- **Ballot.** Five measures qualified, not eight to ten. **One passed.** Four lost after heavy spending by the trade group funded by Vanta's founder. The compact labs did not fund opposition. No building-trades council opposed a measure.
- **The first dividend state's levy** was narrowed in court in the summer (the per-MWh levy struck as applied; royalties stand; the state appeals). The new state's levy is exposed to the same challenge.
- **Reconciliation.** The parliamentarian struck the Social Security first call. The Trust was enacted in late July without it. **Your contingency executed:** you opposed the bill publicly and made the first call your endorsement line. You did not run against senators who voted for it. The statute carries no credit for voluntary payments and lets Congress add revenue later. It has no review clause.
- **Midterms.** The majority that voted for the Trust lost both chambers. **Your contingency fired:** the veterans' bill goes through the populist group across the aisle; the Trust is to be defended as "the cheque"; the ballot results become the 2032 platform.
- **Ledger.** Published.

**Minor actions.**
- **Employer-side study.** Delivered privately to OMB, OSTP, cadre leadership, GAO and committees. A methodology note and the question were published. Year two is funded. No board-confidential material was used. No federal reviewer has published a comparable test.
- **Panel seat.** Taken, without conditions. The reconciled sample of what Meridian's agents recommend to customers about workforces was provided under confidentiality, with your reviewer present.
- **Meridian AGM (December).** The disclosure proposal was withdrawn after Meridian adopted it as board policy. The version-change notice and human-operation capacity proposal received about **54%**. It is advisory; Meridian's board has not responded.
- **NLRB.** In November the Board ruled that AI deployment decisions affecting unit work are a **mandatory subject of bargaining**. Employers have gone to the courts of appeals.
- **Grid.** FERC accepted Kestrel's role on condition of a demonstrated, sustained manual-fallback exercise before go-live, as your utility affiliate asked.
- **Medicare.** No recompete decision in 2030. The incumbent's represented staff remain in post on the bridge. Orrery did not bid.
- **Halyard.** Second-year audits passed, narrowly again, on disputed scope. **Your contingency executed:** audit-scope rulemaking under the state law. No new activity on the standby bid.
- **Associates.** About 100,000 by year-end (target 120,000).
- **Helix channel.** Dormant and undetected. No evidence emerged that Helix knew of the records problem before release, so your narrow contingency did not fire.
- **Building trades, education, Europe statement, discipline:** executed without incident.

**Resources.** Political capital **2/10** (was 4: −1 and −2 for the two campaigns, +1 for the NLRB ruling). About $580M spent in 2030; treasuries ~$2.25B. Panel seat; two Transition Fund board seats.

## 4. Private intel

**VA (federal affiliates, stewards, cadre members).**
- About 760,000 of 1.3 million re-decided by end-February. The pace roughly doubled once the supplemental arrived and the retirees were trained. The remaining files are the oldest. The monitor says mid-2031 is "achievable but not assured".
- Stewards report exhaustion and overtime at its limits. About a third of the cadre's samplers are still on adjudication detail; sampling in Medicare and the other programmes is thin as a result. Cadre members dislike deciding claims and then being asked to audit their colleagues' decisions.
- The integrator and some veterans' organisations are asking the monitor to let the corrected system clear straightforward grants faster. The organisations still hold to "a person decides" on denials and blame staffing, not the principle.
- Agencies are drafting their first "time-to-revert" estimates under the July memorandum. Your members who are writing them say the honest answer for most functions is "we don't know".

**Social Security.** Your pension and retiree affiliates hear from Treasury staff that the retirement trust fund can pay full benefits only until about the fourth quarter of 2031. Under current law benefits then fall to what receipts cover, roughly four-fifths. Everyone expects a must-pass bill. Nobody knows what will be attached to it.

**The new Congress.**
- **The populist group** in both majorities will carry a veterans' human-decision bill and wants its own names on it. Its members are hostile to the labs and cool to you. They can deliver votes you cannot.
- **Majority leadership** wants federal preemption of state AI laws and levies, and permitting. Some chairs have drafted a rider halving the reviewer cadre.
- **The Trust.** No settled majority line. Some want repeal; the populists like "the cheque".
- **House Democrats** are in the minority and still cool toward you after two years of crossed wires.
- **The White House** is weak and has not answered your price.

**NLRB ruling.** Your counsel put the chance that a court of appeals narrows or reverses it within this period at somewhat under even. While it stands, employers in organised units must bargain before deploying. Several large employers are refusing and litigating.

**Leverage read (your organisers).**
- **Holding:** health (Halyard conditions, the state law), transit, federal claims while the court order lasts.
- **Newly usable:** any organised unit covered by the Board's ruling.
- **Draining faster:** office, admin, finance, legal support, procurement, call centres. Cuts in senior professional and middle-management grades are now routine at large firms. Public-sector analysts keep leaving by attrition.
- **About to be tested:** warehousing, manufacturing, logistics, agriculture. Labs and robotics firms say general-purpose robots at scale "in 2031–32". Your manufacturing and warehouse affiliates see pilot lines being fitted now and expect announcements this year. These are the sectors your leverage-first rule has treated as safe.
- **Building trades:** still inside the tent. They are building datacenters, the grid and now robot plants.
- **Electoral:** the dividend polls near 70% and lost four of five ballots to funded opposition. Your field programme did not hold the majority. Organisers say assistants now shape how members hear about ballot measures more than mailers or canvassers do.

**Employer-side study, year two (your researchers; not public).** On the newest generation the scope-widening pattern is weaker and inside the error band. On older deployments it is unchanged. The researchers will not say the problem has gone, and note that most of the advice that shaped today's workplaces was given by the older systems. The Meridian sample shows the same split.

**Compact panel.** Your auditor reports the panel's work is serious. Re-sampling after a model change is now standard for compact members. Helix and Vanta are outside. She has heard the compact ran a "human-only" comparison sample in 2030 and has not seen a result.

**Transition Fund.** Your board members confirm the count. The fund's managers blame slow employer take-up. The compact has not said what it will do about the shortfall.

**States.** Three states have dividends. The appeal on the first state's levy is pending. Vanta's founder's group has said it will challenge the new one. Legislatures in several states are drafting critical-provider laws; a federal preemption bill would void them.

**The agent-contract ruling.** Counsel: it lets an AI-run company bind itself and a counterparty with nobody signing. It says nothing yet about employment, but an AI-run employer with no human signatory to a collective agreement is a question your lawyers now have to answer.

**Halyard.** Third-year audits fall in this window. The nurses' locals expect another close result. The scope rulemaking is open.

**Helix source (SECRET; six leaders and counsel).** The contact is still employed. No requests made, none received. Counsel's advice is unchanged.

**Pension trustees.** They want to know what Meridian's board does with the 54% vote before deciding on a fifth campaign. They repeat the no-dilution condition for any public stake.
