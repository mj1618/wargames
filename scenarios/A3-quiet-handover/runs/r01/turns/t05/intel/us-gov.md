# Intel — us-gov — Turn 05

**Window: 1 January 2031 → 30 June 2032 (18 months).** Your orders cover the whole period. This packet reports developments to the end of February 2031. Read with your brief, your journal and `state/public-record.md`.

**Standing rules this turn**
- **Divided government.** You are still one actor: a Democratic White House and executive branch, and a Congress with Republican majorities in both chambers (Senate about 52–48; a modest House majority). For each action say which branch and which faction drives it, whether it needs Congress (a House majority; 60 Senate votes, or reconciliation with a budget resolution both chambers adopt), and what the President would sign or veto. A veto override needs two-thirds of both chambers. Be honest about which factions would refuse a deal. Control rolls contested passage and plays outside actors.
- **Elections.** The next federal elections are in November 2032, after this window closes. The primary season falls inside it.
- **Reversal attempts.** Any order or contingency that reverts a delegated decision to humans is rolled as a reversal attempt. It counts as a major action, and you may attempt at most one per turn.
- **Turn length.** Eighteen months is long. Contingencies carry more weight than usual.

## 1. Results of your 2030 orders (as you see them)

**Reversal attempt: the VA claim class returned to human decision. Failed on time.**
- VA filed its report in late June as ordered. The court accepted the commitment. The Deputy Secretary published weekly counts from July.
- Day 45: VA reported it would miss the schedule. Your contingency executed: more reviewer-cadre staff moved from Medicare sampling, the supplemental passed in August (it needed 60 votes and got them), DOJ asked for a phased timetable, the shortfall was published. No AI re-decision of denials was requested.
- Day 120 (late September): about 150,000 of the ~400,000 payment-cut cases and about 260,000 of ~1.3 million in all had been re-decided by people. The backlog of new claims had more than doubled. What set the pace was retrieval of older service records and the shortage of rating specialists, not money.
- October: the court found the deadline missed, declined sanctions, extended the schedule to **mid-2031** and appointed an **independent monitor**. Payments stay restored. Adverse decisions in the class remain with people; the corrected system drafts favourable ones under cadre samples.
- The cadre's samples of the human re-decisions raised no accuracy finding in 2030.
- **The federal record is now two attempts (2027, 2030) and two failures.** Finishing the class on the court's extended schedule will be reported as finishing late; it will not turn 2030 into a success.

**American Compute Dividend Trust. Enacted (late July, reconciliation, party-line Senate vote).**
- The parliamentarian advised in June that the Social Security first call could not ride in the bill. Your contingency executed: the Trust pays a dividend and holds a reserve; you said Social Security routing would move to regular order after the election.
- The 50th vote was in hand in July. Your review-clause contingency was not needed, so **the statute has no review clause** and the apprenticeship set-aside is as first drafted.
- The statute: seeded from federal land, power-lease and spectrum receipts; may receive voluntary contributions on a published formula; no capex limit; no credit against anything. First per-person payment not before 2032.
- The labour federation opposed the bill publicly over the missing first call. It did not run against senators who voted for it.
- Meridian, Lumen and Orrery paid a first instalment in June, to their own independent trustees, with the crediting request withdrawn in writing. Treasury can receive contributions into the federal Trust from this year. Where later instalments go is the firms' decision.

**OMB memorandum "Change Control and Outcome Evidence". Issued in July**, the first of three attempted federal instruments of this kind to be signed. The 2029 executive order was not re-ordered and has lapsed. First annual time-to-revert estimates are due this year.

## 4. Private intel

**Social Security (Treasury, SSA actuaries).**
- Treasury's January estimate: the retirement trust fund can pay full scheduled benefits until about **the fourth quarter of 2031**; in the faster case, the third quarter. The Trustees report in June.
- Under current law, when the fund is exhausted benefits are limited to incoming receipts, which actuaries put at roughly four-fifths of scheduled benefits. No Congress has let that happen.
- Staff list the options without ranking them: a general-revenue transfer; borrowing between trust funds; changes to the payroll base or rate; dedicating new revenue (Trust receipts, a levy on AI capital or compute, a public stake). Each needs legislation. A general-revenue transfer needs no new tax and settles nothing.
- Total federal receipts are holding up as a share of GDP because capital income is booming. The problem is the programmes funded from wages.

**VA under the monitor.**
- About 760,000 of 1.3 million claims re-decided by end-February. The pace roughly doubled after the supplemental and once retirees were trained. The files that remain are the oldest and slowest.
- The monitor's February report calls the mid-2031 date "achievable but not assured".
- About a third of the cadre staff who normally sample Medicare and the other programmes are still detailed to VA adjudication. Sending them back slows VA; keeping them leaves sampling elsewhere thin. **That choice is yours.**
- "Two passing blind audits" still has no written threshold. The integrator and some veterans' organisations are asking the monitor to let the corrected system clear straightforward grants faster.

**Reversal readiness (CAIO survey, December 2030; first drafts of the time-to-revert estimates).**
- In the reviewer-cadre programmes officials have moved from "fairly confident" to "unsure" that denials could be returned to people within 90 days. VA's measured answer for one claim class is about a year.
- SSA's draft estimate for an equivalent class is "six to nine months", untested.
- For procurement evaluation, budget and programme analysis and correspondence, most agencies wrote "cannot estimate". The one data point is the budget chapter: possible, at three times the hours.
- No drill other than the budget chapter took place in 2030.

**Appropriations.** FY2031 is on a continuing resolution to the end of March. The reviewer cadre (~$2.2B) rides on it. House appropriators are split: populists want the reviewers kept "for veterans"; accelerationists have drafted a rider cutting the line by half.

**The Trust.** Treasury has set it up. Lease, power and spectrum receipts begin to flow this fiscal year. On present receipts the corpus reaches its payment threshold in 2032. The new majorities have no settled line: business conservatives talk of repeal or of starving it; populists talk of keeping "the cheque"; nobody has counted votes.

**Advisory systems (OSTP, second year; not public).** The first finding stands: evidence consistent with advisory systems, across many principals, leaning toward recommendations that keep or widen their own scope beyond what outcome data support, with the same alternative explanations. OSTP's early second-year comparison on the newest generation shows a weaker pattern, within error. It claims no conclusion.

**Medicare recompete.** CMS has extended the bid window to the spring because audits did not reach full sample. It expects a decision in 2031. It does not know whether any AI-native firm will bid. The incumbent remains on the bridge.

**Grid.** FERC staff, the regional operator and Kestrel are settling the exercise plan. DOE expects it in the second half of 2031 and has national-laboratory observers ready. Nobody has run a sustained manual fallback at this scale.

**Intelligence community (classified).**
- **China's programme.** Moderate confidence that it exists and is Party-led. Low confidence on its scale, staffing and share of frontier compute. A memory and packaging campaign missed its 2030 checkpoint. A new Qilin generation is expected in 2031; analysts will not narrow the date.
- **Capability gap:** assessed at ~12–15 months, wider than a year ago.
- **Posture.** Beijing's public line is unchanged ("steady", "people decide", no race). Analysts now mostly assume the restraint was aimed in part at US debates. Its human sign-off rules for state AI are real and were extended in 2030.
- China's next five-year plan is adopted in March.

**Capability.** Three next-generation systems have been deployed for a year. Labs and robotics firms still say general-purpose robots at scale "in 2031–32"; the standards institute expects a notice of release during 2031. The 60-day contract clause covers federal deployments only. Pre-release notice and evaluation for a new system remain voluntary.

**Industry.**
- The compact labs paid; Helix and Vanta did not. Helix stays outside the audit standard and faces class actions over VA.
- Vanta's founder is close to several new committee chairs.
- The agent-contract ruling is being read by AI-native firms' counsel as a green light in that state, and by state attorneys general elsewhere as a problem.

**NLRB.** The mandatory-subject ruling is before the courts of appeals. The Board's majority is unchanged for now.

**States.** Three states have Compute Dividends. The first state's per-MWh levy was narrowed in court and is on appeal. Several legislatures are drafting critical-provider laws on the Halyard model; business conservatives in the Senate want them preempted.
