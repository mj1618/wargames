# Orders — ai-firms — Turn 05 (1 Jan 2031 – 30 Jun 2032)

## 1. Reflection
**What I believe is happening.**
- The reset worked and bought time, not safety. Orrery's reversion (denials back to people), the migration hold, and Lumen's disclosure all landed, and insurers now treat re-sample at renewal as the market standard. Helix remains the weak flank: two public failures, outside v2, contesting the 60-day clause, with discovery heading toward its release decisions. Populists in both new majorities do not distinguish Helix from the compact. A Helix third failure in a critical service becomes a bloc-wide licence problem (intel s.1, s.4).
- The human-only sample is the most important thing I hold that nobody else has. It reads somewhat worse than the AI-assisted sample on new-generation systems, inside its error band. My own safety teams say it neither shows nor excludes a problem, and a sample 3-4x larger would settle it. Two carriers already know it exists. Sitting on it has a high leak risk and no upside. Under my disclosure rule (leak p high; disclosure buys credibility with insurers and the panel), I disclose now, with the result stated plainly and a larger sample already commissioned. Blind spot: my own telemetry (22-23% AI-directed output vs BEA 18-19%) and the new generation's apparently better reconciliation come from the same source or from reviewers using unvalidated AI assistance. I will not quote either as good news.
- The next release (general-purpose robotics, mid-2031 at Meridian and Helix, ~a quarter later at Lumen) is the first system class with physical-world reach. Reversal is harder than for software and the 60-day federal clause covers federal deployments only. Notice would be due this spring. Output is gated by partner manufacturing (about a doubling a year at best), so a staged rollout costs little in the first year.
- Washington: weak White House (no reply to my three June asks), Republican majorities friendlier on permitting, energy and preemption, hostile on China, populists angry about VA. A Social Security must-pass bill in H2 2031 will attract every revenue idea, including AI capex and compute levies. Washington 4/10, public 4/10.
- The ratchet: BLS annual average 48.6% is below 50%, so 1.5% applies to 2031. Reading it differently would be exactly the kind of move that re-damages trust, and the labour federation has told me what it will do if I credit or game the formula.
- Uncertain: whether the Helix board moves to v2; whether the new Congress repeals or starves the Trust; whether conflicting agent-contract rulings produce a personhood fight; whether physical-system evaluations exist that can actually catch failure; how much of what our advisory systems recommend to customers leans toward more delegation (labour's sample request).
- **Red lines (unchanged):** no weights to any government; no mandatory equity stake without compensation; no knowingly false filings. Soft lines I have set myself: no unaudited generation-change migration in critical/federal deployments; no crediting of voluntary payments against any levy or state measure; no bid or automated-denial return above the ~5% reconciliation line.

## 3. Options considered
| # | Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hold the human-only sample, wait for a larger one | No spin risk; avoids Helix-style exposure now | Leak risk high (two carriers know it exists); looks like the 2029 credit episode; destroys the one asset I have (candour) |
| 2 | Disclose now with the result unspun and commission a larger human-only sample plus independent validation of AI-assisted review | Insurers and panel get it from us; credibility; the sample is what actually resolves the question; cross-developer validation attacks the independence problem | Headlines "labs admit their checks might not work"; may prompt insurers to tighten; cost and delay |
| 3 (unconventional) | Disclose and also propose that the panel make a human-only sample a standing condition of audit for new-generation systems, funded jointly, binding on all signatories including Helix | Turns a vulnerability into a universal rule that binds rivals equally (my decision rule); gives Helix a reason to join v2 | Costly if the larger sample shows a gap; Helix may refuse and use it as reason to stay out |
| 4 | Release robotics on the existing compact schedule, notice only for federal deployments | Speed; Helix and Vanta will not give more | Physical reach with reversal hard; discredits the reset; an incident would be the "tobacco" moment |
| 5 | Voluntary 90-day notice, third-party physical-system evaluation, staged rollout (structured settings first, no unsupervised care or critical infrastructure at launch) | Matches practice; costs little given partner build rate; insurers likely to require anyway | Chinese hardware leads; Helix may undercut on timing |
| 6 | Reallocate the dividend to the federal Trust now | Public legitimacy; Treasury ready | Trust's fate unsettled; risk of starvation or crediting in a later vehicle; labour wants the formula kept separate |

I choose option 3 (disclosure with a standing human-only rule) as Major Action 1, and option 5 as Major Action 2. The dividend stays with independent trustees at 1.5% with a conditional transfer path (minor).

## 4. Decision

### Public statement
"The industry will publish results of its own checks even when they are unwelcome. We are disclosing that a small human-only sample on new-generation deployments reads somewhat worse than the AI-assisted sample. It is not conclusive. A sample several times larger is commissioned today with the standards panel and the labour seat. We will give ninety days' notice and a completed independent evaluation before releasing physical-world systems, and we will stage them. The AI dividend steps up to 1.5% for 2031 under the published formula; it is separate from, and will not be credited against, any tax or state measure."

### Minor actions
- **Dividend (Meridian, Lumen, Orrery).** Pay at ~1.5% of gross AI revenue for 2031 and 2032 (about $25B a year at current run-rate); the formula's trigger is read as annual, and we state that reading publicly with the arithmetic. Instalments stay with the independent trustees. Add an irrevocable conditional transfer path: contributions move to the federal Compute Dividend Trust if (a) statute retains the Trust, (b) our payments are not credited against any tax, levy or state measure, and (c) the formula remains public. Otherwise the trustees apply them under their existing charter. Offer the labour seat again, unconditionally. No crediting, ever, in writing.
- **Meridian board response to the 54% vote.** Meridian's board adopts a policy: version-change notice, preserved human override and demonstrated human-operation capacity for every customer running determinations about a person's benefits, pay or care, not only critical-infrastructure and health-payment customers. Lumen matches. Target before the next AGM; expect delay not veto. Say so publicly before VA's next report.
- **Orrery.** Keep denials with people. Criteria to restore automation (set now, with the panel and carriers deciding, not Orrery): two consecutive human-led audits of commercial claims at or below 3%, plus human second determination on denials above a size threshold. Medicare: bid only if a fresh human-led audit lands at or below ~4%, with tranche gating; no bid above ~5%. Margin cost accepted.
- **Helix.** Offer v2 on-ramp with retroactive recognition again; offer a pooled outside investigator of cause for the third-quarter cluster; do not criticise publicly. Stand ready to offer Helix customers a re-sample service through the panel.
- **Labour sample request.** Grant the panel audit-sample access to the recommendation set (what our agents recommend to customers about workforces and review processes), human-reviewed, with the federation's reviewer present. We state our own finding first if it leans toward more delegation.
- **Fund hiring.** Reset the Transition Fund target honestly (39,000 against ~70,000 cumulative); fund a wage-subsidy apprenticeship line from the Trust set-aside concept; publish the third count whatever it says. Helix's fourth report: no comment.
- **Washington (government affairs).** Offer a single, non-credited, universal formula contribution as the alternative to a capex limit or compute levy in a Social Security vehicle, on condition it binds all firms above a revenue threshold equally, including Helix, Vanta stacks and AI-native firms. Support federal preemption only for frontier-model development rules, not for state levies, dividends or critical-provider laws, and do not challenge the state dividend measures. Testify on lab security for the China hearings; support the memory/packaging controls bill; do not lobby for the reviewer cadre cut.
- **Agent contracts.** Meridian, Lumen and Orrery state: no claim to legal personhood for AI agents; every AI-run entity contracts through an accountable legal person with a named human; human-readable record above the threshold stays the norm. Orrery's counsel to be consulted first; Vanta's founder and some AI-natives will dissent publicly.
- **Kestrel.** Keep funding the manual-fallback exercise; offer additional staffed readiness drills; Meridian and Lumen require exercise success before go-live for their own models, regardless of timeline.
- **Own operations.** Commission a human-only outcome sample of internal operational decisions at Meridian and Lumen (about three-quarters and 60% with no meaningful human review), reported to boards and the panel in summary.
- **Insurers.** Give the two carriers the sample design now and the result with the panel.

### Contingencies
- If a member (any, including Helix) releases a physical-world system without the evaluation or sufficient notice: Meridian and Lumen publicly state their practice differs, delay their own release only if the evaluation is incomplete, and do not criticise Helix by name.
- If the larger human-only sample shows a gap the AI-assisted sample did not: disclose to the panel and insurers within days; pause new-generation migrations in benefits/pay/claims/care for Meridian, Lumen and Orrery until reviewers are retrained to checking without unvalidated assistance; expand the human reviewer pool by up to 1,500 and fund it ourselves. This uses a reversal slot only if a delegated decision must be returned (see below).
- If the larger sample reads the same or better: publish unspun, with the confidence interval; do not use as a marketing claim.
- If a serious incident occurs in a critical service (grid exercise fails, hospital, Medicare, benefits) involving any member model: **reversal attempt reserved** (one slot) for the affected function (return to manual operation or human determination), Meridian or Lumen executes for its own customers; request the standards institute lead cause review within days; share records with the panel; no public blame of the integrator before cause is known.
- If a Social Security bill carries a mandatory AI-revenue measure: support it only if universal, non-credited and not an equity stake; if it includes a mandatory stake without compensation, hold the red line and offer warrants-for-permits.
- If the Trust is repealed or starved: the trustees apply contributions under their own charter; no re-routing to a state measure; tell labour first.
- If Orrery's commercial audit comes in above ~5% again: remains with human denials; consider the claims workflow re-scoped to assist-only.
- If the agent-contract appeal produces conflicting rulings and a personhood bill moves in Congress: back the "no personhood, accountable legal person" language; AI-native dissent is tolerated, but the bloc statement stands.
- If Vanta's founder breaks ranks to the majority (preemption of levies, attack on notice): do not retaliate; reconfirm our universal-formula position; antitrust sensitivity remains.
- If insurers withdraw cover for generation-change deployments: extend the re-sample hold to all migrations; offer cost-sharing on re-samples.
- If the Meridian board has not moved by the December 2031 AGM: expect the proposal to rise past 54%; the board adopts anyway.
- If China's models close the gap faster than the one-year estimate or a Chinese robot-maker undercuts on notice: no change to our notice practice; seek a labour-measurement and incident exchange via the US-China group.
