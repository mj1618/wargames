# Orders — labour — Turn 05 (1 January 2031; orders cover January 2031 – June 2032; written on intel to end-February 2031)

## 1. Reflection

**What I believe is happening.** The number we organised around for four years has gone through the floor: labour share ~47.5% in Q4 2030, the first calendar year below 50%, labour-linked receipts down to ~64% of federal revenue (public record T5). The Trustees' 2031 date is no longer a sensitivity; Treasury staff are telling our retiree affiliates the retirement fund pays full benefits only until about Q4 2031 (intel §4). That makes a must-pass Social Security bill the one piece of legislation in this period that *has* to move, through a Congress whose leadership wants preemption and permitting and whose populist wing is hostile to the labs and cool to us (intel §4). Everything we failed to carry in 2029–30 — a first call, a levy, a broader base — now has a vehicle with a hard date.

Our political rung is spent. Political capital 2/10 after two campaigns that executed as written and lost (intel §1). The dividend polls near 70% and lost four of five ballots to one man's money, and our organisers say assistants now shape how members hear about ballot measures more than our mailers do (intel §4). The House Democrats are cool, the White House is weak and never answered our price, the populists can deliver votes we cannot. Lesson of 2030: our arguments win polls and lose votes; we need allies who are trusted *inside* the new majority and we need to be present where our members actually get their information.

But a rung we have not used in years has just re-opened. The Board ruled in November that AI deployment affecting unit work is a **mandatory subject of bargaining** (intel §1, §4). While it stands, every organised employer must bargain *before* deploying. And the sectors our leverage-first rule treated as safe — warehousing, manufacturing, logistics, agriculture — are about to be tested: robot pilot lines are being fitted now; labs and robotics firms say general-purpose robots at scale in 2031–32 (intel §4). This is the 1960 longshore moment. The employers want to automate on a schedule and want labour peace while they do it; we have a legal lever that may be gone within eighteen months (counsel: somewhat under even that an appeals court narrows it) and economic leverage that is real today and may be gone in three years. Spend it now, for a deal that also covers the places we are weak.

**What the VA taught us.** Conditioning works; reversal is slow. Our affiliates delivered the plan, staffed the surge and, with the supplemental and retirees, doubled the pace — 760,000 of 1.3M by end-February (intel §4). But the cadre is exhausted, a third of its samplers are still deciding claims, sampling in Medicare is thin, and the people writing agencies' "time-to-revert" estimates say the honest answer is "we don't know" (intel §4). The veterans' organisations hold to "a person decides" on denials and blame staffing, not the principle. Fine: so do we. We should help the monitor let the corrected system clear straightforward *grants* after a blind sample, keep every denial with a person, and get our samplers back to sampling. "A person decides" was always about adverse decisions.

**The ruling nobody asked us about.** A state court has enforced a contract concluded entirely by agents for an AI-run company with no human signatory (public record T5). Counsel says an AI-run employer with no human party to a collective agreement is now a live legal question (intel §4). State AGs across parties are asking whether AI-run entities are acquiring personhood by the back door. That is a coalition-rule opening: AGs, small business (the supplier that got bound), the populist right and us all want the same thing — a named person who answers for an AI-run company.

**The blind spot.** Year two of the employer-side study: the scope-widening lean is weaker and inside the error band on the newest generation; unchanged on the older deployments that shaped today's workplaces; Meridian's sample shows the same split (intel §4). I draw no conclusion yet. What I now see more clearly is a different channel: 43.5% of adults take their primary advice from an assistant, and our own field programme lost to it. I still can't bargain with a system. I can at least find out what it is telling voters, and make sure our side of the argument is where it looks.

**Key uncertainties.**
- What rides on the Social Security bill: a broader base, benefit cuts, preemption, or all three. Whether the populists will carry "tech pays its payroll" or only "the cheque".
- Whether the NLRB ruling survives the appeals courts long enough to settle a pattern.
- When robots actually arrive at scale, and whether the first announcements come from organised employers or from AI-native entrants who have no unit to bargain with.
- Whether the reviewer cadre survives appropriations (a rider halving it is drafted), and whether the Trust survives at all.
- Whether the White House has anything left to give or veto.
- Whether another silent version-change failure is already under way somewhere we don't sample.

**Red lines as they stand.** No two-tier deals — the Robot Deal is attrition-only and new hires on the same terms, or it is not our deal. No campaign against datacenter, grid or robot-plant permits; the building trades are inside the tent and get the install and maintenance work. No reversal attempt of our own this turn — none planned; "grants to the system, denials to people" at VA is the opposite of a reversal. No strike without a locals' vote and a strike-fund review; strike authorisation votes are a backstop to bargaining, not a plan. Condemn sabotage within the day — and pre-empt the Luddite framing: we are not against robots, we want the deal. No request to the Helix source.

**Resources.** Political capital 2/10 — spend 1 on the Social Security fight, hold 1. Treasuries ~$2.25B; 18-month ceiling ~$650M, of which $400M is a ring-fenced strike reserve that is only spent if a vote is taken and the fund review passes. 2032 political spend begins to rebuild in H1 2032.

## 4. Decision

### Public statement

> "Labour's share of the economy fell below half last year for the first time since anyone counted. Social Security runs short this year because the wage base it was built on is being replaced by machines that pay no payroll tax. There are two honest answers: cut the benefits that retirees and veterans already earned, or ask the companies whose machines replaced the wages to pay what the wages used to. We are for the second. The Compute Dividend Trust exists in law and Congress wrote in a line to add revenue to it; we are asking that its receipts go first to Social Security, and that the largest firms running the economy on AI contribute to the trust funds on a published formula — not a tip jar. The veterans' failure taught Washington that a person must decide and that someone independent has to check: fund the reviewers, pass the veterans' bill, and don't let anyone in Washington forbid a state from requiring a human who answers.
>
> And to the employers now fitting robot lines: we are not against robots. Sixty years ago the longshore unions and the shipping companies agreed to automate the ports together — on a schedule, by attrition, with a share of the gains and nobody thrown away. The law now says you bargain before you deploy. We are ready to make that deal in every warehouse, plant and depot in the country, starting this month."
