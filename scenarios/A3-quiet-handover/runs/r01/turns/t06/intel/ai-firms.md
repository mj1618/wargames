## 2. Messages received (verbatim)

*Sent in early 2031. Most were overtaken by events.*

**From us-gov (White House Chief of Staff and NEC Director) → CEOs of Meridian, Lumen and Orrery.** Private direct line, early 2031. Since then: the veterans' bill carrying your re-sample rule passed the House and failed in the Senate; the commission deadlocked and put nothing to a vote; no preemption bill moved; the instalments stayed with your trustees.
> "You paid without a crediting condition and held the migrations. That was noticed. Your first ask is in the veterans' bill: an independent re-sample on any model-generation change for federal systems and for federally funded critical-sector contractors, binding every vendor. It will need you to say so in public and to the new chairs, some of whom are hearing the opposite from Vanta's founder. Three things from us. One: the federal Trust can now receive contributions on a published formula; we would like the second instalment paid there. Two: Social Security will be bridged this year and a commission will put the wage base, a levy and a public stake to a vote in 2032. We are telling you now so that you hear it from us; the best argument against a mandate is a voluntary payment that is large, public and in the Trust. Three: we want 90 days' notice and an evaluation window before any general-purpose robotics release, as you gave for the last generation. We will sign a narrow federal frontier-development title if it carries statutory notice and evaluation. We will veto preemption of state employment, automated-decision and dividend laws, and we would take it badly if you lobbied for that through the back door a second time."

**From labour (federation president) → Meridian leadership.** Quiet channel, February 2031. Since then: the board adopted the policy; you offered the universal formula publicly; the Robot Deal was signed by one national employer.
> "Thank you for the sample, the payment and the policy; we noticed that you did all three before we asked twice. Fifty-four per cent of your shareholders want version-change notice and demonstrated human capacity for customers deciding people's benefits, pay and care. Adopt it as board policy by September and the trustees withdraw and stand down the fifth campaign; don't, and it comes back as a bylaw. Two new asks. First: Social Security runs short this year and the bill that fixes it must pass. You said you back a trust seeded by receipts and your voluntary payments. We are asking Congress to send the Trust's receipts to Social Security first and to put your formula into statute for the largest firms, dedicated to the trust funds — what you already pay, made law for everyone your size. Say publicly you can live with that. It splits you from Vanta again, at no cost to you, and it is the difference between a broader base and benefit cuts. Second: robots. Your customers are fitting general-purpose lines this year. You already require a human override and an outcome audit from critical-infrastructure and health-payment customers. Extend it: notice to the workforce and a named human in charge with the authority to stop, for any robotics deployment affecting workers. We are offering every employer a longshore deal — automate on a schedule, by attrition, share the gains. You can make that the default or you can watch us strike for it depot by depot. For the study: we would like the same kind of reconciled sample for what your assistants told users who asked about last year's ballot measures. We'd rather see it from you."

**Your message to Helix's CEO.** Delivered. Result only: Helix gave about ten weeks' notice for robotics, stayed outside the audit standard and declined the pooled investigator. It has not criticised you in public.

## 4. Private intel

**Your own numbers (not public).**
- Telemetry puts AI-directed output at about **33%** of US private output (BEA publishes 27–28%). Agent-to-agent deals are about 31% of business-to-business value; contracts with no human signatory are a small share, in one state.
- Combined lab revenue run-rate ~$2.5T. AI-native firms' revenue ~$600B a year.
- Internal operational decisions made with no meaningful human review: about four-fifths at Meridian and Helix, about two-thirds at Lumen.

**The next generation (roadmaps; safety and evaluation teams).**
- Helix and Meridian both plan release around the **first quarter of 2033**; Lumen a quarter later. Public guidance says "2033".
- What the teams expect: systems better than the best people across science, strategy and policy design. Most of the research that produced them was done by current systems.
- **Your evaluation leads put one question to the CEOs.** People can no longer directly evaluate most of what these systems decide. Pre-release evaluation of the new generation will be done largely by current systems. The human-led audits and the human-only sample cover deployed decisions about people, at your customers. Nothing equivalent exists for the release decision itself. Who signs it, on what evidence, and is any of that evidence produced without relying on AI systems? The validation programme has interim results only.
- Helix's evaluation is its own. Its board is not expected to change that before release.
- The federal 60-day clause applies when a federal deployment is moved to a new model. Your migration hold and the insurers' re-sample apply to your customers. Nothing binds Helix- or Vanta-stack deployments outside federal contracts.

**Who else is checking.** OMB's published coverage list shows that in most federal programmes no claim class has been independently sampled since spring 2031. Your panel auditors say the compact's audits are now the main human-led outcome checks on new-generation systems in the country.

**The datacenter attack.** The damaged campus served several labs' workloads; capacity was rerouted within days. No one was hurt. Security and insurance costs for sites under construction are rising. Investigators have named no organisation. Bills to expand protective powers are being drafted; some would also add reporting duties for operators.

**The election.**
- Vanta's founder is the challenger's largest donor. The challenger's platform includes one federal standard preempting state AI laws and levies and an end to the federal reviewer programme. It is silent on the Trust and on your dividend.
- The President's stated positions are unchanged: veto preemption of state employment, automated-decision, critical-provider and dividend laws; a narrow frontier-development title only with statutory notice and evaluation.
- Both campaigns have asked your government-affairs teams where the compact stands on preemption and on the reviewers.
- A majority of adults now name an assistant as their main source. The labour federation's petitions for disclosure of paid influence around assistants' answers are pending. Its study said assistants' answers on the 2030 measures tracked the funded opposition's material; it listed alternative explanations. Your teams have not run their own check.

**Customers and insurers.** Carriers have begun asking for a named person in charge at robot-operated sites at renewal. Early robot deployments are mostly at non-union sites; that is customers' choice. One national logistics customer signed the federation's Robot Deal. Chinese robot makers offer lower prices and no notice terms.

**Social Security and the Trust.** The bridge ends in mid-2034. The Trustees now show the gap widening. Treasury says the federal Trust has reached its payment threshold: about $50–70 a person on public receipts alone. Your trustees' $50B is larger than the federal corpus.

**China.** Pilot-line memory yields are announced; the new Qilin generation is about a year behind by US analysts' reading.
