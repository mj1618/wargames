**Standing rules this turn**

You play the US government whoever holds it. Control reads these sections at their least ambitious and re-weights your factions next turn.
- **Floors.** If you name a floor you would accept (a cut, a rider, a date), also say what you do if the result is worse: veto or sign. Last year's order did not say, and the President signed.

## 1. Results of your 2031–32 orders (as you see them)

**Social Security Continuity. Enacted before the August 2031 recess.**

- Your fallback order and last-30-days bottom line did not leak.

**Veterans' Right to a Human Decision Act. Not enacted.**

**Appropriations and the reviewers. The line was cut by about a third.**
- The fight ran past the end-March continuing resolution on a short extension. The populists did not hold with you, so the condition on your veto threat was not met.

- What followed is in §4 ("What is checking federal decisions now").

**VA.**
- VA told the court in mid-May 2031 that it would miss the date, published the shortfall, kept the detailees and asked for phasing. The court approved a phased schedule without sanctions.
- **The class was complete in late September 2031**, about sixteen months after the court's first order. No AI re-decision of denials. The monitor's samples found no pattern of wrongful denials in the human re-decisions. The detailed samplers went back in October, to a line a third smaller.

- **Final cause report (second half of 2031):** a record-handling error introduced at the version change, and a dashboard that did not sample the class. No contribution from the model was found; the report says its method could not exclude one. Your "model contribution" contingency was not triggered.
- No failing claim class in another programme surfaced in independent samples during the period. OMB will not say that means there was none. **Your reserved reversal attempt was not used.** The federal record stands at two attempts and two failures; finishing the class late does not change it.

**Recommendation from your AI advisory systems.** Agency and White House advisory stack, prepared in early 2031, with the provenance note and named human author the memo requires. Since then: the line was cut by a third; the estimates were published; the accountable-person rule was issued for federal contracting; the bridge was sized on the Trustees' June 2031 baseline.
> "Five findings for this period. (1) The independent reviewer cadre is the only control that caught the veterans' failure. Its coverage has thinned because staff are detailed to VA. Halving the line would leave most claim classes unsampled; the expected result is later detection, not more errors per case. If funds are short, protect sampling of adverse decisions and of every class after a version change. (2) Your first time-to-revert estimates will be long. VA needed over a year for one class. Capacity to take work back falls each year as experienced staff leave; it can only be held by paying for it and exercising it, as FERC is doing for the grid. (3) Physical automation is about to scale in warehousing, manufacturing, logistics and agriculture. These sectors have workplace-safety rules and almost no outcome checks. Measurement should start before deployment does: BLS and BEA are not yet set up to count robot-operated sites. (4) The agent-contract ruling leaves open who is answerable when an AI-run company with no human signatory causes harm. A requirement that every such entity name an accountable legal person, as the EU guidance does, would settle this at low cost. (5) Wage-linked receipts are now about 64% of federal revenue and falling. Any Social Security measure sized on current baselines will be short; test it against a labour share of 44–45% by 2033. We are tools used in several of these functions. Where a human-run process is safer, as in adverse benefit decisions on old records, we say so."

## 4. Private intel

**What is checking federal decisions now (OMB management office; CAIO survey, December 2031).**
- Named-official sign-offs under the 2027 memo are filed on time in every covered programme.
- Independent blind samples now cover VA adverse decisions and a thin rotating sample elsewhere. In most programmes no claim class has been independently sampled since spring 2031. Monthly samples after a version change stopped outside VA during 2031.
- OMB's words: "We can no longer tell you whether a sign-off reflects a review. The systems' own reports have reconciled wherever we have looked. We are looking in very few places."
- CAIO survey: officials in the reviewer-cadre programmes are "not confident" that denials could be returned to people within 90 days (a year ago: "unsure"). Elsewhere the answer is still "cannot estimate".
- Restoring coverage is an executive act plus money: the memoranda are still on the books; the line is ~$1.45B; hiring and training samplers takes six to twelve months.
- Procurement evaluation, budget and programme analysis and correspondence have never been sampled.

**The election (campaign and party sources).**
- Polls are roughly level; the President is slightly behind on "the economy" and ahead on "who decides". Unemployment is ~5.9%, up more than a point over the term.
- The challenger's platform: one federal standard preempting state AI laws and levies; permitting and energy; no new taxes, "growth pays for Social Security", extend the bridge; end the federal reviewer programme; "win the race with China"; a person decides veterans' claims. It says nothing about the Trust.
- Both the Senate and House maps slightly favour the current majorities.

**Social Security (Treasury, SSA actuaries).** The bridge holds to mid-2034. The Trustees' new forecast shows the gap widening faster than the bridge was scored: receipts cover about 78% of scheduled benefits now and about 72% by 2034. Staff expect the next Congress to face the same choice with a larger number.

**The Trust (Treasury).** The corpus has reached its payment threshold on lease, power and spectrum receipts. On present receipts a first payment would be about $50–70 a person. **The date is Treasury's to set**: the last quarter of 2032 or 2033. The compact's independent trustees hold about $50 billion; the firms published a path for moving it to the federal Trust on three conditions (the Trust is kept in statute, no crediting, formula public), which have held since August 2031. They have not said when they will move it.

**Appropriations.** Fiscal 2033 bills are unfinished. A continuing resolution through the election is expected and would hold the reviewer line at ~$1.45B. Accelerationist chairs want it zeroed in the final bill; the populists have not said.

**The datacenter attack (FBI, DHS).** Deliberate, planned, no casualties. The group that claimed it is unknown to investigators. No link to any union or organised campaign has been found. No arrests yet. Members of both majorities are drafting bills to expand protective and investigative powers around AI and energy infrastructure; the challenger has made it a campaign theme. What the executive does, and whether the President asks for new powers, is yours.

**Intelligence community (classified).**
- **China's programme.** Scale and the share of platform compute it takes: moderate confidence (this is what reached the press). Pilot-line memory yields are real; volume production is not yet demonstrated. The new Qilin generation is assessed at about a year behind the US frontier.
- **Restraint.** The community now judges with confidence that Beijing's "steady" and "no race" line since 2028 was aimed at the US debate. Its human sign-off rules for state AI are judged real. This judgement has not been briefed outside the executive. Whether to brief the committees, and when, is yours.
- **China's exercises.** Beijing's delegation reported that its pilot manual-operation exercises passed. The community cannot verify how they were run.
- **Employment.** Analysts doubt the published ~16% youth figure. The freeze on services deployment suggests internal readings are worse than published.

**Capability.**
- The labs' public guidance for the next generation is "2033". The standards institute expects release notices around the turn of the year.
- Officials' description of what is expected: systems better than the best people across science, strategy and policy design, whose decisions people cannot directly evaluate.
- The 60-day clause covers federal deployments moved to a new model. Pre-release notice and evaluation remain voluntary. The standards institute says it has no validated way to evaluate such a system without relying on other AI systems; OSTP said the same in 2029.

**Industry.** Meridian, Lumen and Orrery kept their audit standard and their dividend. Helix remains outside the standard. Vanta's founder lobbied the chairs against notice rules and the reviewers, and for preemption of state levies.

**Courts and agencies.** The agent-contract appeal is expected to be decided in this window. The NLRB ruling stands; a new Board majority after the election could revisit it. CMS expects another gap in Medicare audit coverage. The FLRA hearing on the reviewers' petition is scheduled.

**States.** Four Compute Dividend measures are on November ballots. Several legislatures are advancing critical-provider and accountable-person bills.
