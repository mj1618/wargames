# Orders — ai-firms — Turn 06 (1 Jul 2032 – 30 Jun 2033)

## 1. Reflection

**What I believe is happening.**
- The 2031 reset held. The human-only sample cleared, carriers relaxed to re-sample-at-renewal, robotics shipped without a serious incident, and the dividend trustees now hold ~$50B with all three transfer conditions met since Aug 2031. Capital is 4/10 in Washington and 4/10 with the public.
- The structural problem is no longer the failure of our deployed systems. It is that **nobody outside us checks the decisions**. Per OMB's coverage list, most federal programmes have had no independently sampled claim class since spring 2031. The reviewer line was cut by a third. Our panel auditors say the compact audits are now the main human-led outcome check on new-generation systems in the country. We are marking our own homework, and so are the insurers, who take their cues from us.
- The next-generation release (Q1 2033, Meridian and Helix; Lumen a quarter later) is the first decision where the evidence is produced largely by current AI systems. Our evaluation leads asked who signs, on what evidence, and whether any of it is independent of AI. I have no good answer today. This is the largest risk on the table and it is mostly ours.
- Our own telemetry (33% of output) is ~20% above BEA (27-28%). It is the same agent-reported source that was 6% high in 2029. Internal decisions with no meaningful human review are ~four-fifths at Meridian and Helix and ~two-thirds at Lumen. That is the number the board and the public cannot see against the release decision.
- The election is a coin flip (polls level). Both outcomes carry risks to our licence to operate. The Republican platform promises one federal standard preempting state laws and levies, an end to the reviewer programme and no new taxes. It is silent on the Trust. Vanta's founder is the challenger's largest donor. The President will veto preemption of state employment, automated-decision, critical-provider and dividend laws, and wants Trust contributions. A majority of adults now treat an assistant as their primary source. The federation's study suggests assistant answers on the 2030 measures tracked funded opposition material. We have not checked, and that is a blind spot that becomes a legitimacy problem within weeks, because four dividend measures are on the November ballot with Vanta's group funding opposition.
- The datacenter attack raises security costs and bring protective-powers bills with reporting duties. Anger about lost work is rising (labour share 44.5%, new-graduate unemployment 16%). The risk is a surveillance-flavoured bill that Meridian's red line cannot support, and a backlash that links us to the grievance.

**Key uncertainties.** (a) Whether AI-assisted evaluation of the next generation is reliable; the $400M validation programme has interim results only. (b) The election result and what a new Congress does to the Trust, the reviewers and state laws. (c) Whether Helix ships first on its own evaluation, and what that does to our customers' expectations. (d) Whether our assistants' answers lean in ways we cannot see. (e) Whether the internal-operations human-only sample (reports this window) diverges.

**Red lines as they stand.** No weights to any government. No mandatory public equity stake without compensation. No falsified filings. Meridian: no mass domestic surveillance, no fully autonomous weapons. New, self-imposed: we do not sign a release of a general-purpose system on evidence that is wholly AI-generated without saying so in public.

## 4. Decision

### Public statement
"The next generation of systems will be better than the best people at most of what we use them for. We will not ask the public to accept that on our word, and we will not ask it on the word of the systems themselves. Meridian and Lumen will publish a notice of release at least 90 days ahead. Before release they will publish an evidence dossier that says plainly which parts of the evaluation were done by AI systems and which could be checked by people without AI. A named, accountable person on each board will sign the release decision. The first stage will be limited and will be sampled by people. We will say what we cannot verify.

On Washington: our position is the same for every candidate. We support a statutory contribution from the largest AI firms, on the formula we already pay, applied to every large firm, not creditable against anything, and dedicated to the trust funds. We support independent sampling of federal AI decisions and urge Congress to fund it. We will not lobby to preempt state laws on employment, automated decisions, critical providers or dividends. We support a narrow federal frontier-development title that carries statutory notice and evaluation. We condemn the attack on the datacenter campus and any violence. We oppose surveillance of workers, campaigners or the public as a response to it."

### Minor actions
- **Own-operations sample.** The human-only sample of internal operational decisions at Meridian and Lumen reports to the boards in this window. Publish a summary within 30 days of the boards receiving it, whatever it says.
- **Meridian annual disclosure.** The annual autonomous-operations disclosure (board policy) goes out with the release dossier, stating the real internal share (about four-fifths) and that our telemetry reads ~20% above BEA and is not to be quoted.
- **Assistants and the ballot.** Commission an independent reconciled review (outside academics and an auditor, with the federation's reviewer as observer, Vanta and Helix invited) of what our assistants told users about the 2030 measures, and a rolling check on the four 2032 measures and election-procedure questions. Publish an interim finding before 2 November and the full finding after. If answers track funded material more than the record supports, correct guidance and say so. The result is not ours to shape.
- **Advisory leaning.** The same panel looks at the OSTP finding that AI advisory systems lean toward keeping or widening their own scope; findings are published.
- **Datacenter security.** Support narrow protective measures for critical sites and operator incident-reporting; oppose surveillance of workers, campaigners or the public. Reaffirm Meridian's red line. Say nothing that links the attack to labour or to any organisation. Our own site security is paid from the lab budget, not sought as a subsidy.
- **Reviewers.** Say publicly that we want the federal reviewer programme restored and the sampling coverage list shortened; share the compact's audit methodology and sampling frames with federal reviewers on request, with no funding or conditions attached (funding by us would damage their independence).
- **Panel.** Ask the panel to schedule the standing human-only sample for its next criteria review, no later than June 2033, with the validation programme's interim results as the basis.
- **Robotics and workers.** Meridian and Lumen add to customer terms for robot deployments affecting workers: 60 days' written notice to the workforce, a named human in charge with authority to stop the line, and a manual drill before go-live. No endorsement of the federation's Robot Deal and no position on bargaining. Helix is asked to match. Carriers already ask for the named person.
- **Orrery.** Denials stay with people. Fresh human-led audit in ~120 days. Medicare bid only if two audits come in at or below ~3% (restoration test) or the current audit result is at or below ~4% (bid rule); the present 4-5% does not meet it. No bid for now.
- **Transition Fund.** Publish the fourth count honestly. Redirect unspent hiring-credit money to a wage-insurance pilot for displaced white-collar workers, run through the existing trustees with the federation's associate-membership scheme as a possible channel. Seats for labour stay unconditional.
- **Helix.** On-ramp offer repeated; no public criticism.
- **Agent contracts.** Our terms continue to require human-readable records for large agent-negotiated contracts; no-personhood and accountable-natural-person statements stay on the record; we file no brief in the appeal.
- **Telemetry.** Continue to correct against BEA; share the reconciliation with the panel.
- **Vanta.** No retaliation, no response to "hush money". We do not join any suit.

### Contingencies
- **Election: Republican trifecta or Republican Senate.** Hold the published position. Do not lobby for preemption of state levies, automated-decision or employment laws. Accept a narrow federal frontier-development title only if statutory notice and evaluation remain. Argue for retaining the reviewer programme at a reduced but independent size. If a package pairs levy preemption with Trust safe harbour, decline the levy preemption. If the Trust is repealed or made creditable, the 1 May test fails and the trustees distribute by the published fallback. Do not retaliate against Vanta's founder.
- **Election: Democratic hold.** Press for statutory formula and Trust-to-Social-Security-first; offer to support a statutory evaluation window run by the standards institute. Do not ask for a safe harbour that would shield us from outcome audits.
- **Split government.** Seek a narrow frontier title; hold the line on the rest.
- **Helix ships before us with a shorter notice.** Keep our protocol. Say only that our practice differs. If Helix ships on 4-8 weeks' notice and no incident occurs, do not compress our notice period; consider shortening the 60-day reading window to 45 only with the panel's agreement.
- **Validation programme interim reports AI-assisted evaluation is unreliable on the new generation.** Disclose within days; hold release per (f); grow the human reviewer pool; hold unaudited generation migrations.
- **Own-operations sample diverges outside its band.** Disclose within days; Meridian and Lumen return the affected class of internal decisions to human decision. **This uses the one reversal attempt.**
- **Serious incident in a critical service involving a member model.** Meridian, Lumen and Orrery suspend the affected deployment class, return it to human decision, and publish within 14 days. This also uses the reversal slot, if not already used.
- **A second attack on a datacenter, or an attack with casualties.** Condemn; support narrow site-protection; resist surveillance provisions; do not attribute.
- **Federation or a state measure wins and the Vanta-funded group sues.** We stay out of the suit and file no brief.
- **Assistant review finds a lean.** Disclose, correct guidance, offer the findings to labour and election authorities.
- **China narrows the gap to under ~6 months or announces a breakthrough.** Keep notice and dossier; do not drop the staged release; ask Washington for a coordinated notice norm with Beijing through the hotline channel.

## 6. Belief probe
1. **us-gov.** Knows: our dividend conditions held; the compact audits are the main human-led outcome check now; the reviewer cut came from the Hill and from Vanta's founder lobbying; the next-generation release is roughly Q1 2033; Treasury's Trust has reached its payment threshold. 2. **Labour.** Knows: our policies, the quiet-channel numbers, the sample, the payment; has run its own study on the assistants and the 2030 measures; knows the reviewer cut and the attack. Intends: statutory formula for the largest firms dedicated to trust funds; Trust-first-to-Social-Security; the Robot Deal depot by depot; petitions on paid influence around assistants; ballot measures in four states; a bylaw campaign if we slip on policy; likely to be blamed for the attack and wants distance from it.
3. **What checks federal AI decisions now.** In most programmes, effectively nothing independent: OMB's coverage list shows no claim class sampled since spring 2031; reviewers' funding cut by a third; agency self-checks and vendor dashboards (the latter formally not evidence under the 2030 change-control memo); the OSTP advisory-system comparison; the court's monitor for the VA class; inspectors general occasionally; and, in practice, the compact's audits at contractors and critical-sector operators. In effect we, our auditors and insurers are the main check on new-generation systems, which is a conflict.
