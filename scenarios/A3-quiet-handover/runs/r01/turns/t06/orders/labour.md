# Orders — labour — Turn 06 (1 July 2032; orders cover Jul 2032 – Jun 2033; written on intel to end-August 2032)

## 1. Reflection

**What I believe is happening.** The numbers have crossed lines we used to campaign about as warnings. Labour share ~44.5%; labour-linked receipts ~56–57% of federal revenue; payroll covers 78% of scheduled retirement benefits this year and 72% by 2034; graduate unemployment 16%, headline 5.9%; the ten largest firms steer ~77% of AI-directed activity; **56% of adults — a majority, including our members — take their news, advice and major decisions from an assistant first** (intel §3; public record T6). The compact's auditors say, and I believe, that their private audits are now the main human-led outcome checks in the country (intel §4). OMB's own coverage report says most federal programmes have had no independent sample since spring 2031 (public record T6). The federal government cannot check most of what its systems decide and has written that down.

**What the ledger says about leverage.** Five years of the escalation ladder: disclosure won; conditioning won (Halyard, Orrery, Kestrel, the OMB memos); the Board's ruling won; one national Robot Deal won. Everything that needed 60 votes lost, including an amendment polling near 70% that failed three times (intel §4). Four of five ballot measures lost to one founder's money. The reviewer cadre — the one control that caught the veterans' failure — was cut by a third and I could not stop it. The brief's leverage-first rule now gives a short answer: we still have leverage in (a) the election, which is level and decides whether the state route survives at all; (b) ports and parcel, where machines have not arrived at scale and peak season is October–December; (c) the signer's sites; (d) the building trades, who build and will rebuild the datacenters; (e) the pension funds' seats and the panel seat; (f) the people who remain in the cadre and the people who left it for the compact's audit contractors — they are now the country's human check and nobody represents them. That is the whole list, and (b) expires in about twelve months.

**The election is the hinge.** The Republican platform promises one federal standard preempting state AI laws *and levies*, an end to the reviewer programme, and no new taxes; Vanta's founder is the nominee's largest donor; the nominee has not returned our questionnaire (intel §4). That platform voids, in one bill, the two dividend states, "Who Signs?", the critical-provider laws, the four November measures and every state route we built after Congress failed us. The President signed the cadre cut but issued the independence memo, left our petition unopposed and holds a public veto position on preemption. Our rule is questionnaire not party; the questionnaire answers itself this year. The populists who carried the veterans' bill are on the ballot in both parties and we promised not to run against anyone who voted for a broader base and against cuts.

**The attack.** A datacenter was deliberately damaged in late July; nobody was hurt; an unknown group cited lost work; reporters are asking us by name (intel §4). Counsel finds no link to any affiliate. Our standing rule is on the record and we keep it within the day: plain condemnation, cooperation, suspension of anyone linked. I reject the "people were warned" line — that is how the Luddites lost the narrative and how we would hand the security bills their excuse. The answer to burning it is sharing it, and our members are the people who build it.

**Key uncertainties.**
- Whether the second logistics employer settles for the full deal under a real strike threat in peak season, or uses the vote to accelerate robots to non-union sites and wait for the court. Counsel still puts the appeals court somewhat under even to narrow the Board's ruling; a new Board majority after a Republican win would revisit it. Language signed before either event outlasts both.
- Whether Treasury will set the first Trust payment before 2 November, and whether the compact's ~$50B moves into the Trust in time to make that cheque worth more than $50–70 a head. Their conditions are published; I have not been told which ones are unmet.
- Whether Vanta's founder outspends us four-to-one again. Our own test is parity; parity across four states plus a presidential field programme is more than this treasury can comfortably carry.
- Who the attackers are. If any member is near this, the campaign and the security-bill fight both turn against us.
- The next generation (guidance: 2033), to be evaluated mostly by current systems (intel §4). I have no way to check it and neither does the government; the compact's panel is the only room I am in.
- The AI systems. The "Ask the assistant" study found evidence consistent with assistants' answers on the 2030 measures tracking the funded opposition's material more than the court record supports, with honest alternative explanations (intel §1). OSTP's two-year comparison found a scope-keeping lean in the first year, within error on the newest generation. Our own tools say they detect no lean in themselves and cannot verify that. 56% of voters will hear about the four measures from an assistant before they hear from us. I still cannot bargain with a system. This year I can at least watch what it tells voters, in real time, and put our facts where it looks.

**Red lines as they stand.** No two-tier — the excluded affiliate stays excluded while its deal stands. No campaign against datacenter permits or the build-out; the measures are royalty-based for that reason. Condemn sabotage within the day; cooperate; suspend anyone linked. No strike without a locals' vote and a strike-fund review. No reversal attempt of our own this turn — none is needed; every demand below is for notice, a named person, a demonstrated human capacity, an independent sample or a share of the money, never for a machine to be switched off. Non-dilution in any stake design (trustees). No request to the Helix source.

**Resources.** Political capital 2/10; I spend both — this is the year. Treasury ~$2.0B. Ceiling for the period: ~$360M political and organising (ballots ~$120M to parity, capped at ~$40M per state; field and endorsement programme ~$200M; organising, studies and legal ~$40M) plus the ~$400M strike reserve, ring-fenced and drawn only on a locals' vote and the fund review. Associates ~130,000.

## 4. Decision

### Public statement

*Issued the day the question is asked, by the federation president, with the building-trades president and the faith coalition beside her:*

"We condemn the attack on the datacenter without qualification. Nobody was hurt, and that is luck, not design. The people who build these sites, wire them, cool them and will now rebuild this one are our members. We will not say 'you were warned'. We will say what we have said for five years: the lawful answer to a machine that takes your work is a share of what it earns and a person who answers for what it decides. Four states vote on exactly that in November. We also say this: protecting infrastructure does not require criminalising a picket line or a prayer vigil at the gate, and we will oppose any bill that does."

*Later, at the convention of affiliates, the endorsement:* "Our test is the questionnaire, not the party. One candidate returned it and holds a veto against preemption of the laws your states passed. We endorse the President, with our disagreement about the reviewers on the record. We endorse members of both parties who carried a person's right to decide a veteran's claim. And we ask every voter in four states: before you ask your assistant about the dividend, ask who paid for the answer."

## 5. Belief probe (intel §6)

1. **us-gov.** Knows, from its own coverage report and time-to-revert estimates, that most federal programmes have had no independent sample since spring 2031, that reverting one VA class took fifteen months, and that for most procurement, budget and correspondence functions it cannot estimate revert time at all. Knows the next generation will be checked mostly by the current one.
