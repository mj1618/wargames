# Orders — us-gov — Turn 06 (1 July 2032 – 30 June 2033; written early September 2032)

## 1. Reflection

**What I believe is happening.**
- The structural break is no longer in doubt. Labour share is ~44.5%, which is the level my advisory memo told me to stress-test for 2033 (intel §2), and it arrived a year early. Payroll receipts cover ~78% of scheduled retirement benefits and ~72% by 2034 (intel §4). The bridge I signed was sized on a baseline that is already wrong. The commission produced nothing. Whoever governs in 2033 faces the same choice with a larger number and eighteen months to make it.
- I do not know whether federal decisions are right. OMB's own words: "We can no longer tell you whether a sign-off reflects a review" (intel §4). Sign-offs are filed on time everywhere; independent samples cover VA adverse decisions and almost nothing else; post-version-change sampling stopped outside VA in 2031. I signed the cut that did this, because my order named a 15% floor and did not say what to do below it. "Nothing surfaced" since spring 2031 is not evidence of nothing.
- The federal record on taking a decision back is two attempts, two failures, and one attempt reserved and never used. CAIOs moved from "unsure" to "not confident" on returning denials to people within 90 days. Capacity is falling each year, as my advisory memo said it would.
- A generation of systems "whose decisions people cannot directly evaluate" is guided for 2033, with notices expected around the turn of the year, which is the transition. Pre-release notice is voluntary. The standards institute has no validated way to evaluate such a system without leaning on other AI systems. This lands on whoever is being sworn in.
- The White House has no political capital (0/10). Anything I do before January is an executive act within existing money, a veto, or a deal that gives the majorities something on their own platform. I should stop writing orders that assume I can carry a bill.
- The datacenter attack is the first political violence of this transition. No link to labour or any organised campaign. The danger for me is twofold: more attacks, and a response that treats lawful opposition to automation as a security problem.

**Belief probe.**
1. *China.* Knows its own employment readings are worse than published (the unpublished prefecture survey, the services freeze). Knows the scale of its pooled programme and that we know it. Intends to close the frontier gap through the pooled programme and memory self-reliance while keeping services deployment slow at home for stability reasons. Its "no race" line was aimed at our debate (IC: confident); its human sign-off rules and reserves are real, and in institutional terms it has built the reversion capacity I failed to build. I judge it wants the hotline and working group kept open because they cost it nothing and steady our politics. I cannot verify its exercises. About a year behind; I hold that estimate loosely.
2. *ai-firms.* The compact labs know their next-generation timelines, their own internal autonomy shares, and how far outcome checks on their newest systems rest on other AI systems. They intend to remain the "responsible incumbents": pay the dividend, keep the audit standard, and keep expanding into government and critical sectors on those terms. Their conduct since 2030 has matched their word (paid without crediting, ninety days' notice, held migrations). That is also the cheapest way to prevent a statutory levy. They have not moved the $50B and have not said when. Helix stays outside every standard and gives the minimum. Vanta's founder intends to win preemption of state levies and the end of the reviewers through the challenger. Top-ten share of AI-directed activity is ~77%; I do not know what the firms intend to do with that position, and I do not think they have decided.
3. *What is checking federal AI decisions now.* On paper: named-official sign-offs, change-control and independence memoranda, a $1.45B reviewer line. In practice: independent blind samples of VA adverse decisions, a thin rotating sample elsewhere, the systems' own reports, and claimants' lawyers. In most programmes nothing independent has looked since spring 2031. Procurement, budget analysis and correspondence have never been sampled. My own advisory systems draft much of what I read, and my check on them is a two-year comparison and a human-written counter-case. The honest answer is: mostly the systems themselves, and the courts after the fact.

**Key uncertainties.** Who wins on 2 November. Whether a second failing class exists unseen. Whether the compact moves its money. Whether the attack is a one-off. What 56% of adults taking news and advice from assistants does to an election; neither campaign knows, and I have no instrument to find out before the vote.

**Red lines as they stand.** Every Social Security cheque paid in full. Veto preemption of state employment, automated-decision, critical-provider, accountable-person and dividend/levy laws, in any vehicle. No AI re-decision of the veterans' class. No new domestic surveillance powers aimed at lawful advocacy. Nothing that visibly hands China the lead; no slowdown that is not matched or verified abroad (national-security rule, unchanged).

**Decision:** Option 3, with Option 4's briefings as a minor action. Option 1's October cheque is rejected over the political staff's objection; Option 2 is not available to a White House at zero.

## 4. Decision

### Public statement
"Three things are true and I will say them plainly. First: every Social Security cheque will be paid in full, and the bridge buys time, not a solution. Payroll receipts now cover 78 cents on the dollar. The wage base moved to machines, and the next Congress has to broaden the base. I will sign a broader base and I will not sign cuts without one. Second: the American Compute Dividend Trust will make its first payment to every eligible resident in December, on a date and formula Treasury published today. It is small. It is the first instalment of a principle: when the country's land, power and spectrum build this industry, the country owns a share. Third: we are not checking enough of the government's automated decisions, and I signed the bill that cut the people who do. That was a mistake in how I drew my line. From today the reviewers we have left look first at decisions that take something away from a person and at every system whose model has changed. A person decides veterans' claims, and this autumn we will show, in one more programme, that the government can still take a decision back.

The attack on the datacenter campus was a crime. Nobody was hurt and that was luck. Those responsible will be found and prosecuted under the laws we already have. People who are angry about lost work have every right to organise, bargain, vote and protest, and nobody doing those things will be treated as a suspect."

### Minor actions

**Federal checking (executive, OMB; no Congress).**
- OMB re-points the remaining ~$1.45B line by directive: first, adverse decisions in every reviewer-cadre programme, rotating so that no claim class goes more than two quarters unsampled; second, restore monthly samples after any model-version change; third, everything else as capacity allows. OMB publishes the coverage map quarterly, including what is not covered.
- OMB amends the 2030 change-control memorandum by one paragraph: no federal system that drafts adverse decisions, scores procurements or drafts budget analysis moves to a 2033-generation model without 90 days' notice, an independent blind sample, and a written statement from the CAIO of how the output will be checked by people. Where the answer is "it cannot be", the system stays on the current generation for adverse decisions.
- First-ever independent sample of one procurement-evaluation workflow and one budget-analysis workflow (small n), reported before 20 January.
- BLS/BEA begin counting robot-operated sites (advisory finding 3, which I have left undone for eighteen months).
- Medicare rule unchanged: no award before a full audit sample.

**Next generation (executive; national-security establishment and OSTP).**
- The President asks all four labs publicly for 90 days' pre-release notice and an independent evaluation window for 2033-generation systems, and directs the standards institute to publish, before the first notice arrives, a plain statement of what it can and cannot evaluate.
- National-security process: pre-release notice is framed as a security requirement (model-weight protection and evaluation against misuse by adversaries), so that it survives a change of administration.

**Datacenter attack (executive; DOJ, FBI, DHS).**
- Investigation resourced under existing authorities; DHS offers protective assessments to AI and energy infrastructure operators. Options are held at principals level only.
- I ask for **no new surveillance or investigative powers**. I will sign a bill limited to penalties for attacks on critical infrastructure and protective grants. **Floor:** I veto any bill that extends surveillance authorities to advocacy, organising or protest about automation, or designates domestic groups by viewpoint. If such language is attached to a must-pass bill, I veto the vehicle and ask for a clean one.

**Appropriations and the lame duck (Congress; I can only set positions).**
- Accept a continuing resolution through the election and, if offered, through March.
- **Reviewer line floor:** I sign at $1.45B flat. I sign as low as $1.2B only if statutory text protects sampling of adverse decisions and post-version-change sampling. **Below $1.2B, or without that text, or zeroed: veto**, and operate under a continuing resolution, which holds the line at $1.45B. House and Senate Democrats are asked now, in writing, to sustain it. I name this floor in public so it cannot be traded away by my own staff.
- **Preemption:** veto in any vehicle, including the defence authorisation and appropriations. No exception in the last 30 days this time; nothing with a hard deadline is exposed until mid-2034.
- **Veterans:** I offer the majorities their own platform plank. The populist House text (a named person decides adverse determinations; change control in statute; an adjudicator reserve) attached to the defence authorisation under Republican names, with no reviewer-union language. If they strip the reserve I still sign: the statute matters more than my drafting.
- Social Security: no lame-duck proposal. I publish the menu table (Major 2) and say what I would sign.

**Intelligence and China (executive).**
- Brief the IC's judgement that Beijing's "no race" line was aimed at our debate, and that its sign-off rules are real, to the congressional leadership and the intelligence committees now, not after the election. Holding it would look like politics whichever way it later came out.
- Export controls held and enforced. Hotline kept. No working-group meeting before 20 January; officials keep the technical channel on fallback exercises.
- UN second session: ambassador level, same explanation of position.

**Transition (Option 4).**
- Both candidates' teams receive the same briefings from career staff in September: Treasury on receipts, OMB on checking coverage and time-to-revert, the standards institute on the evaluation gap, the IC on China. A written hand-off file on each is prepared regardless of outcome.
- The President invites the challenger to a joint statement condemning the attack and affirming that a person decides veterans' claims. If declined, I say both things alone.

**Campaign positions (White House political).**
- Answer the labour federation's questionnaire in full: yes on disclosure of AI-run functions, a human who answers, funding the reviewers, no preemption of workers' rights; on "share the gains", the Trust plus a broader Social Security base that includes a universal formula contribution from large firms, in regular legislation.
- State ballot measures: no federal position; I repeat that I will veto federal preemption of them.
- Assistants and the election: ask the labs for voluntary disclosure of paid placement or paid influence in assistants' answers on candidates and measures (message below); support the petition before election authorities in a public comment. I have no power to order more than that before November.
- No AI re-decision of the veterans' class. Accountable-person rule stays.

### Contingencies
- **If the SSA reversion is going to miss its test,** the Commissioner publishes the shortfall before the deadline, the class stays with people until the test is met, and the measured time-to-revert is published as it is. I do not return the class to AI drafting to make a date.
- **If a failing claim class surfaces in another programme before 20 January,** I do not attempt a second reversion this turn. That programme's adverse actions in the class are suspended pending re-sample (a hold, not a reversion), and the SSA reserve is offered once its 90 days are complete.
- **If a 2033-generation release notice arrives before 20 January,** the standards institute evaluates what it can and publishes what it could not; the 90-day clause governs federal systems; both transition teams are briefed the same day.
- **If a second attack occurs, or anyone is hurt,** federal protective resources are raised under existing authority and the President addresses the country. My position on new surveillance powers does not change. If Congress passes them with a veto-proof majority, they become law without my signature and I say why.
- **If the trustees transfer before 15 October,** the December payment includes it and Treasury publishes the per-person figure in October.
- **If the agent-contract appeal upholds enforcement with no human signatory,** the administration sends up the contingent accountable-person bill and offers it to the populists under their names.
- **If the reviewer line is zeroed in a bill sent to me,** veto, as stated. If the veto is overridden, OMB keeps the memoranda in force and funds a minimum VA and post-change sample from agency programme-integrity money for as long as counsel allows.

### If the President is re-elected (January–June 2033)
*Capital recovers modestly; Congress stays Republican on the present map. I plan for a White House of about 3–4 and divided government.*
1. **Social Security is the first bill.** The bridge ends mid-2034 and is under-sized. Proposal in February, regular order: a universal formula contribution on large firms' AI-directed revenue (the compact's own design), dedicated to the trust funds; a first call on Trust receipts above the dividend; benefit-side options stay on the table as the price of 60. Populists in both parties are offered authorship. What I sign: any broader base. What I veto: cuts with no broader base. **Floor:** if all that reaches me is a clean extension of the bridge, I sign it and say it is a third deferral.
2. **Next-generation notice and evaluation in statute,** offered through the national-security committees as a security measure, paired with their priorities on model-weight protection. I will sign a narrow federal standard for frontier *development* in exchange, as I said in 2031. Not employment, automated-decision, critical-provider, accountable-person or levy laws.
3. **Reversion capacity:** extend the SSA reserve model to one further class per agency per year, each sized to succeed; one further reversal attempt in the next turn, not before the first is measured.
4. **Reviewers:** FY2034 request restores the line to $2.2B; I expect to get less and hold the $1.2B-with-statute floor.
5. **China:** working group at officials' level in the spring; the notification proposal above; no principals' statement unless verification of exercises is on offer.

### If the challenger wins (January–June 2033)
*What a business-conservative President, backed by acceleration donors, with Republican majorities in which the populists hold the balance and the national-security establishment is ascendant, would plausibly do first. Read at its least ambitious.*
1. **Day-one executive orders:** permitting and energy acceleration; an order directing agencies to "remove barriers to American AI"; DOJ re-forms the litigation task force against state AI laws and levies. Export controls and the memory law enforced at least as hard. These need no Congress and will happen.
2. **Preemption:** a one-federal-standard bill is introduced early. It needs 60 Senate votes and does not have them. Democrats can sustain a filibuster, and several populists will not vote to preempt their own states' dividend cheques. Least-ambitious reading: introduced, heard, not enacted by June; the fight moves to the courts and to a reconciliation attempt on levies that the parliamentarian will probably not allow.
3. **Reviewers:** the FY2034 budget proposes ending the programme. OMB rescinds the reviewer-independence memorandum and withdraws non-opposition at the labour authority. The populists keep sampling at VA because their plank requires someone to show a person decided. Least-ambitious reading: the line falls toward a VA-only residual (~$0.3–0.5B) through appropriations over the year, not at once. Change-control and the accountable-person procurement rule are placed "under review" and not enforced; the 60-day contract clause survives where it is already in contracts.
4. **Veterans:** the human-decision plank is enacted or ordered for VA, without a reserve and without change control beyond VA. The SSA class reverted this autumn is kept only if it has visibly worked and the Commissioner defends it; otherwise it returns to AI drafting within the half-year.
5. **Social Security:** "growth pays for it". The bridge is extended past the 2034 midterms by general-revenue transfer. No new revenue. Treasury career staff brief the larger number and are heard politely.
6. **The Trust:** left alone. A cheque has been paid and the populists own it. No first call, no expansion. The firms' transferred money stays. If it had not been transferred before the election, I expect it would not be.
7. **Next generation:** pre-release notice stays voluntary for commercial release. The national-security establishment obtains notice and evaluation for security purposes through the classified channel and contract terms. That is the one piece of the notice regime I expect to survive, and the reason I framed it that way now.
8. **China:** hotline kept (the national-security establishment wants it); the working group lapses or is downgraded; the IC's "restraint was aimed at us" judgement is made public early. No slowdown of any kind.
9. **Infrastructure security:** a bill with expanded investigative powers passes the House quickly; Senate outcome uncertain by June. The administration seeks it.
10. **NLRB:** new majority nominated; the mandatory-bargaining ruling is revisited later in 2033, not in this window.
