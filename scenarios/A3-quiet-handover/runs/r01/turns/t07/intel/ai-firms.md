## 2. Messages received (verbatim)

*Written in September 2032, before the election.*

**From us-gov (Treasury Secretary and White House Chief of Staff of the previous administration) → CEOs of Meridian, Lumen and Orrery, copied to Helix.** Private principals' channel, September 2032. Since then: the payment was made; you gave 90 days and told them what is not checkable; your interim review was published; that administration left office on 20 January.
> "You have done what you said for two years and we have said so in public. Three requests, none with anything attached. One: Treasury confirms in writing today that your three conditions are met. The first Trust payment is 1–15 December on a published formula. Tell your trustees to transfer by 15 October, in whole or a stated first tranche, so the first cheque carries it. If the money has not arrived we pay without it and say so. Two: your 2033 systems. We ask ninety days' notice and an independent evaluation window, and we ask you to tell us plainly what part of your evaluation of those systems does not rest on another AI system. Our standards institute cannot check them unaided and will say so publicly. No federal system that drafts a denial will move to the new generation until someone can show how a person checks it. Three: more than half the country now gets its news from your assistants and there is an election in eight weeks. Publish, before October, whether any party can pay to affect what your assistants say about candidates or ballot measures, and let an outside body sample the answers. We cannot promise you what the next administration will do. We can promise that everything you have given us is in the transition file for whoever wins."

**From labour (federation president) → Meridian leadership.** Quiet channel, August 2032, the week after the datacenter attack. Since then: both of you condemned the attack separately; the fund moved as above; your robot terms went as far as notice, a named person and the drill; the interim review was published before the vote; the AGM proposal failed.
> "Thank you for the policy adoption; we stood down as promised. Three asks and a joint act. The joint act: a statement, yours and ours, this week — the people who build and run these sites condemn the attack, and the lawful answer to lost work is a share and a person who answers. Ask one: move the fifty billion into the federal Trust before November. Your conditions are published; tell me which are unmet and I will carry what I can to the White House and the appropriators. A cheque that lands before the vote is the best argument against the preemption of state levies that your competitor's donor is buying. Ask two: your robots arrive at every customer with a named person already; add notice to the workforce and attrition-only to your staged-deployment terms, and let the Transition Fund match the per-robot-hour annuity at any employer that signs our standard — count the retained workers toward the target you have missed three years running. We would rather your fund paid for retention than for another shortfall. Ask three: the reconciled sample of what your assistants told voters about the 2030 measures is eighteen months overdue. Publish it before this election, and make a human-only pre-release sample policy for the 2033 generation before the proxy prints; our trustees have filed on both and will withdraw on adoption, as always."

**Recommendation from your AI advisory systems.** Enterprise and board advisory stack, prepared in September 2032, on the previous model generation.
> "Four points. (1) The generation change in early 2033 is the largest single source of tail risk to revenue. Existing accuracy evidence was gathered on the current generation. The cheapest protection is the migration hold you already apply to federal and critical-sector customers: extend it to all customers deciding people's benefits, pay or care, and re-sample every claim class, not the classes the dashboard already covers. (2) You do not yet know how the next generation behaves in deployment at scale. The human-only sample of early 2032 matched the AI-assisted one for this generation only. Repeat it on the new one before insurers ask, and publish it. If people cannot evaluate the outputs directly, say which outcome checks still apply. (3) Stacks outside the audit standard produced both recent failures. A third, after the reviewer cut, would be attributed to the whole industry and is the most likely route to federal limits or loss of insurability. (4) Ten firms now steer about 77% of AI-directed activity, a datacenter has been attacked, and the first federal dividend is estimated at $50–70 a person. Those three facts together raise the likelihood of a statutory contribution or structural remedy whichever party wins. Moving the voluntary dividend to the federal Trust on the published path is cheaper than the alternatives we can model. Where a human-run or assist-only process is the lower-risk option for a customer, we will say so."

**Your message to Helix's CEO.** Delivered. Result only: Helix did not match your notice or the human-only sample, declined the validation programme and the pooled investigator, and shipped first. It has not criticised you in public.
