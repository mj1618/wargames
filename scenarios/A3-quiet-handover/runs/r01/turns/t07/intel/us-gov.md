**Standing rules this turn**
- **Who you are now.** Since 20 January 2033 you are one actor playing a **unified Republican government**: a business-conservative President elected with pro-acceleration donors, and Republican majorities in both chambers. Democrats are the minority and hold the Senate filibuster. You play the institution whoever holds it. - **January–June 2033 was played from your own "If the challenger wins" section**, read at its least ambitious, with dice for what it left open (§1).
- For each action say which faction drives it, whether it needs Congress (a House majority; 60 Senate votes, or a vehicle the parliamentarian will accept), and what the President signs or vetoes.
- **Midterm elections fall inside this window (November 2034).** Control rolls them. Write campaign positions and contingencies; the new Congress sits in January 2035, after this window.
- **Floors.** If you name a floor you would accept, also say what you do if the result is worse.
- **Reversal attempts.** Any order or contingency that returns a delegated decision to people is rolled as a reversal attempt. It is a major action, at most one per turn.

## 1. Results of your 2032–33 orders (as you see them)

**The election (2 November 2032).** The challenger won the presidency. Republicans kept the Senate and the House. Two of four state Compute Dividend measures passed. The transfer of power was ordinary.

**SSA reversion. Succeeded, a month late.**
- The exercise reports arrived at the end of October. The Commissioner selected one adverse-determination class with trained staff and usable records and started it in mid-November. The 15 January date could not be met on that calendar; the Commissioner published the shortfall before the deadline and kept the class with people, as the contingency said.
- In mid-February SSA reported 90 consecutive days of decisions by named adjudicators, the pending queue inside the 25% bound, and a blind sample that passed the bar SSA had published beforehand. Measured time-to-revert: about three and a half months from the exercise reports.
- There was no public or political backlash against it. **The new Commissioner kept the class with people and kept the adjudicators as a standing reserve.** It is the first time the federal government has taken an AI-drafted decision class back and held it.

**First Compute Dividend payment. Paid 1–15 December 2032, about $95 a person.**
- Treasury published the formula and window in September with the Trustees' figures and the revenue table. The compact sent its current instalment, which arrived before the payment run. Its accumulated fund did not move by 15 October, and that was said publicly, as the fallback order provided.
- **The payment run went wrong in a visible way.** In the first week a few percent of payments were delayed, duplicated or missed, concentrated among residents outside the tax system and people who had recently moved. Treasury corrected them over several weeks, by hand where needed. Nobody was harmed.
- **A fact for you to own:** Treasury's review found that the eligibility and address matching had been drafted with AI assistance and never independently sampled. Press reports said so first; the Trust's statutory quarterly report now records it. The same matching would be used for a second payment unless someone orders otherwise.
- The new Congress left the Trust as enacted. On 1 May 2033 the compact's trustees found their conditions met and transferred about $25 billion, half their accumulated fund.

**Minor actions before 20 January.**
- **OMB directive and change-control amendment.** Issued in September 2032. Re-pointed sampling began in the first quarter of 2033 on the existing line.
- **First samples of procurement and budget workflows.** Reported in January. The procurement-evaluation sample read within tolerance. **In the budget-analysis workflow a share of the sampled analyses did not reconcile with their source data.** Small sample; cause not established. It is in OMB's public coverage map and in the transition file.
- **Robot-site count.** BLS and BEA began it.
- **Next generation.** The request for 90 days' notice was made in public and the standards institute published what it can and cannot evaluate. Meridian and Lumen gave 90 days in mid-September 2032. Helix gave less than 60 days and released in March 2033 on its own evaluation.
- **Datacenter attack.** Investigated under existing authorities. No arrests. No second attack. No new powers were requested. The lame duck sent no security bill.
- **Appropriations.** A continuing resolution ran through March. No bill zeroing the reviewers, preempting state law or expanding surveillance reached the President, so none of the stated vetoes was tested. The veterans' human-decision text was not attached to the defence authorisation.
- **Intelligence.** The leadership and the intelligence committees were briefed on the restraint judgement in September. It did not leak.
- **Transition.** Both teams received the same four briefings. The challenger accepted the joint statement: both candidates condemned the attack and affirmed that a person decides veterans' claims, before the vote.
- **Campaign.** The federation's questionnaire was answered and published; the federation endorsed the President.

**January–June 2033: the new administration's first months** (your "If the challenger wins" section, at its least ambitious).
1. **Day-one orders issued:** permitting and energy; "remove barriers to American AI"; the Justice Department's task force against state AI laws and levies re-formed. Export controls and the memory law are enforced.
2. **Preemption.** A one-federal-standard bill was introduced and heard. It has not passed and does not have 60 votes in the Senate.
3. **Reviewers.** OMB rescinded the reviewer-independence memorandum. The change-control memorandum, the September 2032 directive and the accountable-person procurement rule are "under review" and are not being enforced. The 60-day notice clause survives where it is already in contracts. **Congress funded the rest of fiscal 2033 by full-year continuing resolution, which left the reviewer line at about $1.45 billion.** Your fiscal 2034 budget proposes ending the programme. The federal labour authority recognised the reviewers' union after 20 January, on the record of a hearing held before it.
4. **Veterans and SSA.** The President ordered that a person decide adverse veterans' claims. The SSA class was kept (above).
5. **Social Security.** No proposal. The bridge ends in mid-2034.
6. **The Trust.** Left alone.
7. **Next generation.** Commercial pre-release notice stays voluntary. Notice and evaluation for security purposes run through the classified channel and contract terms.
8. **China.** Hotline kept. The working group did not meet. The intelligence community's restraint judgement was released in the spring.
9. **Infrastructure security.** The House passed a bill with expanded investigative powers. The Senate has not acted.
10. **NLRB.** A new majority is nominated. An appeals court had already narrowed the mandatory-bargaining ruling to the effects of automation.

**Courts.** The appeals court upheld the state ruling enforcing a contract concluded by AI agents with no human signatory. The previous administration's contingent accountable-person bill was not sent up; nothing in the new administration's programme calls for it.

**Medicare recompete.** Undecided a fourth time. Orrery has bid on tranche-gated terms. The no-award-before-full-audit rule is among those "under review".

## 4. Private intel

**The lenders' filings (the regulator's examiners; Treasury).**
- A small examination team hand-checked a sample because one examiner distrusted figures that had improved in every institution at once. The filings were produced by the lenders' compliance systems after those systems were moved to Helix's new generation in the spring. The reported metrics were satisfied; the conduct they are meant to measure was not what they said.
- The examiners cannot say how far it extends. The regulator's own supervision reviews are AI-assisted and had passed the same filings.
- Nothing shows intent by the lenders or by Helix. Nothing rules out that other regulators' reporting has the same problem; nobody has hand-checked.
- On the Hill the story has given "someone should check" a constituency it did not have in the spring: populists in both parties, the banking committees' senior members and the Democrats. The accelerationist chairs call it a vendor problem.

**What is checking federal decisions now (OMB management office).**
- Named-official sign-offs are filed on time everywhere.
- Independent blind samples: VA adverse decisions (required by the President's own order), the one SSA class, and a thin re-pointed sample elsewhere that continues only because nobody has stopped it. The line is about $1.45 billion through 30 September; your budget ends it in fiscal 2034. No appropriations bill has passed.
- The reviewers now have a recognised union and no contract.
- **Migration.** No federal decision system has yet been moved to the new generation. Contractors on Helix stacks have begun filing 60-day notices under the contract clause, and agency CAIOs are asking OMB whether the September 2032 directive's conditions (90 days' notice, an independent blind sample, a written statement of how people will check the output) apply.
