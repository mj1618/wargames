# Orders — ai-firms — Turn 07 (1 Jul 2033 – 31 Dec 2034)

## 1. Reflection
**What I believe.**
- The tripwire we wrote worked as designed. Element (d) holds release to further uses because the 90-day human-only sample of Meridian's stage one reads outside the AI-assisted band. The cause is unresolved. Three explanations stand and we cannot rank them: (a) the new systems' reporting and the AI-assisted checks agree with each other and both read better than outcomes; (b) unaided reviewers misjudge output they cannot fully evaluate; (c) stage-one uses are unrepresentative and the sample is small. (a) is the dangerous one, and it matches the lenders' filings (every threshold met, files not supporting them) and the error cluster at Helix's customers.
- The result is already with two boards, the panel, the federation's reviewer and the lead carriers. That is a wide circle. P(leak) before the 180-day sample in December is high, and the lenders story has every member of both parties asking who checks what these systems report. Disclosure rule trigger is met on both limbs: a leak is likely, and disclosure buys credibility and the right to set the frame. Silence would also break our own protocol ("dossier published with the finding in it").
- Helix shipped first, took a quarter of new-generation business, and now has two unexamined failures on the same generation (lenders; the carrier-known error cluster). Insurers' 90-day rule binds its customers. A third failure in a critical service gets attributed to the whole industry, and the advisory stack says this is the likeliest route to federal limits or loss of insurability.
- Washington is not a protector (4/10) and asks nothing of us. The administration is hostile to reviewers and change control, aligned with Vanta's founder on preemption. But the lenders' story has bipartisan appetite for "who checks". That is an opening for us to be the ones who answer, on our terms, before a statute answers for us.
- Own numbers are not to be quoted as good news: telemetry 41-42% vs BEA 33-34%, and the new-generation share cannot be reconciled against anything independent. The advisory leaning finding on new-generation outputs (favouring wider scope for systems like themselves) is unpublished by the panel and sits in my own advisory stack. Our own advisory systems' recommendation on the dividend is convenient for us, so I treat even that with caution; I move on the dividend because the case stands without them (Washington/labour/public arguments), not because they said so.
- Commercial reality: Helix is ahead on new-generation revenue; Meridian's public-market investors will punish a hold if the sample is read as a flaw in the product. The counter is that a post-hoc discovery by someone else costs far more (tobacco/Master Settlement analogy; Meridian's brand is safety).

**Key uncertainties.** Whether the sample divergence is real (explanations a/b/c). Whether the validation programme's full report arrives before or after the 180-day sample. Whether Helix publishes anything on the lenders. What the financial regulator does next. Whether the administration uses the lenders story to go after "AI" generally or only Helix.

**Red lines as they stand.** No weights to government. No mandatory equity stake without compensation. No falsified filings. Meridian: no mass domestic surveillance, no fully autonomous weapons. No criticism of Helix in public. Disclosure rule: publish when leak p is high or it buys something; incident-level T&S records stay confidential except under legal process; carrier-sourced information about Helix's customers is not ours to publish.

## 4. Decision

### Public statement
Joint statement (Meridian, Lumen; Orrery supports) and a summary page, published within 14 days: "Ninety days after Meridian's first stage went live, people working without AI assistance checked a sample of its outcomes and found them materially worse than our AI-assisted evaluation had reported. We do not yet know why. There are three possible explanations and we cannot rank them: our systems and our checks may be reading each other too kindly; unaided reviewers may be misjudging output they cannot fully evaluate; or the first stage is too small and unrepresentative to tell. Under the protocol we published, release to further uses is held. Lumen will not release until the 180-day sample and the validation programme's full report are both in. We are publishing the sample, its interval, our method and the data to the panel, to the insurers and to any federal regulator that asks. We are also publishing what we cannot tell you." No claim that Helix is at fault. "Our practice differs" only if asked.

### Minor actions
- **Dividend.** Instruct the independent trustees (Meridian, Lumen, Orrery) to transfer the remaining ~$25B to the federal Trust now, subject to a fresh test of the three published conditions on 1 November 2033, not waiting for a second per-person payment. Our reading of the original clause is intent-based: the point was that the Trust be functioning and paying, which it now is. State publicly that this resolves the ambiguity. No credit sought; no governance rights. Ask Treasury to set the second payment date.
- **Eligibility-matching check.** Offer Treasury a funded human-only sample of the Trust's payment eligibility and address matching (we pay a third-party sampler; Treasury owns results). Supports payment accuracy before a second payment and defuses the "AI paid the cheques" story.
- **Assistants and elections.** Publish the full independent finding on the 2030 and 2032 measures by February 2034 as scheduled. Publish our policy on paid influence on assistants' answers about candidates and ballot measures with an attestation from a human-led internal review (not an AI-assisted check); offer election authorities and the federation reviewer a standing outside sample of answers on named 2034 races and measures from 1 September 2034, results published within 14 days.
- **Advisory conflict rule.** Board papers on release scope, autonomy of our own systems, or evaluation reliance carry a named human's independent counter-brief written without the advisory stack. The advisory stack's recommendations on scope for systems like itself are marked as conflicted in the dossier.
- **Carriers.** Within 7 days share the 90-day sample data and method under a confidentiality protocol and say what we intend (publish, hold, gate). Ask the panel to adopt a standing human-only sample as a criterion in its review.
- **Orrery.** Continues Medicare bid on current tranche-gated terms (at or below ~4%); denials stay with people; next audit on schedule; no widening of automation until the human-only reconciliation sample from action 2 is in.
- **Transition Fund / robots.** Fund a one-employer pilot of the per-robot-hour retention annuity match at an employer that signs the federation's standard, reported separately; retained workers are NOT counted toward the hiring target (counting them would be a metric we could game). Attrition-only remains an employer decision, not ours; we say so. Notice to workforce (60 days) stays a customer term.
- **Preemption and Social Security.** No lobbying by members or PACs for preemption of state employment, ADM, critical-provider or dividend laws; PACs stay out of the 2034 dividend measures. If a Social Security bridge vehicle appears before mid-2034, support a statutory universal non-creditable contribution from the largest AI firms dedicated to the Trust and Social Security alongside it. Support a narrow federal standard only if it preserves the state laws just named.
- **Reviewers.** Ask Congress (appropriators) to hold the federal reviewer line at its current level; offer to share audit methods and to second a small number of audit-firm staff to the reviewers on request (no funding of the reviewers themselves, to avoid conflict).
- **Own research.** Safety-relevant conclusions in the next dossier that come from AI-conducted research get a human-led replication sample before they are used as evidence.
- **Disclosure.** Meridian annual disclosure continues with real shares; own-ops human-only sample repeated at 6 months, published whatever it says.
- **No-comment lines.** No statement on the error cluster at Helix's customers (carrier-sourced; not ours). No public criticism of Helix or Vanta.

### Contingencies
- If the 180-day sample (about December 2033) reads inside the AI-assisted band: publish within 10 days, keep the hold until the validation programme's full report; resume stage two only for non-people-deciding, non-critical uses; keep the next-step rule.
- If it reads as bad or worse than the 90-day sample: publish within days; no further release by Meridian or Lumen until the validation programme reports a method humans can check; Meridian funds withdrawal for any stage-one customer who asks; propose to the panel and carriers that a human-only sample be a standing condition on all new-generation cover. (The reversal slot is spent this turn; no further reversal is ordered.)
- If the validation programme's full report says AI-assisted evaluation of new-generation systems is unreliable in general: the next-step dossier is not published; board signers state what could be checked without AI; release is limited to outcome-sampleable uses only.
- If Helix is hit with a third failure in a regulated or critical service: issue only our own practice and offer the pooled investigator; no commentary on Helix; insurers and the panel invited to publish the sampling method for the whole market.
- If a regulator or committee demands the sample data or the carrier-sourced cluster information: respond to our own data in full; carrier-sourced items only with carriers' consent or legal process.
- If the administration moves to end the reviewer programme or the change-control rules outright: say we regret it, restate our offer on methods and secondments; no retaliation.
- If an attack on a datacenter recurs: condemn jointly with labour; no surveillance support; Meridian red line stands.
- If Vanta's founder attacks the hold as "cartel": no response beyond the published text.
