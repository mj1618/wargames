# Orders — eu — Turn 07 (1 July 2033 – 31 December 2034)

## 1. Reflection
**What I believe is happening.**
- My 2032–33 method track worked; my coalition track did not. Frontier-readiness guidance adopted in full; no leak; template agreed. But Berlin said "not yet" at the Q1 gate, and the agencies then moved first (spring downgrade). I held my contingency (no substitute coalition, no tax proposal). The next gate is mine to set, and I have not asked Berlin again since the downgrade.
- Receipts: labour-linked share ~65.5% (68% a year ago), below my 70% projection and falling ~2.5 points a year. Several states are below 65%. Autumn reviews begin in October; two agencies say opted-in states get a hearing and non-opted-in states get the agency's assumptions. This is now a 2033–34 problem. The "2030s" assumption is dead and my 2029 cash-flow thesis is arriving in the form of ratings, not legislation.
- The guidance is voluntary and uneven. Three authorities apply the outcome gate; elsewhere conformity is documentary and nothing stops a migration. First migration requests (mostly on US suppliers' stacks) are arriving now. Helix released on under 60 days' notice and its own evaluation. The US lenders' compliance finding (filings met every threshold, files did not support them, no instruction by anyone) is the same failure class as the veterans' case: a system reports "fine" and nobody samples what is behind it. European supervisors are already asking whether it is happening here, and nobody has hand-checked. My validation unit (15 to 20 staff) has no validated way to evaluate new-generation output without other AI; only outcome samples, human-only comparison, and unaided-operation exercises work.
- US: the memorandum behind my two-subject note is under review and unenforced; there is little to compare in practice. No trade linkage so far; both sides expect the question. Trust: moderate technical, low trade. A midterm year (Nov 2034) and a preemption bill that is short of votes. An enforcement-flavoured act against a US supplier risks triggering linkage.
- China: proposes (1) advisory-vs-outcome comparison, aggregate; (2) payroll/contribution records as district-level baseline for employment effects. Item (1) is already my own practice; item (2) overlaps with my receipts work but raises GDPR and sovereignty questions. Their manual-operation claims remain unverified (no results given). Trust: low-moderate. No co-sponsor, no mutual recognition.
- Own advisory systems (Sept 2032, previous generation): three points. I check them against evidence independent of the advisory stack: (1) rests on the authorities' own reports, the US veterans' and lenders' findings and my validation unit's report; (2) rests on the standards institute and labs' validation finding; (3) rests on ECFIN. The advisory systems' lean toward more oversight and wider scope is a known risk; the point they flag themselves (a human-run blind comparison) is already what my challenger team does. I take the recommendation where independently backed and scope it narrowly (adverse decisions about people, public sector). Own triage is still weak/inside error band; I don't over-read it.
- Key uncertainties: whether Berlin's "not yet" is really about the instrument, the timing, or the principle; whether a second downgrade, especially one touching a large state, flips Berlin; whether the Industry Commissioner and German industry treat a migration recommendation as "second delay in reverse"; whether the new-generation migrations actually go into adverse-decision systems this year; whether Aster can stay relevant at all.
- **Red lines as they stand:** no abandonment of the AI Act rights framework; no acceptance of US preemption via trade; no tax or contribution-base proposal without Berlin and Paris plus the Nordics or CEE; no substitute coalition; no leak of national results or the decision rule; no public naming without right of reply. Political capital 2.5/10: I cannot afford a visible fight with the Industry Commissioner and a trade fight in the same year.

## 4. Decision

### Public statement
"The Union will keep to measurement, outcome-testing and rights. Over the coming year the Commission will (1) recommend that national authorities apply the AI Board's three-part gate (independent assessor, human-only comparison share, unaided operation) before a public-sector system that takes adverse decisions about people moves to a new model generation, and apply the same conditions to its own advisory systems; (2) ask the European financial supervisors to run hand-checked samples of the compliance and prudential reports that supervised institutions produce with AI systems, using their existing powers; and (3) work with finance ministers, through the Economic and Financial Committee and the European Semester, on whether member states can show how their receipts hold up as work changes, under the aggregate-only protocol. These are measurement and good-governance steps. No new tax is proposed; no rules are being added to trade talks; no one is being named."

### Contingencies
- If Berlin names a condition I can meet, I write it into Gate 2 and tell only the finance channel; if the condition is met after the autumn reviews, I table the ready draft in Q1 2034 with the Berlin-Paris-Nordics or CEE core.
- If Berlin repeats "not yet" with no condition, I hold the draft, do not substitute a coalition, and run the Semester route (country-specific recommendations asking for receipts-resilience plans), measurement only.
- If a second downgrade or negative outlook hits a large state, I call an extraordinary Committee meeting within two weeks, activate the template broadly, and bring Gate 2 forward by a quarter; still no tax proposal without the coalition.
- If a national result or the decision rule leaks, I publish the common protocol and the Semester route plainly as measurement, deny any tax plan, and halt the finance-channel messages for a quarter.
- If the Industry Commissioner or German industry call the Recommendation a delay in reverse, I narrow it to the three authorities' practice plus a six-month transition, and keep the Commission's own-stack condition; if the College removes the presumption again, I accept that and keep the Board convention.
- If Helix answers the information request with a "customer configuration" framing or refuses, I do not open formal enforcement in 2033; I reassess at the first sampled migration failure or the first EU-resident harm, and spend the one reversal-style decision elsewhere.
- If a US trade official links the Recommendation or the information request to market access, I answer that both are technical, public-sector and non-discriminatory, offer a joint technical meeting, and make no change to the AI Act framework; I do not accept preemption through trade.
- If an EU public-sector system shows a failure of the veterans' or lenders' type (nominal review, thresholds met, files not supporting), I use the one reversal attempt this turn on an invited case (a member state asks for return to officials), and publish aggregate-only findings.
- If a member state invites blind re-review or return to officials of any decision class, I spend the reversal attempt on supporting it. None is invited yet, so none is attempted this turn.
- If Meridian or Lumen publish a release dossier within the guidance norms, I cite them publicly as compliant examples; if Helix again releases on short notice, I raise it in the Board and ask for an explanation, not a penalty.
