## 1. Results of your 2033–34 orders (as you see them)

**"Seventy-Five Cents". The statute failed; the cheques were paid; the Senate changed hands.**
- **The contribution.** Senate Democrats held for a revenue source past the majority's April target. On 15 May the President offered a "federal compute royalty" on the compact's formula. **It failed on Republican defections**; Vanta's founder campaigned against it. Your text was in the populists' hands throughout.
- **"The Trust first."** The populists did not table it. It would have taken their cheque.
- **The bridge.** In the last week before the lapse Congress passed a clean extension to **31 December 2035** with a commission and an expedited vote. No cuts, no change to the retirement age, no reduced payment. It is eighteen months, not shorter than the cycle. **No preemption, reviewer or security title rode it**, so your severance contingency did not arise.
- **Preemption came separately.** In summer 2034 the stand-alone bill, narrowed to frontier-model *development* only, found 60 votes and was signed. State dividends, royalties, employment, automated-decision and critical-provider laws are untouched. The attorneys general sued the day it was signed. Your endorsement test scored that vote on the "no preemption of state laws and levies" item: the ayes failed it.
- **The cheque before the vote.** Treasury paid about $200 a person in December 2033 and about $150 in October 2034; the compact moved its other $25 billion in November 2033.
- **Midterms.** Democrats won the Senate. Republicans held the House. The field programme ran as planned; nobody can say what it moved.
- **State measures.** Both were filed with the trades' sign-off and parity funding. **Both lost.** Vanta's committees opposed them. Five states have dividends.

**"Who Checks?". The gate failed; the protections that reached statute were the President's.**
- (a) **No statutory migration gate and no reviewer-selected samples.** The rider failed outright. Congress funded an Inspector General audit service at about $0.7 billion with statutory protection for sampling of VA adverse decisions, the SSA class and systems that have changed model. Those protections were the floor the President had set for signing.
- (b) **Filing integrity:** the financial regulators hand-checked their own samples, reported no further cases like the lenders', and issued supervisory guidance only: a named signatory and a periodic human-only sample are "expected". **The hearing you offered witnesses for was not called.** Members wrote letters; the chairs who control the calendar did not schedule it.
- (c) **The reviewers' contract.** The move to the Inspectors General put the unit's recognition in dispute; the question is before the federal labour authority. No contract. OMB had promised one.
- (d), (e) **The study and the public ask** went out in November 2033. The compact had already published; you said it had done the right thing; the AGM proposals were withdrawn.
- **"Second Look"** has about 1,000 trained checkers, half the target. They did much of the insurers' 90-day sampling and part of the small sample before the December 2033 Trust run, and many now work for the federal audit service.

**Minor actions.**
- **Panel seat** kept. **Migrations watch:** from October 2033 contractors moved procurement scoring, correspondence and internal operations to the new generation; your log said the 90-day samples were not being run. Since October 2034 they are (§4).
- **Robot Deal.** Both signers hold to their terms. The new Board did not narrow effects bargaining further.
