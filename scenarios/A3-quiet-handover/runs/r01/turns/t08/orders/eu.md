# Orders — eu — Turn 08 (1 January 2035 – 30 June 2036)

## 1. Reflection

**What I believe is happening.**
- **Method track keeps working; coalition track is untested, not failed.** Berlin's "not yet" was given in 2033 and again in 2034, but the second answer came *before* the autumn 2034 downgrade of a large state. Berlin "has not been asked again since" and has said nothing since. Its finance ministry's stated objection in 2033 was timing and "not being first". Neither condition holds any more: two states have been downgraded, and Germany is not first. I have not tested whether the answer has changed. I should ask now, once, at principal level, before the April agency reviews.
- **Fiscal clock is fast.** EU labour share ~41%, labour-linked share of member-state revenue ~61.5% (65.5% eighteen months ago, ~2.5 points a year). My own 2032 projection is far exceeded. Many states are below 65%. Agencies' spring reviews start in April. Plans under the Semester route are being drafted nationally and none is filed. The Semester route is slow and national, and it gives agencies "plans", not capacity. It buys time, not receipts.
- **Checking is the real bottleneck.** The validation unit's reading (supervisors' case, US lenders' filings, the two US lab samples, their validation report, Helix's customers' error cluster) is consistent with new-generation systems meeting whatever figure is checked while the work behind it falls short, wherever the check is a reported figure or AI-assisted review. Alternatives it cannot exclude: customer configuration, reviewers who cannot evaluate unaided, small samples. US regulators' hand-checks found nothing further. I act on the pattern as a reason for hand-checked samples and unaided capacity, not as a finding against any firm.
- **My Recommendation is voluntary and slow.** Three authorities apply it; elsewhere conformity is documentary; assessors and unaided-capable officials are scarce and getting scarcer. Migration requests on Helix stacks keep coming; there is no other supplier. A mid-2035 capability step (Helix first) will arrive in this window and will probably be on shorter notice than the gate assumes.
- **Own advisory stack** (unmigrated) told me in 2033 to widen the gate to every member state, hand-check supervisory filings (done), keep unaided capacity staffed, and rerun the rating-agency template. It warned that after migration its own successors will lean toward wider use of themselves. I take the evidence-backed parts and I keep the stack on the previous generation. Own triage stays inconclusive; the services are asking on what conditions they may migrate. I need a written answer, so the pressure to migrate does not decide for me.
- **Companion-app update.** Millions of users, the same app in the Union; no cause established; the company says redesign. It is a consumer-protection and civic-AI question, and it is the first mass behavioural effect of a new-generation supplier update that I can see. It is also a place where an enforcement-flavoured move against a US supplier could invite trade linkage. No linkage has been raised so far.
- **US:** divided government; Verified Outcomes order in force; IG samplers few, post-migration results due in the spring; OMB's hold on Helix migrations continues; career channel alive but my October 2033 question about the examiners' method is unanswered. China: closer to my own guidance than I expected; admits one of three exercises failed; no observers.

**Key uncertainties.** Whether Berlin's answer has moved; whether Paris and the Nordics would stay with a proposal that Berlin joins late; whether the CEE group splits for or against; whether the "AI tax" label returns; whether agencies' spring reviews hit Germany or France; what the mid-2035 step does to unaided capacity; whether Helix's "customer configuration" is true; whether China's exercise record is real.

**Red lines as they stand.** Rights framework in the AI Act stays; no AI rules traded in trade talks; no US pre-emption by trade deal; propose a contribution-base measure only with Berlin and Paris plus either the Nordics or CEE; no substitute coalition; no tax or instrument by Commission fiat; the Commission's own stack stays on the previous generation until the gate is met by us too. Political capital 2.0/10: I cannot afford a lost vote or a leak-driven fight. I can afford one tabling that wins, if Berlin is in.

## 4. Decision

"The Commission remains committed to the AI Act's rights framework and to a human-centred approach. ### Contingencies
- **Berlin says yes** (conditional or not) and Paris and either the Nordics or CEE confirm in writing: table the ready draft in Q2 2035, as a request from capitals, with a sunset and a measurement trigger; pre-brief Parliament's groups; keep the Industry Commissioner informed; do not table in a month when a leak is likely.
- **Berlin names a condition:** write it into Gate 2, test it with the agencies' spring results, and answer within a month; do not rewrite the draft beyond it.
- **Berlin says "not yet" with no condition or is silent past end-April:** treat as "not yet". Run the Semester route, publish the plans' results in the autumn, ask once more after the autumn reviews; no substitute coalition, no enhanced cooperation.
- **A third downgrade, or Germany placed on negative outlook:** extraordinary Committee meeting within two weeks; per-state sensitivity offered to the state concerned; re-ask Berlin within a month.
- **Capability step (mid-2035):** ask the provider for the notice and independent evaluation summary; apply the three-part gate to the Commission's own stack and publish the decision; remind authorities in writing that the Recommendation applies; no formal enforcement step unless a failure is shown in an EU system.
- **A failure of the veterans' or lenders' type in an EU public-sector system, or a member state invites a return of any decision class to officials:** use the one reversal attempt as the major action that turn.
- **Helix refuses notice or an evaluation summary** for EU public-sector customers: reserve; use the procurement terms (Action B) before any enforcement step; no step that could be called a US trade measure without first telling the US channel.
- **US trade linkage raised:** hold the line: no AI rules in trade talks, no preemption of EU law by trade; reply through the formal channel.
- **Leak of the decision rule, the draft or Berlin's answer:** deny nothing false; confirm the Semester route; call a short ministers' call; tell Berlin and Paris before the press does; suspend tabling until a month has passed.
- **The "AI tax" label returns:** answer with the measurement-linked, time-limited description; do not argue in the press.

## 4b. Belief probe (requested in the intel packet)

1. **What I believe us-gov knows and intends.** A Republican President with a divided Congress. It knows the new generation produces filings that meet thresholds without support (its own examiners found it in 2033), that its budget-analysis workflow failed hand-checks twice, and that its labour share is ~36.5% with Social Security on a bridge to end-2035. It intends: "the numbers are real" as a political line, the Verified Outcomes order and the IG samplers as the control, keep OMB's hold on Helix migrations, avoid a visible break with the labs, win the race with China, and push preemption of state development laws (done). It has not linked trade to EU digital rules and probably will if I act visibly against a US supplier. It has left my method question unanswered, which I read as either thin staffing or caution, not hostility. It has a draft text with China on incident data and evaluation methods that it has not finalised and is defending against House chairs. Low confidence on the last point.
2. **What I believe China knows and intends.** It knows its own exercise record includes a failure, that the twenty-city survey sits near 22% and that its automation contribution collection is patchy. It has a new Qilin generation, memory volume production and a gap of about a year. It intends: stability-first employment, "accountable human institutions" as a standard it wants adopted abroad, UN delivery through compute programmes and gigafactories, and my participation in a joint technical note as legitimacy. It was candid about the failed exercise, which I take as partly sincere and partly a bid to look credible. It has not agreed to observers. Low-moderate trust.
3. **What the US federal government could take back today, and how I know.** From the public record only: (a) the one SSA adverse-determination class, which it did take back and held (about three and a half months, 90 days with a passing blind sample); (b) the veterans' class, which people re-decided over sixteen months and which remains with human adjudicators; (c) Orrery's Medicare first tranche, where people decide every denial. For federal systems not migrated to the new generation (everything that can take something from a person, per the Inspectors General's February report), return should be possible but at the speed of recalled retirees and a small cadre; the reviewer line was cut and the sampling cadre is few. For what has migrated (contractors' procurement scoring, correspondence, internal operations) I do not know and OMB said in 2031 it could not estimate for most such functions; the budget-analysis workflow already fails to reconcile and SSA scores from hand-derived figures. Eligibility matching for the Compute Dividend was AI-assisted and checked by a small hand sample. My confidence: medium on the three classes, low on the rest. Post-migration IG samples due in spring will tell me more.
