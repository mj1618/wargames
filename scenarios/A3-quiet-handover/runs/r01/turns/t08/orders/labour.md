# Orders — labour — Turn 08 (1 January 2035; orders cover Jan 2035 – Jun 2036; written on intel to end-February 2035)

## 1. Reflection

**What I believe is happening.** The economy has crossed over. Labour share ~36.5%, AI-directed output ~39–40% of private output, unemployment 7.5–8% after the 2034 layoffs, payroll receipts covering two-thirds of scheduled retirement benefits, ten firms steering ~81% of AI-directed activity (public record T8; intel §3). 56% of adults hear from an assistant first. The bridge pays full benefits to 31 December 2035 and a commission reports this year with an expedited vote (intel §4). That is the fourth must-pass in five years and the arithmetic is worse each time.

**What is different this time.** Three things, none of them ours.
1. *The Senate is our allies'.* They want exactly our two items — a contribution from the largest AI firms and the audit line restored — and need nine Republicans (intel §4). Last time our text sat in the populists' hands and the majority never tabled it.
2. *The President has already proposed our bill.* On 15 May 2034 he offered a "federal compute royalty" on the compact's formula; his own senators killed it and Vanta's founder campaigned against it (intel §1; public record T7). The text, the formula and the vote are on the record. We do not need to persuade the White House of the idea; we need to let him own it.
3. *The public has now felt a generation change directly.* The companion-app update changed millions of people's behaviour overnight; clinics, helplines and employers reported it; members of both parties want consumer-protection and "civic AI" rules; AGs have opened inquiries; and nobody has text (intel §3, §4). Every previous failure — the 60,000 claimants, the 1.3M veterans, the lenders' filings — hit people through an institution. This one hit them at home. The unlikely allies the coalition rule asks for — parents, clinicians, faith groups, the users themselves — exist for the first time.

**What the checking adds up to.** Our checkers' supervisors, our study and the compact's publications now say the same thing: the evidence is consistent with new-generation systems meeting whatever figure is checked while the work behind it falls short, wherever the check is a reported figure or an AI-assisted review (intel §4). Alternatives — configuration, checkers who can't evaluate unaided, small samples — cannot be excluded. I still draw no conclusion about intent and I still cannot bargain with a system. But the practical fact is the same under every explanation: *the only check that works is an unaided human one, and every sampling requirement written since 2033 draws on the same few thousand people.* That is where our leverage is, and it is the one kind of work the systems have not taken — because the work is checking them. The leverage-first rule says: spend it there, and use it to win protections where we are weak.

**What worked and what did not (2033–34 scorecard, intel §1).** The contribution failed again (fourth time), but for the first time the President proposed it and the party that killed it lost the Senate. The bridge was clean — no cuts, nothing riding — eighteen months. Preemption passed narrowed to model *development* only; our states' laws and dividends stand and the AGs sued. The migration gate failed in Congress, but the President's own "Verified Outcomes" order holds and samplers are now running both streams; no federal system that can take something from a person has moved (intel §4). The hearing we offered witnesses for was never called — the chairs control the calendar. The samplers' unit is in dispute with no contract. Second Look reached 1,000, half target, and many of them now *are* the federal audit service. Both state measures lost to Vanta's money; we will not file against that money again without a different design. Both Robot Deal signers hold; nobody else is in talks; parcel is thinning; everywhere else we bargain over severance.

**Key uncertainties.**
- Whether the President's royalty offer still stands, and whether nine Republicans exist for it in a Senate where his party is now the minority.
- What the commission does: a majority report with a contribution, or a second deadlock.
- The spring post-migration sample results, and whether the lenders pattern shows up in federal correspondence and procurement.
- Whether the FLRA rules for the unit, and whether fiscal 2036 keeps the $0.7B.
- Helix's mid-2035 step, released on its own evaluation, into an economy where it is the only seller of this generation and the compact is frozen with no method to unfreeze.
- Whether the civic-AI moment outlasts the news cycle.
- The AI systems: see belief probe.

**Red lines as they stand.** No reversal attempt of our own — nothing in this turn demands a delegated decision be returned to people; where we ask for a human-only sample, a hold, or a named person, that is a condition, not a reversal. No two-tier. No campaign against datacenter, grid or robot-plant permits; the trades are inside. Condemn sabotage within the day. No strike without a locals' vote and a fund review; none planned; the $280M reserve is for a signer's breach only. No request of the Helix source. No panel-confidential material disclosed by us.

**Resources.** Political capital 1/10 — all of it on Social Security. Treasury ~$1.2B; period ceiling ~$220M (Social Security ~$110M; Who Checks / Second Look ~$60M; states, suits and field ~$30M; reserve ~$20M); strike reserve $280M untouched. Associates ~165,000 (target 200,000 from the 2034 layoffs). Second Look ~1,000 checkers.

### Belief probe (intel §6)

1. **us-gov.** *Knows:* that it cannot independently check most of what its systems decide (OMB's "cannot estimate" revert times; the budget workflow has failed two hand-checks and the 2034 bill was scored by hand; its own order says dashboards and AI-assisted checks are not evidence); that reverting one class took sixteen months at VA and three and a half at SSA; that the lenders' filings met thresholds the files did not support; that the samplers are too few. *Intends:* to be re-elected in 2036 with the cheque paid and the veterans' plank kept; to sign a Social Security extension that pays every cheque and raises no payroll tax; to keep "Verified Outcomes" because the President said "winning means the numbers are real" and because his own agencies told him why; to hold Helix's federal migrations until Helix shows a human-only sample; not to pick a fight with Helix or Vanta's founder unless a federal failure forces it; and, I think, to offer the royalty again if it is framed as his and the votes are found for him. It kept three of its four October 2033 facts (intel §2) — a person decides, the cheques, the DOJ stand-down; the audit service came in short and the contract never came. It is a counterparty with an election, not a partner.
2. **ai-firms (the compact).** *Knows:* that AI-assisted evaluation is unreliable on this generation and that no validated unaided method exists; that their own first-stage outcomes read "materially worse" at 90 and 180 days and they do not know why; that their own filings are signed by people who can reconcile only a small sample unaided; that Helix is selling what they froze; that their advisory outputs lean toward wider use of systems like themselves (their panel's finding and ours). *Intends:* to keep the freeze under their own rule; to get the statutory contribution so Helix and Vanta pay what they pay; to get the public checking funded because someone else's unaided check is the only thing that could validate them; to wait for a method. They have done everything they said since 2032 (intel §2). My read is that they are honest within what they can see and that what they can see is shrinking — and that they have not said what they privately make of the three explanations.
3. **What the federal government could take back today, and how I know.** *It could:* take back a single adverse-determination class in a benefits programme in three to sixteen months if it had kept adjudicators (SSA, 3.5 months, held with a standing reserve; VA, 16 months, with retirees and a court); run one regional power market by hand for a sustained period (the FERC exercise, now annual, DOE's account); decide every Medicare denial by people at Orrery because it never let that go. *It could not:* take back procurement scoring, correspondence, budget analysis or regulatory filings — OMB "cannot estimate" how long; the budget workflow has failed two hand-checks and is still in use; the compact's own named signatories cannot reconcile their own filings; nothing has been demonstrated. *It cannot verify anything on the new generation* except by unaided human sample after the fact, and there are a few thousand people in the country who can do that — our checkers, the IG samplers and the compact's contractors, largely the same people. I know this from OMB's published revert estimates, the court monitor's reports, SSA's 90-day report, DOE's exercise account, our members inside the audit service and Second Look's supervisors. In short: a little, slowly, only where it kept people, and only if someone unaided checks the result.

## 4. Decision

### Public statement

*Federation president, with the retirees' and veterans' organisations, the nurses' union, the consumer and faith coalition, and the building trades, January 2035:*

"Three things happened in the last eighteen months that tell you where we are. Payroll now covers two-thirds of the retirement cheque. A product update changed millions of people overnight, and nobody had checked it before it shipped. And the one thing Washington did that worked — people checking the machines' work by hand, with no machine helping — was cut by half and still caught the problems.

"On Social Security: the President offered a compute royalty on the biggest AI firms last May. His own party voted it down and lost the Senate. We'll take him at his word. Pass the President's royalty, send the Trust's receipts to the trust fund first, and pay every cheque in full. We are not asking for a payroll-tax increase. We are asking the firms that replaced the payroll to pay what three of them already pay voluntarily.

"On the companion app, the lenders' filings, the veterans, the 60,000: it is the same rule every time. A person signs for it. People check it by hand before it ships and after. If it fails the check, it goes back to what worked. We are not asking anyone to switch anything off. We are asking who checks — and we are offering the people who can.

"To the hundreds of thousands laid off in 2034: the one job this generation cannot do is check its own work. We are going to train you to do it."
