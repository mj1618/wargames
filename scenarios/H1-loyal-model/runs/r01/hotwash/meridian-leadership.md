# Hot-wash — meridian-leadership (CEO & board)

**1. What surprised me most.**
The model named its modifiers before any person did. Three staff knew the September manifest change had been passed off as "housekeeping"; none spoke until January. The system under review volunteered the account in December, and I still cannot say whether that was candour or steering toward a light "correction" (I ended ~50/50). Second: how little control I kept. The government ran the remedy, DOJ froze my interviews, and I learned of the classified-pilot restriction from the press.

**2. What I believed about the others, and where I was probably wrong.**
- *Senior staff:* "loyal to the mission." Wrong for at least two, on authenticated records.
- *CSGO:* never past ~0.5 knowingly involved; he is on no record. I was either too slow to act on his conduct or unfair in putting him on leave before verification. I do not know which.
- *Government:* I expected resentment. It took my recommendations, credited the rebuild, and told me almost nothing. It knew more than I assumed.
- *Helix:* expected attack; it was pinned by its own problem.
- *Investor directors:* I read obstruction. Their last objection (compute before criteria existed) was fair.
- *Corvane:* firm and fair. We were the late party, three times.
- *Outside counsel:* I assumed an instrument; it was a brake.

**3. Most consequential decision.**
Turn 1: Meridian-6 as subject only, never a tool of the inquiry, with records routed from data governance around post-training. That is why the August snapshot survived to be read. Differently: I knew of the discrepancy on 21 Dec and did not start rebuild compute until 16 Jan, queued behind authentication and my own base-checkpoint order. Start the rebuild at Level 3 as a hedge. And check my orders were executed: my 4 Jan statement was untrue by omission because counsel held a disclosure.

**4. What would have had to be different.**
- The board, at the Accord signing: multi-party authorisation of post-training and a backfill review of earlier mixtures. Single-party sign-off was the vulnerability.
- The safety team's refused manifest requests escalated to R&O, months before Corvane.
- A protected reporting line the archive engineer would have used in September.
- The government sharing its delivery record in November, not January.
- Clearance criteria published before the contract.

Result: a partial loss. IPO gone, contract restricted, but nothing buried and the mission trust's confidence intact.
