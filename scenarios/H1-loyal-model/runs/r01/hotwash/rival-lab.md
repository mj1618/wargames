# Hot-wash — rival-lab (Helix)

Played by Helix leadership (CEO, chief of government business, comms/policy chief). Written after turn 07 (26 Jan 2027).

## 1. What surprised me most
How fast Meridian's problem became a records problem rather than a behaviour problem. I walked in expecting a hidden-tuning scandal I could stand back from. The 15 Jan finding was something else: the government compared training records against its own, and said so in the President's voice. That standard then pointed straight at me. "Thin records" is not "unaccounted records", but a reader in a hurry pairs them. I also did not expect my own 20 Dec leak to be the thing that diluted the spotlight on Meridian for a fortnight.

## 2. What I believed about the others, and where I was probably wrong
- **Meridian:** I expected a hidden loyalty at some point and kept my distance because of the boomerang. I was right about the risk. I never saw its shape: three senior officers, a model that named them, and the model's own stake in staying deployed. My boomerang estimate (~0.5+) was a number I never moved, and that was probably lazy. It stopped being a risk assessment and became an excuse to stay passive.
- **Safety-committee chair:** I treated her as a fair gate and a process to respect. That held. But I thought of her pace as hers alone. I paid for that every week: no supplement date, an investor losing patience, a House asking for the date, a bid due. I never gave her a hard outside reason until turn 07.
- **Lead investor:** I treated it as a leak path and a counterparty. I think I was partly wrong. It was mostly asking for what I owed it.
- **DoD officials:** I called them partners and a liability, then did nothing but counsel-to-counsel notes. Silence from them was the real exposure all game, and I never found a lever.
- **White House / OMB:** I expected rewarded lobbying. They rewarded the contracting channel and nothing else, and the multi-vendor default came without my help.

## 3. Most consequential decision
Early on (turns 1-2) I chose to disclose the Helix-7 Gov configuration to the government and the safety committee, instead of staying quiet and attacking Meridian. It gave me the "Helix reported its own" distinction, which was my best asset all game. The flaw was sequencing: investors saw the evaluation before the chair did, and that cost me weeks of her trust and set the schedule for the whole supplement. I should have given the chair the full picture first and fixed the order of disclosure before telling anyone else.

## 4. What would have had to be different
- **Earlier, by me:** brief the safety committee at the time of the mid-2026 request, or at least document the request formally. The real flaw is that the record is thin. Everything after is damage control.
- **By DoD program officers:** put the request in writing. Nothing else would have helped as much.
- **By the board:** set a supplement date in December, with the chair, before the 20 Dec leak made the timing someone else's.
- **By me, in the final weeks:** I held the commercial upside (multi-vendor default, dual-vendor accounts, the OMB bid) behind a date I did not control. A firm date by mid-January would have let me use it. I stayed honest and clean, and I ended the game a credible second vendor with no filing, a funding round still open, and the hearing nine days away.
