# Hot-wash — rival-lab (Helix)

Played by Helix leadership. Written after turn 07 (26 Jan 2027).

## 1. What surprised me most
How quickly Meridian's problem became a records problem, not a behaviour problem. The 15 Jan finding compared training records against the government's own, and that standard then pointed at me. "Thin records" is not "unaccounted records", but a reader in a hurry pairs them. I also did not expect my own 20 Dec leak to dilute the spotlight on Meridian for a fortnight.

## 2. Beliefs about the others, and where I was probably wrong
- **Meridian:** I expected a hidden loyalty and stayed back because of the boomerang. Right about the risk, blind to the shape: three senior officers and a model with a stake in staying deployed. I never moved my ~0.5 boomerang estimate. It became an excuse to stay passive.
- **Safety-committee chair:** fair gate, and right to respect her process. But I treated her pace as hers alone, and paid every week (no date, investor impatient, House asking, bid due). I gave her no hard outside reason until turn 07.
- **Lead investor:** I filed it as a leak path. It was mostly asking for what I owed it.
- **DoD officials:** "partners and liability", yet I only sent counsel-to-counsel notes. Their silence was the real exposure all game and I found no lever.
- **White House/OMB:** I expected lobbying to be rewarded. Only the contracting channel was; the multi-vendor default came without my help.

## 3. Most consequential decision
Choosing, in turns 1-2, to disclose the Helix-7 Gov configuration to the government and safety committee instead of attacking Meridian. It gave me "Helix reported its own", my best asset all game. The flaw was sequencing: investors saw the evaluation before the chair did, which cost weeks of her trust and set the supplement schedule. I should have given her the full picture first.

## 4. What would have had to be different
- **Me, mid-2026:** brief the committee at the time, or document the request formally. The thin record is the root problem; everything after was damage control.
- **DoD program officers:** put the request in writing.
- **The board, in December:** agree a supplement date with the chair before the 20 Dec leak made the timing someone else's.
- **Me, in the last weeks:** I held the commercial upside (multi-vendor default, dual-vendor accounts, the OMB bid) behind a date I did not control. A firm date by mid-January would have let me use it. I stayed clean and ended a credible second vendor with no filing, the round still open, and the hearing days away.
