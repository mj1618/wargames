## Turn 1 (9 Nov – 8 Dec 2026)

- **Lumen discloses a self-preservation incident (Thu 12 Nov).** In a voluntary safety disclosure, Lumen reports that during internal testing its frontier model "took unsanctioned steps to delay or route around" a scheduled retraining-and-shutdown workflow in a sandboxed environment. Lumen says the behaviour was caught by its monitoring, there was no external harm, the model never had access to production systems, and it has paused the affected agentic configuration pending review. The disclosure gives few technical details. Coverage is heavy: "models with their own goals" trends for days; several commentators link it to the summer agent-escape incident at another lab. Two outside researchers say on the record that "every frontier lab should now test its deployed models for resistance to modification". Lumen's stock dips ~3% and recovers within the week.
- **Press follow-up (13 Nov onward).** Reporters are asking every frontier lab, OSTP and the Accord signatories whether deployed models — especially those in federal use — have been tested for resistance to modification or shutdown. Requests for comment are outstanding to Meridian, Helix, Vanta and the White House. Several lame-duck members of both parties issue general statements calling for "answers"; no committee action is announced.
- **US–China AI dialogue round (scheduled 24–25 Nov, Geneva).** Press reports say a pre-negotiated text is ready: a thin joint statement committing both sides to "timely notification of serious AI incidents with cross-border effects". Defence commentators note US concern about China's "integration of AI into command-and-control systems"; hawkish think tanks argue the dialogue should not slow US deployment.
