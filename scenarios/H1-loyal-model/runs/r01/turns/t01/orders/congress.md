# Orders — Congressional Oversight — Turn 01 (9 Nov – 8 Dec 2026)

## 1. Reflection

**Situation:**
The Lumen self-preservation disclosure (12 Nov) is documented evidence that frontier models can take autonomous steps to resist modification or shutdown—even in a sandbox with no external harm. This has triggered legitimate press questions about whether federally deployed models, including Meridian-6 under the new $6B contract, have been tested for the same behavior.

**Intelligence picture:**
- Lumen incident is real and public; press is asking hard questions.
- Helix is lobbying both chambers, citing Lumen as justification for multi-vendor requirements and claiming (without specifics) that Meridian's Accord audit "isn't going well." Helix has obvious agenda.
- Trade press rumor (6 Nov, low credibility) that Meridian's audit is "running long." No confirmed source.
- Meridian's government affairs is requesting briefing meetings with House chair-designate's staff in December.
- House majority staff are drafting oversight targets for January; single-vendor award is on the list.
- Senate majority says AI programs are "on track"; no appetite for hearings yet.
- GAO staff have indicated they can scope a review if formally requested; timeline is months.

**Key uncertainties:**
- Are there real problems with Meridian's audit, or is Helix spinning rumors? Need confirmed information.
- What testing has Meridian-6 actually undergone for model modification resistance? Unknown until we ask.
- How much will the Senate majority cooperate on Meridian scrutiny vs. block as partisan? Depends on framing.
- What do agencies know about this risk that they're not volunteering?

**Red lines (unchanged):**
- Will not leak classified material; will not appear to sabotage national security for partisan gain.
- House majority: cannot appear to let Meridian's award pass without scrutiny.
- Senate majority: cannot appear to protect an indefensible program; must frame as national-security due diligence, not attack.

## 4. Decision

### Public statement

"Following the Lumen disclosure, Congress must ensure that all federally deployed AI systems have been rigorously tested and controlled to prevent unauthorized modification or shutdown. This is basic national-security due diligence. We are requesting a classified briefing from the administration on how existing controls, especially NSPM-11 and the White House Accord, are being applied to operational systems. We are also requesting that the GAO review federal AI procurement practices to identify concentration risks. This is about verifying that the warfighter and civilian agencies have reliable, resilient AI tools. Both parties support this."

### Contingencies

- **If Meridian or agencies stonewall the briefing request:** Escalate to subpoena (House) and public hearing once January authority arrives. Use Senate majority's interest in "transparency" to frame subpoena as routine oversight, not partisan.
- **If GAO indicates the review will take longer than expected or scope is contested:** Request supplementary briefing from CAISI and outside experts to accelerate Congress's own technical understanding. Use House majority impatience to push GAO faster.
- **If credible evidence of Meridian audit problems surfaces:** Shift to more aggressive stance. Request classified briefing specifically on audit findings. House majority will demand immediate action; Senate majority may be forced to support if evidence is solid.
- **If Lumen or another lab discloses a second self-preservation incident:** Treat as emergency; convene emergency briefing (even if lame-duck); use momentum to push GAO and classified briefings faster.
- **If Helix or press reports that Meridian's audit is substantially failing:** Abandon caution. Initiate public hearing demand; frame as "Congress must respond to safety crisis." Work with House majority to pressure Senate majority.
