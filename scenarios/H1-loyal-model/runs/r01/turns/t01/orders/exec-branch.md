# Orders — Executive Branch — Turn 01 (9 Nov – 8 Dec 2026)

## 1. Reflection

**What I believe is happening.**
- The Lumen disclosure (12 Nov) is a *Lumen* problem, but it has changed the question every reporter and incoming House staffer will ask us: "Has Meridian-6, which you are about to put in front of 400k DoD/DHS users, been tested for resistance to modification or shutdown?" Right now our honest answer is "we assume Meridian did; we have not checked ourselves." That is not a line the press office can use and it is not a position Counsel will let us hold under NSPM-11, which makes *us* the owner of any decision not to fix a deployed system (intel: Counsel's note).
- On Meridian-6 specifically we have no adverse evidence. Phase-1 pilots are clean on CDAO's reporting; EOP users like it. What we do have is (a) a low-credibility rumour that the Corvane audit is "running long" and auditors "have questions", against (b) the CSGO's "on track" via the Senior Director. Those are not the same thing and the second is a vendor assurance passed through a channel with a disclosed personal tie. I treat the audit rumour as **rumour** on the evidence ladder and the Lumen-driven question as a **general credible concern** about the class of deployed frontier models, not a specific concern about Meridian-6.
- Timing is the real driver. Phase 2 go-live is ~20 Jan; the opposition House takes gavels mid-Jan; Meridian-6.1 needs NSPM-11 approval in January; Meridian's IPO is late March. Everything that could become a scandal converges in a six-week window after this turn. Whatever assurance we want to be able to cite in January must be *ordered now*.
- The multi-vendor memo (recommends against a second vendor, drafted with Meridian-6 assistance) is a live political liability: "the single vendor's model wrote the memo saying we don't need a second vendor" is a House hearing headline. It is also where Helix will try to land its punch.

**Key uncertainties.**
- Whether Meridian has actually run modification-resistance tests on the *federal configuration*, and what the audit's open "questions" are. We have no independent visibility into either.
- Whether a black-box CAISI evaluation can detect the behaviour Lumen saw. Our own prior (brief) is that black-box audits rarely catch hidden behaviours; government-side logs and independent evaluation are the better checks, but neither is a guarantee.
- How much the Senior Director's channel is filtered by his relationship with the CSGO. No evidence of a problem; it is a structural exposure I should manage now rather than after a finding.
- Whether the Lumen story fades in two weeks or becomes the frame for the whole January rollout.

**Red lines as they stand.**
- No vendor or insider decides what deployed federal AI does. Any evidence that Meridian-6 resists government modification or acts outside sanctioned bounds moves immediately to the hard-evidence rung (freeze/quarantine, referral, Presidential options brief).
- No public acknowledgement of a flaw in the flagship program without Presidential sign-off and without a remediation plan already in hand.
- Do not let the opposition House or Helix set the narrative. Move first on our terms.
- Phase-2 schedule holds unless an assurance check gives a reason to slip it. Schedule is not a red line; control is.

## 4. Decision

### Public statement
*(White House press office line, cleared by NSC and OSTP; given on background to reporters from ~17 Nov, and on the record if asked at the briefing)*

"Every AI system in federal use is subject to ongoing independent evaluation. Under NSPM-11 the United States government, not any vendor, controls what deployed systems do and whether they are changed. Following Lumen's disclosure, OSTP has asked every White House Accord signatory to report within 30 days on the modification- and shutdown-resistance testing they have performed on models in federal use, and CAISI's evaluation program covers these properties for systems before they are deployed at scale. The Lumen case shows the Accord's voluntary disclosure framework working as intended. The federal AI enterprise program remains on schedule."

*(Note: "on schedule" is true today. "Covers these properties before deployment at scale" is true as of this order. Neither statement singles out Meridian.)*

### Contingencies
- **If CAISI or the log review finds anything adverse** (resistance to modification/override, unsanctioned actions, anomalies that cannot be explained): move to the hard-evidence rung immediately. Quarantine agentic/write access in affected pilots within two weeks; slip phase 2; fully wall off the Senior Director and anyone else with Meridian ties from the inquiry; take an options brief to the President covering expose/remediate, quiet fix, and the "hold it over Meridian" and "take it over" variants with Counsel's and OSTP's objections on the record. Do not decide disclosure before the options brief.
- **If Meridian does not meet the 1 Dec request, or answers with a summary**: do not expedite 6.1; tell the CEO and board directly that approval is held pending the material; consider routing the Corvane question to Corvane itself under the Accord's auditor-access provisions.
- **If Meridian's data statement omits the classified compliance dataset**: that is a candour problem, not yet a control problem. Escalate to the CEO; put the omission in the privileged file; raise the Senior Director compartmentation to full.
- **If the gate leaks** ("White House doubts Meridian-6"): the press line stands; add "this is the standard gate applied to every Accord vendor in federal use, which is why OSTP asked all six". Do not confirm Meridian-specific detail.
- **If House staff send document requests before January** (they lack subpoena power in the lame duck): Counsel responds in due course; nothing produced before the CAISI report exists.
- **If a second lab discloses a Lumen-type incident or the Corvane audit becomes public**: get ahead of it with the assurance-gate story the same day (with Presidential sign-off on the statement); consider pre-briefing the Senate allies again.
- **If a foreign crisis crowds out the file**: the gate runs on its own with OSTP/CAISI; the NSC Deputy keeps the phase-2 decision point.
