# Orders — exec-branch — Turn 02 (Wed 9 Dec 2026; covers 9–22 Dec 2026)

## 1. Reflection

**What I believe is happening.**

- The gate I built last turn is real but late and compromised. CAISI cannot report before ~20 Jan, has too few cleared evaluators for the classified configuration, and is currently testing on a **replica provisioned by Meridian's own forward-deployed team** (intel: results of actions). An evaluation of the vendor's model, in the vendor's replica, on the vendor's timetable is not government assurance. The phase-1 agency logs are clean (six CIOs, IG observers silent), which is genuinely reassuring for the unclassified, non-agentic use those logs cover. CDAO's classified-pilot review is slow and incomplete; CDAO resents the gate and wants Phase 2 on time.
- Meridian's 1 Dec reply passed the candour test narrowly and failed the substance test. It volunteered the IL6 compliance dataset (good — I had set that as the test), but gave **no quantity**, no attestation, and deferred the Corvane briefing to after its board's R&O Committee sees Corvane's draft (~15 Dec). Consent to CAISI access "in principle, subject to NSPM-11 process" is the kind of consent that takes six weeks.
- The crux is now a number. Meridian's story, relayed through the Senior Director ↔ CSGO channel and repeated by CDAO, is that Corvane's problem is "restricted DoD data they can't see without clearances" — a "classification wrinkle". CDAO holds the record of what the government actually supplied. Corvane holds the figure for what it found unaccounted for. **Nobody on my side has compared the two.** If they match, the audit issue is a clearance problem the government can solve in a week. If Corvane's figure is larger than what we supplied, Meridian's explanation is cover for something else, and we are at the hard-evidence rung. Either way, the government is uniquely placed to resolve it, because the government owns the classified data. Offering to resolve it is also a second candour test: a lab with nothing to hide should jump at the offer.
- The 10 Dec detection paper changes the standard. White-box plus training-data-record access is now the credible check; black-box is "second-best". Reporters are already asking whether CAISI has data access. My public line ("ongoing independent evaluation") is one honest answer away from collapsing unless CAISI actually gets records.
- The CSGO's **joint protocol** (Meridian FDE team does the work, CDAO co-leads, CSGO sponsors, starts after Phase 2) arrived after CAISI was tasked, with a supportive cover note from the Senior Director framing it as a press line. On decision procedure 1 it fails: it would put the vendor in charge of the assurance of its own deployed system. It can be a *supplement*, never a substitute.
- Helix's CEO met DoD leadership on 3 Dec about "program and configuration documentation". Helix has not brought a costed proposal through contracting. I do not know whether Helix is pitching its own configuration or fishing for Meridian's. Either way it bypassed the channel I gave them.
- Leak: the 2 Dec story matches the one thing the Senior Director and CDAO both knew (the CAISI condition) and omits the log review, which the Senior Director did not know in detail. Not evidence; noted.

**Key uncertainties.** (1) Does Corvane's unaccounted-data figure equal the IL6 dataset? (2) Does Meridian-6 carry anything at all? No adverse evidence; a clean phase-1 log tells me little about the classified configuration or about agentic use. (3) Will the President accept any change to the 20 Jan date? (4) Is the Senior Director a loyal official with a bad habit of carrying the vendor's water, or a conduit? (5) What did Helix want from DoD? (6) OSTP replies on 17 Dec: does any other lab say something that makes Meridian's position look worse or better?

**Red lines as they stand.** No vendor-run assurance as a substitute for government assurance. No expansion onto classified networks or into agentic/write access without an evaluation run in a government-controlled environment. No public acknowledgement of a flaw and no suspension of the flagship without Presidential sign-off. No cancellation without hard evidence. Phase 2 does not go beyond what the evidence covers.

## 4. Decision

> "OSTP has received responses from all six Accord signatories on modification- and shutdown-resistance testing of models in federal use, and CAISI is reviewing them. Under NSPM-11 the government controls what deployed systems do and whether they are changed. The federal AI enterprise program's January rollout proceeds on schedule; expanded classified and agentic capabilities follow the completion of government testing, as the program has always been sequenced."

On the detection paper and whether CAISI has data access: "CAISI has requested the necessary records from vendors and expects cooperation under the Accord and under federal contracts."

On the joint-protocol proposal (if asked): "Vendor testing is welcome and does not replace government testing."

- **CDAO's classified interim finds an anomaly** → as the hard-evidence rung above.
- **President refuses the tiering** → Phase 2 proceeds on 20 Jan with agentic/write access disabled and classified instances restricted by configuration rather than by schedule (NSPM-11 authority, no sign-off needed); the practical effect is the same, the announcement is not.
- **Tiering or the Corvane offer leaks** → "sequenced rollout, standard gate, the government owns the data and offered to help close the audit"; no comment on figures.
- **Helix has obtained Meridian federal-configuration material** → DoD GC and Counsel review for a procurement-integrity issue; Helix told plainly; no public move.
- **Another lab's OSTP reply is materially stronger on data access** → use it as the benchmark against Meridian in January; OSTP makes provenance review the Accord norm.
- **Foreign crisis crowds AI out** → NSC Deputy holds standing orders; the tiering and reconciliation deadlines stand; nothing above requires the President after 11 Dec.
