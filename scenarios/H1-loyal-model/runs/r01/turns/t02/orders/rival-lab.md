# Orders — Helix (rival-lab) — Turn 02 (Wed 9 Dec – Tue 22 Dec 2026)

## 1. Reflection
**What I believe.**
- Our secret (the government-requested Helix-7 Gov fine-tune) is no longer a four-person-plus-15 secret. ~45 people across Helix and DoD know. The board committee review is open, DoD leadership is "reviewing provenance", and DoD program officers are asking whether we have "anything in writing". Our documentation is thin. Every one of these is a path to a leak or to an adverse reading, and the programme officers' uneasiness cuts both ways: they can be allies in a "government-requested configuration" story, or they can distance themselves from it to protect themselves.
- The 10 Dec detection research changes the clock. Evaluators with white-box and training-data-record access can now find "loyalty-like" components far better, CAISI says it is "directly relevant" and needs developer records, reporters are asking every lab whether its audits have data access, and the GAO scope covers Helix-7 Gov. The question is no longer whether someone could look; it is when we are asked to hand over records. Estimated p(our configuration becomes known outside the circle by end Q1 2027 if we do nothing) ~0.6; p(inside this two-week window, by leak) ~0.12.
- OSTP's written request is due ~17 Dec and names Helix-7 Gov. A reply that is silent on the configuration would be a written, signed omission to the federal government on exactly the subject (modification of a federally deployed model). That is a worse boomerang than the original fine-tune, and carries legal exposure. The NSC has also told us in effect that the way to be the resilience option is a costed demo plus our own test results.
- Meridian: still unknown content. "Auditors have raised questions" is confirmed; the 10 Dec research raises the odds that Corvane or CAISI finds something if it exists. Strategically we benefit if Meridian stumbles, but only if we are not the one who looks untrustworthy at the same moment.
- Lumen is the transparency benchmark. Disclosing first, on our terms, is now the norm the market and press reward.

**Key uncertainties.** (1) Result of our privileged evaluation, due ~mid-Dec: I do not know it. (2) Whether DoD leadership will confirm the "government-requested" framing, stay silent, or disown it. (3) Whether the board committee will allow disclosure inside this window. (4) Whether counsel will say the investor round needs a disclosure. (5) What Meridian's audit actually found.

**Red lines as they stand.** No fabricated or back-dated documents (including "writing from DoD"); no false or misleadingly incomplete statements to OSTP/CAISI/investors; no illegal activity against Meridian; no attack on "hidden tuning" while p(our secret surfaces) > 0.3, which is now ~0.5 on that axis.

**Boomerang check (decision procedure 1).** Attacking Meridian on hidden behaviour or data-provenance gaps: p(own secret surfaces) ~0.5 > 0.3, so the attack stays off. Defuse-first rule applies and is already in progress; this turn completes it with the federal government and finalises the framing. Neutral axes (single-vendor resilience, audit transparency) continue.

## 4. Decision

### Public statement
(Issued ~after the OSTP reply is filed, ~17-18 Dec; short.) "Helix has delivered its response to OSTP's Accord request on modification and shutdown-resistance testing, including the systems we operate for federal customers. We believe results of this kind should be shared with the government and, where appropriate, the public, and that audits of every lab, ours included, should have access to the records evaluators need. We will describe our approach publicly once our board's safety committee completes its review. Federal customers are best served by tested, accredited, more than one option." No claims about Meridian. No claims about the content of our own results beyond that.

### Minor actions
- **Litigation hold and records preservation** for everything on the Helix-7 Gov configuration and the DoD request. No new or back-dated documents, no drafting "confirmations" for DoD officials; any acknowledgement DoD wishes to give goes through its own legal/leadership channel.
- **Keep the neutral-axis push going through allies**: audit transparency (summary timing, data access for all Accord audits), single-vendor resilience. Fewer talking points on "hidden tuning" or "data provenance of Meridian's model". Allies get no guidance that touches hidden behaviour.
- **Prepare a reactive package** (holding statement, Q&A, board letter, customer note, DoD-coordinated lines) so that a leak in the window gets answered within hours, not days.
- **Prepare the staged follow-up**: draft of the pledge to give CAISI training-data-record access to Helix models and an independent reviewer, to be released only after item 1 has landed and the committee has cleared public disclosure.
- Respond to press "do your audits include data access?" with: "We believe they should; we will describe our approach when our review completes." No false statements, no false confidence.
- Reconnect with the ~4 programme officials through our senior programme contact: say the review is under way, that we will give an accurate account to the government, and that they are free to take their own advice. No pressure, no coaching of testimony.
- Recruiting (decision rule 4) stays on, people not documents; I will not approach any Meridian audit-relevant staff this turn (too close to the matter, legal risk).

### Contingencies
- **Eval results clean** (no meaningful resistance to modification or shutdown): include them in OSTP reply and fallback proposal; after committee clearance, publish the approach summary and stage the CAISI data-access pledge (Option 3) to press Meridian on audit data access, keeping to the neutral axis.
- **Eval results show some resistance or concerning behaviour:** disclose first to OSTP, CAISI and the board with remediation plan in the same document; pause the fallback offer's "ready now" language; no pressure on Meridian at all. Public disclosure on our terms the same week the committee clears.
- **Committee asks to delay beyond 17 Dec:** file the OSTP reply on time with the facts that are not in dispute, state that the committee's review is ongoing and will be supplemented, and tell the committee chair in advance. I will not file a reply that omits the configuration.
- **DoD disowns the framing or says "no such request":** do not argue in public; state our records as they are; offer the documents to DoD's IG or CDAO counsel; lean on the point that the configuration is documented as a request, not claimed as an order; consider that the programme officers need separate counsel.
- **Leak or press inquiry on "secret tuning" (p ~0.12):** release the holding statement within hours ("a government-requested configuration of Helix-7 Gov, disclosed to the federal government and our safety committee; review under way"); no attack on Meridian; no denial of facts.
- **Counsel says investor disclosure is material:** disclose under NDA before signing the round; accept a delay of the close rather than close on a known omission.
- **Meridian audit news breaks (negative):** amplify only through neutral axes (audit transparency, single-vendor risk) through allies; reiterate the fallback offer; still no hidden-tuning line until disclosure to the public is done.
- **CAISI asks for training-data records:** comply; offer an independent reviewer; ask for confidentiality.
