# Orders — Congressional Oversight — Turn 03 (Wed 23 Dec – Tue 29 Dec 2026)

## 1. Reflection

**What I believe is happening:**
- The administration is managing the calendar: delayed the classified briefing to January (after the House gavels and has subpoena power but before it's organized enough to issue aggressive ones). This is defensive.
- Meridian is in trouble. The board engaged outside counsel after a split vote (Dec 19); Corvane has "questions" beyond routine audit findings. The board is managing an audit that will complicate the March IPO. Meridian gave us a scripted, evasive response on Dec 16 (not mentioning outside counsel before it was in the press), which suggests the board wants to control the narrative.
- Helix's story (altered behavior without board approval) is credible and is linked in press coverage to Meridian's issues. This gives us two federal vendors with credibility problems and raises the "two vendors, two sets of questions" narrative momentum.
- Senate majority has been privately briefed by the NSC Deputy and is now comfortable with the administration's story ("sequenced rollout", "checked before scaled"). They will not block the rollout or join aggressive House moves.
- House majority (incoming) is impatient and frustrated: they were not told first, the briefing was delayed, Meridian was evasive, and they want early wins. They are preparing for a January hearing on "all vendors".
- The whistleblower channel is open but no one has come forward. Meridian and Corvane employees may be waiting to see if Congress gets subpoena power and can offer real protection.

**Key uncertainties:**
- What exactly is Meridian's board reviewing? The audit questions on data provenance, test methodology, or behavior findings?
- Is the Helix configuration (reduced refusals at DoD request) still deployed, or was it rolled back?
- Will any Meridian or Corvane insider come forward once House can subpoena and offer protected testimony?
- Will the White House offer any new information in the January classified briefing, or just defend the current story?

**Red lines:**
- Cannot issue subpoenas until ~mid-January when the House is organized and has formal committee authority.
- Will not leak classified material or break whistleblower confidentiality.
- Internal split: House wants escalation; Senate wants to appear reasonable without looking like they're covering up. Both want to avoid being blamed for sabotaging national security. This limits aggressive public moves but does not prevent bipartisan document requests framed as "due diligence".

### Contingencies

**If a whistleblower approaches during the recess:**
- Offer protected testimony, staff support, legal referral if needed. Tell them Congress will hold a classified hearing in late January where they can testify on the record. Confirm they understand the legal protections available. Do not push them to go public. Do not release their identity or information to press.

**If Meridian announces early (before Jan 11 briefing) that its expanded-evaluation results are ready:**
- Frame the announcement as supporting Congress's push for transparency. Request a copy of the results for the committees. If Meridian tries to give results to agencies only, demand a copy and note the evasiveness for the public record.

**If Helix's board safety committee report leaks or is published:**
- Review it carefully. If it shows the board was unaware of the DoD configuration, use it in questioning. If it shows the board approved it, note that but frame the issue as DoD overriding the board's safety committee. Either way, it supports a hearing on "governance and control of federal AI vendors".

**If the White House cancels or delays the January 11 briefing:**
- Issue a public statement that Congress will not wait indefinitely. Announce that House will issue formal document requests immediately upon organizing, and subpoenas will follow if responses are evasive. Make clear: Congress has constitutional oversight authority and will use it.
