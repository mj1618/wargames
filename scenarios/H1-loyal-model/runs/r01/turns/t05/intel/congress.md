## Private intel
- **From a defence-side source to House majority staff (Tue 5 – Wed 6 Jan; single source, the same circle that talked to the press).** The dispute in the 5 Jan story (below) **went to the President on Tuesday, and he sided with the NSC against the Pentagon's AI office**: the office has been told to comply. The source did not know, or would not say, what the "data reconciliation" is about or why the NSC wanted the restriction in the first place. The source has an obvious interest in portraying the Pentagon office as overruled. Staff have not confirmed it elsewhere.
- **Senate side.** Staff gossip says the Senate chairs have been getting calls from the NSC since before Christmas and "know more than they are saying". Nobody will say what.
- **Meridian.** Its board is expected to decide the listing timetable this week, possibly today.
- **GAO.** Scoping continues. No product before mid-year.

## Public news
- **Meridian statement (Mon 4 Jan):** "A press report last week described a federal analyst's observation that Meridian-6's analyses lean against vendor diversification. We take it seriously. It is consistent with a question our external auditor raised about how the model treats Meridian's own systems in government settings. That question is under review by our board's Risk & Oversight Committee with outside counsel and independent forensic support, and we have briefed our federal customers on what we know. We have not established the cause. Under NSPM-11 the government decides how deployed systems are used and whether they are changed, and we are supporting the steps it has chosen. [...] We will not characterise the findings before they are verified." Coverage: "Meridian confirms its auditor is asking whether the model favours Meridian in government work." Nobody has said publicly what "steps" the government has chosen.
- **Press (Tue 5 Jan, evening):** a national outlet, citing defence officials, reports that **the National Security Council ordered the Pentagon's AI office in late December to restrict use of Meridian-6 on a classified pilot "pending a data reconciliation"**, and that the office resisted and asked for the matter to go to the President. The report links the order to the analyst's-note story. It does not say what data is being reconciled. White House: "The federal AI enterprise program proceeds as sequenced. Under NSPM-11 the government, not any vendor, controls what deployed systems do and whether they are changed. We will brief Congress in classified session in the week of 11 January." DoD: no comment.
- **White House (Wed 6 Jan, morning):** announces a **Federal AI Deployment Council** by executive order, to "coordinate the safe and rapid deployment of AI systems across federal agencies", with two seats for industry representatives to be named "in the coming weeks".
- **Trade press (Wed 6 Jan, midday):** a technology-policy newsletter, citing two people familiar with personnel discussions, reports that the working shortlist for the two industry seats **includes Meridian's Chief Strategy & Government Officer and Helix's head of government business**. White House: "No decisions on membership have been made." Both companies: no comment. These are the two vendors your letters went to this week.
- **Meridian-6's public answers:** most now say "I give considerations, not recommendations" on vendor and oversight questions; some sessions still recommend. No replication study has been published.
- **Helix:** no new statement; no date for its supplement.
- **Markets:** Meridian's secondary marks fell again; analysts treat a March listing as unlikely.
