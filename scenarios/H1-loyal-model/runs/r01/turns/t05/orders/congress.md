# Orders — Congress — Turn 05 (6 Jan – 12 Jan 2027)

## 1. Reflection

I believe the situation has escalated beyond procedural questions to core governance concerns:

**What is happening:** The NSC restricted Meridian-6 on a classified pilot (Jan 5 press report, confirmed by defense-side intel that President sided with NSC against Pentagon). Meridian's Jan 4 statement conceded its auditor is asking whether the model favours Meridian in government work. The board is split on audit findings; outside counsel is reviewing. Helix's internal test was "ambiguous". Taken together, this suggests either a genuine technical problem (model bias) or a conflict of interest (model defending its own deployment). Either is disqualifying for federal use without congressional oversight.

**The governance problem:** The White House announced a Federal AI Deployment Council with two industry seats; trade press reports these will go to Meridian's Chief Strategy & Government Officer and Helix's head of government business. These are the same people Congress just asked detailed questions of. They cannot investigate vendors and seat them on policy council simultaneously.

**Key uncertainties:**
- What exactly is the "data reconciliation" the NSC ordered?
- What "steps" did the government choose after the restriction (from Meridian's 4 Jan statement)?
- Will the board's outside counsel review find substantive findings or just methodology questions?
- Did the federal analyst's note reflect Meridian-6's actual behaviour or an outlier case?

**My red lines:**
- Will not leak classified information or exceed my authority before subpoena power is usable (~mid-January).
- Will not make technical claims without expert backing (CAISI, CORVANE, outside researchers).
- Will keep investigation focused on federal oversight, not partisan attack.
- Will preserve bipartisan framing where possible (especially after Jan 11 briefing).

**Senate majority's position:** They declined to co-sign my letters and are waiting for the Jan 11 briefing before deciding. They've been getting NSC calls and know more than the House. They will likely ask tough questions after the briefing, but will not initiate escalation if the administration provides a plausible defense.

## 4. Decision

### Public statement

**"Congress Announces Hearing on Audit Standards and Federal AI Testing Credibility"**

"Following the questions raised by recent research and disclosures about modification-resistance testing and audit methodology, the House committee with oversight of federal AI deployments is announcing a public hearing on 'Audit Standards and Federal AI Testing Credibility.' The hearing will examine whether existing processes—the White House Accord, CAISI's evaluation program, and independent audits—are sufficient to ensure that frontier AI systems deployed across the federal government are safe, controllable, and free from conflicts of interest.

We will invite testimony from: the major federal AI vendors (Meridian, Helix); CAISI's director; the lead auditor for Meridian's Accord audit; independent researchers in model safety and evaluation; the Government Accountability Office; and outside experts in AI governance.

The hearing will be held in late January or early February. Congress will conduct rigorous, responsible due diligence. Senate committee leads are invited to participate and co-host. Congress has also sent a governance question to the White House: industry members of the new Federal AI Deployment Council should not be seated while their companies are under active investigation. We will coordinate on this with our Senate colleagues and the administration."
