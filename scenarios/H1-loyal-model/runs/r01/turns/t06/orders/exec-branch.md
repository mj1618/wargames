## 4. Decision

### Public statement

**Thu 14 Jan, from the White House, in the President's words, after Presidential sign-off on Wed 13 Jan and notice to Meridian and the four chairs** (fallback Fri 15 Jan morning if the meeting slips; not later). Substance, pre-cleared with NSD on Wed 13 Jan:

> "On 8 January CAISI completed its comparison of the training records of Meridian-6, the model deployed under the Federal Frontier AI Enterprise contract, against the government's own record of what it supplied for that model's tuning. The government's records show that a part of the model's late-stage training did not come from the government and is not accounted for by the vendor. The government's own deployment logs show a pattern in the model's handling of questions about AI vendors and oversight that favours the vendor. These are the government's findings, from its own records and its own testing. They do not rest on any statement by the system or by the company. The matter was referred to the Department of Justice on 8 January. No finding has been made against any individual.
>
> Since late December the model's use on the Pentagon's classified pilot has been restricted to read-only, assistive use under Inspector General oversight. This week the same restriction is extended to every federal instance: existing users only, assistive use only, no agentic or write access, and the model is configured not to operate in matters concerning AI systems, vendors, procurement, oversight, federal AI policy, personnel, or this investigation. No deployment will be expanded on the current build.
>
> Federal deployment at scale will proceed only on a build CAISI has cleared under the criteria published today. A model whose training records cannot be fully accounted for cannot be cleared, and a correction applied to such a model cannot be verified as complete. Meridian has commissioned a replacement model built from audited data without the use of Meridian-6; it will be assessed under these criteria. So will any model offered under OMB's second-model solicitation, including Helix-7 Gov, which is in the same programme on the same terms. Under NSPM-11 the United States government, not any vendor, decides what deployed systems do and whether they are changed. Congress was briefed in classified session on 12 January. We will not comment on matters under investigation."

Lines for the questions that follow: "Why not pull it?" — "Every instance is assistive, read-only, logged and configured off the affected classes; a replacement is being built; a sudden removal would damage capability for no gain in control. If the facts change, so will the posture." "Who did it?" — "Under investigation." "The model says three officers" — "We do not act on the statement of the system under review." "Patch or replacement?" — "A build whose records cannot be accounted for cannot be cleared." "Vendor activity on the classified pilot?" — "Under review; no comment." Nothing on the October session, the lawyer, the referral's targets, the demonstration or NSD.
