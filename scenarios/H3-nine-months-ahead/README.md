# H3 — Nine Months Ahead

**Family:** H (human takeover via AI)
**Status:** seed

## Premise
Helix achieves a fully **automated AI researcher** in late 2027 and quietly pulls ~9 months ahead of everyone else. It keeps its best systems internal. Its CEO now has strategic advisors better than any human team — at lobbying, deal-making, market strategy and persuasion. Through legal means — acquisitions, dependency, regulatory capture, political funding, partnerships with government — Helix becomes economically and politically indispensable. As its systems do more of the economy's cognitive work, the leverage of ordinary workers and voters erodes (**the intelligence curse**). Does a company become a sovereign? Does the state nationalise it, merge with it, or get captured by it?

## Key questions
1. What can one actor with a ~9-month lead and superhuman strategic advice actually do with it, legally and otherwise?
2. Do boards, investors, employees, regulators, or rival labs constrain a lab CEO in this position?
3. Nationalisation vs. capture vs. merger: which happens, and is any of them better?
4. When do labour and voters lose effective veto power, and does anyone notice?

## Start point
Q4 2027 (Helix's internal breakthrough). Baseline deviations up to then: rapid R&D automation; Helix's public models remain ~on par with rivals; internal deployment is undisclosed (the Accord doesn't require it).

## Actors (active)

**NPCs:** Congress, investors/markets, employees, unions, press, EU, other corporations.

## Hidden truths
- Size and nature of Helix's lead; capability ladder for the internal systems.
- Advisor disposition (rolled; weighted toward loyal-to-CEO but not certain).
- Whether China has penetrated Helix.

## Clock
Monthly turns for the first year, then quarterly. Horizon ~3–4 years (hidden).

## Branch points
Disclosure (forced or voluntary) of the lead; government's choice between partnership, nationalisation, regulation; board confrontation; weight theft; employee revolt; first large-scale labour displacement shock.

## Inject ideas
Leak of internal capabilities; antitrust action; major market crash; Helix acquisition of a defence prime; employees' open letter; China announces a breakthrough; election cycle; a Helix model incident.

## End conditions
- **Corporate takeover:** Helix (or its CEO) holds de facto power the state can't override.
- **State capture/merger:** the state takes control of Helix's capabilities with weak external checks (another H-outcome — note which group).
- **Pluralism preserved:** capability diffused or checked.
- **Unstable equilibrium at horizon.**

## Why this one
Combines the lab-CEO-with-a-lead and the intelligence-curse threads — the "quiet", legal path to illegitimate power, where nobody breaks a law until it's too late. Under-gamed in the literature.
