# H4 — The Last Mover

**Family:** H (human takeover via AI) — at the scale of states
**Status:** prepped

## Premise
In 2028 one great power's AI program (default: the US with Helix/Meridian; option: China wins the roll) reaches strongly superhuman AI R&D and cyber capability ~12 months ahead of its rival. Its leaders face the question nobody has had to answer: **use the window** to lock in permanent dominance — disabling the rival's AI program, enforcing an AI "non-proliferation" regime on everyone else — or share, negotiate, or wait. The rival sees the window closing and weighs sabotage, preemption (including of datacenters), escalation over Taiwan, or capitulation. Allies and third countries pick sides. This is a takeover of the *world* by one state using AI, or a war trying to prevent it.

## Key questions
1. Is a decisive strategic advantage actually decisive, and how fast can it be converted into durable control?
2. Does "Mutual Assured AI Malfunction" deterrence stabilise or destabilise the window? Is it observable/credible?
3. Can a leader credibly commit *not* to use a DSA? What verification would the rival need?
4. Where are the escalation tripwires (Taiwan, datacenter strikes, nuclear C2 fears)?

## Start point
Mid-2028. Deviations: US–China AI dialogue (from Nov 2026) has produced an incident channel but no verification; Taiwan tensions elevated; leader's lab has fully automated AI research internally.

## Actors (active)
| ID | Actor | Core goal | Notable private info |
|---|---|---|---|
| leader-exec | Leading state's executive & NSC | Security; possibly permanent primacy (factions split) | True size of the lead |
| leader-lab | Leading lab(s) (a joint project) | Mission + survival + influence over state decisions | Capability + safety state of the systems |
| rival-exec | Rival state's leadership | Avoid permanent subordination; regime security | Its own lag estimate (noisy); covert programs |
| rival-lab | Rival national-champion lab | Catch up | Stolen material? |
| allies | Key allies bloc (Japan/UK/EU/Taiwan-adjacent) | Security; avoid war; get a seat | — |
| swing-states | Swing powers (India, Gulf, etc.) | Leverage; access | — |

**NPCs:** markets, militaries' lower echelons, UN, publics, the AIs themselves (Control plays them as tools unless prep says otherwise).

## Hidden truths
- True lead size (both sides have noisy estimates).
- Alignment/reliability of leading systems (rolled; misbehaviour can appear at the worst time).
- Covert programs and penetrations.

## Clock
Monthly turns; weekly during crises. Horizon ~2 years (hidden).

## Branch points
Leader's first coercive use of AI capability; rival's decision on sabotage/preemption; any verification offer; Taiwan; ally defection; a misalignment incident in the leading system.

## Inject ideas
Datacenter fire/sabotage (attribution unclear); rival announces matching capability (bluff?); leak of leader's "lockout" plan; AI-enabled financial attack; election in leading state; ally demands access; rogue AI incident muddies attribution.

## End conditions
- **Unipolar lock-in:** one state durably controls frontier AI globally and can prevent rivals' development.
- **Negotiated regime:** verifiable shared governance.
- **Great-power war.**
- **Unstable race at horizon.**

## Why this one
The geopolitical version of "human takeover" — the AI 2027/Superintelligence Strategy question of what a leader does with a window. Intelligence Rising found endgame deals fail and defections spike; this tests whether that's an artefact.
