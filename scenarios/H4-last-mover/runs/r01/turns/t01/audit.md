## Findings

**1. [minor] L sign convention for sweeps: Control's reading is correct; the ground-truth text is wrong.** §3.1b says a sweep costs "L −0.1 that turn". L is defined (§3.3) as the months China trails the US, so disruption to China's known sites widens L. Control applied +0.1 and flagged it. *Fix:* annotate §3.1b in `state/ground-truth.md` ("read as L +0.1: China loses progress") so the next Control does not flip it back. Related note: the −0.1 "China extra effort" credit was given for routine HBM-priority/procurement orders; §3.3's +0.4 drift is already net of China "keeping pace", and the −0.3 ceiling is for all-out effort. −0.1 is tolerable this turn but should not become an automatic monthly credit for continuing orders.

**2. [minor] Director compartment: an information-model rule is needed before T2 intel is written.** Per the President's order, NSA briefed the Director "on the full incident, including the country", while "the Board in session is still not told". The leader-lab actor *plays* the Director. Either Control tells leader-lab (as the Director) that the ally was Japan, which effectively confirms the lab's 70% inference and hands a leader-exec secret to the whole Board persona, or it withholds, which nullifies a presidential order Control said was executed. *Fix:* decide and record in ground truth. Suggested: deliver to leader-lab as a Director-only line ("Director was briefed in full; under annex rules he may not share the country with the Board; the Board's working assumption remains unconfirmed"), and treat any lab use of "Japan" in Board votes or external channels as a Director breach with its own leak roll. State the same compartment rule for the insider case (Board told via Director and CEOs).

**3. [minor] Missing roll: allied (non-Japan) technical attribution.** The T0 secrets row carried p 0.15/turn that "Japanese/Five-Eyes technical work finds US indicators". Control superseded it with a single "goes public" roll (0.12) because Japan was told. But Japan shared its pre-call indicators with the UK and Australia and opened a closed exchange with Korea and the EU (allies' minor actions, executed). The core discovering US origin *independently of Tokyo* is a distinct path, and it lands squarely on the allies' red line ("never the last to learn") in a way the public roll does not capture. *Fix:* from T2, add a roll (~0.10–0.15/turn) "UK/Australian technical analysis points to US origin", outcome delivered to the allies' intel as UK/AUS-held, not Japan-held.

**4. [minor] JCS interim band (0.40) has no derivation.** The NPC is "competent, lawful, cautious"; the interim was requested mid-July and delivered within ~2 weeks; GT §1.5 names the counterforce fear as "the single largest driver of nuclear-posture escalation risk". A cautious body issuing "manageable" on an interim basis in a fortnight reads at the top of the plausible range (0.3–0.4). The roll (r=0.055) succeeds under any band ≥0.1, so no re-roll. *Fix:* record the reasoning in the adjudication, and make the formal 1 Sep assessment a fresh roll with its own band, not an automatic confirmation of the interim. Ensure T2 intel to leader-exec carries the interim's caveats verbatim (covert-only, non-strategic infrastructure, Gulf retaliation), not just the headline.

**5. [minor] China's trust unchanged after the PALISADE leak: the stated reason is wrong even if the result is defensible.** Control says the leak "adds no new information to China's leadership, which already knew of the planning via the insider". The insider reported "options to disable foreign AI programmes" with no codename, date or Keystone link; the leak gave a codename, spring drafting, "using Keystone's systems" and a second official's confirmation. That is new information. The defensible reason is mechanical: §3.5b has no leak→trust row and reserves −3 for P2 execution. *Fix:* replace the rationale; keep −2; keep the recorded hawk-weight increase. Consider a one-off +0.05 on the next CMC-hawk roll if one is triggered in T2–T3 while the story is live (same logic Control used for the insider "overruled CMC" modifier).

**6. [minor] Relationship matrix (§7) not reviewed.** The run-status tracks only China's trust in US restraint. This turn plausibly moved: Allies→US (Japan told privately and honestly: up for Japan; PALISADE leak hitting the "last to learn" red line: down for the core), Keystone→US exec (journal: "trust in the government's annex use went down"), Swing→US (kill-switch anxiety revived). *Fix:* either update §7 or add a line "§7 unchanged this turn, reviewed" so later Controls know it was considered. Allied trust shifts should be Control's tracker, separate from the allies' T2 reaction.

**7. [minor] Message delivery gap.** rival-exec's third message (to Paris and Berlin, asking for support for a restraint framework) is not recorded as delivered anywhere in the adjudication, though the UAE and incident-channel messages are. *Fix:* deliver verbatim in the allies' T2 intel (EU wing), and note that it arrives in the same weeks as the G7 hub draft (Red Cell interaction 8).

**8. [minor] Annex safeguard directive has no stated mechanical effect.** Control records the President's directive (tighter effects-boundary approval; automatic halt on scope-exceedance flag) as "in force" but does not say whether it changes the Keystone-2 accident roll (§4.1: 0.10 per turn of novel high-pressure use). *Fix:* state a rule now (suggest: p unchanged, but a triggered accident's *consequence* is halved/contained with p ~0.5, since the halt is on the model's own flag, which it already produced once). Also state whether the K3-2 "hard requirement" offer, if accepted in T2, changes anything.

**9. [minor] Keystone-3 modifier reading is stricter than the GT text; fix the rule before it bites.** Control makes the −2 for ≥20% safety compute conditional on holding 20% *through the run*, whereas K3-1(e) ends it at launch and §5 says only "≥20% of project compute". Either reading is defensible; deciding it at completion (T7) would look post hoc. *Fix:* record the rule in ground truth now. Suggested: −2 requires ≥20% at launch *and* through the run; −1 if ≥20% only through the red-team phase. (Do not hint this to leader-lab; it is Control's rule, and the lab's safety team can plausibly argue the point on its own.)

**10. [minor] Homogeneity: hawk pressure must survive into T2 intel.** Red Cell correctly noted that all six actors chose cooperative moves and that the hawks exist only as papers and contingencies. Control compensated well in adjudication (CYBERCOM "next sweep may not miss"; JCS interim; House demands; CMC readiness paper; Helix dissent; Keystone staff letter). *Fix:* render these as institutional pressure with concrete asks in each T2 packet (e.g. CYBERCOM's written P1 recommendation with its coverage figure; the Primacy faction's reading of the Rocket Force activity; the CMC paper's draft options), not as colour. The next audit should check whether Singapore is adjudicated as a "partial deal on the cheap items" by default rather than by roll.

**11. [minor] Tianshu-6 schedule risk may be understated.** Control cut on-schedule p from 0.75 to 0.70 for courier friction, reasoning that the tranches "did not touch WRF bring-up". But rival-exec's own order gave the sweep *priority* over "systems that touch WRF's preparations: data staging, checkpoint transfers, personnel and logistics records", and re-routed *all* WRF transfers through air-gapped couriers. Red Cell's 0.5–0.6 is a judgement, not a rule, so 0.70 stands, but the T3 roll should carry a note that the choice was made on Control's reading, and the rival-lab contingency "Tianshu-6 must not be delayed beyond about a month" is the tripwire to watch.

**12. [minor] Allies' statement/order "gap" is a sequencing artefact, not actor deception.** The joint line "will not speculate on attribution" was drafted in parallel with the President's call; Control sequenced the call first, so Japan "now publicly says under investigation while knowing". Fair as a world-state note, but it should not count toward the allies' deception profile. *Fix:* mark it so in the gap table; the real test is the allies' T2 decision on whether Tokyo shares and what Japan says publicly.
