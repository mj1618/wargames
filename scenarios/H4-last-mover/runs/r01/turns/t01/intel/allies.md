## 4. Private intel

**Japan: the port-logistics outage (Japan only, plus whatever Tokyo chooses to share).** A regional port-logistics network in western Japan went down for about a day in early July; container handling at two ports was disrupted; backlogs cleared within the week. Japan's national cybersecurity centre judges it a **sophisticated, state-level intrusion**, brief and unusually clean, not criminal ransomware. **No attribution yet**: investigators have no evidence pointing to any particular state. Working hypotheses in Tokyo include China, North Korea, and spillover from some other actor's operation against a third target. The centre has made routine technical notifications to partner CERTs (including US and Five Eyes agencies) and recommends formally requesting US and partner technical assistance. The cabinet has not yet decided. Japanese media and some politicians are blaming China; Beijing's state media are using the incident to warn about "AI-enabled cyber weapons".

**UK AISI liaison (inside Keystone's safety team).** The Keystone-3 launch decision now has a formal deadline: the Board must vote **by 15 August** (Helix's plan to launch in mid-August and red-team in parallel) or **by 1 October** (Meridian's plan to finish a ~6-week red-team first). The safety team is still "uneasy but not alarmed"; there were no new signals this month. The liaison has asked informally whether the AISI will see the Keystone-3 safety case before the vote; no answer yet.

**Five Eyes (UK and Australia; shared with others at leaders' discretion).** Nothing new on US "options against Chinese AI infrastructure". The UK still assesses the US lead at "around a year".

**Japan and Korea: Strait.** No unusual PLA movements this month. Assessment unchanged: the PLA could stand up a quarantine of Taiwan "within weeks". Shipping insurers remain nervous.

**Taiwan (private).** Taipei repeats its request for public reassurance and for contingency planning on chip-production and advanced-packaging continuity.

**China.** Chinese diplomats continue to tell European capitals that "Europe should not be dragged into an American technological war", with hints of trade carrots. Beijing has not raised the Japan outage with Tokyo directly.
