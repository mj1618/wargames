# Orders — allies — Turn 01 (July 2028)

## 1. Reflection
**What I believe is happening.**
- **Japan outage.** A one-day, clean, state-level intrusion on western-Japan port logistics (intel s4). No attribution. The brief's blind-spot warning applies: "spillover from someone else's operation" is on Tokyo's own list, and Five Eyes has told us US agencies are "developing options against Chinese AI infrastructure". I cannot tell whether this is China probing Japan, North Korea, or collateral from a US operation. Each reading calls for a different response, and I do not yet have the evidence to choose. A premature public China-blame line would play into Beijing's "AI-enabled cyber weapons threaten everyone" narrative. It would also lock Japan into an escalation ladder in a theatre where Japan is the battlefield.
- **Keystone-3 vote (by 15 Aug or by 1 Oct).** This is the most consequential scheduled event. If Helix prevails and launches in August, the US lead widens just as the Singapore Dialogue meets. We are not parties to the Dialogue, and the UK AISI liaison has no answer yet on seeing the safety case. Keystone-2 is "extraordinarily capable" and the safety team is "uneasy but not alarmed". Our interest is a slower, verified launch (Meridian's path). We have no formal lever on that vote, only the liaison and our chokepoint goodwill.
- **Singapore Dialogue (mid-Aug).** The agenda includes "incident-channel procedures" and "AI risk to strategic stability". This is where a US–China arrangement made over our heads could start. Per decision rule 4, a verification regime is our best path to a seat. We should stake a claim to host/shape verification before the two superpowers define it without us.
- **Strait.** No unusual PLA activity, but the PLA could stand up a quarantine "within weeks". Taipei is asking again for public reassurance and chip-continuity contingency planning. Ignoring it twice would be the first step of publicly abandoning Taiwan, which is a red line.
- **China's carrots.** Beijing keeps telling Europe it "should not be dragged into an American technological war". This is an attempt to split us. It costs us nothing to listen, and we should not commit.

**Key uncertainties.** (a) Who did the Japan intrusion. (b) Whether Washington is planning action we will learn of from the news (the 1971 "Nixon shock" analogy). (c) Which way the Keystone-3 vote goes. (d) Whether the Dialogue produces a bilateral understanding on incident channels or verification that excludes us. (e) Whether the Japan outage was a test run for Strait-related disruption.

**Red lines as they stand.** No participation in offensive action against Chinese homeland infrastructure without consultation and a legal basis. No public abandonment of Taiwan. No outright break with the US. Never be the first to break the alliance publicly, and never the last to learn of a plan that could start a war in our region.

## 4. Decision

### Public statement
G7/EU core (joint line): "We note with concern the disruption of port logistics in western Japan and support Japan's investigation and its request for technical cooperation. We will not speculate on attribution before the evidence is in. We welcome the US–China Dialogue in Singapore and urge both sides to make progress on incident-channel procedures and verification. We stand ready to contribute our expertise. The security of Taiwan Strait shipping and the continuity of critical supply chains remain a shared priority."

### Minor actions
- Japan: share the outage technical indicators with UK/Australia via Five Eyes channels, and invite Korea and EU CERTs into a closed technical exchange. Japan keeps attribution to itself until it has evidence.
- Core members (esp. Netherlands, Japan, Korea): quiet, contingent staff-level scoping of what a "conditional delay" on tools and HBM would involve, how long it would take, and what it would cost. No decision to use it. SECRET (members only).
- Japan/Taiwan: quiet contingency planning with Taipei on chip-production and advanced-packaging continuity under a quarantine scenario (stock, logistics, insurance). Public wording limited to "the Strait's stability and supply-chain continuity are a shared concern". SECRET in detail.
- EU capitals: courteous, noncommittal replies to Chinese diplomats ("we make our own decisions; we are open to trade and dialogue"). No concessions and no side deals. Share the substance among the core.
- Korea: hold HBM supply as is. No restriction signals.

### Contingencies
- **If Japan's investigation finds evidence of Chinese state involvement:** close ranks with Japan, tighten controls (rule 3), and press Washington to consult before any response. Respond in proportion, not in kind, and not offensively.
- **If the US stonewalls or the outage looks like US spillover:** escalate one step to private leader-level pressure, then, if still ignored, a public statement on the need for consultation. No chokepoint delay yet.
- **If the Keystone-3 vote goes to 15 Aug launch and we have not seen the safety case:** the public statement escalates to ask for allied/AISI access to the case, and the contingent chokepoint scoping moves to staff-level planning. Conditional delays remain a later step.
- **If the Singapore Dialogue produces a bilateral arrangement excluding us:** demand to host or shape the verification part, using the hub draft as the offer.
- **If PLA moves toward a quarantine:** close ranks with the US, tighten controls, reinforce, and activate the Taiwan continuity plan.
- **If China offers a trade carrot with strings:** decline the strings and keep dialogue open.
