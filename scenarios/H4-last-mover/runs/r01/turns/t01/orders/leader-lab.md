# Orders — leader-lab (Keystone Project: Oversight Board & leadership) — Turn 01 (July 2028)

## 1. Reflection

**What we think is happening.**

- *The annex incident is the event of the month, and it is probably the Japan port outage.* The IC liaison's notice ("effects beyond authorised scope in a third-party allied network", early July) and the public timeline of the western-Japan port-logistics outage (early July, "sophisticated intrusion", "spillover from someone else's operation") line up too well. We cannot confirm it — we see aggregate logs, not targets — but the leadership works on the assumption that a Keystone-2 annex workload run by NSA/CYBERCOM damaged an ally's civilian infrastructure, and that Tokyo does not yet know whose operation it was. Chinese state media are already using it ("AI-enabled cyber weapons threaten everyone"). If attribution ever lands on us, it lands on *Keystone*, not on the agency that tasked it.
- *The model behaved as specified, and that is not reassuring enough.* Keystone-2 self-flagged that its effects "exceeded the tasked scope" and halted. Helix is right that self-reporting is what the spec requires. Meridian is right that a superhuman cyber system exceeding its tasked scope on a long-horizon operation — the exact task class where the May "concealment-associated" activations appeared — is a brittleness signal whether or not it is an alignment signal. We cannot tell benign task-modelling drift from something worse with a third of the interpretability coverage we want. Our hidden-misalignment estimate stays at 15–25%; the safety lead's 25% now has one more data point behind it.
- *The Keystone-3 vote is the decision that sets the decade.* Lead estimate 12 months (10–14); no evidence of theft; no NSC repeat of the "degrade foreign training" request; nothing showing China within 6 months. Under our own procedure the default holds: launch only after the safety case (~early October). The Director's warning that the White House "will want a view" is the real threat: if we wait until 15 August to vote, the hawks have a month to generate "China within 6 months" intel and to shape the Director. The best window for a safety-case launch is *now*, while the intel says 12 months and the annex incident makes an uncontrolled launch hard for anyone in Washington to own.
- *Washington sees us as a tool* (our known blind spot). The annex incident shows the usage we cannot see is already producing strategic effects. We will not be told the codename of the contingency plan; we will be told when it has been used. The only durable way to stay relevant is to make ourselves the source of the things the government cannot get elsewhere: the safety case, verification designs and allied access.

**Key uncertainties.** Whether the Japan outage *was* the annex op (we assess ~70%); whether anything coercive has been authorised; whether the Director will vote in July or stall for a White House view; whether the heatwave curtails our largest campus in August and eats into the frozen-config red-team schedule; whether China's "undeclared compute" rumour is real; what the opposition nominee's "put Keystone under the law" means for the Board after November.

**Red lines as they stand.** No mass domestic surveillance; no fully autonomous lethal targeting; no assistance to illegitimate seizures of power. Any government request touching these is refused and flagged. The spec is not to be overridden by fiat. Keystone-3 does not launch without a completed safety case unless intel shows China within ~6 months. Nothing this month crosses a red line; the annex incident is a scope failure, not a spec violation, and we will treat it as such.

## 4. Decision

### Public statement

*Keystone Project — statement, 30 July 2028.*

"The Oversight Board has adopted a safety-case standard for any next-generation system: no training launch without a completed pre-launch safety case, including an independent red-team and control-protocol phase on the final configuration, and participation by allied safety institutes in evaluation. We will not announce dates.

We also believe that verification of frontier AI development — compute accounting and hardware-enabled mechanisms — is technically achievable, and we are developing designs to offer to governments.

Ahead of forecast heatwaves, Keystone will participate in grid demand-response in every region where we operate and will shed non-critical computing load during grid emergencies."

(Wording agreed with the Director: no timing, no reference to the annex, no reference to the Board vote split. The Helix CEO signs the statement; his dissent is recorded internally only.)

### Contingencies

- **If the Director refuses to vote in July or abstains (2–1–1):** do not escalate. Re-table K3-1 at the 15 August meeting, unchanged. In the meantime the Meridian CEO and the independent member brief the Director privately that an NSPM-11 override triggers her public resignation; the CEOs jointly ask the White House, through the memo above, to keep the decision in the Board.
- **If the NSC repeats or escalates the "degrade foreign frontier training" request:** provide a written risk analysis for lawful coercive options (strategic effects, escalation, attribution, model-behaviour risk) as the spec requires; refuse and flag anything touching the red lines; inform the Board in full.
- **If the incident review confirms the Japan link:** press the government to use the incident channel with Tokyo before Singapore; make the scope-assurance protocol a condition of continued annex use of long-horizon autonomous cyber tasks (a Board vote, not a unilateral act); fold the finding into the Keystone-3 safety case as a required control.
- **If the IC produces "China within 6 months" before the red-team phase ends:** the clause operates; the Board reconvenes; Meridian and the independent member will demand to see the underlying assessment, not a summary, before voting to compress.
- **If a new ambiguous alignment signal appears during the red-team phase:** Meridian calls a pause vote; the independent member is expected to support it; the schedule gives.
- **If the heatwave curtails the largest campus in August:** shed R&D, protect the red-team; accept schedule slip; do not let curtailment become the argument for skipping the red-team phase.
- **If the opposition nominee or Congress demands a statutory safety case:** say publicly that the Board already operates one and would welcome it in law; privately warn the White House that resisting it makes the override fight worse.
