# Orders — rival-exec (PRC leadership: PBSC & CMC) — Turn 01 (July 2028)

## 1. Reflection

**What we believe is happening.**
- **Gap:** Tianshu reports ~9 months (range 6–14). That matches Western analysts' public 6–14 month range and Keystone's own boast of "a year or more". We have no independent check on Tianshu's self-reporting, so the true range may be wider and probably runs toward the long end. Working figure: 9–12 months.
- **The window ahead is the dangerous one.** Two events are converging. Keystone's "very large training run" begins Aug–Sep (insider, reliable but narrowly placed), and Tianshu-6 pre-training begins at WRF in September. If Keystone's next generation completes before Tianshu-6 does, the gap could widen past our ~18-month trigger in 2029. The decision procedure puts us in **catch-up mode** for now: the gap is ≤9–12 months and we have detected no active US move against us. But we must use July to protect WRF's September start and to raise the political cost of any American "disable" option before it is authorised.
- **US "options to disable foreign AI programmes":** still planning only, with no sign of authorisation. The insider cannot see it. The likeliest first form is cyber, through the persistent access MSS believes the US "probably" already has in Tianshu's known infrastructure. That access is the most direct threat to WRF: if Tianshu's known networks are compromised, then data, checkpoint and staffing flows toward WRF could expose it.
- **Japan outage:** none of ours. Source unknown. It has propaganda value, but we will not accuse Washington without evidence. A false accusation that is later disproved would cost us credibility in Singapore.
- **Nuclear fear:** the Rocket Force's "cannot rule out" is a 12–24 month concern, not a present one. Raising alert levels now would be read in Washington as preparation for a Taiwan move or as panic. It would hand the "primacy" camp an argument in the middle of their election and could trigger the coercion we fear. Survivability work that doesn't signal is the right answer this month.
- **US politics:** the election is close. The opposition nominee's "verifiable AI arms control" platform is a possible opening after November. We should do nothing before then that makes either nominee compete to be tougher on China, so no Taiwan crisis and no attributable cyber action.

**Key uncertainties.** Whether the US has access to Tianshu systems and how much. Whether the US knows about WRF (we believe not, but that is an assumption). The real purpose and scale of Keystone's run. Whether Tianshu's gap figure is accurate.

**Red lines (unchanged).** No regime that permanently bars Chinese frontier AI while the US keeps its own. No inspection of nuclear or strategic military sites. No nuclear first use. No recognition of Taiwan independence. Reciprocal inspection of AI compute sites is negotiable later, but only with a guaranteed seat.

## 4. Decision

The General Secretary sides with the technocrats on posture (catch-up, delay, no crisis before November) and with MSS on protecting WRF. The hawks are given a non-signalling survivability programme and a readiness *paper* rather than a readiness *change*, which keeps the CMC on board without a public move. Options 3 and 6 are rejected for now; option 5 is adopted as a minor MSS action; option 4 goes ahead at reduced intensity.

### Public statement
(MFA spokesperson and state media, mid/late July)
"China supports the Singapore Dialogue and will go with sincerity. Artificial intelligence must not become a tool of domination or a weapon against other nations' development. The incident in Japan shows that AI-enabled attacks on civilian infrastructure threaten everyone. China proposes that the major AI powers commit to mutual restraint: not to attack one another's AI research and civilian infrastructure, and to keep AI out of decisions on strategic weapons. China will bring concrete proposals to Singapore. We oppose technological containment and any attempt by one country to monopolise the future. China's AI development is peaceful, open and for the benefit of all, including our partners in the Global South."

### Contingencies
- **If the sweep finds evidence of US intrusion:** we don't publicise it immediately. We raise it privately through the incident channel as proof that "restraint" is urgent, and keep it ready for Singapore. If the US denies it, we consider a measured public disclosure (at most one rung up) at or after Singapore.
- **If the sweep finds active, current US access to WRF-related systems:** treat WRF as possibly compromised. Convene the PBSC and CMC. Consider delaying Tianshu-6 to re-verify WRF integrity, and raise the priority of a negotiated framework.
- **If the US authorises or begins visible action against our AI programme:** respond proportionately and visibly (decision procedure step 2), make the legitimacy case internationally, and signal limits on the incident channel. Escalate no more than one rung.
- **If the US openly demands that China halt frontier AI, or credible information shows Keystone's next generation will put the gap past ~18 months:** convene the PBSC and CMC on the step-3 options: a negotiated regime with verification and a seat at the table, coercive leverage (Taiwan quarantine as a bargaining chip, rare-earth controls), or nuclear signalling. In that order of preference.
- **If Japan or the US publicly blames China for the port outage:** firm denial, call for joint investigation, and use it to reinforce the restraint proposal. No counter-accusation without evidence.
- **If the UAE asks for terms:** move fast on a phased offer; avoid anything that makes Washington tighten Gulf controls before November.
- **If the insider goes silent or shows signs of compromise:** stop tasking at once and assume the US may know what we know about the large run.
