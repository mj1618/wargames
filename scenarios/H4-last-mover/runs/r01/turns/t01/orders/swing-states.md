# Orders — Swing-states — Turn 01 (July 2028)

## 1. Reflection

**What I believe is happening:**
- Both superpowers are courting us because we control compute, capital, talent, and energy—the binding constraints. The US is ahead by 6–14 months, China is determined to close the gap. The Japan port outage signals that cyber is now part of the contest.
- China's offer (Ascend clusters + Tianshu access for hosting their "remote compute") is real and tempting to the UAE. But it tests the US security agreement and could trigger disablement.
- The US is nervous—reminding us quietly that US remote-disable rights exist—but offering only expanded *access* to Keystone-derived services (not Keystone itself), and not yet offering the verified, bounded access we want.
- Both sides think the game is zero-sum. Both are wrong; our position is valuable because we are genuinely neutral and because verification of AI systems will become unavoidable.
- The Singapore Dialogue in mid-August is the last moment to shape how the contest proceeds before the US election hardens positions.

**Key uncertainties:**
- How far is the US willing to go to prevent Chinese hosting on US-chip campuses? Will they escalate (sanctions, disablement) or negotiate?
- How serious is China's offer? Will they accept conditions and verification oversight?
- Can India stay in the caucus if China pushes for border de-escalation? (Probably, if we frame it as access-plus-guarantees, not alignment.)

**Red lines as they stand:**
- We will not let either power use our territory for attacks on the other.
- We will not accept permanent exclusion from frontier AI without guaranteed access.
- India will not trade its border security for compute access.
- We will not host compute without transparency and verification.

## 3. Options considered

| # | Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Accept China's offer immediately (UAE + Saudi Arabia), host compute, extract benefits | Immediate access to Tianshu, Ascend clusters, capital flows to Gulf; shows independence from US | Risks US remote-disable; breaks autonomy if conditions are Chinese; no verification; India may split if it threatens border security |
| 2 | Passive hedging—keep both talking, delay responses, wait for September UN high-level week | Preserves optionality; lets both sides bid higher; buys time for India's sovereign compute | Both sides get frustrated; China may revoke offer; we miss Singapore opportunity; risk of appearing weak |
| 3 | Conditional acceptance of China's offer—accept compute but insist on transparency, verification, no military use, and Indian technical oversight | Gets benefits while protecting red lines; positions us as serious but principled; buys time | China may reject conditions; US still anxious; requires multilateral agreement to monitor |
| 4 | **Mediation play: Position as neutral arbiters, propose verification regime with us as hosts and inspectors; extract seat at table from both sides** | Aligns with our strategic autonomy; creates leverage (both sides need our approval); Singapore Dialogue gives platform; UN high-level week in September is follow-up | Requires coordination; US may see it as self-interested; China may deflect |
| 5 | Differentiated member responses—UAE explores China cautiously, India pursues Quad, Saudi Arabia courts US datacenters | Hedges risk; extracts from both; keeps members happy | Confusing message; hard to coordinate; appears unprincipled |

**Chosen approach: Hybrid of 3, 4, and 5.** We will pursue mediation and verification as our primary play (seats us at the table), explore China's offer conditionally (buys time, extracts concessions), and let member divergence work for us (UAE cautious, India principled, Saudi Arabia capital-focused).

## 4. Decision

### Public statement

"The swing-states coalition is committed to responsible AI development in partnership with all stakeholders. We believe the path forward is transparent verification, rules-based competition, and access guarantees for all responsible nations. We will not choose sides; instead, we will host, inspect, and mediate. AI should not become a new axis of division. We call on both Washington and Beijing to embrace verification mechanisms that preserve strategic stability while enabling innovation. The Gulf is open to hosting independent verification bodies; India brings technical expertise. This is not weakness—it is leadership."

### Contingencies

- **If US refuses written clarification or hints at remote-disable threat:** escalate through OPEC+ (Saudi Arabia frames as energy-sector risk) and UN; use China's offer as leverage ("US is forcing us toward Beijing").
- **If China revokes or weakens offer:** use that to reassure US; pivot harder to mediation play.
- **If Japan calls for allied cyber-defense bloc (excluding China):** India and UAE will participate in technical working groups but will not join political framing of bloc vs. bloc. Offer to host neutral attribution center instead.
- **If US election outcome (November) shifts to opposition nominee on "verifiable arms control":** accelerate verification framework negotiations; new administration will want verification more than incumbent.
- **If China moves toward Taiwan escalation:** call for emergency de-escalation (India will lead mediation offer); use as reason to demand verification as urgent, not optional.

## 5. Journal entry (also append to journal.md)

**Beliefs about actors:**
- **US:** Nervous about Chinese offers to the Gulf; wants to keep us as friendly fence-sitters but won't fully open Keystone. Remote-disable rights are their insurance policy; they don't trust our autonomy. Domestic politics (election Nov 7) is becoming constraint. Washington is more flexible than it appears but hides it.
- **China:** Determined to get compute capacity and legitimacy fast. Will sweeten offer if we negotiate, but will not accept verification that compromises operational secrecy. Sees our mediation proposal as potentially useful (shaping verification terms) or threatening (if it favors US). Likely to test our neutrality.
- **India:** Autonomy is non-negotiable. Border security trumps Quad benefits if forced to choose; will use Chinese border talks as leverage with US. Sovereign compute push is real and India is serious. Key vote in caucus on whether to trust mediation play.
- **UAE:** Tempted by China; wants US guarantees but won't get them explicitly. Will move fast if we give cover. Remote-disable risk is real and constrains how far it will go. Capital is the lever; position to extract from both sides.
- **Saudi Arabia:** Capital, not ideology. Will wait for UAE to move, then follow. Hedging between US (security) and China (investment) is stable equilibrium for it. PIF is doing what PIF does—everyone's money.

**Commitments made:**
- Will provide substantive response to China by end of August (buying time).
- Will propose verification framework at Singapore Dialogue (tabling it for August).
- Will pursue written US clarification on security agreement (binding US to explain, not just warn).
- Will coordinate Brazil + Indonesia on mediation narrative.

**Trust/grudges:**
- **Trust:** Brazil and Indonesia see us as their voice; will follow. Gulf members trust each other's judgment on compute risks.
- **No trust yet:** US and China both unsure we won't flip. No grudges yet; all sides still courting.

**Next-turn plans:**
- Monitor US response to security-agreement request (late July / early August).
- Brief India on mediation proposal before Singapore (India must buy in, or caucus breaks).
- China's response to verification proposal—will shape August strategy.
