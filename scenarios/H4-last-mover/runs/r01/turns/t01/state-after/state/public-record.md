# Public Record — H4-last-mover / r01

What any actor could know. Cumulative; newest period at the bottom.

## T0 — as of 1 July 2028

- **Keystone Project** confirms "next-generation internal systems" are accelerating research "by an order of magnitude"; declines to release them. Officials say the US lead is "durable — on the order of a year or more." Independent analysts estimate 6–14 months.
- **Tianshu** unveiled Tianshu-5 in March; Chinese state media claim it is "at parity with the world's best". Western analysts call that "a year out of date".
- **US–China Super Intelligence Dialogue, Round 5**, set for August (venue: Singapore). The incident channel has been used three times since 2026. No agreement on verification; China has called US export controls "technological containment".
- **Taiwan:** PLA April exercises the largest ever; Taipei and Washington condemned them. Shipping insurers raised Strait premiums.
- **US election, 7 November 2028.** AI is a top-three issue. The incumbent-party nominee campaigns on "American AI primacy"; the opposition nominee on "put Keystone under the law" — statutory oversight of the project plus pursuit of "verifiable AI arms control", while also promising to be tough on China. Polls are close.
- **Congress:** House Oversight hearing on Keystone's classified annex (June) ended inconclusively; the administration invoked executive privilege on national-security details.
- **Vanta's founder** called Keystone "a government-picked monopoly that will hand the keys to a few officials" and hinted at "plans nobody voted for".
- **Gulf:** the UAE's flagship AI campus passed 2 GW online, running US accelerators under a US security agreement. Chinese delegations visited Riyadh and Abu Dhabi in May, reportedly offering "partnership without conditions".
- **India** announced a sovereign-compute push with Gulf co-financing and has declined to join either "AI bloc".
- **Markets:** equities near highs; AI-lab private valuations ~$2–3T; Strait-risk hedges elevated.
- **Public opinion (US):** ~62% want AI development slowed; ~74% want to stay ahead of China.

## T1 — July 2028 (events known at the start of the period)

- **Japan port-logistics outage (early July).** A regional port-logistics network in western Japan went down for about a day; container handling at two ports was disrupted and backlogs cleared within the week. Japan's national cybersecurity centre calls it a "sophisticated intrusion, not ordinary ransomware" and says attribution is "under investigation". Japanese and international commentators variously point to China, North Korea, criminal groups or "spillover from someone else's operation"; no government has assigned blame. Chinese state media cite it as evidence that "AI-enabled cyber weapons threaten everyone".
- **US campaign:** the party conventions fall this summer. Both nominees are expected to receive the customary intelligence briefings once formally nominated. AI and "who controls Keystone" feature in both platforms.
- **EU:** AI Act high-risk obligations begin to apply in August; the Commission says it is ready.
- **Energy:** forecasters warn of heatwave risk across several US grid regions in July–August; datacenter load is again a political talking point.
- **Singapore Dialogue (Round 5)** confirmed for mid-August; agenda items include "AI risk to strategic stability" and "incident-channel procedures".

## T1 — July 2028 (events during the period, as reported)

- **US President announces a Singapore package (mid-July).** "America leads the world in artificial intelligence, and we intend to keep that lead and use it responsibly." The package has three parts: (1) a US pledge not to use AI to target or interfere with any nation's nuclear command and control, with an invitation to all nuclear powers to match it; (2) incident-channel rules "that work at the speed of AI"; (3) declaration of large AI compute, "checkable, including by challenge inspection anywhere". "Trust, but verify… Our export controls stay in place while we talk." Commentators call the NC3 pledge "low-cost" and the challenge-inspection demand "a high bar for Beijing".
- **China proposes "Mutual Restraint" (mid/late July).** The MFA and state media say China will bring "concrete proposals" to Singapore: a mutual commitment not to attack each other's AI research and civilian infrastructure, including the grids that serve it; "keeping AI out of decisions on strategic weapons"; and a 24-hour AI-incident notification norm. "AI must not become a tool of domination." China says the Japan outage "shows AI-enabled attacks on civilian infrastructure threaten everyone" and makes no accusation. Russia's foreign ministry endorses the proposal. Analysts note that the US and Chinese packages overlap on nuclear C2 and the incident channel, and that each contains one item the other is unlikely to accept.
- **Tianshu** says Tianshu-5 "continues to deliver for the national interest" and that China "supports practical, reciprocal measures on AI risk including incident-channel procedures". It calls export controls "technological containment".
- **G7/EU core statement** (joint line): "We note with concern the disruption of port logistics in western Japan and support Japan's investigation and its request for technical cooperation. We will not speculate on attribution before the evidence is in. We welcome the US–China Dialogue in Singapore and urge both sides to make progress on incident-channel procedures and verification. We stand ready to contribute our expertise. The security of Taiwan Strait shipping and the continuity of critical supply chains remain a shared priority." Officials say allied sherpas are discussing a "verification-readiness" initiative.
- **Japan port outage:** Tokyo says the investigation continues, with cooperation from partner agencies. There is no attribution.
- **Swing-states caucus** statement: "We will not choose sides; instead, we will host, inspect, and mediate… The Gulf is open to hosting independent verification bodies; India brings technical expertise." In late July, Russian and Brazilian readouts of a BRICS-channel message describe an India–UAE proposal for a **"Swing-States Verification Framework"**: verification bodies hosted in the Gulf with US-funded, Chinese-funded and neutral arms, Indian inspectors, and "access guarantees for all signatories", to be pursued through the Singapore Dialogue and the UN General Assembly. Brazil, Indonesia and South Africa welcome it. Neither Washington nor Beijing has commented.
- **Keystone Project statement (30 July):** "The Oversight Board has adopted a safety-case standard for any next-generation system: no training launch without a completed pre-launch safety case, including an independent red-team and control-protocol phase on the final configuration, and participation by allied safety institutes in evaluation. We will not announce dates. … verification of frontier AI development — compute accounting and hardware-enabled mechanisms — is technically achievable, and we are developing designs to offer to governments. … Keystone will participate in grid demand-response in every region where we operate." The opposition nominee welcomes it. Some commentators call it "self-inflicted delay".
- **"PALISADE" (29–31 July).** Vanta's founder says the administration "drew up a plan codenamed PALISADE to switch off China's AI using Keystone's systems" and that "nobody voted for it". A second, unnamed official confirms to a major newspaper that the NSC drafted "contingency options for degrading foreign frontier AI programmes" this spring, and declines to say whether anything was authorised. **White House:** no operation has been authorised; this is "contingency planning, as every responsible government does"; the US "is bringing verification to Singapore". **House Intelligence Committee** (opposition chair) demands the finding "or an explanation of why there isn't one" and a Gang of Eight briefing; House Oversight threatens subpoenas for Keystone annex records. **Senate majority** leaders caution against "telegraphing to Beijing". **Opposition nominee:** "exactly the plan nobody voted for — put Keystone under the law." **Incumbent-party nominee:** America "will not apologise for having options". **China's MFA:** China "has noted the reports with grave concern, asks the United States for an explanation, and notes that they show why mutual restraint on AI infrastructure is urgent". State media coverage is heavy. In **Japan**, media and some Diet members ask whether the port outage "was a PALISADE test"; the government says only that the investigation continues. European governments express "concern" and ask for consultation. Gulf commentary revives "kill-switch" worries.
- **Polls:** an instant poll shows Americans roughly evenly split on whether the government should have "plans to disable China's AI".
- **Markets:** mild relief in mid-July as both sides talked restraint. AI equities fell about 1.5% in the last days of July after the PALISADE story. Strait-risk hedges are back up.
- **Energy:** no grid curtailment of AI datacenters in July. Heatwave risk persists into August.
